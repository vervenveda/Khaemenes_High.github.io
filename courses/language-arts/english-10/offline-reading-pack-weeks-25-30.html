WEEK 25 · UNIT 09

Question, Scope & Inquiry Design

Broad topic

School start times.

Background packet

A fictional district summary reports that secondary schools begin between 7:20 and 8:45 a.m. across six campuses. It lists attendance and transportation concerns but does not provide health outcomes or explain why schedules differ.

Worked narrowing

Too broad: “Are school start times good?” Better: “How might moving a high school start from 7:25 to 8:15 affect attendance and transportation in a district with shared bus routes?” The narrower question identifies population, change, outcomes and a local constraint.

Independent task

Turn one broad topic into a focused question, three subquestions, keyword/synonym list, evidence types needed, and one boundary stating what the project will not try to prove.

WEEK 26 · UNIT 09

Reliability, Bias & Lateral Reading

Source A · institutional report

A fictional transportation department publishes route times, methodology and a downloadable dataset. It has direct access to operational records but may emphasize system feasibility.

Source B · advocacy page

A fictional student coalition summarizes sleep research and argues for later starts. It links to several studies but omits one study that found mixed attendance effects.

Source C · unattributed post

A viral image claims “later start times raise grades by 20%” without naming a study, population, date or measure.

Worked evaluation

Bias does not automatically make a source useless; relevance, authority, method, evidence transparency and corroboration matter. The institutional source may be strongest for route logistics, while a peer-reviewed study might be stronger for health outcomes.

Independent task

Build an include/caution/reject matrix. For each source, identify author/organization, purpose, evidence, method visibility, date, relevant expertise, lateral verification target and the claim the source can actually support.

WEEK 27 · UNIT 09

Traceable Notes, Paraphrase & Synthesis

Original note

Source sentence: “Across three semesters, first-period tardiness declined from 12.4% to 8.1% after the schedule change, while overall daily attendance changed by less than one percentage point.”

Bad paraphrase

“Later starts dramatically improved attendance.” This distorts the source by converting a specific tardiness change into a broad attendance claim.

Accurate paraphrase

“In the fictional three-semester record, first-period tardiness fell by 4.3 percentage points, but overall daily attendance changed little.”

Synthesis model

A synthesis paragraph can place logistics and outcome evidence in conversation: a later start may reduce first-period tardiness, but transportation constraints and limited change in overall attendance complicate claims of broad improvement.

Independent task

Create traceable notes with source ID, locator, quotation/paraphrase distinction and your own commentary. Write one synthesis paragraph using at least two sources and label one disagreement or limitation.

WEEK 28 · UNIT 10

Expository Research Writing

Source set · Cooling school grounds

A fictional facilities report compares shade trees, shade sails and reflective coatings. Trees provide long-term shade but require years to mature; sails provide immediate shade but require installation and inspection; coatings can reduce surface temperature but do not create overhead shade.

Expository challenge

The goal is not to pick a winner. The writer must explain how each option works, where evidence is strong or limited, and which factors matter under different conditions.

Worked synthesis

Organize by decision factor—speed, maintenance, cost, shade coverage—rather than by one source per paragraph. This prevents a research paper from becoming a stack of summaries.

Independent task

Draft a multi-source explanatory section with a controlling idea, at least three source integrations, one limitation, and a visual/table concept that clarifies rather than decorates.

WEEK 29 · UNIT 10

Research-Based Argument

Competing evidence

A student group favors tree planting for long-term benefit. A facilities manager favors shade sails for immediate relief. A budget memo warns that both options have ongoing maintenance costs.

Argument lesson

A nuanced research argument defines the decision standard before judging evidence. Cost, speed, durability, accessibility and environmental effect may point in different directions.

Worked model

A defensible thesis might recommend a phased combination rather than forcing a false either/or choice. The argument should explain why the selected criteria justify that recommendation and acknowledge what the evidence cannot resolve.

Independent task

Write a claim, evidence chain, counterclaim, rebuttal and works-cited plan. Audit every source sentence for accurate scope and level of certainty.

WEEK 30 · UNIT 10

Digital Publication, Multimedia & Presentation

Publication problem

The research paper must become a public-facing digital product. A slide deck that simply copies paragraphs from the paper fails to adapt the material.

Design principle

Use hierarchy, direct labels, captions, descriptive links, readable contrast, alt text, transcript/captions for audio or video, and a visible source record. Design must not exaggerate a weak result through scale, animation or cropped axes.

Presentation lesson

A formal presentation should state the question, evidence, conclusion, limitation and audience action. Delivery choices should improve comprehension rather than replace evidence.

Independent task

Create a storyboard for an accessible publication and a 5–7 minute presentation. Include source credits, one visual-data integrity check, one accessibility audit and one reflection on how the medium changed your explanation.
