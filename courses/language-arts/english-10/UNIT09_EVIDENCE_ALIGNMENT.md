# English 10 · Unit 09 Evidence Alignment
## Research Foundations

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 09 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Focus Question → Evaluate Source → Capture Accurately → Synthesize → Document**

Grade 10 establishes the full research cycle with stronger independence than Grade 9: scope control, lateral verification, accurate notes, paraphrase, citation, and cross-source synthesis.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 25 · Research Questions, Scope & Inquiry Design
1. **Focus Question:** Convert broad curiosity into a question with a clear subject, scope, significance, and feasible evidence base.
2. **Search:** Generate a search plan using subquestions, key terms, synonyms, dates, organizations, and source-type targets.
3. **Test Scope:** Run preliminary searches and identify whether the question is too broad, too narrow, biased, or effectively unanswerable with available evidence.
4. **Refine:** Revise the question and subquestions based on what the source landscape actually supports.
5. **Document:** Submit an inquiry rationale that states the question, scope, why it matters, search strategy, and one expected limitation.

### Week 26 · Source Reliability, Bias & Lateral Reading
1. **Evaluate Source:** Identify author/organization, publication venue, date, purpose, evidence base, and the claim the source is actually making.
2. **Lateral Read:** Investigate the source externally using independent references rather than relying on its self-description.
3. **Compare:** Compare two sources on the same issue for reliability, relevance, expertise, and framing using the same criteria.
4. **Test:** Identify one bias or credibility concern and determine whether it affects the source’s facts, interpretation, scope, or only presentation.
5. **Document:** Complete a source-evaluation matrix with strengths, limitations, corroboration, and a justified use/no-use decision.

### Week 27 · Note-Taking, Paraphrase, Citation & Synthesis
1. **Capture Accurately:** Take notes that clearly distinguish quotation, paraphrase, summary, and your own reasoning at the moment of capture.
2. **Paraphrase:** Rewrite a source idea in genuinely new language and structure while preserving meaning and recording the citation.
3. **Synthesize:** Group notes by idea or relationship across sources rather than by source sequence.
4. **Test:** Audit one paragraph for patchwriting, dropped quotations, unsupported synthesis, or citation mismatch and revise it.
5. **Document:** Produce a synthesis paragraph plus source trail showing exactly how each source contributed to the conclusion.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 09 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 25–27. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, synthesis, source evaluation, contextualization, research, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 09 methods;
- a cumulative literary-analysis, research, synthesis, scholarly-argument, or limitation audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved research sources. Exact source, author, publication date, edition, translator, database/archive location, research method, and provenance remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 09 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds sustained-reading tracking, research design, source evaluation, lateral verification, synthesis, citation, scholarly integration, peer review, qualification, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 09 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. primary-text, scholarly-source, and research custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
