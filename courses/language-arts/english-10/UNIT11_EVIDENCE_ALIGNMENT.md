# English 10 · Unit 11 Evidence Alignment
## Drama, Shakespeare & Performance

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 11 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Read Stage Evidence → Decode Language → Test Performance → Analyze Interpretation → Defend**

Grade 10 deepens drama study by linking stagecraft, Shakespearean language, rhetoric, irony, and performance decisions to evidence rather than treating performance as decoration.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 31 · Drama Elements, Conflict, Irony & Stagecraft
1. **Read Stage Evidence:** Identify how dialogue, stage direction, entrance/exit, silence, props, or spatial relationships contribute meaning beyond plot.
2. **Interpret:** Explain how dramatic irony or conflict changes the audience’s understanding of a character’s words or choices.
3. **Compare:** Compare two plausible staging choices for the same moment and identify which textual details support each.
4. **Test Performance:** Rehearse or storyboard the scene with one changed performance variable—pace, blocking, tone, pause, or emphasis—and observe the interpretive effect.
5. **Defend:** Write a stagecraft claim supported by dialogue and performance evidence, with one reasonable alternative interpretation.

### Week 32 · Shakespearean Language, Rhetoric & Performance
1. **Decode Language:** Paraphrase a difficult passage while preserving syntax relationships, imagery, rhetorical moves, and important ambiguity.
2. **Trace Rhetoric:** Identify one pattern of image, syntax, repetition, antithesis, or appeal and explain how it shapes character or persuasion.
3. **Perform:** Test two deliveries of the same lines and note how stress, pause, pace, and gesture change the meaning.
4. **Counter:** Develop an alternative interpretation of the passage and identify which language supports each reading.
5. **Defend:** Present a language-to-performance argument explaining why one interpretation is strongest without claiming it is the only possible reading.

### Week 33 · Drama Analysis & Performance Project
1. **Frame Interpretation:** State the interpretation the performance will make visible and identify the exact textual evidence behind it.
2. **Design Performance:** Choose blocking, delivery, visual elements, and pacing that support the interpretation rather than merely entertain.
3. **Test Audience:** Gather audience evidence about what character relationship, conflict, irony, or theme the performance communicated.
4. **Revise:** Adjust the performance where audience evidence shows ambiguity, contradiction, or unsupported emphasis.
5. **Defend:** Submit a performance reflection connecting textual evidence, interpretive choices, audience response, revision, and one limitation.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 11 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 31–33. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, publication, rhetoric, interpretation, accessibility, and long-form reading habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 11 methods;
- a cumulative publication, performance, long-form inquiry, seminar, or oral-defense audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, dramatic texts, long-form works, mentor texts, and approved multimedia sources. Exact source, edition, translator, creator, performance version, publication venue, media provenance, and citation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 11 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds publication planning, source credit, editing, accessibility review, dramatic/performance analysis, sustained-reading tracking, critical-lens evaluation, seminar leadership, oral defense, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 11 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. source, performance, publication, and long-form reading custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
