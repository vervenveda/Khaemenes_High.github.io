Read now · The Unfinished Map

Original Khaemenes Academy practice passage · included offline

The student council posted a map of the walking routes to school. Blue lines crossed the neighborhood, but one narrow footbridge was absent. A note below the map said the routes were safe for everyone. Amara, who crossed the footbridge every morning, asked how the council had tested that claim.

At the next meeting, the mapmaker explained that the map included only streets surveyed by the school bus team. The footbridge had not been evaluated; its absence was not evidence that it was unsafe. The council revised the note to say, “These marked routes were surveyed by the bus team; other routes have not yet been assessed.”

Amara kept both versions of the map. The changed wording did not solve every travel problem, but it made the limits of the evidence visible to readers.

Worked reasoning model

Observation: the first map omits the footbridge. Inference: its safety status remains unknown, because the survey did not evaluate it. Unsupported inference: the footbridge must be dangerous because it is missing.

Practice with the passage

  1. Identify two details that establish what the mapmakers actually investigated.
  2. Write a supported inference and quote the detail that warrants it.
  3. Explain how the revised note improves precision without claiming that every route is safe.

Preserve your evidence: Keep your responses in the existing weekly notes or portfolio. No new record-storage mechanism is required.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading & Evidence Lab

Objective: Apply close-reading routines to annotate purposeful details, distinguish observation from inference, and explain significance.

Mini-Lesson

Model a two-pass reading: first for comprehension and structure, then for evidence, author choices, patterns, contradictions, and significance. Review the difference between quotation, paraphrase, inference, and commentary.

Assigned reading: The Unfinished Map above. Reread and annotate this included passage for today's lab.

Guided Practice

Read or reread the assigned selection. Annotate at least eight purposeful details and classify each annotation by skill or standard. Use the week’s language focus—phrases, clauses, sentence patterns, and precise evidence integration—when writing notes.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence ladder: observation → quotation/detail → paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance. Identify the strongest and weakest evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated text, evidence ladder, and one defensible inference

Support and access options

Reduce the text span, provide guided annotation symbols, pre-highlight one model passage, or allow oral rehearsal before writing.

Extension

Develop a plausible alternative interpretation and identify the evidence that would support or weaken it.

Thursday · Day 4

Discussion, Feedback & Revision

Objective: Use collaborative reasoning and targeted revision to improve clarity, accuracy, audience fit, and intellectual fairness.

Mini-Lesson

Practice the feedback protocol: affirm a strength, ask a genuine question, identify one evidence or reasoning gap, and recommend one precise revision. Review active listening, paraphrasing a peer, and respectful disagreement.

Guided Practice

Discuss the essential question using at least two pieces of evidence. Compare interpretations or approaches, then test each against the text, source quality, or task criteria.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product. Complete a revision memo naming at least three changes, the reason for each change, and the effect on the reader or listener.

Evidence to Preserve

Discussion record, peer/teacher feedback, revised product, and revision memo

Support and access options

Allow written discussion, partner rehearsal, teacher conference, checklist-based feedback, or one focused revision target.

Extension

Write a brief response to the strongest counterinterpretation or counterargument raised during discussion.
