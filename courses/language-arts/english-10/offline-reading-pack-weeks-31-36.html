WEEK 31 · UNIT 11

Stagecraft, Conflict & Dramatic Irony

Original scene

MARA: You locked the side door? / ELIAS: Before sunset. / [A key falls from Elias’s coat. Mara sees it; Elias does not.] / MARA: Then no one can enter. / ELIAS: No one. / [A knock sounds from the side corridor.]

Close-reading lesson

Drama communicates through speech and stage action. The audience may know more than a character, producing dramatic irony. Stage directions can contradict dialogue, reveal power or create pacing that silent summary misses.

Worked interpretation

Elias’s certainty is undermined by the dropped key and knock. An actor could deliver “No one” confidently or nervously; either choice must be justified by the scene’s evidence and surrounding action.

Independent task

Annotate dialogue, stage direction, conflict, irony and one unanswered question. Propose two defensible performance choices and explain how each changes audience interpretation.

WEEK 32 · UNIT 11

Shakespearean Syntax, Rhetoric & Performance

Practice lines · course-created in early-modern style

If honor wear a borrowed face tonight, / let daylight ask whose name it truly bears. / I praise the crown, yet fear the hand beneath it.

Language method

Do not paraphrase word by word first. Identify subject, verb, clauses, figurative image and rhetorical turn. Then restate the thought in current syntax without erasing ambiguity.

Worked paraphrase

“If honor is only pretending tonight, daylight will reveal who actually deserves the name. I respect the office, but I distrust the person exercising its power.” The paraphrase clarifies syntax while preserving tension between institution and individual.

Independent task

Mark syntax, image, contrast and rhetorical appeal. Produce a modern paraphrase and two performance versions using different pauses or emphases, then defend the version that best fits the words.

WEEK 33 · UNIT 11

Performance as an Interpretation

Director’s problem

A scene can be staged as reconciliation, manipulation or uneasy compromise depending on pace, distance, gesture, prop use and tone.

Evidence rule

Performance choices are arguments. A director should be able to point to words, stage directions, structural position or recurring motifs that justify each major choice.

Worked model

If a character says “I forgive you” immediately after concealing evidence, a warm embrace and a guarded physical distance create very different readings. Neither is automatically correct; the production must explain its evidence.

Independent task

Create a performance plan with blocking, vocal choices, one prop or visual choice, one alternative interpretation and a reflection explaining what the performance reveals that silent reading did not.

WEEK 34 · UNIT 12

Long-Form Novel: Character, Motif & Structure

Model pattern

In a fictional novel, a train station appears at the opening, midpoint and final chapter. At first it represents escape; later it becomes a place of delay; by the ending, the protagonist chooses to remain there and meet someone arriving.

Reading method

Track how repeated settings, objects, phrases and conflicts change function over time. A motif claim should explain development, not just repetition.

Worked claim

The station shifts from imagined escape to chosen encounter, showing that the protagonist’s idea of freedom changes from leaving others to meeting them on different terms.

Independent task

For the approved novel, build a motif tracker, character arc and structural map using evidence from at least four separated locations. Identify one detail that complicates your current interpretation.

WEEK 35 · UNIT 12

Novel Analysis & Portfolio Conference

Conference principle

The novel analysis and portfolio conference should work together: one proves close reading of a long work; the other proves broader development across the course.

Evidence architecture

Use exact novel evidence, one counterreading, one meaningful revision and selected course artifacts that show change in reading, writing, research, speaking or publication.

Worked model

A conference claim such as “I became better at analysis” becomes stronger when tied to a specific early/later comparison and a novel paragraph where evidence is interpreted rather than merely quoted.

Independent task

Prepare a novel-analysis outline, select portfolio artifacts, and write a conference script that names three specific changes in skill or independence.

WEEK 36 · UNIT 12

Final Review, Corrections & Transition to English III

Unfamiliar-text practice

Use a short unseen passage to identify claim/theme, evidence, author choice, inference and one alternative interpretation. Do not memorize a model response.

Correction method

Classify errors as comprehension, evidence selection, reasoning, source use, organization or language control. Correction should target the cause, not just the answer.

Transition planning

Use final evidence to choose one reading goal, one writing/research goal and one speaking/listening goal for English III.

Independent task

Complete a review map, corrections log and final reflection that cites specific course evidence and states a realistic next-step plan.
