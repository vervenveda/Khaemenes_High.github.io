WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Universal Themes Across Texts

Practice pair

TEXT A: A traveler keeps a house key years after the house is sold. TEXT B: A family removes every name from a shared recipe book before giving it away. Both concern memory, but one preserves an object while the other deliberately erases attribution.

Comparative model

A topic such as memory, family or belonging is not yet a theme. A thematic claim states what a text suggests about that topic, then compares how another text confirms, revises or contests the idea.

Independent task

Compare two approved authentic texts from different cultures, eras or genres. Record exact source custody, then build a matrix for theme, evidence, form, context and meaningful difference.

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Diction, Syntax, Voice & Tone

Practice contrast

A: ‘The room was empty when the vote ended.’ B: ‘By the time the vote ended, even the chairs seemed to have withdrawn.’ The factual situation overlaps, but diction and syntax create different attitudes toward the event.

Style model

Name the linguistic choice precisely—sentence length, clause order, repeated sound, concrete image, register, connotation—then explain how it shapes voice or tone. Avoid treating tone as a list of adjectives disconnected from language.

Independent task

Annotate an approved passage for four style features. Create a short style imitation, then explain which features you intentionally retained and where your own voice diverged.

Return to Week 08 classroom and saved work

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Literary Analysis Essay & Revision

Analysis architecture

A literary essay should make a contestable claim, use passage-specific evidence, explain how author choices produce meaning, address a complication or alternative reading, and revise for reasoning—not merely grammar.

Revision model

Before: ‘The author uses imagery to show sadness.’ After: ‘The repeated images of emptied rooms shift from literal absence to chosen withdrawal, complicating the narrator’s claim that the separation was accidental.’

Independent task

Use an approved previously studied text. Build a thesis/evidence map, draft one analytical paragraph, identify the strongest counterreading, and write a revision memo naming at least two substantive changes.

WEEK 10 · UNIT 04

Central Ideas & Structure

Original practice civic excerpt

Our town can widen the bridge this year, or continue repairing the same bottleneck every season. The first choice costs more now; the second has already cost us time, missed deliveries, and emergency delays.

Rhetorical model

Central ideas develop through structure: problem/solution, cause/effect, comparison, concession or chronology. Identify how sections build on one another before judging whether the argument is persuasive.

Independent task

Select an approved historical American speech or essay. Record speaker/writer, title, date, stable source and assigned passage. Map the central idea through at least three structural moves.

Return to Week 10 classroom and saved work

WEEK 11 · UNIT 04

Purpose, Audience & Rhetorical Situation

Practice situation

A mayor addresses residents after a factory closure. A union representative speaks to workers the same day. Both discuss economic recovery, but their audiences, authority and desired actions differ.

Model

Rhetorical situation includes speaker, audience, occasion, purpose, constraints and available evidence. Ethos, pathos and logos are not ingredients added mechanically; they are choices shaped by that situation.

Independent task

Analyze an approved speech/essay for audience, purpose, occasion, constraints and appeals. Include one appeal that may work differently for a second audience.

Return to Week 11 classroom and saved work

WEEK 12 · UNIT 04

Rhetorical Devices & Analysis Essay

Original practice lines

We repaired the road. We reopened the clinic. We rebuilt the school. Now we must rebuild trust.

Device model

Repetition and parallelism matter when their placement and variation shape emphasis. Figurative language, analogy and contrast should be interpreted in context rather than counted.

Independent task

Compare two approved speeches or essays using repetition, analogy, parallelism or figurative language. Build a device/effect/evidence matrix, then draft a thesis for the rhetorical analysis essay.
