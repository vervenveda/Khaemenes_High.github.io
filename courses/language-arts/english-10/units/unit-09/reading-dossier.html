WEEK 25 · UNIT 09

Question, Scope & Inquiry Design

Broad topic

School start times.

Background packet

A fictional district summary reports that secondary schools begin between 7:20 and 8:45 a.m. across six campuses. It lists attendance and transportation concerns but does not provide health outcomes or explain why schedules differ.

Worked narrowing

Too broad: “Are school start times good?” Better: “How might moving a high school start from 7:25 to 8:15 affect attendance and transportation in a district with shared bus routes?” The narrower question identifies population, change, outcomes and a local constraint.

Independent task

Turn one broad topic into a focused question, three subquestions, keyword/synonym list, evidence types needed, and one boundary stating what the project will not try to prove.

WEEK 26 · UNIT 09

Reliability, Bias & Lateral Reading

Source A · institutional report

A fictional transportation department publishes route times, methodology and a downloadable dataset. It has direct access to operational records but may emphasize system feasibility.

Source B · advocacy page

A fictional student coalition summarizes sleep research and argues for later starts. It links to several studies but omits one study that found mixed attendance effects.

Source C · unattributed post

A viral image claims “later start times raise grades by 20%” without naming a study, population, date or measure.

Worked evaluation

Bias does not automatically make a source useless; relevance, authority, method, evidence transparency and corroboration matter. The institutional source may be strongest for route logistics, while a peer-reviewed study might be stronger for health outcomes.

Independent task

Build an include/caution/reject matrix. For each source, identify author/organization, purpose, evidence, method visibility, date, relevant expertise, lateral verification target and the claim the source can actually support.

WEEK 27 · UNIT 09

Traceable Notes, Paraphrase & Synthesis

Original note

Source sentence: “Across three semesters, first-period tardiness declined from 12.4% to 8.1% after the schedule change, while overall daily attendance changed by less than one percentage point.”

Bad paraphrase

“Later starts dramatically improved attendance.” This distorts the source by converting a specific tardiness change into a broad attendance claim.

Accurate paraphrase

“In the fictional three-semester record, first-period tardiness fell by 4.3 percentage points, but overall daily attendance changed little.”

Synthesis model

A synthesis paragraph can place logistics and outcome evidence in conversation: a later start may reduce first-period tardiness, but transportation constraints and limited change in overall attendance complicate claims of broad improvement.

Independent task

Create traceable notes with source ID, locator, quotation/paraphrase distinction and your own commentary. Write one synthesis paragraph using at least two sources and label one disagreement or limitation.
