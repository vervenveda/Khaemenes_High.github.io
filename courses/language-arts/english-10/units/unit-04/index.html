Unit 04

American Speeches, Essays & Rhetoric

Study historical American speeches and essays for structure, central idea, purpose, audience, appeals, devices, and rhetorical effectiveness.

Begin Week 10Unit AssessmentPortfolio
OverviewThree WeeksAssessmentStandards

Essential Question

How do historical writers shape central ideas and persuade particular audiences?

Learning Priorities

  • Read complex literary and informational texts with purposeful annotation.
  • Use precise evidence and reasoning rather than unsupported opinion.
  • Draft, revise, edit, and publish work for a clear audience and purpose.
  • Discuss ideas fairly, listen actively, and respond with intellectual honesty.
  • Preserve major artifacts and reflections in the English II portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Rhetorical analysis essay on a historical American speech or essay.

Three-Week Sequence

Learn, practice, compose, and demonstrate.

Week 10

Historical American Speeches: Central Ideas & Structure

How do historical speeches and essays build central ideas through structure and evidence?

transitional logic, semicolons, colons, and organizational signals
Open Week

Week 11

Purpose, Audience, Rhetorical Situation & Appeals

How do speakers adapt choices to purpose, audience, occasion, and context?

rhetorical questions, purposeful repetition, and sentence emphasis
Open Week

Week 12

Rhetorical Devices, Figurative Language & Analysis Essay

How do rhetorical devices and figurative language intensify an argument or message?

parallel structure, punctuation for emphasis, and cohesive analysis
Open Week

Performance Evidence

Rhetorical analysis essay on a historical American speech or essay.

Students submit planning, text evidence, at least one meaningful draft, feedback or conference notes, the finished product, and a reflection explaining the strongest revision and the next goal.

Standards Emphasis

Measured through authentic reading, writing, speaking, and reflection.

  • ELA.10.R.2.1 — Analyze the impact of multiple text structures and the use of text features.
  • ELA.10.R.2.2 — Analyze the central idea(s) of historical American speeches and essays.
  • ELA.10.R.2.3 — Analyze an author’s choices in establishing and achieving purpose(s) in historical American speeches and essays.
  • ELA.10.R.3.4 — Analyze an author’s use of rhetoric in a text.
  • ELA.10.C.1.4 — Write expository texts to explain and analyze information from multiple sources using logical organization, purposeful transitions, and appropriate tone/voice.
  • ELA.10.V.1.1 — Integrate grade-level academic vocabulary in speaking and writing.
  • ELA.K12.EE.1.1 — Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning.
