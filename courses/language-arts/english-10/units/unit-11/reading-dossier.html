WEEK 31 · UNIT 11

Stagecraft, Conflict & Dramatic Irony

Original scene

MARA: You locked the side door? / ELIAS: Before sunset. / [A key falls from Elias’s coat. Mara sees it; Elias does not.] / MARA: Then no one can enter. / ELIAS: No one. / [A knock sounds from the side corridor.]

Close-reading lesson

Drama communicates through speech and stage action. The audience may know more than a character, producing dramatic irony. Stage directions can contradict dialogue, reveal power or create pacing that silent summary misses.

Worked interpretation

Elias’s certainty is undermined by the dropped key and knock. An actor could deliver “No one” confidently or nervously; either choice must be justified by the scene’s evidence and surrounding action.

Independent task

Annotate dialogue, stage direction, conflict, irony and one unanswered question. Propose two defensible performance choices and explain how each changes audience interpretation.

WEEK 32 · UNIT 11

Shakespearean Syntax, Rhetoric & Performance

Practice lines · course-created in early-modern style

If honor wear a borrowed face tonight, / let daylight ask whose name it truly bears. / I praise the crown, yet fear the hand beneath it.

Language method

Do not paraphrase word by word first. Identify subject, verb, clauses, figurative image and rhetorical turn. Then restate the thought in current syntax without erasing ambiguity.

Worked paraphrase

“If honor is only pretending tonight, daylight will reveal who actually deserves the name. I respect the office, but I distrust the person exercising its power.” The paraphrase clarifies syntax while preserving tension between institution and individual.

Independent task

Mark syntax, image, contrast and rhetorical appeal. Produce a modern paraphrase and two performance versions using different pauses or emphases, then defend the version that best fits the words.

WEEK 33 · UNIT 11

Performance as an Interpretation

Director’s problem

A scene can be staged as reconciliation, manipulation or uneasy compromise depending on pace, distance, gesture, prop use and tone.

Evidence rule

Performance choices are arguments. A director should be able to point to words, stage directions, structural position or recurring motifs that justify each major choice.

Worked model

If a character says “I forgive you” immediately after concealing evidence, a warm embrace and a guarded physical distance create very different readings. Neither is automatically correct; the production must explain its evidence.

Independent task

Create a performance plan with blocking, vocal choices, one prop or visual choice, one alternative interpretation and a reflection explaining what the performance reveals that silent reading did not.
