WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Universal Themes Across Texts

Practice pair

TEXT A: A traveler keeps a house key years after the house is sold. TEXT B: A family removes every name from a shared recipe book before giving it away. Both concern memory, but one preserves an object while the other deliberately erases attribution.

Comparative model

A topic such as memory, family or belonging is not yet a theme. A thematic claim states what a text suggests about that topic, then compares how another text confirms, revises or contests the idea.

Independent task

Compare two approved authentic texts from different cultures, eras or genres. Record exact source custody, then build a matrix for theme, evidence, form, context and meaningful difference.

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Diction, Syntax, Voice & Tone

Practice contrast

A: ‘The room was empty when the vote ended.’ B: ‘By the time the vote ended, even the chairs seemed to have withdrawn.’ The factual situation overlaps, but diction and syntax create different attitudes toward the event.

Style model

Name the linguistic choice precisely—sentence length, clause order, repeated sound, concrete image, register, connotation—then explain how it shapes voice or tone. Avoid treating tone as a list of adjectives disconnected from language.

Independent task

Annotate an approved passage for four style features. Create a short style imitation, then explain which features you intentionally retained and where your own voice diverged.

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Literary Analysis Essay & Revision

Analysis architecture

A literary essay should make a contestable claim, use passage-specific evidence, explain how author choices produce meaning, address a complication or alternative reading, and revise for reasoning—not merely grammar.

Revision model

Before: ‘The author uses imagery to show sadness.’ After: ‘The repeated images of emptied rooms shift from literal absence to chosen withdrawal, complicating the narrator’s claim that the separation was accidental.’

Independent task

Use an approved previously studied text. Build a thesis/evidence map, draft one analytical paragraph, identify the strongest counterreading, and write a revision memo naming at least two substantive changes.
