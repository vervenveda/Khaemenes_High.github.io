# English 11 · Unit 09 Evidence Alignment
## Literary Research, Source Evaluation & Synthesis

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 09 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Frame Literary Inquiry → Select Evidence Ecology → Evaluate Scholarship → Integrate → Preserve Interpretation**

Grade 11 requires research to deepen literary interpretation without allowing secondary sources to replace the student’s own close reading.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 25 · Literary Research Questions and Scope
1. **Frame Literary Inquiry:** Develop a question that begins in the primary literary text and identifies what contextual, historical, critical, or theoretical research could clarify.
2. **Define Scope:** Specify the text, passage range, historical frame, concept, or critical problem so the research remains manageable and relevant.
3. **Map Evidence:** Identify which claims require primary-text evidence and which require external research.
4. **Test:** Ask whether the proposed research question risks becoming biography, plot summary, or topic research detached from interpretation.
5. **Refine:** Submit a literary research question with primary-text anchor, external-evidence need, likely source types, and one limitation.

### Week 26 · Primary and Secondary Sources
1. **Classify:** Distinguish primary, secondary, contextual, quantitative, and multimedia evidence by function rather than prestige.
2. **Evaluate:** Assess authority, method, relevance, date, bias, and limitation for each source in relation to the literary question.
3. **Select:** Choose sources that contribute different kinds of evidence instead of collecting repetitive commentary.
4. **Counter:** Identify one scholarly or contextual source that complicates your preferred interpretation and explain why it must be addressed.
5. **Document:** Complete a source ecology showing what each source can establish, what it cannot establish, and how it relates to the primary text.

### Week 27 · Synthesis and Source Integration
1. **Integrate:** Build a paragraph where the literary text remains central and secondary sources enter only where they clarify, complicate, or contextualize the interpretation.
2. **Synthesize:** Map agreement, disagreement, method, and gap across secondary sources without flattening them into one consensus.
3. **Attribute:** Use signal phrases and citation to make ownership of ideas unmistakable while keeping the student’s reasoning in control.
4. **Test:** Remove one secondary source from the draft. If the interpretation collapses, decide whether the research is supporting analysis or substituting for it.
5. **Preserve Interpretation:** Write a synthesis claim that clearly distinguishes textual evidence, scholarly contribution, student inference, and unresolved disagreement.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 09 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 25–27. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, synthesis, source evaluation, contextualization, research, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 09 methods;
- a cumulative literary-analysis, research, synthesis, scholarly-argument, or limitation audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved research sources. Exact source, author, publication date, edition, translator, database/archive location, research method, and provenance remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 09 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds sustained-reading tracking, research design, source evaluation, lateral verification, synthesis, citation, scholarly integration, peer review, qualification, and exit-audit prompts only.
