Monday · Day 1

Launch, Context & Vocabulary

Objective: Frame Rhetorical Devices and Figurative Force and build the cultural, historical, disciplinary, and vocabulary knowledge needed for independent analysis.

Mini-Lesson

Introduce the essential question: “How do rhetorical devices make language memorable and persuasive?” Model how an advanced Grade 11 reader previews context, genre, source conditions, audience, and key vocabulary: antithesis, chiasmus, asyndeton, synecdoche, metonymy.

Guided Practice

Preview the text/source family: Speech or essay excerpt with antithesis, rhetorical question, irony, metonymy, synecdoche, asyndeton, or chiasmus Use competing civic, historical, scientific, and public-policy texts with transparent sourcing and varied perspectives. Complete a notice–wonder–context routine and identify how standards ELA.11.R.3.4, ELA.11.R.3.1, ELA.11.V.1.3 will be demonstrated.

Independent Work

Create an advanced vocabulary and context record, then write an initial 8–10 sentence response to the essential question that includes one qualification or uncertainty.

Evidence to Preserve

Context notes, vocabulary applications, source preview, and initial interpretation

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Locate a credible comparative text, translation note, historical artifact, or disciplinary source that complicates the week’s central question.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading, Evidence & Evaluation Lab

Objective: Apply advanced close reading to evaluate rhetorical choices, claims, evidence, assumptions, audience, purpose, and consequences.

Mini-Lesson

Model a multi-pass reading: comprehension and structure; evidence and author choices; context and assumptions; then evaluation of significance, effectiveness, limitations, and alternative interpretations.

Guided Practice

Annotate at least ten purposeful details in the assigned source. Classify evidence by standard, rhetorical or literary function, context, and reliability. Apply the language focus: rhetorical devices, syntax, repetition, figurative force, and analytical commentary.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence matrix: observation → quotation or data → paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance → limitation or counterreading. Rank the evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated source, evidence matrix, defensible interpretation, and limitation/counterreading

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Develop and test a plausible counterinterpretation, alternate line of reasoning, or competing explanation using different evidence.

Wednesday · Day 3

Advanced Composition & Craft Studio

Objective: Transform reading, research, and discussion into the week’s major product: Rhetorical device analysis paragraph.

Mini-Lesson

Analyze a mentor text for thesis or controlling idea, line of reasoning, organization, integrated evidence, commentary, style, audience, and rhetorical devices, syntax, repetition, figurative force, and analytical commentary.

Guided Practice

Plan the product with a rhetorical situation or interpretive problem, a nuanced claim, evidence sequence, reasoning notes, counterpoint, and audience/purpose check. Draft one demanding section through modeling or collaborative rehearsal.

Independent Work

Draft the major product: Rhetorical device analysis paragraph. Highlight the strongest synthesis or evaluation, identify one gap in evidence or reasoning, and note one ethical or accessibility consideration.

Evidence to Preserve

Plan, substantive draft, evidence integration, and self-identified revision targets

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Recast a section for a substantially different audience, medium, culture, or disciplinary context and explain the consequences of each change.

Thursday · Day 4

Seminar, Feedback & Substantive Revision

Objective: Use collaborative inquiry and revision to improve intellectual fairness, precision, coherence, evidence, style, and audience impact.

Mini-Lesson

Practice advanced feedback: represent the writer’s claim accurately, identify a strength, test assumptions and evidence, ask a genuine question, identify a reasoning gap, and recommend a precise revision.

Guided Practice

Conduct a seminar or conference around “How do rhetorical devices make language memorable and persuasive?” Use multiple pieces of evidence, paraphrase another perspective fairly, and distinguish disagreement about evidence, reasoning, values, or definitions.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product substantially. Complete a revision memo documenting at least four meaningful changes, the evidence or feedback that prompted them, and the effect on the reader, listener, or viewer.

Evidence to Preserve

Seminar record, feedback, revised product, source/ethics check, and revision memo

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Respond to the strongest counterargument or counterinterpretation and state what evidence could legitimately change your conclusion.

Friday · Day 5

Mastery Check, Corrections & Portfolio

Objective: Demonstrate weekly mastery, complete error analysis, preserve evidence, and determine whether to advance or repeat targeted practice.

Mini-Lesson

Review standards language, vocabulary, and a common misconception. Model correction as a reasoning process: identify the tested skill, diagnose the error, cite the governing evidence or rule, and show the corrected reasoning.

Guided Practice

Complete retrieval practice and a short transfer task using an unfamiliar passage, source, or scenario. Identify one priority before the quiz.

Independent Work

Take the weekly quiz. Complete corrections for missed items. File the strongest artifact and process evidence in the portfolio. Write a 4–6 sentence reflection connecting mastery evidence to the essential question and the next goal.

Evidence to Preserve

Quiz score, corrections, transfer task, portfolio artifact, and reflection

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Create one rigorous original question with four plausible choices, one definitive answer, an explanation, and the standard or skill assessed.

Friday Mastery Check

Weekly Quiz

Answer every question. The best score is retained. A score below 80% signals targeted review, corrections, and a retake.
