Open Monday Deep Lesson · Worked Example & Practice

Day 1 · Original short-short story

The Petition and the Speech

The mayor's speech described the new school as proof of unanimous civic progress. In a file box, Anika found a petition written before the ceremony asking for lower fees and safer roads. The petition did not reject the school; it asked that the promise of access become practical. Anika resisted calling the speech dishonest without further evidence. Instead, she wrote that public celebration had concealed unresolved demands.

Close-reading question: Analyze the rhetorical difference between the speech and the petition.

Monday · Day 1

Launch, Context & Vocabulary

Objective: Frame Course Launch: English III Scholar Habits and build the cultural, historical, disciplinary, and vocabulary knowledge needed for independent analysis.

Mini-Lesson

Introduce the essential question: “How do advanced readers evaluate meaning, style, rhetoric, and evidence?” Model how an advanced Grade 11 reader previews context, genre, source conditions, audience, and key vocabulary: evaluation, interpretation, evidence, portfolio, revision.

Guided Practice

Preview the text/source family: Diagnostic literary passage, informational passage, and writing sample Include literary, historical, scientific, and civic texts from more than one culture or discipline. Complete a notice–wonder–context routine and identify how standards ELA.K12.EE.1.1, ELA.K12.EE.2.1, ELA.11.C.1.5 will be demonstrated.

Independent Work

Create an advanced vocabulary and context record, then write an initial 8–10 sentence response to the essential question that includes one qualification or uncertainty.

Evidence to Preserve

Context notes, vocabulary applications, source preview, and initial interpretation

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Locate a credible comparative text, translation note, historical artifact, or disciplinary source that complicates the week’s central question.

Open Tuesday Deep Lesson · Worked Example & Practice

Day 2 · Original short-short story

The Date in the Margin

An archived letter was dated September, but the editor who published it had added a note twenty years later. Eli first treated both statements as the author's words. On rereading, he noticed the different handwriting and the editor's initials. The distinction changed his analysis: one voice described an event, while another attempted to explain it for a later audience.

Close-reading question: Discuss how editorial framing affects source interpretation.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading, Evidence & Evaluation Lab

Objective: Apply advanced close reading to read closely, reason from evidence, communicate clearly, revise purposefully, and reflect on mastery.

Mini-Lesson

Model a multi-pass reading: comprehension and structure; evidence and author choices; context and assumptions; then evaluation of significance, effectiveness, limitations, and alternative interpretations.

Guided Practice

Annotate at least ten purposeful details in the assigned source. Classify evidence by standard, rhetorical or literary function, context, and reliability. Apply the language focus: sentence boundaries, paragraph coherence, diagnostic honesty, and academic tone.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence matrix: observation → quotation or data → paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance → limitation or counterreading. Rank the evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated source, evidence matrix, defensible interpretation, and limitation/counterreading

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Develop and test a plausible counterinterpretation, alternate line of reasoning, or competing explanation using different evidence.

Open Wednesday Deep Lesson · Worked Example & Practice

Day 3 · Original short-short story

The Contradictory Witnesses

Two witnesses described the same public meeting. One said the applause lasted several minutes; another wrote that the room fell silent after a difficult question. Their accounts might refer to different moments, or their memories might differ. Jae refused to flatten them into a single invented sequence. He organized the conflicting details and identified what an agenda or recording might resolve.

Close-reading question: Explain why disagreement is evidence to examine rather than erase.

Annotate two precise details and preserve a written response in your existing lesson workspace.

Wednesday · Day 3

Advanced Composition & Craft Studio

Objective: Transform reading, research, and discussion into the week’s major product: Diagnostic literary analysis paragraph and personal growth plan.

Mini-Lesson

Analyze a mentor text for thesis or controlling idea, line of reasoning, organization, integrated evidence, commentary, style, audience, and sentence boundaries, paragraph coherence, diagnostic honesty, and academic tone.

Guided Practice

Plan the product with a rhetorical situation or interpretive problem, a nuanced claim, evidence sequence, reasoning notes, counterpoint, and audience/purpose check. Draft one demanding section through modeling or collaborative rehearsal.

Independent Work

Draft the major product: Diagnostic literary analysis paragraph and personal growth plan. Highlight the strongest synthesis or evaluation, identify one gap in evidence or reasoning, and note one ethical or accessibility consideration.

Evidence to Preserve

Plan, substantive draft, evidence integration, and self-identified revision targets

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Recast a section for a substantially different audience, medium, culture, or disciplinary context and explain the consequences of each change.

Open Thursday Deep Lesson · Worked Example & Practice

Day 4 · Original short-short story

The Borrowed Phrase

A student essay used a striking sentence from an article but placed no quotation marks around it. The student had changed three words and thought that was sufficient. Her mentor asked her to trace both the idea and the phrasing. She rewrote the sentence in her own analytic language, cited the article, and placed the most precise original wording in quotation marks.

Close-reading question: Explain the ethical distinction between paraphrase and patchwriting.

Thursday · Day 4

Seminar, Feedback & Substantive Revision

Objective: Use collaborative inquiry and revision to improve intellectual fairness, precision, coherence, evidence, style, and audience impact.

Mini-Lesson

Practice advanced feedback: represent the writer’s claim accurately, identify a strength, test assumptions and evidence, ask a genuine question, identify a reasoning gap, and recommend a precise revision.

Guided Practice

Conduct a seminar or conference around “How do advanced readers evaluate meaning, style, rhetoric, and evidence?” Use multiple pieces of evidence, paraphrase another perspective fairly, and distinguish disagreement about evidence, reasoning, values, or definitions.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product substantially. Complete a revision memo documenting at least four meaningful changes, the evidence or feedback that prompted them, and the effect on the reader, listener, or viewer.

Evidence to Preserve

Seminar record, feedback, revised product, source/ethics check, and revision memo

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Respond to the strongest counterargument or counterinterpretation and state what evidence could legitimately change your conclusion.

She identified the records that supported her argument and the voices still absent.

Friday · Day 5

Mastery Check, Corrections & Portfolio

Objective: Demonstrate weekly mastery, complete error analysis, preserve evidence, and determine whether to advance or repeat targeted practice.

Mini-Lesson

Review standards language, vocabulary, and a common misconception. Model correction as a reasoning process: identify the tested skill, diagnose the error, cite the governing evidence or rule, and show the corrected reasoning.

Guided Practice

Complete retrieval practice and a short transfer task using an unfamiliar passage, source, or scenario. Identify one priority before the quiz.

Independent Work

Take the weekly quiz. Complete corrections for missed items. File the strongest artifact and process evidence in the portfolio. Write a 4–6 sentence reflection connecting mastery evidence to the essential question and the next goal.

Evidence to Preserve

Quiz score, corrections, transfer task, portfolio artifact, and reflection

Support and access options

Use chunked text, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary supports, guided annotation, sentence frames, reduced visual load, oral rehearsal, extended time, or an equivalent accessible pathway while preserving the same reasoning target.

Extension

Create one rigorous original question with four plausible choices, one definitive answer, an explanation, and the standard or skill assessed.

Quiz Reading · Original Short Story

The Editor's Silence

A newspaper praised an opening ceremony but did not mention a petition filed the previous week. The petition requested access for families living far from town. A historian did not assume the paper had intentionally concealed the petition; she recorded the omission and investigated the editorial process.

Evidence question: Distinguish a verifiable omission from an unsupported accusation of motive.
