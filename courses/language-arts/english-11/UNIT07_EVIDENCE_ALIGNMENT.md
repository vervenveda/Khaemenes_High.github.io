# English 11 · Unit 07 Evidence Alignment
## Poetry Across Periods, Places & Traditions

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 07 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Locate Context → Choose Lens → Compare Tradition → Test Translation → Synthesize**

Grade 11 integrates close reading with historical and cultural context while requiring learners to avoid flattening distinct poetic traditions into universalized similarities.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 19 · Poetry and the Issues of Its Time Period
1. **Locate Context:** Identify the historical issue or pressure relevant to the poem and distinguish verified context from assumptions about the period.
2. **Choose Lens:** Decide whether form, speaker, image, diction, sound, or historical context provides the most productive entry point and justify the choice.
3. **Connect:** Explain how a textual feature engages the period without treating the poem as a simple historical document.
4. **Counter:** Develop an interpretation that relies less on context and more on textual form. Where does it clarify or weaken the analysis?
5. **Synthesize:** Write a qualified claim about the relationship between poetic form, historical pressure, and meaning.

### Week 20 · Poetry Across Classical, Medieval, Renaissance, British & Colonial Traditions
1. **Map Tradition:** Identify one formal, thematic, rhetorical, or cultural convention in each selected tradition without assuming direct continuity.
2. **Compare:** Choose one defensible criterion for comparison and explain why it matters across the selected poems.
3. **Contextualize:** Identify which similarities arise from shared human concerns and which may reflect specific literary or historical conventions.
4. **Counter:** Identify one comparison that appears meaningful at first but collapses under contextual scrutiny.
5. **Synthesize:** Produce a cross-period interpretation that preserves both continuity and difference.

### Week 21 · Realism, Naturalism, Modernism, and Contemporary Poetry
1. **Locate Shift:** Identify a formal, thematic, or philosophical shift that distinguishes one later movement or period from an earlier one.
2. **Choose Lens:** Select the feature that best reveals how the poem confronts reality, instability, social change, or uncertainty.
3. **Compare:** Compare responses to a similar concern across two periods while avoiding claims that one movement simply replaces another.
4. **Test:** Ask what historical, cultural, or aesthetic evidence would weaken your period-based interpretation.
5. **Synthesize:** Write a comparative claim explaining both literary change and persistent human or cultural concerns.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 07 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 19–21. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, synthesis, interpretation, rhetoric, research, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 07 methods;
- a cumulative research, comparative, contextual, translation, or interpretation audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved research sources. Exact source, author, publication date, edition, translator, archive/database location, historical context, and provenance remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 07 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, research, lateral reading, ambiguity testing, contextualization, cross-cultural comparison, translation/intertextual analysis, synthesis, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 07 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. authentic-source, translation, and research custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
