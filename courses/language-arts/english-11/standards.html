Course-wide competency guarantees

Reading

Complex literature and nonfiction; close reading; evidence; inference; structure; rhetoric; perspective; comparison; global context; long-form reading.

Writing

Argument, analysis, exposition, research, narrative, synthesis, timed writing, revision, publication, and reflection.

Communication

Seminar, active listening, oral interpretation, presentation, debate, student-led conference, and accessible multimedia.

Language & Research

Grammar, syntax, usage, vocabulary, etymology, citation, source evaluation, quantitative evidence, media literacy, and academic integrity.

Official verification sources

  • U.S. Department of Education — Federal Role in Education — Confirms that curriculum and graduation requirements are primarily state and local responsibilities.
  • Common Core State Standards — English Language Arts — Widely used Grade 11–12 college- and career-readiness reading, writing, speaking/listening, language, and disciplinary literacy benchmarks.
  • Florida B.E.S.T. Standards — English Language Arts — Grade 11 benchmark source reflected in the uploaded curriculum and the course crosswalk.
  • AP English Language and Composition — Advanced rhetoric, argument, synthesis, organization, evidence, and style skill framework; this course is AP-ready but not AP-authorized.
  • AP English Literature and Composition — Advanced literary analysis framework emphasizing character, setting, structure, perspective, figurative language, and comparison.
  • SAT Reading and Writing Content Domains — Information and Ideas, Craft and Structure, Expression of Ideas, and Standard English Conventions.
  • ACT College and Career Readiness Standards — Empirically derived English and reading readiness standards.
  • International Baccalaureate — Studies in Language and Literature — International reference for global texts, critical literacy, context, cultural perspective, written analysis, and oral communication.
