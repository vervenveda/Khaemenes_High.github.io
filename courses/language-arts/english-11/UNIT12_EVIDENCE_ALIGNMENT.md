# English 11 · Unit 12 Evidence Alignment
## Global Synthesis, Portfolio & Final Examination

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 12 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Synthesize Long-Form Evidence → Compare Globally → Curate Readiness → Demonstrate Mastery → Build Senior Plan**

Grade 11 closes with global synthesis, long-form interpretation, student-led portfolio evidence, cumulative assessment, correction analysis, and a readiness plan for senior-level literacy.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 34 · Novel Themes, Motifs, and Author Choices
1. **Synthesize Long-Form Evidence:** Trace theme, motif, perspective, mood, or structure across the complete work and identify where development is nonlinear or contradictory.
2. **Compare Globally:** Connect the long-form work to another text or tradition using one defensible comparative criterion without flattening context.
3. **Evaluate Author Choices:** Assess which authorial choice most strongly shapes meaning and justify why another plausible choice is less central.
4. **Counter:** Identify a whole-text counterinterpretation and test both readings against representative evidence.
5. **Synthesize:** Write a qualified long-form/global claim including strongest evidence, counterevidence, context, and limitation.

### Week 35 · Final Portfolio and Student-Led Conference
1. **Curate Readiness:** Select evidence across literary analysis, rhetoric, research, writing, speaking, and publication that demonstrates readiness for senior work.
2. **Map Growth:** Compare earlier and later artifacts to show changes in independence, complexity, evidence use, revision, and audience awareness.
3. **Prepare Conference:** Develop conference questions and evidence-based responses rather than a scripted celebration of accomplishments.
4. **Lead:** Conduct the conference by presenting claims, evidence, limitations, goals, and responding to follow-up questions.
5. **Reflect:** Write a senior-readiness reflection naming strongest evidence, weakest area, most important revision habit, and next benchmark.

### Week 36 · Final Review and Final Test
1. **Retrieve:** Prioritize review using performance patterns across literary evaluation, rhetoric, research, argument, language, and synthesis.
2. **Demonstrate Mastery:** Complete the cumulative final under established assessment rules and preserve the distinction between completion and mastery.
3. **Correct:** Analyze missed or weak responses by underlying skill, method, or reasoning failure and document corrections.
4. **Evaluate Readiness:** Integrate final-test evidence with portfolio and conference evidence to assess readiness for English 12.
5. **Build Senior Plan:** Create a senior literacy plan with one independent-reading, research, writing, and oral-communication goal tied to measurable evidence.

## Full-year retrieval and transfer

The Unit 12 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 34–36. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- full-year reading, writing, research, rhetoric, media, speaking, publication, and revision habits;
- earlier portfolio artifacts;
- prior assessments and corrections;
- long-form or capstone evidence;
- a cumulative final, oral defense, and next-stage literacy plan.

Each prompt requires students to demonstrate growth with evidence rather than rely on completion, self-description, or score alone.

## Reading/source custody

Required texts and evidence remain in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, student portfolio, approved authentic works, research sources, capstone materials, and assessment records. Exact source, author/creator, edition, translator, publication date, provenance, and citation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 12 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no final-exam answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion, portfolio presence, or oral participation into mastery;
- adds curation, revision, synthesis, cumulative retrieval, corrections, oral defense, readiness evaluation, transfer planning, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 12 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved through final defense;
3. portfolio and capstone claims are tied to concrete evidence;
4. corrections remain separate from simple completion;
5. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
6. JavaScript parses cleanly;
7. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
8. the unit landing page communicates the final learning progression;
9. the learner exits with a documented next-stage literacy plan.
