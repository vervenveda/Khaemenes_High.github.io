Evidence-Based Grade 11 Learning Rhythm

Interpret → Research → Classify Evidence → Map Debate → Integrate

Unit 09 teaches literary research that deepens rather than replaces close reading. Students distinguish primary and secondary authority, evaluate mixed evidence, map scholarly disagreement, and preserve their own interpretation.

Week 25 · Literary Research Questions & Scope

Interpretive significance, context/factual question distinctions, scope boundaries, and selective use of background research.

Week 26 · Primary & Secondary Sources

Source taxonomy, authority, mixed evidence forms, scholarly disagreement, and relevance/limitation notes.

Week 27 · Synthesis & Source Integration

Agreement/disagreement mapping, integration grammar, quotation/paraphrase choices, authorial ownership, and synthesis.

Literary-research rule: scholarship supports and complicates the student’s interpretation; it never substitutes for analysis of the primary text.

Essential Question

How do researchers build trustworthy knowledge from primary, secondary, quantitative, and multimedia evidence?

Learning Architecture

Develop research questions, refine scope, evaluate authority and bias, distinguish primary and secondary evidence, cite ethically, and synthesize sources without flattening disagreement.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English III portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Research proposal, annotated bibliography, source credibility audit, synthesis matrix, and conference.

Required close reading · A Story Across Generations

Original Academy instructional source, supplied offline.

The grandmother's account called the journey a triumph; her granddaughter's version emphasized the people who had helped along the way. Their retellings revealed the assumptions of different audiences.

Weekly assignment: Annotate competing claims and source limitations, present an interpretation supported by two textual details, and respond to a credible counterreading. Develop or revise this work through Weeks 25–27; preserve it in the course portfolio. Read full-length assigned literature in addition to this exercise.

Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1

Unit 09 Learning Progression

Frame Literary Inquiry → Select Evidence Ecology → Evaluate Scholarship → Integrate → Preserve Interpretation

Grade 11 requires research to deepen literary interpretation without allowing secondary sources to replace the student’s own close reading.

Week 25

Literary Research Questions and Scope

Submit a literary research question with primary-text anchor, external-evidence need, likely source types, and one limitation.

Week 26

Primary and Secondary Sources

Complete a source ecology showing what each source can establish, what it cannot establish, and how it relates to the primary text.

Week 27

Synthesis and Source Integration

Write a synthesis claim that clearly distinguishes textual evidence, scholarly contribution, student inference, and unresolved disagreement.

The 15-day sequence revisits prior evidence habits while increasing independence in sustained inquiry, source selection, synthesis, academic argument, and limitation analysis.
