Essential Question

How do form, style, culture, and historical context shape enduring human questions?

Learning Architecture

Evaluate literary elements, diction, syntax, voice, figurative language, and universal themes through globally diverse short fiction, essays, and literary nonfiction.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English III portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Comparative literary analysis evaluating style and universal theme across texts from different periods or cultures.

Unit 2 scholarly reading · The Assembly Speech

Original Academy short source; offline accessible.

A speaker told graduates their generation could solve every civic problem. The applause was loud, yet the claim offered neither a plan nor evidence. Later, a second speaker described a smaller project with documented results.

Required scholarly analysis: Examine this text through rhetorical context. Annotate its claims, tensions, and omissions. Develop a defensible interpretation with two cited details and one credible alternative reading. Revisit in Weeks 4–6; save work to the existing portfolio. This microtext supplements longer unit literature.
