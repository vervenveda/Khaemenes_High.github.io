WEEK 04 · UNIT 02

Evaluating Layers of Meaning

Original practice fiction · The Inventory Room

Every object donated to the town museum received two labels. The official label named material, date and donor. The second, handwritten label recorded why someone had kept it. When the museum digitized its collection, the director proposed scanning only the official labels because the handwriting was inconsistent. A volunteer quietly photographed both.

Advanced model

Meaning can emerge through tension between institutional classification and personal memory. Evaluate how setting, conflict, point of view and motif interact; do not treat one device as a detachable symbol.

Independent task

Using an approved authentic text, trace one image or structural choice across three locations. Add cultural/historical context only where a source supports it, and state what the context explains versus what remains textual interpretation.

WEEK 05 · UNIT 02

Style, Diction, Syntax & Author Voice

Practice contrast

VERSION A: ‘The committee postponed the vote.’ VERSION B: ‘By dusk, the committee had discovered one more reason not to decide.’ The event is similar, but syntax, connotation and implied attitude create different voices.

Style model

Style analysis should connect a linguistic feature to an effect and then to the larger meaning. Avoid empty labels such as ‘the author uses diction.’ Name the word pattern, sentence movement, rhythm, register or image and explain its consequence.

Independent task

Select a passage from the approved authentic work. Annotate diction, syntax, figurative pattern and one structural choice. Write a style imitation, then explain two deliberate changes and one feature you could not ethically or meaningfully imitate.

WEEK 06 · UNIT 02

Universal Themes Across Time & Place

Comparative caution

Two texts may both address exile, duty, grief or power without expressing the same idea about it. A thematic category is only a starting point; the claim must preserve differences in context, genre, speaker and outcome.

Worked model

Weak: ‘Both texts show family is important.’ Stronger: ‘Both texts link family obligation to identity, but one treats inherited duty as continuity while the other frames it as a pressure the protagonist must renegotiate.’

Independent task

Compare two approved authentic works from different periods/cultures. Record author, title, translation/edition if relevant, passage locators and provenance. Build a similarity/difference/context matrix and draft a thesis that includes a meaningful contrast.
