WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Juxtaposition & Character Perspective

Original practice sequence

Scene 1 ends with a mayor announcing that the town is ‘finally quiet.’ Scene 2 opens inside a laundromat where three families are packing belongings into plastic bins after rents rise. The scenes never mention each other.

Advanced model

Juxtaposition creates meaning through placement. The second scene can challenge, complicate or redefine the first scene’s language without an explicit narrator explaining the connection.

Independent task

In an approved text, locate two scenes, images or perspectives placed in meaningful relation. Record exact locators and explain at least two plausible effects of their juxtaposition.

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Mood Through Figurative Language

Original lyrical prose

The hallway lights buzzed like insects trapped in glass. Every classroom door stood open, but the building felt more locked than it had during the day.

Figurative model

Mood is an effect produced by patterns of image, sound, syntax, setting and expectation. A simile matters because of what it makes the reader notice and how it interacts with the rest of the passage.

Independent task

Select an approved poem or lyrical prose passage. Trace three linked figurative choices and explain how their combined pattern shapes mood. Include one alternative reading of the same image.

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Literary Analysis, Revision & Portfolio

Argument model

Advanced literary argument should distinguish observation from inference, preserve competing readings, and make revision evidence visible. Publication readiness includes source accuracy, coherent structure and a defensible claim.

Revision challenge

Take one paragraph whose evidence is accurate but whose reasoning is thin. Add a warrant, a complication, and a sentence explaining why the chosen evidence matters more than another available detail.

Independent task

Use an approved unit text and mentor analysis. Produce a claim map, revised analytical section, publication checklist and brief oral-defense note explaining one interpretive decision you expect a reader to challenge.
