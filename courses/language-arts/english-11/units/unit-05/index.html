Unit 05

Comparative Argument & Civic Discourse

Compare multiple arguments on shared issues; detect fallacies and bias; evaluate evidence, reasoning, counterclaims, and contemporaneous perspectives across civic, scientific, historical, and cultural texts.

Begin Week 13Unit OverviewUnit Assessment
OverviewWeeksAssessmentStandards

Essential Question

How should informed people evaluate competing claims, evidence, assumptions, and consequences?

Learning Architecture

Compare multiple arguments on shared issues; detect fallacies and bias; evaluate evidence, reasoning, counterclaims, and contemporaneous perspectives across civic, scientific, historical, and cultural texts.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English III portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Source-based argument evaluation, deliberative seminar, and evidence-based civic argument.

Three-Week Sequence

Read, evaluate, compose, revise, and demonstrate.
