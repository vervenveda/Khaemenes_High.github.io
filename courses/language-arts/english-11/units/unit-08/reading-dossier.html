WEEK 22 · UNIT 08

Two Poets, One Historical Pressure

Poem A · Factory Bell

At six the windows catch the gray / before the street has learned its name. / The bell divides the dark from pay; / we enter separately, leave the same. / I keep one button from my coat / polished bright beneath my thumb— / proof that a small and useless thing / can still refuse to become a sum.

Poem B · Ledger Margin

The column closes at the edge / where names become a weekly rate. / I write my sister in the margin, / outside the numbers, running late. / The page is clean. The room is not. / A cough moves softly through the floor. / If order means the total balances, / what is the margin for?

Worked comparison

Both speakers confront systems that reduce people to measurable labor, but Poem A uses the retained button as a private object of resistance while Poem B uses the physical margin of a ledger as a structural metaphor for what accounting excludes. Their shared pressure does not produce identical voices.

Independent task

Compare speaker, image, structure and implied historical pressure. State what context the poems suggest and what they do not prove. Write a counterreading for one image.

WEEK 23 · UNIT 08

From Close Reading to Comparative Essay

Poem A · Translation Note

My mother says the word means shelter. / My dictionary says roof. / At school I write residence / because the form has one empty line. / At night the same word crosses three rooms / and changes what it carries.

Poem B · Address Form

Block letters ask for HOME. / I give a number, street, and code. / The clerk accepts the answer. / My brother laughs: / “That is where the mail goes.”

Worked model thesis

Both poems question administrative definitions of home, but “Translation Note” emphasizes semantic movement across languages and contexts, while “Address Form” contrasts institutional acceptance with familial correction. The difference matters because one poem stages translation inside the word and the other stages conflict between bureaucratic and lived definitions.

Independent task

Draft a thesis, two comparative body paragraphs and one counterinterpretation. Each paragraph must pair evidence from both poems rather than treating them in separate blocks.

WEEK 24 · UNIT 08

Performance, Medium & Ethical Transformation

Performance script

Read “Address Form” once with the word HOME emphasized as an institutional demand, then again with “mail” emphasized as the brother’s corrective. The same words can produce different interpretive centers through pace, pause and stress.

Digital adaptation problem

A student wants to place the poem over dramatic music and images of houses. Those choices may overwhelm the poem or impose a single emotional reading. Ethical adaptation asks whether added media clarifies, complicates or distorts the text.

Accessibility requirement

A multimedia interpretation should provide readable text, captions or transcript, meaningful contrast, alt text for informative visuals, and an equivalent path for anyone who cannot hear or see a chosen mode.

Independent task

Create a performance plan or accessible storyboard. Annotate five choices in pace, emphasis, image, sound or typography and explain the interpretive effect of each.
