WEEK 28 · UNIT 10

Research-Supported Literary Analysis

Primary-text pattern

A fictional novel repeatedly places scenes of reconciliation beside broken public monuments.

Critical source

A fictional critic argues that the novel treats repair as incomplete whenever public memory remains contested.

Context source

A fictional archival essay explains that several monuments in the setting were rebuilt after a civil conflict.

Worked synthesis

The critic can sharpen a reading, but the literary text remains primary evidence. A strong paragraph uses the critic as a lens or challenge, then returns to the novel’s language and structure to make an original claim.

Independent task

Draft a research-supported literary paragraph containing primary evidence, one secondary source, one contextual source, a counterreading and your own interpretive reasoning.

Return to Week 28 classroom and saved work

WEEK 29 · UNIT 10

Expository Analysis of Complex Texts

Complex-text set

A policy memo, a literary excerpt and a data visualization all address public memory, but each uses a different evidence system and audience.

Expository lesson

Advanced exposition clarifies relationships among unlike texts without flattening them. Define technical terms, distinguish evidence types, and show why one text can answer a question another cannot.

Worked model

A data chart may establish frequency; a policy memo may reveal institutional priorities; a literary text may explore subjective meaning. Synthesis becomes stronger when those differences stay visible.

Independent task

Write a 600–800 word expository section organized around two analytical categories. Include transitions that explicitly state how the source types relate.

Return to Week 29 classroom and saved work

WEEK 30 · UNIT 10

Narrative Perspective, Digital Publication & Presentation

Narrative problem

A researched project includes a short braided narrative showing three perspectives on the same public event.

Craft lesson

Perspective is built through selection, distance, syntax, sensory detail, chronology and withheld information. Multiple perspectives should not become stereotypes or invented testimony presented as fact.

Digital-publication lesson

Label reconstructed or fictionalized passages clearly, preserve citations for factual material, and make the digital version accessible through logical heading structure, alt text, captions/transcript and printable fallback.

Independent task

Create one original 500–700 word multi-perspective scene, a digital storyboard, and a publication checklist explaining what is factual, reconstructed, fictionalized, cited and accessibility-tested.

Return to Week 30 classroom and saved work

Unit 10 synthesis
