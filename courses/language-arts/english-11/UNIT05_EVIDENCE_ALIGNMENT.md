# English 11 · Unit 05 Evidence Alignment
## Comparative Argument & Civic Discourse

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 05 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Map → Test Assumptions → Compare Frameworks → Counter → Judge**

Grade 11 asks learners to compare multiple arguments across shared issues, surface assumptions, distinguish reasoning from rhetoric, and make calibrated civic judgments.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 13 · Rhetorical Devices and Figurative Force
1. **Map:** Identify a recurring rhetorical or figurative pattern and map where it appears, what claim it supports, and what audience response it invites.
2. **Test Assumptions:** Ask what belief, value, or assumption must be accepted for the rhetorical force to work as intended.
3. **Compare Frameworks:** Analyze the same passage as style, reasoning, and audience strategy. Which framework best explains its persuasive power?
4. **Counter:** Construct a reading in which the device is memorable but not logically persuasive. What evidence supports that distinction?
5. **Judge:** Evaluate whether the rhetorical force strengthens, distorts, or merely decorates the argument, and justify the judgment with criteria.

### Week 14 · Multiple Arguments on the Same Topic
1. **Map:** Create an argument map for at least two positions: central claim, subclaims, evidence, warrants, assumptions, and consequences.
2. **Test Assumptions:** Identify one hidden or contested assumption in each argument and test how the conclusion changes if that assumption fails.
3. **Compare Frameworks:** Evaluate the arguments using the same criteria—validity, evidence quality, scope, fairness, and consequence.
4. **Counter:** Construct the strongest counterargument to your current preferred position using evidence the opposing side would consider legitimate.
5. **Judge:** Make a provisional comparative judgment that distinguishes factual strength, logical strength, rhetorical effectiveness, and value disagreement.

### Week 15 · Contemporaneous Authors and Shared Topics
1. **Map:** Place two or more authors in the same historical conversation and identify what they share, dispute, assume, or define differently.
2. **Test Assumptions:** Identify where historical context shapes assumptions that a modern reader might otherwise overlook.
3. **Compare Frameworks:** Choose criteria for comparing the authors fairly across purpose, audience, evidence, reasoning, and historical constraints.
4. **Counter:** Challenge your own comparison: what difference in genre, audience, or context makes direct comparison potentially misleading?
5. **Judge:** Produce a civic or historical synthesis that explains where the authors genuinely disagree, where they talk past each other, and what evidence supports your conclusion.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 05 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 13–15. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, interpretation, structure, rhetoric, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 05 methods;
- a cumulative Unit 05 defense, deliberation, verification, or calibrated-judgment task on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, and approved authentic works. Course-created practice texts remain clearly identified as instructional examples. Exact source, edition, author/speaker, publication date, historical context, data provenance, and translation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 05 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, transfer, fair representation, counterargument, verification, ethical evaluation, performance, deliberation, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 05 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. authentic-source and data custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
