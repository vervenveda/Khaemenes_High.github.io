WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Juxtaposition & Character Perspective

Original practice sequence

Scene 1 ends with a mayor announcing that the town is ‘finally quiet.’ Scene 2 opens inside a laundromat where three families are packing belongings into plastic bins after rents rise. The scenes never mention each other.

Advanced model

Juxtaposition creates meaning through placement. The second scene can challenge, complicate or redefine the first scene’s language without an explicit narrator explaining the connection.

Independent task

In an approved text, locate two scenes, images or perspectives placed in meaningful relation. Record exact locators and explain at least two plausible effects of their juxtaposition.

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Mood Through Figurative Language

Original lyrical prose

The hallway lights buzzed like insects trapped in glass. Every classroom door stood open, but the building felt more locked than it had during the day.

Figurative model

Mood is an effect produced by patterns of image, sound, syntax, setting and expectation. A simile matters because of what it makes the reader notice and how it interacts with the rest of the passage.

Independent task

Select an approved poem or lyrical prose passage. Trace three linked figurative choices and explain how their combined pattern shapes mood. Include one alternative reading of the same image.

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Literary Analysis, Revision & Portfolio

Argument model

Advanced literary argument should distinguish observation from inference, preserve competing readings, and make revision evidence visible. Publication readiness includes source accuracy, coherent structure and a defensible claim.

Revision challenge

Take one paragraph whose evidence is accurate but whose reasoning is thin. Add a warrant, a complication, and a sentence explaining why the chosen evidence matters more than another available detail.

Independent task

Use an approved unit text and mentor analysis. Produce a claim map, revised analytical section, publication checklist and brief oral-defense note explaining one interpretive decision you expect a reader to challenge.

WEEK 10 · UNIT 04

Classical Speeches & Essays: Context

Original practice address

Citizens, a law written for quiet years may fail during a flood, but emergency power that never ends becomes a different danger. We must decide not only what authority can do, but when it must stop.

Context model

Classical rhetoric should be read with source/date/audience/political context, but context must not replace textual analysis. Identify the problem, speaker position and constraints, then connect rhetorical choices to the occasion.

Independent task

Use an approved classical or comparative rhetorical text. Record author/speaker, translator if relevant, title, edition/source and passage. Add one credible contextual source and explain exactly what it clarifies.

WEEK 11 · UNIT 04

Evaluating Text Structures & Features

Structure contrast

One speech begins with shared values before presenting evidence. Another begins with a narrative example, delays its claim, then closes with a direct appeal.

Advanced model

Evaluate what a structure makes possible: establishing authority, managing resistance, clarifying logic, pacing emotional pressure or reframing a dispute.

Independent task

Map the structure of an approved speech/essay in sections. For each section, identify function, evidence type, audience effect and one alternative organization the speaker could have chosen.

Return to Week 11 classroom and saved work

WEEK 12 · UNIT 04

Logos, Ethos, Pathos & Kairos

Practice problem

A speaker has strong statistics but addresses them after the audience has already lost trust. Another speaker has high credibility but offers no evidence for a new factual claim.

Appeal model

Appeals interact. Kairos concerns timing/opportunity; ethos concerns credibility and character; logos concerns reasoning/evidence; pathos concerns emotional framing. None guarantees sound argument.

Independent task

Analyze an approved rhetorical text for all four appeals, including one place where an appeal succeeds rhetorically but remains weak evidentially. Prepare a timed-analysis claim and counterassessment.
