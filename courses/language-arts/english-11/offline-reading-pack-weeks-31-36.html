WEEK 31 · UNIT 11

Juxtaposition, Rhetoric & Conflict

Original paired scene

Scene A ends with a leader promising “order at any cost.” Scene B opens with a family silently repairing a broken table after a public disturbance.

Advanced close reading

Juxtaposition asks why scenes are placed beside one another. The leader’s abstract rhetoric about order can be tested against the concrete domestic cost shown immediately afterward.

Worked model

A claim might argue that the sequence exposes the human consequences hidden by political abstraction. A counterreading could argue that the family scene emphasizes resilience rather than critique.

Independent task

Map one rhetorical pattern, one structural contrast, one character motive and one counterreading. Explain how sequence changes the meaning of at least one line.

WEEK 32 · UNIT 11

Performance & Oral Interpretation

Performance problem

A public speech within a drama can be delivered as sincere persuasion, strategic manipulation or unstable self-persuasion.

Evidence hierarchy

Base performance on diction, syntax, repeated images, audience relationship and scene context. Gesture and music should not substitute for textual evidence.

Worked model

A repeated phrase can become increasingly confident, increasingly desperate or mechanically rehearsed. Track what each delivery implies about motive and compare it with later consequences in the play.

Independent task

Create an annotated performance script with at least six justified vocal or physical choices, then write a reflection comparing your interpretation with one plausible alternative.

WEEK 33 · UNIT 11

Launch a Long-Form Reading Dossier

Reading architecture

A long work cannot be reduced to isolated favorite passages. Track character arcs, motifs, structural turning points, setting changes, unresolved tensions and patterns that gain or lose significance over time.

Notebook model

For each reading block record locator, event, recurring pattern, interpretive hypothesis, complication and question. Mark when a later section changes an earlier interpretation.

Conference preparation

A strong conference does not summarize the plot. It explains one evolving pattern, cites evidence from separated points in the work and identifies what remains uncertain.

Independent task

Build a realistic reading schedule, motif tracker and first conference note for the approved long-form work. Include one interpretation you expect the text may force you to revise.

WEEK 34 · UNIT 12

Long-Form Themes, Motifs & Comparative Perspective

Long-form task

Track one theme or motif across the approved long work, then compare it with a text from another cultural, historical or formal context.

Comparison boundary

A global comparison should not flatten cultures into universal slogans. Identify source context, genre and specific differences before claiming a shared pattern.

Worked model

Two works may both address migration while framing belonging differently because one emphasizes family memory and the other legal status. The comparison becomes stronger when those differences remain visible.

Independent task

Create a theme/motif tracker plus a comparative matrix with context, form, evidence, similarity, difference and limitation. Draft one synthesis paragraph that avoids false equivalence.

WEEK 35 · UNIT 12

Final Portfolio & Student-Led Conference

Readiness question

What evidence proves the student can enter senior English with increasing independence, sustained reading, research judgment, revision discipline and public communication skills?

Portfolio architecture

Select representative work from literary analysis, research, argument/exposition, performance/publication and long-form reading. Preserve drafts or process evidence for at least two items.

Conference model

Lead with claims about readiness, then point to artifacts, revision evidence and one unresolved weakness. A conference is stronger when it includes a realistic improvement plan.

Independent task

Build a standards-tagged portfolio map, conference agenda, two artifact comparisons and a senior-readiness statement with one measurable goal.

Cumulative Final, Corrections & Senior-Readiness Plan

Final transfer

Practice with unfamiliar literary, informational and rhetorical material by applying course methods rather than recalling unit-specific answers.

Corrections analysis

Readiness plan

Translate the final into a targeted plan for Grade 12: sustained reading, academic writing, research/synthesis, oral communication and independent revision.

Independent task

Complete a corrections log, final reflection and senior-readiness plan supported by specific portfolio/final evidence.
