# English 9 · Unit 06 Evidence Alignment
## Rhetoric & Public Voice

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 06 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Identify Situation → Test Reasoning → Shape Language → Rehearse → Reflect**

Grade 9 keeps rhetoric concrete by tying audience, purpose, evidence, reasoning, fallacy, language, and delivery to visible choices students can explain and revise.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 16 · The Rhetorical Situation
1. **Identify Situation:** Name the speaker, audience, purpose, occasion, and context. Distinguish what the text proves about the situation from what you are inferring.
2. **Interpret:** Choose one rhetorical choice and explain why it fits this audience or occasion rather than a generic audience.
3. **Compare:** Compare how the same message would need to change for a different audience. Identify which changes affect tone, evidence, or structure.
4. **Transfer:** Rewrite a short passage for a new audience while keeping the core claim intact. Annotate the changes.
5. **Reflect:** State one claim about how rhetorical situation shapes the text and support it with two specific choices.

### Week 17 · Reasoning, Evidence and Fallacies
1. **Retrieve:** Recall one Week 16 claim and identify what evidence made it persuasive or limited.
2. **Test Reasoning:** Break one argument into claim, evidence, and reasoning. Explain whether the evidence actually supports the claim.
3. **Compare:** Compare a strong and weak piece of evidence for the same claim. What makes one more relevant or reliable?
4. **Detect:** Identify one reasoning error or fallacy in context and explain the exact step where the logic fails.
5. **Reflect:** Revise one weak argument so that its claim, evidence, and reasoning become more responsible and precise.

### Week 18 · Speech Writing and Delivery
1. **Plan:** Define your audience, purpose, central claim, strongest evidence, and intended audience response before drafting.
2. **Compose:** Draft an opening and one body section designed for listening rather than silent reading. Mark where structure and repetition help comprehension.
3. **Rehearse:** Practice pace, pause, emphasis, and eye contact. Record one delivery choice that improved meaning and one that distracted.
4. **Revise:** Use feedback to revise both wording and delivery. Explain which change most improved clarity or credibility.
5. **Reflect:** Complete a speech defense: strongest rhetorical choice, strongest evidence, delivery decision, limitation, and next improvement.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 06 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 16–18. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- first-semester evidence, interpretation, rhetoric, argument, synthesis, and language habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 06 methods;
- cumulative midyear assessment evidence, corrections, and a next-step improvement plan.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic review routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved source sets. Course-created practice texts remain clearly identified as instructional examples. Exact source, author/speaker, publication date, edition, translator, data provenance, and research limitations remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 06 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz or midterm answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, synthesis, rhetorical analysis/composition, source evaluation, corrections analysis, reflection, and evidence-based planning prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 06 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. authentic-source and research custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression;
8. midyear reflection is tied to demonstrated evidence rather than self-report alone.
