# English 9 · Unit 10 Evidence Alignment
## Argument, Debate & Civic Reasoning

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 10 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Frame Issue → Test Evidence → Represent Opposition → Rebut → Deliberate**

Grade 9 formalizes civic argument by requiring fair issue framing, evidence testing, accurate counterclaims, respectful rebuttal, and revision after debate.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 28 · Issue Framing & Counterclaims
1. **Frame Issue:** Define the issue narrowly enough to argue responsibly. Distinguish the question, stakeholders, key terms, and what evidence would matter.
2. **Map Claims:** Identify the main claims, assumptions, and values on more than one side without reducing either side to a slogan.
3. **Represent Opposition:** Write the strongest reasonable version of an opposing position so a supporter of that view would recognize it as fair.
4. **Test Scope:** Identify where a claim is too broad, too certain, or dependent on an unstated assumption. Revise the wording.
5. **Deliberate:** State a provisional position that includes the strongest counterclaim and one unresolved question.

### Week 29 · Evidence, Rebuttal & Ethical Persuasion
1. **Test Evidence:** Evaluate evidence for relevance, reliability, sufficiency, date, and whether it actually supports the claim being made.
2. **Compare:** Rank two pieces of evidence for the same point and explain which is stronger and why.
3. **Rebut:** Respond to a counterclaim by conceding what is valid before explaining where the reasoning or evidence remains weaker.
4. **Audit Ethics:** Identify any emotional appeal, framing choice, or omission that risks manipulating rather than informing the audience.
5. **Deliberate:** Revise one argument passage so evidence, rebuttal, tone, and ethical persuasion work together.

### Week 30 · Debate & Position Paper
1. **Prepare:** Build a debate map with claim, strongest evidence, counterclaim, rebuttal, definitions, and one question you expect from the other side.
2. **Debate:** Present the position and respond directly to opposing evidence without changing the subject or attacking the speaker.
3. **Listen:** Record the strongest point made by the other side and identify whether it changes, narrows, or leaves your position intact.
4. **Revise:** Use debate evidence to revise the position paper, especially overstatement, weak rebuttal, or ignored evidence.
5. **Deliberate:** Submit a final civic-reasoning statement: claim, strongest evidence, strongest opposing point, revision made, and remaining uncertainty.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 10 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 28–30. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit argument, research, source evaluation, synthesis, publication, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 10 methods;
- a cumulative debate, publication, professional-communication, accessibility, or consequence audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading and research remain in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, civic/professional documents, and approved multimedia sources. Exact source, author/creator, publication date, edition, platform, media provenance, permissions, and citation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 10 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds argument framing, evidence testing, research writing, counterclaim, publication design, accessibility, professional/civic communication, ethical technology review, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 10 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. source, media, attribution, and publication custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
