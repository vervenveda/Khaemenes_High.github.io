Essential Question

What becomes visible when a reader follows characters, patterns, and ideas across an entire book?

Text and Source Family

One teacher-approved full novel, with public-domain possibilities including Frankenstein, Jane Eyre, The Time Machine, or The Call of the Wild. Text selection should account for student readiness, accessibility, instructional purpose, and lawful availability rather than treating this list as mandatory.

Learning Priorities

  • Sustain a reading schedule while preserving questions, evidence, vocabulary, and evolving interpretations across a full-length work.
  • Track characters, motifs, conflicts, symbols, settings, narrative patterns, and thematic developments across separated passages.
  • Distinguish a recurring detail from a meaningful motif by demonstrating pattern and development.
  • Build historical/cultural context where it clarifies the text without using context to predetermine interpretation.
  • Compare plausible interpretations and evaluate each against the novel's evidence.
  • Use critical perspectives as questions/lenses rather than machines that produce predetermined answers.
  • Write through planning, evidence selection, drafting, feedback, substantive revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Preserve major reading and analytical work in the Grade 9 portfolio.
