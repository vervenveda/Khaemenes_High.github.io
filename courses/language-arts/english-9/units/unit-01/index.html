Unit 01

Foundations of Scholarly Reading & Writing

Establish annotation, inference, paragraph architecture, grammar control, academic integrity, productive revision habits, and the evidence-based routines that will carry through the entire English I course.

Essential Question

How do careful readers turn observations into defensible ideas?

Text and Source Family

Original course texts, short public-domain essays, selected speeches, and teacher-approved informational passages.

Learning Priorities

  • Read closely and cite precise textual evidence.
  • Distinguish observation, inference, claim, evidence, warrant, and unsupported assertion.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Use sentence boundaries, paragraph unity, vocabulary, and conventions as tools for meaning.
  • Attribute borrowed ideas and language clearly and preserve source boundaries.
  • Discuss, present, listen, question, and respond with intellectual fairness.
  • Preserve major work and revision evidence in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Evidence-based analytical paragraph, revised diagnostic essay, and language conventions/integrity check.

Mastery: 80% minimum on defined scored evidence.

Mentor: Eiren · Language Arts specialist.

Beta: Same public Academy improvement pathway used upstream.

Three-Week Sequence

Baseline → evidence → revision → mastery.

Weekly evidence records document the learning process. They do not independently satisfy the Unit 1 mastery gate.
