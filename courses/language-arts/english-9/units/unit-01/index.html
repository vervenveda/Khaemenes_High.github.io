Essential Question

How do careful readers turn observations into defensible ideas?

Learning Priorities

  • Read closely and cite precise textual evidence.
  • Distinguish observation, inference, claim, evidence, warrant, and unsupported assertion.
  • Explain what assumption connects evidence to an inference and what new evidence could weaken or change that inference.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Use sentence boundaries, paragraph unity, vocabulary, and conventions as tools for meaning.
  • Attribute borrowed ideas and language clearly and preserve source boundaries.
  • Preserve major work and revision evidence in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Open Evidence Contract

Critical-Thinking Entry Point

Observe before interpreting.

Unit 1 establishes the reasoning discipline used all year: observation → inference → claim → evidence → warrant → challenge → revision. Students learn that an interpretation becomes stronger when they can show where it comes from and explain what evidence would make them reconsider it.

Observe

Identify what is actually present in the text before adding explanation.

Infer

State a reasonable interpretation and identify the detail or pattern that supports it.

Warrant

Explain why the evidence supports the claim instead of assuming the connection is obvious.

Challenge & Revise

Ask what another careful reader could question, what evidence is missing, and whether the claim should be kept, narrowed, qualified, or changed.

Canonical Mastery Evidence

Unit 1 has four required authoritative components.

35% analytical paragraph · 30% revised diagnostic essay · 20% language/integrity check · 15% fresh reading-evidence transfer. Every required component and the weighted unit result must reach at least 80%.

35% · Analytical Paragraph

Teacher-reviewed claim, textual evidence, warrant/reasoning, organization, and language.

30% · Revised Diagnostic Essay

Substantial revision with the earlier draft and revision explanation preserved.

20% · Language & Integrity

Fresh evidence covering sentence control, quotation/paraphrase/summary, attribution, and source boundaries.

15% · Fresh Reading Transfer

Unrehearsed detail → inference → claim → evidence → warrant response to a new passage.
