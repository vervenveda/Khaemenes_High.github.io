Essential Question

How do careful readers turn observations into defensible ideas?

Learning Priorities

  • Read closely and cite precise textual evidence.
  • Distinguish observation, inference, claim, evidence, warrant, and unsupported assertion.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Use sentence boundaries, paragraph unity, vocabulary, and conventions as tools for meaning.
  • Attribute borrowed ideas and language clearly and preserve source boundaries.
  • Preserve major work and revision evidence in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Canonical Mastery Evidence

Unit 1 has four required authoritative components.

The unit-mastery map assigns 35% to the analytical paragraph, 30% to the revised diagnostic essay, 20% to the language/integrity check, and 15% to fresh reading-evidence transfer. Every required scored component must reach at least 80%, and the weighted unit result must also reach at least 80%.

35% · Analytical Paragraph

Teacher-reviewed claim, textual evidence, warrant/reasoning, organization, and language.

30% · Revised Diagnostic Essay

Substantial revision with the earlier draft and revision explanation preserved.

20% · Language & Integrity

Fresh evidence covering sentence control, quotation/paraphrase/summary, attribution, and source boundaries.

15% · Fresh Reading Transfer

Unrehearsed detail → inference → claim → evidence → warrant response to a new passage.
