Integrated English I Readiness Laboratory

This week removes the labels from the toolbox. On fresh material, decide for yourself whether the problem requires close reading, vocabulary reasoning, sentence repair, evidence-based writing, source investigation—or several at once.

Begin Readiness Lab

Mission

Recognize the problem before choosing the tool.

Read

What is explicit? What is inferred? Which detail matters most?

Interpret

What does the language mean here—not merely in isolation?

Clarify

Does the sentence communicate the intended relationship without ambiguity?

Reason

Does the evidence support the claim, and what connects them?

Verify

Where did the information originate, and is corroboration truly independent?

Calibrate

How certain should the conclusion be? What would make you revise it?

Readiness habit: Do not ask, “Which lesson is this from?” Ask, “What kind of thinking does this problem require?”

Integrated Case File

The Quiet Study Trial

For one school week, the library tested a “quiet study hour” from 3:00 to 4:00 p.m. A sign near the entrance asked students to silence phones and keep conversation low. On Monday, staff counted 31 students using the study area. By Thursday, 46 students were present. Several students told the librarian they appreciated the quieter space.

The librarian's notes also recorded that a large history project was due Friday. No attendance count from the previous week was available. The library did not survey students who chose not to enter.

On Friday, the student council posted: “Quiet Hour Is a Huge Success—Library Use Jumps Nearly 50%!” The post cited Thursday's count of 46 and Monday's count of 31. A school news account repeated the headline and linked to the council post.

In a separate reflection, a student wrote: “The atmosphere was conducive to concentration; even when groups arrived, their conversations remained brief and subdued.”

Writing Laboratory

Turn the case into a defensible paragraph.

Source Chain Laboratory

Trace before counting.

Student council post → uses Monday and Thursday library counts.

School news account → repeats the student council headline and links to that post.

Librarian notes → contain the original daily counts and mention the Friday history-project deadline.
