Foundations & Readiness · Week 3

Sentence Foundations

Grammar is not a collection of traps. Its purpose is to help a reader answer one question without confusion: What exactly does the writer mean?

Begin

Part 1 · Sentence Boundaries

A complete sentence expresses a complete thought.

Complete Sentence

The class checked the source. It has a subject, a finite verb, and a complete thought.

Fragment

Because the source lacked a date. The dependent idea leaves the reader waiting for the main thought.

Run-on

The source looked reliable it contained no citations. Two complete thoughts have been joined without a valid boundary.

Comma Splice

The source looked reliable, it contained no citations. A comma alone cannot normally join two independent clauses.

Repair choices: “The source looked reliable, but it contained no citations.” · “The source looked reliable; however, it contained no citations.” · “The source looked reliable. It contained no citations.” Different structures create different pace and emphasis.

Part 2 · Control

Agreement, tense, and pronouns keep relationships visible.

Subject–Verb Agreement

The grammatical subject controls the verb: The list of sources is useful. “Sources” is inside a prepositional phrase; “list” is the subject.

Tense Control

Use tense changes when time changes—not accidentally. Yesterday we reviewed the claim and found two errors.

Pronoun Clarity

A reader should know what a pronoun refers to. When Ava spoke to Lina, she was frustrated is ambiguous if context does not identify “she.”

Modifier Placement

Place descriptive language near what it modifies. Walking to class, Maya saw the poster means Maya was walking; Walking to class, the poster caught Maya's attention illogically makes the poster the walker.

Part 4 · Independent Editing

The Research Note

Unedited paragraph:

Yesterday our group reviews two webpages about the same event, the first page include an author and publication date. The second page had no author. Because it made several confident claims. Maya and Jordan compared the pages, she noticed that both repeated the same statistic. Looking for the original report, the statistic became easier to evaluate. The group decide that confidence alone do not establish credibility.

Compare after you finish

One possible revision: “Yesterday our group reviewed two webpages about the same event. The first page included an author and publication date. The second page had no author, although it made several confident claims. When Maya and Jordan compared the pages, Maya noticed that both repeated the same statistic. As the group looked for the original report, the statistic became easier to evaluate. The group decided that confidence alone does not establish credibility.” Other correct revisions are possible.

Part 5 · Syntax & Emphasis

Correct sentences can still make different choices.

Three facts: The rain began. The runners continued. The course became slippery.

Possible structures include coordination, subordination, introductory phrases, or two deliberately paced sentences. Grammar gives you choices; rhetoric determines which choice serves the meaning.
