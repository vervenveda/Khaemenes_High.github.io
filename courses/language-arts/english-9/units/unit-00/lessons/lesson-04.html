Paragraphs & Evidence-Based Writing

A strong paragraph does more than state an opinion. It makes a focused claim, supplies relevant evidence, explains the connection, and keeps every sentence working toward the same purpose.

Begin

Part 1 · The Reasoning Chain

Claim → Evidence → Warrant → Qualification

Claim

The focused idea you want the reader to accept.

Evidence

The specific detail that genuinely bears on the claim.

Warrant

The reasoning explaining why the evidence supports the claim.

Qualification

Language that keeps the conclusion proportional to the evidence.

Writing rule: Evidence does not explain itself. If the reader must guess why a detail matters, the paragraph's reasoning is incomplete.

Part 2 · Worked Example

From opinion to defensible paragraph

Source note: During a student cleanup, volunteers first collected litter without sorting it. At the end, recyclable bottles and cans were mixed with food waste. During the next cleanup, organizers placed labeled containers at three collection points. Volunteers then separated most bottles and cans as they worked.

Unsupported

“Labeled containers are better.”

This may be reasonable, but better in what way? What evidence supports it?

Defensible

“For this cleanup, labeled collection containers appear to have improved sorting because volunteers separated most bottles and cans while working instead of mixing them with food waste. The change does not prove labeled containers solve every recycling problem, but it supports using them in similar events.”

The second paragraph identifies the context, uses the observed change as evidence, explains why it matters, and avoids claiming more than the evidence can establish.

Part 4 · Relevance

Interesting evidence is not always relevant evidence.

Claim: The school garden's new watering schedule reduced unnecessary watering during one trial week.

Evidence A: Soil checks showed three beds were still damp on Wednesday, so those beds were not watered again that day.

Evidence B: The garden contains tomatoes, herbs, and sunflowers.

Evidence C: The previous routine watered every bed each weekday regardless of soil condition.

Evidence D: Several students said sunflowers were their favorite plants.

Part 5 · Independent Writing

Write, audit, revise.

Fresh prompt: A library tested two signs near its return slot. For three days the sign read “RETURNS.” Staff recorded 18 books mistakenly left on the nearby information desk. The next three days, the sign read “RETURN BOOKS HERE” with an arrow pointing to the slot. Staff recorded 6 books left on the information desk. The library was open the same hours, but the number of visitors varied each day.
