Unit 2 Educator Key

Reward precise reading, not confident generalization.

Accept varied interpretations when the learner identifies the actual source, cites precise evidence, explains reasoning, preserves relevant context, and acknowledges what the edition cannot establish.

Fifteen Sessions

Recommended pacing

  • Week 4: category/function; source and edition; Odyssey opening; motif/symbol/context; cultural-function paragraph.
  • Week 5: epic conventions; course-original practice text; heroic evidence matrix; tense/voice; timed analysis.
  • Week 6: comparison criteria; translation difference; thesis/parallelism; conference/revision; submission and defense.

One session is approximately 45–60 minutes. Extend reading, conferencing, oral performance, and revision rather than cutting source documentation or context.

Completion evidence

Do not mark the unit complete from a Hall score, translator output, time counter, or self-scored concept check alone.

  • Two authentic source passages
  • Edition records
  • Annotations and matrix
  • Week 4 paragraph
  • Week 5 timed response
  • Draft, feedback, revision
  • Final essay, map, defense, reflection

Text Selection

Approve the exact passages before instruction.

Minimum source test

  1. The passage is long enough to support structural and stylistic analysis.
  2. The tradition, author/community, translator/editor/collector, edition, and location are identifiable.
  3. Access and reproduction are lawful in the learner’s jurisdiction.
  4. A living tradition is represented through community-authorized public material where possible.
  5. The two selections create a meaningful question rather than a superficial “same archetype” match.
  6. Any historical translation is framed as historically situated, not definitive.
Popol Vuh: The Spence rendering is offered only as an openly accessible historical pathway. Its dated mediation must be taught explicitly. Prefer a contemporary, lawfully accessed K’iche’-informed edition for serious study.
Gilgamesh: Thompson’s 1928 text provides open access but predates later tablets and scholarship. Do not score a learner down for differences caused by another named translation.

Response Guidance

Look for reasoning, not identical wording.

Session 4.1 concept sort

  1. Observation: verifiable repetition in the described passage.
  2. Inference: a reasonable possible meaning that still requires evidence.
  3. Unsupported generalization: it collapses diverse peoples, invents motive, and claims identity without evidence.

A strong definition treats myth as a culturally meaningful narrative category and avoids equating it with factual falsehood.

Session 4.3 Odyssey opening

Accept details such as travel after Troy, knowledge of cities/customs, suffering at sea, attempted leadership, the companions’ folly, divine consequence, and failed homecoming. Strong responses recognize distributed agency: Odysseus attempts to save his companions, but the opening attributes their deaths to their own choice and the god’s response. Diction analysis may examine “ingenious,” “manners and customs,” “sheer folly,” “Sun-god,” or another exact phrase, provided the learner explains how an alternative would alter tone or judgment.

Session 5.2 The Bronze Gate

  • Epithet: first signals expertise, achievement, and public promise; later it becomes ironic because the seventh bridge is sacrificed and moral growth matters more than monumental completion.
  • Duty conflict: the council defines duty as protecting civic property and obeying authority; Saren defines it as distributing risk fairly and accounting for people outside the walls.
  • Hammer symbol: begins as power to build, becomes power to interrupt harmful design, and ends as a broken memorial to revised understanding.

Because this is a course-original practice text, it may test technique but may not serve as one of the two authentic traditions in the final essay.

Session 5.4 language laboratory

Possible revisions:

  • “Saren breaks the lock, and the farmers later honor the hammer.”
  • “The narrative shows that leadership has consequences.”

Other grammatically controlled revisions are valid.

Weeks 5–6 analytical work

Do not prescribe a single heroic model. Strong work compares an analytical criterion such as responsibility, relationship to authority, use of power, treatment of knowledge, response to loss, community obligation, or consequence. It then explains why the same broad pattern means something different in each passage.

Major Assessment

100-point analytic scoring model

Interpretive claim · 20

Defensible comparative thesis, meaningful criterion, complexity, and consistent line of reasoning.

Evidence & reasoning · 25

At least four precise details, accurate representation, explanation after evidence, and no unsupported leap.

Context & difference · 20

Source-specific cultural/historical framing, resistance to stereotyping, and preservation of meaningful difference.

Source integrity · 15

Translator/editor/collector and edition records; accurate quotation/paraphrase; rights/protocol and limitation awareness.

Organization & style · 10

Criteria-based structure, purposeful transitions, parallelism, tense/voice control, and readable prose.

Revision, map & defense · 10

Meaningful change from draft, analytical map, evidence-based defense, and honest reflection.

Mastery: 80/100 or higher with no source-integrity violation. Permit revision after targeted feedback. A polished essay containing fabricated evidence, concealed machine-generated prose, or unattributed language does not demonstrate mastery.

Universal Access

Change access—not the intellectual target.

  • Provide audio or educator read-aloud for lawful passages.
  • Preteach names, context, and vocabulary without pre-solving interpretation.
  • Permit home-language planning, bilingual notes, oral rehearsal, and translator-supported directions.
  • Allow typed, dictated, handwritten, signed, or augmentative responses where the mode does not change the construct.
  • Use shorter passages with equivalent complexity rather than replacing Grade 9 analysis with recall.
  • Offer a written alternative to every network tool and oral activity.

Multilingual scoring

Score the intended construct. If the final task measures English literary analysis, preserve explicit English evidence. Do not penalize home-language planning or mediation that helps the learner reach the same standard.
