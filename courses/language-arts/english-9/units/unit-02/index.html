Myth, Epic & Archetype

Compare origin stories, heroic patterns, archetypes, cultural values, and transformations of source material across civilizations.

Essential Question

How do cultures use stories to explain duty, identity, power, and the unknown?

Text and Source Family

The Epic of Gilgamesh, The Odyssey, Popol Vuh, and teacher-selected passages from African, Asian, Indigenous, and Near Eastern traditions.

Learning Priorities

  • Read closely and cite precise textual evidence.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Use language conventions and vocabulary as tools for meaning and style.
  • Discuss, present, listen, question, and respond with intellectual fairness.
  • Preserve major work in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Comparative literary analysis with a visual archetype map.

Multilingual Access · Literary Integrity

Translation & Tradition Comparison

Use the translator to clarify directions, explore vocabulary, compare language choices, or keep learner-owned notes. In Literary Compare mode, record what changes in voice, register, rhythm, imagery, idiom, syntax, or cultural context.

Academic boundary

  • Name the translator and edition of every published literary passage used as evidence.
  • Treat online machine translation as an unreviewed draft, never as an authoritative literary edition.
  • Do not submit machine-translated prose as evidence of English writing mastery.
  • The complete lesson and assessment remain available without the online service.
