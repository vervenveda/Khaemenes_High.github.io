Essential Question

How can compressed language carry sound, image, argument, memory, and emotion?

Learning Priorities

  • Analyze image, diction, figurative language, sound, syntax, rhythm, ambiguity, and form through precise evidence.
  • Compare poetic and translation choices without treating one version as automatically definitive.
  • Compose original poetry through experimentation, feedback, revision, and performance.
  • Use language conventions and intentional deviation as tools for voice and precision.
  • Preserve analytical, creative, revision, and performance evidence in the portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Canonical evidence: Four-poem portfolio, comparative analysis, oral reading/accessibility-equivalent performance, and fresh unfamiliar-poetry transfer.

Mastery: Every component ≥80% and weighted Unit 4 result ≥80%.

Image → sound/form → comparison/performance → transfer.
