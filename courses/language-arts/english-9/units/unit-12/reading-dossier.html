VERIFIED SOURCE CUSTODY · UNIT 12

Portfolio and capstone provenance inheritance

Week 34 · Portfolio curation. Every selected artifact must retain its original source record: title/work, author/creator, edition or stable locator, source date, passage/item used, attribution, and any rights/license note carried from the originating unit. A polished artifact with detached citations is incomplete.

Week 35 · Capstone synthesis. New mentor/research sources are CONDITIONAL until the same custody fields are complete. Existing authenticated sources from Units 01–11 may be reused without recreating provenance, but the capstone must point back to the original custody record.

Week 36 · Cumulative examination and oral defense. Academy-created unfamiliar passages may be used directly. Any outside unfamiliar passage must carry source metadata in the assessment package before release.

Capstone rule: portfolio curation does not erase source history. The final artifact and oral defense should be able to answer “Where did this evidence come from?” without relying on memory.

Capstone and grading continuity

This read-only companion provides practice structures and reflection models. It does not replace the approved long-form work, capstone, graduation portfolio, cumulative final examination, timed writing, oral defense, completion review or evaluator judgment.

WEEK 34 · UNIT 12

Portfolio Curation & Evidence of Growth

Artifact set

A Grade 9 student has an early diagnostic paragraph, a revised literary analysis, an argument map, a technical explanation, a multimedia publication and a research-based reflection.

Curation principle

The strongest portfolio is not simply the highest-scoring work. It should demonstrate breadth, revision, transfer and increasingly independent judgment.

Worked model

Pair an early and later artifact to show a concrete change: stronger evidence integration, narrower claims, more precise attribution, better counterargument, improved structure or more effective audience adaptation.

Independent task

Select four to six artifacts. For each, write a standards-based evidence claim, identify what changed during revision, and name one area where growth is still incomplete.

WEEK 35 · UNIT 12

Synthesis Capstone & Revision Dossier

Capstone problem

The student must connect two course texts and one research or public-communication source around a focused question.

Synthesis principle

A capstone should create a new relationship among sources rather than summarize them one by one. Organize around agreement, tension, pattern, limitation or transfer.

Worked model

A literary text may reveal subjective experience, a research source may establish a documented pattern, and a public notice may show how institutions communicate. Their differences are part of the synthesis rather than a problem to erase.

Independent task

Create a synthesis matrix, draft a qualified thesis, include one fair counterreading, preserve a substantive before/after revision, and prepare a five-minute defense outline.

WEEK 36 · UNIT 12

Cumulative Final, Corrections & Oral Defense

Final boundary

The standalone final is the authoritative cumulative assessment under evaluator-agreed conditions. Practice material on this dossier must not become an answer key.

Correction model

A meaningful correction explains what reasoning failed, identifies the correct evidence or method, and states how the new understanding changes future work.

Defense structure

Prepare to explain one strong artifact, one major revision, one limitation, one course concept transferred to a new task, and one next-step goal.

Independent task

Complete retrieval practice, preserve corrections separately from original responses, and prepare a concise oral-defense record that points to specific portfolio evidence.

Unit 12 synthesis

Preserve artifact provenance, source locators, process records, drafts, revisions, corrections and reflections in the existing course workspaces. Practice examples are instructional material only and must not be treated as live final-exam answers, real scholarship or real public data.
