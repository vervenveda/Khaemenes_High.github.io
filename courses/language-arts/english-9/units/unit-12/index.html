Essential Question

How can a student demonstrate growth, judgment, independence, and a distinctive command of language?

Text and Source Family

The student's complete body of Grade 9 work, selected mentor texts, research sources, feedback records, revision histories, assessments, and reflective evidence.

Learning Priorities

  • Curate portfolio evidence because it demonstrates a claim about learning—not merely because it received a high score or looks polished.
  • Compare earlier and later work to identify specific changes in reading, reasoning, writing, research, language, speaking/listening, revision, and independence.
  • Distinguish completion, improvement, mastery, transfer, and independence when making claims about growth.
  • Substantially revise selected major work using current Grade 9 knowledge rather than cosmetically correcting old drafts.
  • Synthesize literary, informational, research, argument, creative, and multimodal learning in a capstone without collapsing the modes into a scrapbook.
  • Complete cumulative assessment through independent application, with the 80% mastery standard and targeted reassessment where required.
  • Defend portfolio claims orally by locating artifacts, explaining decisions, responding to unfamiliar questions, and acknowledging remaining areas for growth.
  • Use Eiren and editorial/research tools as mentors and verification supports while preserving independent student authorship and assessment integrity.
  • Preserve the final Grade 9 portfolio as a usable bridge into English 10.
