Essential Question

How can I prove that my reasoning has become independent, transferable, and responsive to evidence?

Learning Priorities

  • Curate evidence because it supports a precise claim about learning—not because it is attractive or highly scored.
  • Distinguish completion, improvement, mastery, transfer, and independence.
  • Compare earlier/later work and identify what actually changed in reasoning, reading, writing, research, language, speaking/listening, and revision.
  • Identify limitations, counterexamples, and artifacts that complicate claims about personal growth.
  • Substantially revise prior work using current knowledge rather than cosmetically correcting it.
  • Synthesize literary, informational, research, argument, creative, and multimodal reasoning without collapsing the capstone into a scrapbook.
  • Apply Grade 9 reasoning independently to unfamiliar texts, evidence, source problems, and writing situations.
  • Respond to unpredictable oral questions by locating evidence, explaining warrants, revising overbroad claims, and acknowledging uncertainty.
  • Distinguish confidence from demonstrated competence and effort from mastery.
  • Form English 10 goals from evidence of remaining limitations rather than generic aspiration.

Evidence Contract

Cumulative Critical-Thinking Spine

The student now carries the reasoning system.

Claim

Make a narrow claim about a text, source, argument, product, decision, or your own learning.

Locate

Point to the exact textual, source, portfolio, performance, or revision evidence that supports the claim.

Test

Explain the warrant, inspect provenance/context, and identify what the evidence establishes and what it does not.

Countertest

Search for alternative explanations, anomalies, counterevidence, failure modes, opposing interpretations, or limitations.

Recalibrate & Transfer

Keep, narrow, qualify, revise, or abandon the claim—then demonstrate the underlying skill again in an unfamiliar context.
