Essential Question

How can we know whether an explanation, procedure, or visual actually works?

Learning Priorities

  • Choose explanatory structures according to reader needs, content relationships, and purpose.
  • Define terms and expose prerequisite knowledge and hidden assumptions.
  • Explain mechanisms and causal relationships without claiming more than evidence supports.
  • Test procedural writing for accuracy, safety, completeness, sequence, branches, edge cases, and reproducibility.
  • Use failed tests as evidence rather than hiding them.
  • Interrogate data displays for provenance, population/sample, units, denominator, scale, aggregation, omissions, uncertainty, and visual framing.
  • Separate observation from inference and causal explanation.
  • Design accessibility as a second path to meaning rather than decorative compliance.
  • Revise from actual reader/user evidence and retest.

Evidence Contract

Critical-Thinking Spine

Technical communication must survive contact with reality.

Define the Problem

Who is the user? What do they need to understand or accomplish? What prior knowledge and constraints are assumed?

Model the Explanation

Why should this structure, sequence, mechanism, comparison, or visual representation work?

Test It

Use a reader, user, verified data, or structured audit. Record what succeeds and what fails instead of relying on author intention.

Find Failure Modes

Inspect ambiguity, edge cases, missing prerequisites, unsafe steps, unsupported causal claims, misleading scales, inaccessible cues, and alternative interpretations.

Revise & Retest

Make a targeted repair and gather new evidence. Explain whether the repair solved the original failure and what uncertainty remains.
