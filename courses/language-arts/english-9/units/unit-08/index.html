Essential Question

How do writers make complex processes and ideas accurate, usable, accessible, and clear?

Text and Source Family

Science, environment, health, technology, and public-information texts drawn from appropriate public and Academy learning resources.

Learning Priorities

  • Choose explanatory structures according to reader needs, content relationships, and purpose.
  • Write precise definitions, classifications, comparisons, cause/effect explanations, and technical procedures.
  • Test procedural writing for accuracy, safety, completeness, sequence, and reproducibility.
  • Interpret data displays and design captions/visual explanations without overstating what the data show.
  • Use accessibility principles so information remains available beyond one sensory or presentation mode.
  • Write through planning, evidence gathering, drafting, testing, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Preserve major work in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren tests whether information is genuinely usable.

Bring an explanatory structure, definition, procedure, caption, visual plan, accessibility decision, or draft first. Eiren can act as a questioning reader, expose missing assumptions and ambiguous steps, and ask whether words and visuals divide the explanatory work effectively.
