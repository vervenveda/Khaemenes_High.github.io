WEEK 22 · UNIT 08

Explaining a Complex System

Source A · Heat-island explainer

On a hot afternoon, roofs, pavement, trees, shade, wind and building materials do not warm at the same rate. Dark pavement can absorb and store solar energy, while shaded soil and vegetation may remain cooler because less direct radiation reaches the surface and water can move from leaves into the air. A reader should not conclude that every paved place is always hotter than every planted place; cloud cover, moisture, time of day, surface material and measurement location matter. An effective explanation therefore separates the general mechanism from the conditions that can change the outcome.

Source B · Structure comparison

A definition-first explanation would introduce urban heat islands and key terms before giving examples. A cause-and-effect structure would trace energy absorption, storage and release. A comparison structure could contrast two surfaces measured under similar conditions. Each structure can be accurate, but the best choice depends on what the reader must understand or do.

Worked model

A strong explanatory paragraph states the governing idea, uses a structure that matches the reasoning problem, and signals limits. For example: “Dark surfaces often warm more than shaded vegetation because they absorb and retain more incoming energy, although moisture, weather and measurement conditions can change the size of the difference.” The qualification protects accuracy without weakening the explanation.

Independent task

Create a structure map using definition, cause-effect and comparison. Choose one governing structure for a 250–350 word explanation and justify the choice. Cite at least three exact details and identify one condition that prevents overgeneralization.

WEEK 23 · UNIT 08

Writing a Reproducible Procedure

Source A · Seed-germination protocol

Prepare two labeled containers with equal amounts of the same growing medium. Place five seeds of the same type at the same depth in each container. Add the same measured volume of water to both. Put one container in the assigned light condition and the other in the comparison condition. Observe once each day at approximately the same time. Record visible germination, stem length and notable changes. Do not change watering, container position or measurement method without recording the change.

Source B · Safety and reproducibility note

A procedure is usable only if another person can repeat it without guessing. Terms such as “a little water,” “some sunlight,” or “check often” hide important variables. Reproducibility improves when quantities, timing, sequence, equipment, decision points and exceptions are stated explicitly.

Worked model

Replace “water the seeds regularly” with “add 20 mL of water to each container at 4:00 p.m. daily unless standing water remains visible.” The revision gives a quantity, schedule and exception. It also reveals a decision point that can be evaluated.

Independent task

Find five vague instructions in a familiar procedure and rewrite them for reproducibility. Then test your procedure with a second reader who must mark every place where they still need to guess.

WEEK 24 · UNIT 08

Data, Captions & Accessible Information

Source A · Fictional temperature table

At 2:00 p.m., three fictional schoolyard locations were measured: shaded garden 30.4°C, light concrete walkway 33.1°C, dark asphalt court 36.8°C. Measurements were taken once on one clear day with the same handheld sensor. The table does not establish a yearly average or prove that surface material alone caused the differences.

Source B · Caption choices

Weak caption: “Asphalt is dangerously hotter.” Better caption: “Single-afternoon surface measurements at three schoolyard locations; values describe this observation only.” A useful caption tells readers what was measured, when, where, and what the display cannot establish.

Worked model

An accessible infographic should not require color alone to distinguish categories. Labels, patterns, direct values, logical reading order and descriptive alt text make the information usable in print, grayscale and assistive technology.

Independent task

Design a small table or chart from the three measurements. Write a precise title, caption, alt text and 120-word interpretation that states both the pattern and the limits of the evidence.
