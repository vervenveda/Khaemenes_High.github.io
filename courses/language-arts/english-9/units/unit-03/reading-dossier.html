WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Character, Conflict & Setting

Original practice fiction · The Closed Greenhouse

The school greenhouse had been locked since June, but Devin still watered the tray outside its door every Monday. When the new custodian finally asked why, Devin said, “Because the science club will come back.” He did not mention that the club had dissolved after his best friend moved away. In October, the custodian left the greenhouse key on the windowsill without a note.

Reading model

Characterization emerges through action, omission, dialogue, setting and repeated choices. The locked greenhouse is not automatically a symbol; its meaning depends on how Devin acts around it and how the setting changes the conflict.

Independent task

With an approved authentic short story, identify one character choice, one setting detail and one conflict. Cite exact locators and explain how the setting intensifies or complicates the choice.

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Point of View, Structure & Irony

Original practice pair

VERSION A: I knew the envelope was empty before I handed it to Mara. VERSION B: Tomas handed Mara the envelope and watched her weigh it in her palm. In the first version the narrator reveals hidden knowledge; in the second, the reader must infer what Tomas knows.

Worked model

Point of view controls access to thought and information. Structure controls when that information arrives. Irony often depends on a gap between what a character, narrator and reader know.

Independent task

Choose an approved story using a distinctive viewpoint or structural device. Map what the reader knows at three moments, what a character knows, and how the order of revelation affects one interpretation.

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Narrative Workshop

Mentor practice opening

At 6:42 the bakery sign still said CLOSED, but the first customer was already sitting inside. Lena stopped with the key halfway into the lock.

Craft model

A strong opening creates a specific situation, pressure and question without explaining everything. Dialogue, sensory detail and pacing should serve character or conflict rather than decorate the page.

Independent task

Study an approved mentor opening, dialogue passage or ending. Record exact title/author/source and identify three craft moves. Draft an original scene, then annotate where you borrowed a technique without copying the mentor’s language.
