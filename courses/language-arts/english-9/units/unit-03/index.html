Essential Question

How do writers shape a reader’s experience through character, structure, perspective, and detail?

Text and Source Family

Public-domain short fiction by writers such as Edgar Allan Poe, Kate Chopin, Anton Chekhov, Rabindranath Tagore, and Ryūnosuke Akutagawa.

Learning Priorities

  • Read closely and cite precise textual evidence.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Use language conventions and vocabulary as tools for meaning and style.
  • Discuss, present, listen, question, and respond with intellectual fairness.
  • Preserve major work in the Grade 9 portfolio.

Unit 3 required reading · The Library Key

Original Khaemenes Academy passage for Grade 9; available offline.

A librarian left a spare key hanging near the door, although no visitor was permitted to use it. Every evening the key caught the last sunlight. When the library closed for repairs, Mara noticed the key was gone.

The story never explains who removed it. Mara wondered whether the missing key marked an ending or a promise that the room would open again.

Focus: Theme and voice. Read twice. Underline two specific details, explain how they support or complicate an interpretation, and write an evidence-based paragraph relevant to this unit's focus. Keep the paragraph in your existing portfolio; revisit it during Weeks 7–9.

Unit reading-response assessment · 100 points

After completing the unit reading, submit a response in the existing portfolio. Your teacher can use this rubric for feedback; this page does not automatically change your grade or mastery status.
