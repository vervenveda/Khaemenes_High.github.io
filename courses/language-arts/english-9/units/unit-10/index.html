Essential Question

What makes an argument fair, rigorous, responsive, and persuasive?

Text and Source Family

Paired arguments, policy excerpts, civic documents, editorials, and evidence sets selected for balance, source transparency, accessibility, and age-appropriate complexity.

Learning Priorities

  • Distinguish issue, question, claim, reason, evidence, assumption, warrant, counterclaim, rebuttal, and concession.
  • Analyze how framing and definitions can narrow, broaden, or distort an issue before evidence is considered.
  • Represent opposing arguments in forms their proponents could recognize before attempting rebuttal.
  • Evaluate evidence for relevance, sufficiency, credibility, representativeness, context, and limitations.
  • Recognize uncertainty and use qualifiers/modal language proportionate to evidence.
  • Distinguish persuasive technique from sound reasoning and avoid manipulative shortcuts.
  • Debate through listening, questioning, evidence use, response, and revision rather than performance alone.
  • Write through planning, research, drafting, counterargument, feedback, substantive revision, editing, and reflection.
  • Preserve major work in the Grade 9 portfolio.
