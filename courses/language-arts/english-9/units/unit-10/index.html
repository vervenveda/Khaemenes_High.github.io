Essential Question

How should a responsible thinker reason when intelligent, informed people disagree?

Learning Priorities

  • Define issue, scope, terms, stakeholders, constraints, assumptions, and decision criteria before arguing.
  • Recognize framing effects and reframe issues from multiple legitimate perspectives.
  • Represent an opposing argument in a form its proponents could recognize before rebuttal.
  • Verify evidence for provenance, relevance, sufficiency, representativeness, independence, limitations, and uncertainty.
  • Expose warrants and test causal claims, comparison groups, base rates, tradeoffs, opportunity costs, and unintended consequences where relevant.
  • Search for evidence that could weaken the student's preferred position as seriously as evidence that supports it.
  • Distinguish factual disagreement, value disagreement, prediction, uncertainty, and competing priorities.
  • Use cross-examination to produce information rather than humiliation or rhetorical traps.
  • Concede, qualify, synthesize, or revise when opposition reveals a genuine weakness.
  • Preserve unresolved uncertainty instead of manufacturing certainty or consensus.

Evidence Contract

Critical-Thinking Spine

Reason against yourself before trying to defeat someone else.

Frame

Define the actual decision/problem, scope, terms, stakeholders, values, constraints, and what would count as success.

Steelman

Construct the strongest evidence-grounded version of competing positions. Weak caricatures do not count as counterclaims.

Verify

Trace evidence and test what it actually establishes. Separate facts, predictions, causal inferences, values, and assumptions.

Stress-Test

Ask about counterevidence, edge cases, base rates, costs, tradeoffs, alternatives, unintended consequences, and revision conditions.

Deliberate & Update

Identify surviving disagreements and common ground. Revise when warranted; retain a position when it survives serious challenge; preserve uncertainty where evidence remains incomplete.
