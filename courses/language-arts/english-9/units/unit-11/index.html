Essential Question

How do words, images, sound, layout, and sequence work together responsibly for a real audience?

Text and Source Family

Student-selected mentor texts, literary magazines, visual essays, poems, short nonfiction, art criticism, exhibits, and multimedia compositions selected for accessibility, instructional value, and clear source/rights boundaries.

Learning Priorities

  • Analyze audience, purpose, genre, medium, circulation context, and accessibility needs before composing.
  • Distinguish what each mode—word, image, sound, typography, space, sequence, interaction—contributes rather than duplicating information without purpose.
  • Use visual hierarchy and editorial design to guide attention without manipulating readers through inaccessible or misleading presentation.
  • Develop and apply a house style while preserving intentional authorial voice and justified exceptions.
  • Revise collaboratively through defined editorial roles, respectful feedback, version awareness, and author approval.
  • Credit sources and creative contributions accurately; distinguish public-domain, licensed, permission-based, and original material where relevant.
  • Address privacy, consent, attribution, accessibility, and representation before public-facing publication.
  • Test whether essential meaning survives when a reader cannot access a particular mode such as color, image, audio, or motion.
  • Publish only work that has passed content, source, accessibility, proofreading, and final-consistency checks.
  • Preserve planning, drafts, feedback, rights/credit records, accessibility checks, finished publication, and reflection in the Grade 9 portfolio.
