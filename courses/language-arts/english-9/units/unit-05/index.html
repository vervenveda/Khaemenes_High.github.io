Essential Question

How does drama place competing duties, values, and consequences into public view?

Text and Source Family

Sophocles' Antigone or Shakespeare's Julius Caesar, with teacher-selected shorter scenes from world drama.

Learning Priorities

  • Read dramatic language closely and cite precise textual and stage evidence.
  • Analyze how bodies, space, silence, stage directions, structure, and performance choices affect interpretation.
  • Examine competing duties and claims without reducing complex conflicts to simplistic right/wrong summaries.
  • Listen, question, build, challenge, qualify, and revise during collaborative inquiry.
  • Translate interpretation into evidence-based staging choices.
  • Demonstrate independent transfer on an unfamiliar dramatic scene.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Mastery evidence: 40% dramatic literary analysis · 20% Socratic seminar · 25% staged-interpretation rationale · 15% unfamiliar-scene transfer.
