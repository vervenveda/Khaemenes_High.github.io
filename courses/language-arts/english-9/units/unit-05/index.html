Essential Question

How does drama place competing duties, values, and consequences into public view?

Text and Source Family

Sophocles' Antigone or Shakespeare's Julius Caesar, with teacher-selected shorter scenes from world drama.

Learning Priorities

  • Read dramatic language closely and cite precise textual/stage evidence.
  • Analyze how bodies, space, silence, stage directions, structure, and performance choices affect interpretation.
  • Examine competing duties and claims without reducing complex conflicts to simplistic right/wrong summaries.
  • Write and discuss through planning, evidence, feedback, revision, listening, questioning, and intellectual fairness.
  • Preserve analytical, seminar, staged-performance, and revision evidence in the Grade 9 portfolio.
