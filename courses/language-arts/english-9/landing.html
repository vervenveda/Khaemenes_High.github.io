English I Journey

Build the foundation without lowering the ceiling.

The course begins with explicit support and progressively asks students to make more independent decisions about evidence, interpretation, craft, research, revision, and communication.

Read & Interpret

Annotate, infer, cite, compare, analyze literary craft, examine rhetoric, and develop increasingly independent interpretations of literary and informational texts.

Write & Revise

Develop sentences, paragraphs, narrative craft, analysis, argument, research writing, speeches, creative work, and publication through visible planning and meaningful revision.

Research & Communicate

Evaluate sources, use evidence responsibly, practice citation, participate in discussion and presentation, and learn how language choices affect audiences and communities.

A+++ Instructional Contract

Every week has to teach.

The forensic audit evaluates the actual student experience—not the filename, title, or presence of a polished template.

Distinct lesson identity

Each week must have a genuine academic purpose, appropriate texts or text families, explicit vocabulary/language instruction, models, guided practice, independent work, and reflection.

Evidence before completion

Students should preserve meaningful artifacts and demonstrate learning through appropriate checks, writing, discussion, assessment, and portfolio evidence rather than simple button completion.

Connected learning

Tools, apps, games, archives, and creative resources belong inside the instructional sequence when they strengthen the objective—not as a decorative resource shelf.

Accessible pathways

Support should improve access without lowering the intellectual goal, with readable layouts, keyboard access, printable work where appropriate, and clear alternatives for learners who need them.

Designated Language Arts Mentor

Eiren

Eiren supports English I learners as a mentor for reading, writing, language, research, reflection, and communication. Eiren does not replace the student's thinking, writing, or mastery evidence.

Bring the work to the conversation

Students can bring a difficult passage, an interpretation, notes, a thesis, a draft, a vocabulary question, research evidence, or a revision problem and use mentoring to examine choices and identify a productive next step.

Open Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Reading conference

Ask questions, test an inference, compare interpretations, clarify literary or rhetorical techniques, and return to the text for stronger evidence.

Writing conference

Discuss purpose, organization, evidence, style, clarity, revision priorities, and reflection while protecting the learner's authorship and voice.

Beta Learning Program

Help us find what the curriculum cannot see about itself.

English I is being inspected lesson by lesson. Beta feedback is the human-facing improvement pathway for students, families, educators, and testers.

Report the experience

Flag unclear instructions, broken links, accessibility barriers, factual concerns, duplicated lesson patterns, weak assessment alignment, confusing mastery behavior, or a tool that does not actually help the lesson.

Canonical route pending verification

No internal or guessed Beta destination is published here. Once the approved public Beta route is verified, it can be embedded consistently across the course and every lesson.

Public-Surface Rule

The classroom shows the learning—not the machinery.

Student-facing English I pages may identify approved public educational resources and explain what students should do with them. They must not expose credentials, tokens, private endpoints, internal repositories, protected routes, service topology, administrative maps, orchestration details, storage architecture, or security implementation.

Public destination + educational purpose = appropriate. Internal route + infrastructure explanation = keep out of the classroom surface.
