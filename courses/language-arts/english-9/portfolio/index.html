Cumulative Evidence · Unit 12

Grade 9 Portfolio

Growth made visible through selected, revised, challenged, and defended work.

The portfolio is not a trophy case or a folder containing everything. It is an evidence-based argument demonstrating what the student can now do, how the reasoning changed, where the proof is located, what counterevidence remains, and which skills transfer independently.

Required Artifact Families

  • Baseline diagnostic and revised comparison
  • Literary analysis with substantive revision evidence
  • Polished narrative and/or poetry portfolio with craft reflection
  • Speech, seminar, performance, or debate record with listening/response evidence
  • Research proposal, source audit, annotated bibliography, research trail, and report
  • Informational or technical composition with tested procedure and/or accessible visual evidence
  • Argument with framing analysis, counterclaim, rebuttal, and post-engagement revision
  • Publication or multimodal composition with editorial, trust, rights, and accessibility records
  • Capstone synthesis project
  • Fresh-transfer/cumulative assessment evidence where appropriate
  • Final reflection, oral-defense evidence map, and English 10 transition goals

Artifact Record

For each artifact, identify task, date, competency, exact evidence location, strongest revision, feedback used, support/tools used, counterevidence or limitation, and next-step skill.

Evidence Readiness Check

  • Artifacts are dated and titled.
  • Draft and final versions are distinguishable.
  • Sources, permissions, consent, and credits are recorded where relevant.
  • Rubrics or teacher feedback are attached where applicable.
  • Preparation support is distinguishable from independently demonstrated performance.
  • Reflection addresses evidence rather than vague effort or confidence.
  • At least one limitation, counterexample, or unresolved growth area is acknowledged.
  • At least one skill is shown transferring to unfamiliar material.
  • Files are exported beyond browser storage.

This records organizational readiness only. It does not satisfy Unit 12 or final English 9 mastery.

Growth Claim Matrix

Claim → evidence → warrant → counterevidence → recalibration.

Every important claim about learning should be narrow enough to test and strong enough to matter.

Claim

What precisely can you now do? Avoid “I got better at writing.” Name the competency and scope.

Evidence

Where exactly is the proof? Identify artifact, passage, version, source record, performance record, or assessment evidence.

Warrant

Why does that evidence demonstrate the claim rather than merely accompany it?

Counterevidence

What artifact, criterion, failed attempt, limitation, or unfamiliar task makes the claim harder to defend?

Recalibration

Keep, narrow, qualify, or revise the claim so it matches the complete evidence rather than the student's preferred story.

Substantial Revision Evidence

Show the decision, not just the cleaner final draft.

For at least two major artifacts, preserve an earlier state and a current revision. Identify the original problem, evidence or feedback revealing the problem, alternative revisions considered, chosen change, and the effect on meaning, reasoning, evidence, structure, audience, or accessibility. Cosmetic correction alone does not demonstrate substantial revision.

Critical-Thinking Evidence Across the Year

Show that the reasoning system accumulated.

Verify

Show an example of tracing provenance, checking evidence, identifying uncertainty, or rejecting a weak source/claim.

Failure-Test

Show an explanation, procedure, visual, argument, or design that failed a real test—and the repair that followed.

Revise a Hypothesis

Show a literary, research, or argumentative conclusion that changed because later evidence complicated it.

Steelman / Counterread

Show where you represented a serious competing interpretation or position fairly before responding.

Transfer

Show the same underlying skill succeeding on unfamiliar material without a rehearsed answer.

Oral Defense

Prepare evidence—not canned answers.

  1. State a narrow claim about growth, mastery, transfer, or independence.
  2. Locate the exact artifact evidence.
  3. Explain the warrant connecting evidence to the claim.
  4. Identify what the artifact does not prove.
  5. Respond to a limitation, counterexample, or unfamiliar follow-up.
  6. Keep, narrow, qualify, or revise the claim based on the evidence.
  7. Explain one consequential revision and one remaining growth area.
  8. Identify an English 10 goal with a concrete success criterion.
Changing an overbroad claim because the evidence does not support it is evidence of stronger reasoning, not a failed defense.

Eiren · Rehearsal Examiner

Use Eiren to pressure-test claims, not manufacture them.

Bring the artifact, growth claim, evidence location, revision history, counterevidence, transfer example, or defense question first. Eiren can challenge whether a claim is too broad, whether the portfolio actually proves independence, and whether a supposed revision was consequential.

Practice with Eiren
Eiren cannot invent missing artifacts, fabricate feedback, write the assessed reflection, certify mastery, or participate in independently scored defense portions unless explicitly permitted as an accommodation/support.

Beta Program

Audit the portfolio for authenticity, access, and evidence quality.

Report broken artifact links, inaccessible materials, vague reflections accepted as proof, incomparable before/after evidence, cosmetic edits presented as substantial revision, missing source/rights records, unsupported claims of independence, or any path that confuses portfolio readiness with verified mastery.

Open Beta Program
Public-facing portfolio guidance must not expose private student information, credentials, protected endpoints, repository secrets, internal topology, network mapping, or protected answer material.
