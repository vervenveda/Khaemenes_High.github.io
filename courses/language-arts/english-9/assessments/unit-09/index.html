Unit 9 · The Novel & Sustained Inquiry

Interpretation is treated as a revisable hypothesis. Students must account for longitudinal evidence, anomalies, counterreadings, competing explanations, context, uncertainty, and fresh textual evidence.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four components establish Unit 9 mastery.

45%

Sustained Literary Analysis

A 1,200–1,500-word argument grounded primarily in the novel. The thesis must explain a genuine interpretive problem, use evidence across separated portions of the text, confront a serious counterreading or anomaly, distinguish textual evidence from context/criticism, and show substantive revision.

Literary Analysis Rubric
20%

Longitudinal Evidence & Hypothesis Trail

Reading progress, questions, character/motif/theme records, provisional interpretations, disconfirming passages, hypothesis changes, and at least one documented revision caused by later evidence.

20%

Independent Reading Defense

Use the novel and longitudinal records to locate and explain passages, connect evidence across the work, defend or qualify an interpretation, and respond independently to an unrehearsed text-grounded question.

15%

Fresh-Passage Transfer

Analyze an unfamiliar or previously unstudied passage from the selected novel. Generate more than one plausible reading, identify assumptions, test each against language/form/context, and explain whether the passage strengthens, weakens, complicates, or leaves the student's larger interpretation unresolved.

Critical-Thinking Protocol

Hypothesize → accumulate → challenge → revise → defend → transfer.

A literary interpretation earns strength by explaining evidence, not by sounding sophisticated. Students actively search for passages their interpretation has difficulty explaining. A thesis may be narrowed, qualified, substantially revised, or abandoned when later textual evidence demands it.

Critical lenses are question-generators, not answer machines. Historical context can constrain implausible claims but cannot manufacture textual evidence. Secondary criticism enters the conversation without replacing the novel.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach 80%.

Corrective Learning

Repair the interpretive weakness.

Analysis Below 80%

Identify whether the weakness is thesis scope, evidence selection, warrant, counterreading, synthesis, context boundaries, or revision. Repair the relevant sections and reassess.

Evidence Trail Below 80%

Return to separated passages across the novel and rebuild chronology, pattern, anomaly, and hypothesis-change records.

Defense Below 80%

Revisit direct textual knowledge and practice locating, connecting, and explaining passages without relying on summaries.

Transfer Below 80%

Practice comparative interpretation on another passage, then reassess with fresh text and no supplied conclusion.

Eiren · Counterreading Examiner

Eiren searches for the passage the thesis cannot explain.

Eiren may challenge scope, assumptions, causal claims about character, motif patterns, lens dependence, anomalies, and counterreadings. Eiren cannot invent quotations or page locations, replace reading with summaries, impose an interpretation, write the assessed analysis, answer the independent transfer, assign scores, or override mastery.
