Unit 6 · Rhetoric, Reasoning & Public Voice

Mastery measures rhetorical analysis, reasoning repair, responsible evidence use, public communication, and reflective source integrity—not ideological agreement, charisma, or performance style.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four components establish Unit 6 mastery.

20%

Rhetorical-Situation Analysis

Analyze audience, purpose, context, occasion/exigence, constraints, claims, appeals, style, and relevant delivery choices. Distinguish persuasive effectiveness from factual accuracy, logical soundness, and ethical responsibility.

25%

Reasoning Map & Repair

Map claim → reason → evidence → warrant, identify a genuine weakness such as unsupported inference, misleading appeal, causal overreach, false choice, or other reasoning defect, and repair the argument rather than merely naming a fallacy.

Argument Rubric
40%

Evidence-Based Speech

A four-to-six-minute speech or approved accessible equivalent with a clear rhetorical situation, defensible claim, verified evidence, oral attribution where appropriate, logical architecture, purposeful language, and delivery choices that support audience comprehension.

Speaking & Listening Rubric
15%

Rhetorical Self-Analysis & Source Record

Preserve source/provenance records and explain consequential choices in evidence, structure, language, revision, and delivery. Identify at least one limitation or change the student would make after reviewing the final performance.

Fairness & Intellectual Independence

Assessment measures reasoning and communication—not conformity.

Agreement with a political, civic, cultural, or institutional position cannot determine the score. Students may defend different conclusions when they satisfy the same evidence, reasoning, source-integrity, and communication standards.

Accent, disability, charisma, eye-contact convention, mobility, theatrical confidence, or one preferred speaking style cannot determine mastery. Approved accessible equivalents must preserve the underlying targets.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach mastery.

All four components must be present. Each required scored component must reach at least 80%, and the weighted Unit 6 result must also reach at least 80%.

Corrective Learning & Reassessment

Repair the missed rhetorical competency.

Situation Analysis Below 80%

Return to audience, purpose, context, exigence, constraints, appeals, style, and the distinction between effectiveness, truth, logic, and ethics. Reassess with a fresh rhetorical passage when appropriate.

Reasoning Below 80%

Rebuild claim/reason/evidence/warrant relationships, identify the actual inferential defect, repair it, and explain why the repair is stronger.

Speech Below 80%

Target the weak criterion—evidence, architecture, clarity, attribution, audience access, language, or delivery—then revise/rehearse and complete a new or meaningfully changed performance.

Source/Reflection Below 80%

Repair missing provenance, unsupported factual claims, vague reflection, or superficial revision claims by preserving verifiable source records and comparing actual draft/performance evidence.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren is a reasoning and rehearsal examiner—not a speechwriter.

Eiren may challenge audience assumptions, test warrants, question evidence, simulate a skeptical listener, or help identify a revision target before assessment. Eiren cannot write or deliver the assessed speech, fabricate evidence, supply independently scored answers, choose the student's civic position, assign the verified score, or override mastery.

Open Eiren

Beta Program

Audit rhetoric for rigor, neutrality, access, and source integrity.

Report inaccessible speeches, one-sided context, scoring that rewards ideological agreement or personality, weak source boundaries, broken tools, fallacy exercises that reward labels without reasoning, or any route that appears to bypass the evidence contract.

Open Beta Program
Beta participation is never assessment evidence. Public surfaces must not expose protected answer material, credentials, tokens, private endpoints, internal repository machinery, infrastructure topology, or network mapping.
