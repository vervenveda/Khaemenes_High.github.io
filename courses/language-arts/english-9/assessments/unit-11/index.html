Canonical Mastery Contract · Editorial Critical Reasoning

Unit 11 · Creative Media & Publication

Students must demonstrate not only creative production, but editorial judgment: how medium changes meaning, how design directs attention, what should be published, what should be revised or withheld, what a reader may infer, and what responsibilities follow publication.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four independently reviewed components establish mastery.

35%

Curated Publication & Editorial Statement

A literary magazine, digital exhibit, or themed publication whose curation, sequencing, multimodal choices, attribution, accessibility, and editorial statement form a coherent argument about audience and purpose. Students explain consequential inclusion, exclusion, ordering, and design decisions.

25%

Editorial Decision & Revision Trail

Preserve audience analysis, submissions/prototypes, feedback, version history, author approvals, substantive edits, rejected alternatives, and evidence explaining why revisions improved—or intentionally preserved—the work.

25%

Trust, Rights & Accessibility Audit

Audit attribution, factual/source integrity, permissions/consent where applicable, privacy, representation, captions/transcripts, text alternatives, reading order, non-color-dependent meaning, motion, and accessibility failure modes. Unresolved status remains unresolved.

15%

Fresh Media/Editorial Transfer

Given an unfamiliar multimodal composition or publication scenario, identify audience assumptions, rhetorical effects, omissions, misleading or manipulative design possibilities, accessibility barriers, attribution/consent concerns, and competing editorial choices; then defend a responsible revision/publication decision.

Critical-Thinking Protocol

Interpret → design → interrogate → test → decide → account.

Students ask what each mode contributes, what the design causes a reader to notice or overlook, what assumptions are embedded in curation, whether another interpretation is plausible, who gains or loses access, and what evidence justifies publication decisions.

A beautiful publication can fail mastery. Visual polish, popularity, virality, or technical sophistication cannot compensate for misleading presentation, weak attribution, inaccessible meaning, privacy/consent failures, or unexamined editorial judgment.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach 80%.

Corrective Learning

Repair the editorial failure mode.

Publication Below 80%

Revisit audience, curation, sequence, mode relationships, clarity, editorial statement, or responsible publication boundaries and revise the affected publication evidence.

Decision Trail Below 80%

Reconstruct actual versions, feedback, rejected alternatives, author approvals, and reasoning so substantive editorial judgment is visible.

Trust/Access Below 80%

Repair attribution, provenance, permission/consent, privacy, representation, accessibility, or unresolved-status handling and repeat the audit.

Transfer Below 80%

Practice on a different media scenario, then reassess with unfamiliar material and no supplied editorial decision.

Eiren · Editorial Adversary

Eiren asks what the publication makes the audience believe, notice, or miss.

Eiren may challenge curation, hierarchy, sequencing, representation, omitted context, attribution, accessibility, editorial consistency, and competing design choices. Eiren cannot grant rights or consent, certify accessibility without testing, rewrite contributors without approval, publish on the student's behalf, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
