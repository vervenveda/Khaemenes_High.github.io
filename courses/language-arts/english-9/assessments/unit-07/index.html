Unit 7 · Research, Evidence & Media Literacy

From this unit forward, students must make their reasoning inspectable: claim → evidence → warrant → assumptions → counterevidence → alternative explanation → provenance → uncertainty → conclusion → revision.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four reviewed components establish mastery.

45%

Research Report

A 900–1,200-word synthesis organized by the research question and relationships among evidence. The report must distinguish evidence from inference, represent disagreement honestly, expose important warrants and assumptions, address counterevidence or alternative explanations, calibrate certainty, and revise conclusions when the evidence requires it.

Research Rubric
30%

Source Audit & Annotated Bibliography

Trace provenance, expertise, evidence, incentives, source lineage, context, corroboration, limitations, and missing information. A source is not simply labeled credible/not credible; students judge what particular claims it can responsibly support.

15%

Fresh Source-Verification Transfer

Investigate an unrehearsed claim/source cluster. Separate observation from inference, trace the claim toward origin, compare independent evidence, test alternative explanations, identify uncertainty, and state what would change the conclusion.

10%

Research Trail & Reflection

Preserve question mutations, search decisions, dead ends, source changes, rejected evidence, draft revisions, and at least one documented moment where stronger evidence caused the student to qualify, revise, or abandon an earlier belief.

Critical-Thinking Standard

Skepticism alone is not critical thinking.

Students are assessed on disciplined judgment: asking what the evidence actually establishes, recognizing uncertainty, considering serious alternatives, and changing their minds when warranted. Automatic disbelief, reflexive contrarianism, source-counting, and rhetorical confidence do not earn mastery.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach 80%.

The browser record is a local progression aid, not authentication. Source quantity, polish, Eiren interaction, Beta participation, or tool usage cannot override the reviewed evidence set.

Corrective Learning

Repair the reasoning failure, then reassess.

Weak Synthesis

Rebuild evidence relationships, warrants, counterevidence, alternatives, and certainty language; revise the affected report section.

Weak Source Judgment

Repeat provenance tracing and lateral reading with emphasis on claim-level support, independence, source lineage, incentives, and limitations.

Weak Transfer

Practice on a different claim cluster, then reassess with fresh sources and no supplied conclusion.

Weak Reflection

Use actual search/draft history to identify a consequential decision, rejected hypothesis, or revision caused by evidence.

Eiren · Critical-Thinking Examiner

Eiren asks what could make the student wrong.

Eiren may probe assumptions, missing evidence, source independence, alternative explanations, causal claims, uncertainty, and what evidence would change a conclusion. Eiren cannot become an uncited source, fabricate evidence/citations, write the assessed report, supply the scored transfer conclusion, assign the verified score, or override mastery.

Open Eiren

Beta Program

Audit reasoning, provenance, neutrality, and access.

Report source labels that oversimplify credibility, exercises that reward predetermined conclusions, inaccessible evidence, weak provenance, false-balance pressure, missing uncertainty, broken tools, or bypassable mastery.

Open Beta Program
Public surfaces must not expose credentials, tokens, protected endpoints, repository secrets, internal topology, network mapping, or protected answer material.
