Canonical Mastery Contract · Independent Transfer

Unit 12 · Capstone Portfolio & Oral Defense

The final question is not whether students remember English 9. It is whether they can independently transfer its reasoning systems to unfamiliar evidence, defend claims about their own learning, recognize limits, and revise under challenge.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four independently reviewed components establish Unit 12 mastery.

25%

Portfolio & Growth Defense Record

Curated artifacts support precise claims about improvement, mastery, transfer, revision, and independence. Each major claim identifies evidence, limitations or counterexamples, and what the artifact does not prove.

25%

Capstone Synthesis

A substantial project integrates multiple Grade 9 modes and reasoning systems around a defensible purpose. Students document research/evidence choices, competing approaches, revision, source boundaries, and why the final form fits the intended audience and claim.

30%

Cumulative Independent Transfer

Using unfamiliar literary/informational material and fresh source evidence, students independently interpret, analyze, evaluate, synthesize, write, and make source judgments. Reassessment uses fresh or meaningfully changed evidence.

20%

Oral Defense & Metacognitive Recalibration

Students retrieve portfolio evidence under unfamiliar questioning, distinguish confidence from demonstrated competence, address counterevidence, revise overbroad claims, acknowledge uncertainty, explain consequential revisions, and identify evidence-based English 10 goals.

Cumulative Critical-Thinking Protocol

Claim → locate → test → countertest → recalibrate → transfer.

Students must be able to show where a claim comes from, what evidence supports it, what would weaken it, what remains uncertain, how later evidence changes it, and whether the underlying skill travels to a new context.

The portfolio is not a trophy case. High scores, polished artifacts, confidence, effort, or completion are not automatically evidence of mastery, transfer, or independence.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach 80%.

80% is required on every defined component and weighted Unit 12 result.

Eiren interaction, Beta participation, portfolio decoration, attendance, effort, tool use, or a completion click cannot override reviewed mastery evidence.

Corrective Learning

Reassess the unmet competency with fresh evidence.

Portfolio/Defense Below 80%

Narrow unsupported growth claims, locate stronger evidence, identify counterexamples, distinguish improvement from mastery/transfer, and defend again under new questioning.

Capstone Below 80%

Repair synthesis, evidence/source judgment, mode integration, audience fit, reasoning, or revision and resubmit the affected evidence.

Transfer Below 80%

Complete targeted instruction on the unmet competency and reassess using unfamiliar or meaningfully changed material rather than memorized answers.

Metacognition Below 80%

Rebuild claims about independence and growth using artifact evidence, limitations, revision history, and concrete next-step criteria.

Eiren · Rehearsal Examiner

Eiren increases uncertainty during preparation—then steps out.

Eiren may ask unpredictable questions, challenge inflated claims, demand artifact locations, introduce counterexamples, and ask what evidence would change an answer. Eiren cannot provide answers during independently scored portions, fabricate portfolio evidence, impersonate the student, certify mastery, assign verified course scores, or override the 80% gate.

Practice with Eiren

Beta Program

Audit validity, independence, accessibility, reassessment, and progression.

Report answer exposure, recycled transfer material, unclear permitted-support rules, inaccessible assessment formats, portfolio claims accepted without evidence, oral-defense trivia, weak reassessment, score calculations that bypass 80%, or progression without verified mastery.
