Unit 10 · Argument, Debate & Civic Reasoning

Students must demonstrate that they can reason under disagreement: define the issue, expose assumptions, verify evidence, steelman opposition, test tradeoffs, update positions, and distinguish unresolved uncertainty from rhetorical victory.

Authoritative Evidence Set

Four independently reviewed components establish mastery.

35%

Position Paper & Revision Trail

A 1,200–1,600-word argument with explicit scope and definitions, verified evidence, warrants, meaningful counterclaim/concession work, uncertainty calibrated to evidence, and substantive revision after engagement with opposition.

25%

Evidence / Framing Audit

Analyze how definitions and framing affect possible conclusions; trace provenance; test relevance, sufficiency, representativeness, independence, limitations, causal claims, base rates where relevant, and omitted evidence.

25%

Structured Debate & Listening Record

Accurately represent opposing claims before responding; ask information-producing questions; track claims/reasons/evidence/warrants; rebut what was actually argued; concede or qualify when warranted; and separate argument quality from charisma or fluency.

15%

Fresh Deliberative Transfer

Given a new balanced issue/evidence packet, map stakeholders and competing claims, identify missing information and assumptions, construct the strongest case on more than one side, evaluate tradeoffs, and produce a provisional conclusion plus explicit revision conditions.

Critical-Thinking Protocol

Frame → steelman → verify → stress-test → deliberate → update.

Students must be able to state an opposing position in a form its proponents could recognize before rebutting it. They then test both their own and opposing arguments against evidence, assumptions, consequences, opportunity costs, uncertainty, and alternatives.

A debate is not mastered because a student “wins.” A position is not mastered because it matches the teacher, school, mentor, majority, or source packet. Intellectual independence and evidence-responsive revision are scored.

80% Decision Rule

Every component and the weighted result must reach 80%.

Corrective Learning

Repair the reasoning failure rather than rehearse the same speech.

Position Paper Below 80%

Repair scope, definitions, evidence, warrant, counterclaim, uncertainty, organization, or post-engagement revision and resubmit the affected work.

Evidence Audit Below 80%

Repeat provenance/framing analysis with attention to independence, representativeness, causal inference, missing evidence, and source limitations.

Debate Below 80%

Practice listening accuracy, steelmanning, clarification, cross-examination, concession, and responsive rebuttal before a fresh structured exchange.

Transfer Below 80%

Practice on a different issue packet, then reassess with unfamiliar evidence and no required conclusion.

Eiren · Adversarial Reasoning Mentor

Eiren challenges the student's preferred position as seriously as the opposition.

Eiren may steelman competing arguments, probe assumptions, ask what evidence would change a conclusion, test causal reasoning, identify neglected stakeholders and tradeoffs, and challenge false dilemmas. Eiren cannot choose the student's civic position, fabricate evidence, impersonate stakeholders, write the assessed paper, decide the debate winner, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
