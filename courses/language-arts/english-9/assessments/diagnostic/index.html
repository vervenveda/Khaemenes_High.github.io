Reading Passage

This passage was written for this course and may be printed with the diagnostic.
At the edge of a small town stood a building that everyone called the old station, although no train had stopped there for forty years. The brick walls had darkened, the clock above the entrance had lost one hand, and vines had climbed over the platform sign. For years, people passed it without slowing. Then a group of students asked why an unused public building could not become a public reading room. They did not begin with money. They began with questions. Who owned the station? Was the roof safe? What would local families actually use? They interviewed neighbors, studied town records, measured rooms, and compared the cost of repair with the cost of demolition. Some residents opposed the plan. They argued that the town already had a library three miles away and that old buildings often consumed more money than expected. The students did not dismiss these concerns. They revised their proposal. The new plan used only two rooms, included a repair limit, and created a volunteer schedule. A retired electrician reviewed the wiring plan, and a local carpenter identified which repairs could be completed safely by adults and which required licensed workers. The station did not become a grand library. It became something smaller and more useful: a warm room with shelves, tables, reliable internet, and evening tutoring twice a week. Its success did not prove that every abandoned building should be saved. It demonstrated something more careful—that a community can make a sound decision when curiosity is followed by evidence, opposition is treated seriously, and a plan is revised before it is defended.

Writing Task

Write a 500–700-word analysis explaining how the students’ process turned an idea into a responsible proposal. Use at least three precise details from the passage and explain the reasoning connecting each detail to your claim.

Human review is required for the writing task. Use the Literary Analysis Rubric as a baseline measure. The diagnostic should produce strengths, priority skills, targeted refresh or challenge recommendations, and a learner growth goal.
