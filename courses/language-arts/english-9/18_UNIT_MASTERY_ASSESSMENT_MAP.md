# English 9 · Canonical Unit Mastery Assessment Map

Branch: `audit/language-arts-a-plus-plus-plus`

Purpose: define what actually counts as authoritative mastery evidence for each of the 12 English I units so the 80% gates are not based on a completion click or an undefined manually entered number.

## Course-wide decision rule

A unit is mastered only when its **defined authoritative evidence set** is reviewed and the resulting unit mastery score is **80% or higher**.

The evidence set may combine:

- teacher-reviewed writing/performance rubric evidence;
- fresh or meaningfully changed transfer tasks;
- objective checks whose answer keys have not been exposed as protected public client code;
- portfolio/process evidence where that evidence measures the intended competency.

Weekly games, Eiren conferences, Beta participation, attendance, tool usage, self-checks, public released forms, and weekly evidence-record buttons are **support/process evidence**. They do not independently satisfy a unit gate.

### Scoring rule

Unless a unit-specific plan below states otherwise:

- performance rubric(s): 60%
- unit transfer check / objective or short constructed response: 20%
- revision / reflection / process evidence: 20%

A teacher/parent educator may use a different documented weighting when the same competencies are preserved. No single category should erase a serious failure in a core competency. A unit score of 80% or higher is required.

### Reassessment rule

Below 80%:

1. identify the weak competency;
2. route to the matching corrective-learning task;
3. revise or complete targeted practice;
4. reassess only the weak competency where appropriate;
5. use fresh or meaningfully changed evidence when repeating an assessment;
6. preserve first/latest/best score and prior mastery.

## Unit 1 · Foundations of Scholarly Reading & Writing

**Weeks:** 1–3

**Authoritative evidence**
- evidence-based analytical paragraph scored with Literary Analysis rubric;
- revised diagnostic essay showing substantive revision;
- language/conventions and academic-integrity check;
- short transfer task distinguishing observation, inference, claim, evidence, warrant, and unsupported assertion.

**Suggested weighting**
- analytical paragraph: 35%
- revised diagnostic essay: 30%
- language/integrity check: 20%
- transfer task: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- evidence/inference → Week 2 + Evidence Studio;
- sentence control / integrity → Week 3 + PROSE revision;
- weak claim/warrant → claim/evidence mini-conference with Eiren after student attempt.

## Unit 2 · Myth, Epic & Comparative Literary Analysis

**Weeks:** 4–6

**Authoritative evidence**
- comparative literary analysis scored with Literary Analysis rubric;
- visual/archetype map used as supporting evidence, not a substitute for analysis;
- close-reading transfer passage requiring pattern, archetype, context, and evidence reasoning;
- revision reflection identifying one changed interpretive claim.

**Suggested weighting**
- comparative analysis: 55%
- transfer passage: 25%
- visual evidence map: 10%
- revision reflection: 10%

**Corrective routes**
- summary instead of analysis → Week 4/5 close-reading repair;
- weak comparison → comparison matrix + Eiren counterreading;
- unsupported archetype/context claims → Evidence Studio / ARSHIF verification.

## Unit 3 · Narrative Craft & Voice

**Weeks:** 7–9

**Authoritative evidence**
- polished short story scored with Narrative rubric;
- earlier draft + revision trail;
- craft commentary explaining point of view, structure, scene, detail, dialogue, pacing, and voice;
- fresh narrative-reading transfer task analyzing how a writer creates a specific effect.

**Suggested weighting**
- final narrative: 55%
- craft commentary: 20%
- transfer analysis: 15%
- revision trail/reflection: 10%

**Corrective routes**
- POV/structure → Weeks 7–8;
- scene/detail/dialogue → Week 8;
- revision/voice → Week 9 + PROSE;
- ideation only → Aurora Inspiration / Creative Mind Odyssey may support brainstorming but never count as assessed authorship.

## Unit 4 · Poetry, Sound & Figurative Language

**Weeks:** 10–12

**Authoritative evidence**
- four-poem portfolio with drafts and revisions;
- comparative poetry/translation analysis scored with Literary Analysis rubric;
- formal oral reading / accessible equivalent scored with Speaking & Listening rubric where applicable;
- language/form transfer check involving unfamiliar poetry.

**Suggested weighting**
- poetry portfolio: 35%
- comparative analysis: 30%
- oral reading / accessible equivalent: 15%
- unfamiliar-poem transfer check: 20%

**Corrective routes**
- imagery/diction → Week 10;
- sound/form → Week 11;
- translation/performance → Week 12;
- translator output must be verified and cannot serve as authority by itself.

## Unit 5 · Drama, Choice & Consequence

**Weeks:** 13–15

**Authoritative evidence**
- literary analysis of dramatic text scored with Literary Analysis rubric;
- Socratic seminar scored with Speaking & Listening rubric;
- staged interpretation with written staging rationale grounded in text;
- unfamiliar scene transfer task requiring stagecraft + interpretation.

**Suggested weighting**
- literary analysis: 40%
- seminar: 20%
- staged interpretation/rationale: 25%
- transfer task: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- page-to-stage reasoning → Week 13;
- competing duties/power/consequence → Week 14;
- seminar/staging defense → Week 15;
- AuraInk may support blocking sketches but cannot replace textual rationale.

## Unit 6 · Rhetoric, Reasoning & Public Voice

**Weeks:** 16–18

**Authoritative evidence**
- rhetorical-situation analysis;
- argument/reasoning map and repair task;
- four-to-six-minute evidence-based speech or approved accessible equivalent scored with Speaking & Listening rubric;
- rhetorical self-analysis and source record.

**Suggested weighting**
- rhetorical analysis: 20%
- reasoning/evidence task: 25%
- speech / accessible equivalent: 40%
- self-analysis/source record: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- audience/purpose/exigence → Week 16;
- evidence/warrant/fallacy/causation → Week 17;
- speech architecture/delivery → Week 18;
- Eiren may rehearse but cannot script or participate in independently scored delivery.

## Unit 7 · Research, Evidence & Media Literacy

**Weeks:** 19–21

**Authoritative evidence**
- research question + search strategy log;
- source audit and annotated bibliography;
- 900–1,200-word research report scored with Research rubric;
- source-verification transfer task requiring provenance, lateral reading, corroboration, and uncertainty.

**Suggested weighting**
- research report: 45%
- source audit / annotated bibliography: 30%
- transfer verification task: 15%
- research trail/reflection: 10%

**Corrective routes**
- question/search strategy → Week 19 / PLERA;
- provenance/lateral reading → Week 20 / Verifier;
- synthesis/citation → Week 21 / Evidence Studio + PROSE;
- Eiren cannot become an uncited research source.

## Unit 8 · Informational & Technical Communication

**Weeks:** 22–24

**Authoritative evidence**
- explanatory article;
- reproducible technical procedure with documented user test and retest;
- accessible infographic / visual explanation with source provenance and alt text;
- unfamiliar informational/technical transfer task.

**Suggested weighting**
- explanatory article: 30%
- tested procedure: 30%
- visual/infographic: 25%
- transfer task: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- information architecture → Week 22;
- procedural precision/usability → Week 23;
- data/visual/accessibility → Week 24;
- freehand visual tools may support non-data diagrams but cannot distort quantitative evidence.

## Unit 9 · The Novel & Sustained Inquiry

**Weeks:** 25–27

**Authoritative evidence**
- sustained-reading evidence log;
- 1,200–1,500-word literary analysis scored with Literary Analysis rubric;
- independent reading conference / oral defense;
- fresh passage transfer task involving character arc, motif/theme, counterevidence, and interpretation.

**Suggested weighting**
- literary analysis: 50%
- reading conference/defense: 20%
- transfer passage: 20%
- sustained-reading evidence: 10%

**Corrective routes**
- chronology/character tracking → Week 25;
- motif/theme/counterevidence → Week 26;
- critical lens/thesis/counterreading → Week 27;
- creative prompts may support exploration but the novel remains the primary evidence source.

## Unit 10 · Argument, Debate & Civic Reasoning

**Weeks:** 28–30

**Authoritative evidence**
- issue-framing / stakeholder map;
- evidence/provenance audit;
- structured debate scored with Speaking & Listening rubric;
- 1,200–1,600-word position paper scored with Argument rubric;
- substantive post-debate revision record.

**Suggested weighting**
- position paper: 45%
- debate: 25%
- evidence/provenance audit: 15%
- framing + revision record: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- framing/steelmanning → Week 28;
- evidence/rebuttal/source integrity → Week 29;
- listening/cross-examination/revision → Week 30;
- scoring must remain viewpoint-neutral; agreement with a position cannot determine mastery.

## Unit 11 · Creative Media & Publication

**Weeks:** 31–33

**Authoritative evidence**
- publication proposal with audience/purpose/genre rationale;
- substantive editorial and design record;
- curated publication / exhibit / literary magazine with rights, consent, attribution, accessibility, and preflight evidence;
- editorial statement / publication defense.

**Suggested weighting**
- final publication: 40%
- editorial/design process: 25%
- accessibility/rights/preflight record: 20%
- editorial statement/defense: 15%

**Corrective routes**
- audience/genre/mode → Week 31;
- editing/visual rhetoric/accessibility → Week 32;
- rights/version control/preflight → Week 33;
- PROSE, Bazaar Art, AuraInk, Aurora Inspiration, and Creative Mind Odyssey may support creation, but student authorship and attribution remain required.

## Unit 12 · Capstone Portfolio & Oral Defense

**Weeks:** 34–36

**Authoritative evidence**
- curated portfolio with exact evidence locations and revision history;
- capstone synthesis project;
- fresh unfamiliar-text cumulative transfer assessment;
- oral defense scored with appropriate Literary Analysis / Argument / Research / Speaking evidence as required;
- final reflection and English 10 transition plan.

**Suggested weighting**
- portfolio: 20%
- capstone synthesis: 30%
- fresh cumulative transfer assessment: 25%
- oral defense: 20%
- final reflection/transition plan: 5%

**Corrective routes**
- evidence-of-growth claims → Week 34;
- synthesis/integration → Week 35;
- unfamiliar transfer/oral defense → Week 36;
- public released diagnostic/midterm/final forms may support review but cannot replace fresh final mastery evidence.

# Weekly Check Classification

Every week may contain one or more of the following, but the label must be explicit:

- **Practice check:** immediate feedback; not authoritative for mastery.
- **Teacher-reviewed checkpoint:** scored evidence contributing to a unit plan.
- **Transfer check:** fresh short task used in the unit mastery decision.
- **Evidence record:** completion/process record only.

No weekly page should say “scored check” without identifying which of these classes it belongs to.

# Unit Gate Record

For each unit, the verified unit record should preserve:

- unit ID;
- evidence items reviewed;
- rubric(s) used;
- component scores/weights;
- first score;
- latest score;
- best score;
- attempt count;
- mastered-at timestamp;
- corrective route(s) used;
- educator/reviewer name or local record reference where appropriate;
- note that browser/localStorage records are local records, not tamper-resistant institutional verification.

# Mentor / Beta Boundary

- Eiren may coach, question, rehearse, challenge, and route corrective learning before or between attempts.
- Eiren may not author assessed work, fabricate evidence, assign verified scores, or participate in independently scored portions unless explicitly permitted as an accommodation/support.
- Beta reports system defects only and never substitute for coursework or mastery.

# Public-Surface Security Boundary

No unit mastery page may expose credentials, tokens, protected endpoints, private repositories, internal topology, administrative maps, protected answer keys, or hidden assessment material intended to remain secure.
