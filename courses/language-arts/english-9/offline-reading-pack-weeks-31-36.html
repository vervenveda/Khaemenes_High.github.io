WEEK 31 · UNIT 11

Audience, Genre & Multimodal Composition

Publication brief

A student editorial team must publish a small digital exhibit on the theme “What We Keep.” The collection includes one poem, one short nonfiction piece, one photograph, one audio reflection and a short editorial introduction.

Audience problem

A first-time visitor should understand the theme, navigate the collection, distinguish original from borrowed material and know how to access media in more than one mode.

Worked model

A poem may remain visually spare while the nonfiction piece needs headings and source notes. The photograph needs a caption and alt text. The audio reflection needs a transcript. Consistency should support the collection without flattening every genre into the same layout.

Independent task

Create an audience profile and two-page wireframe. Explain five choices involving sequence, hierarchy, image/text balance, navigation and accessibility.

WEEK 32 · UNIT 11

Editing, Layout & Visual Rhetoric

Draft problem

The exhibit’s home page gives the title, author names and large decorative images, but the theme statement is buried below the fold and two buttons use low-contrast text.

Editing distinction

Revision asks whether the collection communicates its purpose and sequence; editing improves sentence clarity and conventions; proofreading catches final errors. Visual editing also tests hierarchy, whitespace, contrast and reading order.

Worked revision

Move the editorial statement near the title, reduce decorative competition, label navigation by destination rather than “click here,” and verify that essential distinctions do not depend on color alone.

Independent task

Perform a three-pass review: substantive revision, sentence/editing pass and visual/accessibility pass. Preserve a before/after example from each pass.

WEEK 33 · UNIT 11

Publication, Rights & Editorial Record

Rights case

The team wants to include a classmate’s photograph, a public-domain text excerpt, a chart made from a course simulation and background music found online.

Publication principle

Every asset needs provenance. “Found online” is not permission. Public-domain status, license, creator permission, original authorship and course-created simulation labels must remain visible enough for future readers to understand what they are seeing.

Worked model

A release checklist records creator, title, source, permission/license status, required attribution, accessibility alternative and final location in the publication.

Independent task

Prepare a publication manifest, final editorial statement and release checklist. Identify one asset that should be removed or replaced if permission cannot be verified.

WEEK 34 · UNIT 12

Portfolio Curation & Evidence of Growth

Artifact set

A Grade 9 student has an early diagnostic paragraph, a revised literary analysis, an argument map, a technical explanation, a multimedia publication and a research-based reflection.

Curation principle

The strongest portfolio is not simply the highest-scoring work. It should demonstrate breadth, revision, transfer and increasingly independent judgment.

Worked model

Pair an early and later artifact to show a concrete change: stronger evidence integration, narrower claims, more precise attribution, better counterargument, improved structure or more effective audience adaptation.

Independent task

Select four to six artifacts. For each, write a standards-based evidence claim, identify what changed during revision, and name one area where growth is still incomplete.

WEEK 35 · UNIT 12

Synthesis Capstone & Revision Dossier

Capstone problem

The student must connect two course texts and one research or public-communication source around a focused question.

Synthesis principle

A capstone should create a new relationship among sources rather than summarize them one by one. Organize around agreement, tension, pattern, limitation or transfer.

Worked model

A literary text may reveal subjective experience, a research source may establish a documented pattern, and a public notice may show how institutions communicate. Their differences are part of the synthesis rather than a problem to erase.

Independent task

Create a synthesis matrix, draft a qualified thesis, include one fair counterreading, preserve a substantive before/after revision, and prepare a five-minute defense outline.

WEEK 36 · UNIT 12

Cumulative Final, Corrections & Oral Defense

Final boundary

The standalone final is the authoritative cumulative assessment under evaluator-agreed conditions. Practice material on this dossier must not become an answer key.

Correction model

A meaningful correction explains what reasoning failed, identifies the correct evidence or method, and states how the new understanding changes future work.

Defense structure

Prepare to explain one strong artifact, one major revision, one limitation, one course concept transferred to a new task, and one next-step goal.

Independent task

Complete retrieval practice, preserve corrections separately from original responses, and prepare a concise oral-defense record that points to specific portfolio evidence.
