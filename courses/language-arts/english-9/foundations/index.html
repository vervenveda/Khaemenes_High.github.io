Before Official Week 1

Build the reading and writing tools you need

This bridge is recommended when the English 9 readiness check shows a skill gap. It is supportive, not punitive: work through the six weeks, keep the evidence pages, then use the exit assessment to re-enter the same 36-week course.

Mastery rule: the exit assessment requires 80% overall and at least 80% in close reading/evidence, language conventions, and source/argument. Foundation work is not part of the official English 9 grade.

Use the bridge in order

  1. LearnRead the five short daily lesson cards.
  2. PracticeComplete the prompt in the browser or on the printed page.
  3. Keep evidenceSave one response from each week for review.
  4. CorrectExplain what changed when an answer is missed.
  5. ReassessTake the exit check only when your notes are ready.
  6. ReturnAfter mastery, Official Week 1 opens automatically.

Six-week foundation lessons

Week 1 · Notice, annotate, and cite

Essential question: What does a careful reader notice before making a claim?

Day 1Preview with purpose

Skim a course-supplied passage. Write one prediction and the question you want the passage to answer.

Day 2Mark significant details

Underline two precise details; label each detail as setting, action, image, or idea.

Day 3Quote accurately

Copy one short quotation and record its paragraph location. Do not change the writer’s words.

Day 4Explain relevance

Complete: “This detail matters because…” Use the detail, not a plot summary.

Day 5Evidence card

Make a claim/evidence/reasoning card from the week’s passage and save it.

Printable: draw three columns labelled Claim · Evidence · Reasoning and complete one card.

Week 2 · Infer and build a paragraph

Essential question: How does a reader move from a detail to a defensible inference?

Day 1Detail or inference?

Sort five statements into what the text says and what the reader infers.

Day 2Inference ladder

Write Detail → What it suggests → Why that suggestion is reasonable.

Day 3Topic sentence

Turn an inference into a focused sentence that could guide a paragraph.

Day 4Reasoning chain

Add a because-clause that connects evidence to the topic sentence.

Day 5Evidence paragraph

Draft 6–8 sentences; underline evidence and circle reasoning.

Printable: use a paragraph frame with Claim · Evidence · Because · Closing insight.

Week 3 · Control sentences and revise

Essential question: How does sentence control help a reader trust an idea?

Day 1Complete thoughts

Identify fragments and run-ons in five course-supplied examples.

Day 2Join ideas

Combine two related thoughts with a conjunction, semicolon, or dependent clause.

Day 3Precision

Replace vague verbs and pronouns with words that show exactly who did what.

Day 4Revision pass

Read one paragraph aloud; mark one place to clarify, cut, or connect.

Day 5Before/after evidence

Save the original sentence and the revised sentence with a one-sentence explanation.

Printable: copy one paragraph twice; revise the second copy and annotate three changes.

Week 4 · Judge sources and claims

Essential question: What makes a source useful for a particular claim?

Day 1Claim, evidence, opinion

Label statements as claims, evidence, or opinions and explain one choice.

Day 2Source purpose

Record author, audience, purpose, and publication context for one course-supplied source.

Day 3Corroborate

Compare two short sources; mark one agreement and one meaningful difference.

Day 4Bias without dismissal

Identify a perspective or limitation without assuming the source is useless.

Day 5Source judgment

Write a 5-sentence source note: useful for…, limited by…, best paired with…

Printable: complete a source-audit grid: Author · Purpose · Evidence · Limitation · Use.

Week 5 · Ask, search, and synthesize

Essential question: How can a researcher make a question small enough to answer well?

Day 1Question ladder

Turn a broad topic into a focused question with a place, time, group, or cause.

Day 2Search terms

Generate three neutral search phrases and predict what each might miss.

Day 3Note without copying

Paraphrase a short source section, then check that your sentence keeps the original meaning.

Day 4Combine sources

Write one sentence that uses two sources to answer the same focused question.

Day 5Mini synthesis

Draft a claim supported by two source notes and one limitation.

Printable: use a three-source note sheet with a separate box for exact quotations.

Week 6 · Speak, reflect, and transfer

Essential question: How do writers and speakers make their thinking clear to another person?

Day 1Organize an explanation

Arrange claim, context, evidence, reasoning, and closing in a logical order.

Day 2Read aloud for meaning

Mark pauses, emphasis, and one word that needs clearer pronunciation.

Day 3Feedback protocol

Give one specific strength and one usable next step using the evidence rubric.

Day 4Portfolio reflection

Choose your strongest bridge artifact and explain how it improved.

Day 5Exit preparation

Review your three strand cards, correct one old mistake, and take the exit check below.

Printable: complete a one-page reflection: I can now… / I still need… / My next strategy…

Foundations exit assessment

Answer all 12 items. Correct answers stay hidden until you submit. If the result is below mastery, add a brief correction note before the record is saved.

1. Which sentence is evidence rather than an inference?

2. A strong inference should be supported by…

3. Which quotation best supports a claim about persistence?

4. The reasoning in a paragraph should explain…

5. Which sentence is complete?

6. Which revision is clearest?

7. Which option correctly joins two related independent clauses?

8. Editing mainly checks…

9. Before trusting a source, a researcher should first identify its…

10. Corroboration means…

11. A focused research question is…

12. A responsible synthesis should include…

Not submitted

Your score will be recorded separately for this learner on this device.

What happens next

If the exit result reaches mastery, the readiness record is updated and Official Week 1 becomes available. If it does not, keep the correction note, revisit the weakest week, and try the same exit assessment again. Foundation work never changes the official course grade.
