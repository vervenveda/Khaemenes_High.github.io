English 9 Syllabus

National Language Arts · English I

One full high-school English credit designed for approximately 180 instructional days or 120–150 documented hours, adaptable to school, homeschool, co-op, tutoring, and independent-study settings.

Course Home

Course Purpose

English 9 develops disciplined reading, evidence-based writing, command of language, thoughtful discussion, public speaking, research judgment, media literacy, creativity, and independent learning. The course treats standards as minimum competencies and adds advanced rhetorical, research, publication, and portfolio work.

Required Evidence

  • 12 unit products and weekly records
  • At least six polished multi-draft compositions
  • Independent reading notebook and one full novel study
  • Research proposal, source audit, annotated bibliography, and report
  • Speech, seminar, debate, publication, and oral defense
  • Diagnostic, midterm, cumulative final, and standards-based portfolio

Recommended Weekly Rhythm

Five sessions of 50–70 minutes, plus independent reading. Flexible schedules may combine sessions while preserving the reading, writing, language, speaking, and assessment evidence.

Grading Framework

CategoryWeightEvidence
Major Writing and Projects35%Literary analysis, narrative, research, explanation, argument, publication, capstone
Reading and Analysis20%Annotations, notebooks, close readings, independent reading, conferences
Language and Vocabulary15%Grammar application, editing, vocabulary, style exercises
Speaking and Collaboration15%Seminar, speech, performance, debate, oral defense
Assessments and Portfolio15%Diagnostic growth, midterm, final, portfolio quality and reflection

Academic Integrity

Students distinguish their own language from borrowed language, preserve source information, cite quotations and paraphrases, verify facts, and disclose permitted assistance. AI or editing tools may support brainstorming and feedback only under teacher-defined rules; submitted work must remain the student’s own understood and defensible work.

Accessibility and Differentiation

Foundation supports include text chunking, audio, vocabulary previews, guided models, alternate response modes, additional time, and conferencing. Extended work increases complexity and independence rather than simply adding volume.

Core Materials

  • Reading notebook
  • Portfolio folder
  • PROSE or equivalent word processor
  • Dictionary and citation guide
  • Teacher-selected complete novel and drama
  • Access to ARSHIF and research tools

Passing Standard

A passing record requires completion of major assessments, a final portfolio, and evidence across reading, writing, language, speaking/listening, and research—not an average built only from worksheets.
