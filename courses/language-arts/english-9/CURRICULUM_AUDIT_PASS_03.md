# English 9 Curriculum Audit - Pass 03

## Scope

This pass treats `curriculum.json` and the twelve-unit sequence as the academic authority. The course target is global rather than jurisdiction-bound. Mentor, beta, parent-reporting, and backend integration work is paused until the instructional course is complete enough to generate trustworthy evidence.

## Executive finding

The 12-unit, 36-week architecture is intellectually coherent and more ambitious than a typical survey shell. It progresses from evidence and sentence control to literary study, composition, rhetoric, research, technical communication, sustained reading, civic argument, publication, and oral defense. The sequence is suitable as the foundation for a globally portable, A+++ English 9 course benchmarked above the shared academic expectations of major international frameworks.

Units 1 and 2 are now complete instructional references. Units 3-12 describe strong destinations but still lack the texts, explicit teaching, models, guided practice, independent practice, educator keys, dedicated assessments, and scoring instruments necessary to support their claims.

## Pass 03 completion update

Unit 2 now includes:

- a versioned unit map and source/edition guide;
- fifteen explicit sessions across Weeks 4-6;
- authentic-source requirements and a public-domain Odyssey passage;
- a clearly labeled course-original epic-technique practice text;
- translation, transmission, rights, and Indigenous cultural-protocol instruction;
- worked models, guided practice, independent writing, language study, and local workspaces;
- a dedicated ten-item objective and two-response educator-reviewed assessment;
- a 100-point performance scoring model, 80-percent mastery boundary, revision policy, and educator key;
- a 102-language translator with offline verified-pair mode and explicit-consent online mode;
- approved ARSHIF World Classics and Indigenous Storywork routes with evidence boundaries;
- direct coursebook routing from Weeks 4-6.

## Progression strengths

1. Unit 1 establishes evidence, paragraph control, revision, and integrity before major literary analysis.
2. Units 2-5 build genre knowledge through myth, fiction, poetry, and drama rather than isolated terminology.
3. Units 6-8 move from rhetoric to research to public and technical explanation.
4. Units 9-11 demand sustained reading, civic reasoning, editorial judgment, and real-audience publication.
5. Unit 12 requires students to curate and defend evidence rather than merely take a final test.
6. Language study is attached to authentic reading and writing purposes throughout the year.

## Priority defects

### 1. Instructional shell gap

Units 3-12 repeat generic learning priorities and submission language. These statements are sound, but they do not teach the course. Each needs the Unit 1-2 structure: accessible reading, explicit instruction, worked examples, guided practice, student workspaces, mastery checks, reflection, and an educator key.

### 2. Missing dedicated assessments

The course names substantial unit products, but the assessment directory contains only broad diagnostic, midterm, and final surfaces. Each unit needs a dedicated prompt, evidence checklist, analytic rubric, mastery boundary, revision/retake policy, and educator moderation note.

### 3. Rubric mismatch

Most unit pages route to the literary-analysis rubric regardless of product. Required mapping:

| Unit product | Correct rubric family |
|---|---|
| Comparative/literary analysis | Literary analysis |
| Short story and creative work | Narrative/creative |
| Poetry performance, speech, seminar, debate, defense | Speaking/presentation plus product rubric |
| Research report and source audit | Research |
| Position paper | Argument |
| Technical article/procedure | Informational/technical |
| Publication and capstone | Project-specific portfolio rubric |

### 4. Full-novel timing

Three weeks is too compressed for many proposed novels plus a 1,200-1,500-word analysis. The course should begin the independent novel runway no later than Week 22, establish weekly reading targets and conferences, and use Weeks 25-27 for synthesis and writing. A shorter novel or novella pathway should remain available.

### 5. Source and edition control

Ancient and translated works require named editions. “Public domain” must apply to the translation, not merely the ancient source. Unit 2 should route to stable free editions and record translator, publication status, access format, and culturally responsible context. Initial verified candidates include:

- *The Odyssey*, translated by Samuel Butler, Project Gutenberg eBook 1727;
- *The Epic of Gilgamish*, translated by R. Campbell Thompson (1928), Internet Archive;
- Lewis Spence's 1908 English rendering of the *Popol Vuh*, used with an explicit note that it is an older mediated translation and not a substitute for contemporary K'iche' scholarship.

### 6. Standards notation

The Common Core grade-band notation is broadly appropriate, and Florida B.E.S.T. expectations reinforce evidence, complex-text comprehension, inference, revision, conventions, research, and academic vocabulary. The final crosswalk must map each assessed task to exact benchmarks rather than broad ranges such as “all strands.” NCTE standards should remain complementary rather than being presented as a current state accountability framework.

## Unit construction standard

Every completed unit must contain:

1. a versioned `unit-map.json`;
2. essential question, enduring understandings, and measurable objectives;
3. named, legally usable core texts with edition/access notes;
4. three coherent instructional weeks and approximately fifteen sessions;
5. explicit reading, writing, language, speaking, vocabulary, and integrity instruction;
6. worked models and annotated examples;
7. guided and independent practice with saved or printable responses;
8. accessibility supports and extension pathways;
9. formative checks with explanations;
10. a dedicated performance task and analytic rubric;
11. an 80-percent mastery check with review/retry guidance;
12. an educator key that distinguishes acceptable variation from required evidence;
13. portfolio evidence and reflection requirements;
14. verified local routes and print behavior.
15. stable semantic translation keys for every learner-facing direction, label, glossary term, feedback message, and assessment instruction;
16. additive multilingual supports that preserve the English mastery target while allowing home-language access, mediation, and translanguaging;
17. bidirectional and right-to-left layout testing where applicable;
18. culturally flexible text pathways with equivalent complexity and assessment evidence.

## International A+++ benchmark

The completed course will crosswalk its taught and assessed outcomes against:

- Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500/0990: reading comprehension, analysis, evaluation, summary, directed writing, composition, and language accuracy;
- Cambridge IGCSE Literature in English 0475: informed interpretation, close analysis, personal response, and appreciation of literary form;
- IB MYP Language and Literature: listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing, presenting, inquiry, action, and reflection;
- Australian Curriculum Version 9, Year 9 English: language, literature, literacy, spoken/written/visual/multimodal interpretation and creation;
- England Key Stage 3 and early Key Stage 4 English: wide reading, comparison, critical understanding, vocabulary, grammar, spoken language, and accurate composition;
- U.S. Common Core grades 9-10 and Florida B.E.S.T.: evidence, complex texts, argument, information, narrative, research, conventions, speaking/listening, and vocabulary;
- Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume: reception, production, interaction, mediation, online interaction, plurilingual/pluricultural competence, and response to creative texts;
- UNESCO multilingual-education guidance: access in languages learners understand and additive respect for home, minority, migrant, refugee, Indigenous, and signed languages.

“Exceeds” will be demonstrated through an evidence crosswalk, not asserted as a slogan. Every benchmark must point to a taught lesson, student product, mastery check, and scoring criterion.
