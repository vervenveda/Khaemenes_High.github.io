# English 9 · Unit 11 Evidence Alignment
## Creative Media & Publication

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 11 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Define Audience → Curate → Design → Edit Collaboratively → Publish & Reflect**

Grade 9 turns composition into authentic publication by making audience, curation, visual rhetoric, accessibility, source credit, collaborative editing, and editorial judgment visible.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 31 · Audience, Genre & Multimodal Composition
1. **Define Audience:** Identify the real or simulated publication audience, purpose, genre expectations, and what action or experience the publication should create.
2. **Curate:** Choose texts, images, audio, or other media that fit the theme and audience for a reason beyond personal preference.
3. **Compose:** Draft or revise one piece so words, image, sound, layout, or sequence share the communication work deliberately.
4. **Test:** Ask a peer to identify the intended audience, message, and strongest/weakest design choice without explanation from the creator.
5. **Reflect:** Write an editorial rationale explaining audience, genre, multimodal choices, source credit, and one revision still needed.

### Week 32 · Editing, Layout & Visual Rhetoric
1. **Edit:** Use a focused editing pass for meaning, organization, sentence clarity, correctness, and consistency rather than trying to fix everything at once.
2. **Design:** Choose hierarchy, spacing, typography, image placement, captions, and sequence to guide attention without overwhelming the content.
3. **Audit Sources:** Verify attribution, permissions, captions, creator names, and provenance for all borrowed media or quotations.
4. **Test Accessibility:** Check readable text, contrast, alt text, captioning, navigation order, and whether meaning depends on color or sound alone.
5. **Reflect:** Document the strongest editorial change, strongest design change, accessibility improvement, and one unresolved publication risk.

### Week 33 · Publication Studio
1. **Curate:** Finalize the publication sequence so the pieces build a coherent experience rather than simply appear in submission order.
2. **Edit Collaboratively:** Respond to peer/editor feedback by accepting, modifying, or rejecting changes with a reason tied to audience and purpose.
3. **Quality Check:** Check links, credits, spelling, captions, media playback, accessibility, layout, and consistency before release.
4. **Publish:** Release or simulate release to the intended audience and gather one piece of audience-response evidence.
5. **Reflect:** Complete an editorial statement: publication purpose, strongest piece, strongest collaboration, audience response, ethical/source decision, and next improvement.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 11 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 31–33. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, publication, rhetoric, interpretation, accessibility, and long-form reading habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 11 methods;
- a cumulative publication, performance, long-form inquiry, seminar, or oral-defense audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, dramatic texts, long-form works, mentor texts, and approved multimedia sources. Exact source, edition, translator, creator, performance version, publication venue, media provenance, and citation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 11 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds publication planning, source credit, editing, accessibility review, dramatic/performance analysis, sustained-reading tracking, critical-lens evaluation, seminar leadership, oral defense, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 11 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. source, performance, publication, and long-form reading custody remain visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
