Performance Rubric

Argument Rubric

Four levels · Five criteria · Observable evidence

Criterion4 · Advanced3 · Proficient2 · Developing1 · Beginning
Claim & ScopeAdvances a precise, arguable, appropriately qualified claim; defines key terms and boundaries and avoids false binaries or overstatement.Advances a clear, defensible claim with workable scope and appropriate attention to the task.Claim is present but broad, absolute, inconsistent, or only partly responsive; important terms or boundaries remain unclear.Lacks a defensible claim, merely states a topic/preference, or makes an unsupported absolute assertion.
Evidence QualityUses relevant, sufficient, credible, representative evidence; preserves context/provenance and addresses important limitations or counterevidence.Uses relevant and generally credible evidence sufficient for the major claims, with responsible attribution.Uses some relevant evidence, but quality, sufficiency, representativeness, context, or attribution is uneven.Uses little/no usable evidence, relies primarily on assertion/anecdote, or misrepresents sources.
Reasoning & WarrantsMakes warrants explicit where needed, distinguishes correlation from causation, calibrates certainty, and develops a logical chain that survives serious challenge.Explains how evidence supports the claim and maintains generally sound reasoning with only minor gaps.Reasoning is partly developed but contains leaps, weak warrants, causal overreach, contradiction, or unexplained evidence.Reasoning is largely missing or seriously flawed; evidence is listed without explaining why it supports the conclusion.
Counterclaim & RebuttalRepresents a serious opposing view fairly, concedes what is valid, and responds with evidence/reasoning that may refine or qualify the original claim.Addresses a relevant counterclaim fairly and provides a defensible rebuttal or qualification.Mentions opposition but oversimplifies it, offers a weak response, or treats concession/rebuttal as a formula.Ignores meaningful opposition, misrepresents it, or dismisses it without reasoning.
Organization & StyleStructures the argument strategically; transitions signal logical relationships; tone/register fit audience and purpose; language is precise and ethically responsible.Uses a clear structure, appropriate transitions, and generally effective formal style suited to audience and purpose.Organization is uneven or repetitive; transitions/tone may obscure relationships or audience needs.Organization and style make the line of reasoning difficult to follow or rely on inflammatory/manipulative language in place of argument.
Scoring: 20 points possible. A score of 16/20 equals 80% when this rubric is defined authoritative evidence. Agreement with a student's position, confidence, ideology, or rhetorical flair cannot substitute for reasoning and evidence.
