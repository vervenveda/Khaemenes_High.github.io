Performance Rubric

Research Rubric

Four levels · Five criteria · Observable evidence

Criterion4 · Advanced3 · Proficient2 · Developing1 · Beginning
Question & ScopeDevelops a focused, significant, researchable question; defines terms, boundaries, and subquestions and revises scope as evidence changes the inquiry.Uses a focused, researchable question with workable boundaries and a search plan appropriate to the task.Question is partly researchable but too broad/narrow, vague, or weakly bounded; search direction needs substantial refinement.Uses only a topic or unanswerable/unsupported question and lacks a workable research direction.
Source QualityInvestigates provenance, expertise, evidence, context, incentives, date, and limitations; corroborates consequential claims through independent sources where appropriate.Selects appropriate credible sources and evaluates authorship, evidence, relevance, and context with generally sound judgment.Uses a mix of useful and weak sources or evaluates credibility superficially; corroboration/provenance is inconsistent.Relies on inappropriate, unverified, misrepresented, or untraceable sources, or treats search ranking as credibility.
SynthesisConnects sources by agreement, tension, qualification, cause, pattern, or gap to produce an understanding not found in any single source; represents uncertainty honestly.Integrates multiple sources around clear ideas and explains meaningful relationships among them.Uses multiple sources but often summarizes them one at a time, with limited comparison or integration.Stacks quotations/facts without synthesis, relies mainly on one source, or makes conclusions unsupported by the research record.
Citation & IntegrityMaintains traceable provenance from notes to final claims; paraphrases genuinely, quotes accurately/in context, cites borrowed ideas, and distinguishes source evidence from student inference.Uses accurate quotation/paraphrase and consistent citation; source boundaries are clear with only minor errors.Citation/source boundaries are uneven, paraphrases remain too close, or some claims cannot be readily traced.Important borrowed language/ideas are unattributed, citations are missing/unusable, or evidence is fabricated/misrepresented.
Communication & DesignOrganizes findings for the intended audience; calibrates certainty, defines needed terms, integrates visuals/captions/accessibility responsibly, and makes limitations visible.Communicates research clearly in an appropriate structure and style, with usable visuals/accessibility features where required.Communication is understandable but organization, certainty, terminology, visual use, or accessibility is inconsistent.Presentation obscures the findings, overstates evidence, or fails major audience/accessibility/design requirements.
Scoring: 20 points possible. A score of 16/20 equals 80% when this rubric is the defined authoritative evidence. Number of sources alone never determines quality; provenance, fit, synthesis, integrity, and communication matter.
