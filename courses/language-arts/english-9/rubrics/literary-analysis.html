Performance Rubric

Literary Analysis Rubric

Four levels · Five criteria · Observable evidence

Criterion4 · Advanced3 · Proficient2 · Developing1 · Beginning
Interpretive ClaimPresents a precise, arguable interpretation that addresses complexity, tension, or ambiguity and controls the analysis throughout.Presents a clear, defensible interpretation that answers the task and remains consistent through most of the response.Presents a general or partly defensible claim, but it may drift, summarize, or leave important parts of the task unresolved.Lacks a defensible interpretive claim, relies mainly on summary/opinion, or does not address the task.
Textual EvidenceSelects multiple precise, well-chosen details or quotations and uses them where they most strongly support the interpretation.Uses relevant and accurate textual evidence that supports the major claims with only minor gaps in precision or selection.Uses some relevant evidence, but evidence may be broad, repetitive, weakly selected, inaccurate, or insufficient for the claim.Uses little or no relevant textual evidence, misrepresents the text, or relies on unsupported assertion.
Reasoning & AnalysisExplains how specific language, structure, character, image, pattern, or craft choice produces meaning; addresses counterevidence or complication when relevant.Explains the connection between evidence and claim and analyzes important textual choices rather than merely naming them.Provides partial explanation but often repeats evidence, labels devices without effect, or makes leaps between evidence and conclusion.Provides little reasoning, substitutes summary for analysis, or makes claims that are not connected to the evidence.
OrganizationSequences ideas strategically so each paragraph advances the interpretation; transitions clarify relationships among claims, evidence, and complications.Uses a logical structure with focused paragraphs and transitions that make the line of reasoning easy to follow.Shows an emerging structure, but paragraphs may overlap, repeat, wander, or place evidence without a clear analytical purpose.Lacks a coherent analytical structure; ideas are difficult to follow or largely disconnected.
Language & ConventionsUses precise analytical vocabulary, controlled syntax, purposeful sentence variety, accurate attribution, and conventions that support nuance and readability.Uses clear academic language and generally correct conventions; errors do not interfere with meaning.Uses understandable language but with recurring vagueness, awkwardness, or convention errors that sometimes weaken clarity.Language and convention problems frequently obscure meaning, or source/quotation boundaries are not handled responsibly.
Scoring: 20 points possible. A score of 16/20 equals 80% and meets the course mastery threshold when this rubric is the defined authoritative evidence. A single polished feature cannot compensate for missing evidence or reasoning. Human review remains authoritative.
