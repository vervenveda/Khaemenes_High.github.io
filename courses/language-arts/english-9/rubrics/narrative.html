Performance Rubric

Narrative Rubric

Four levels · Five criteria · Observable evidence

Criterion4 · Advanced3 · Proficient2 · Developing1 · Beginning
Purpose & CoherenceEstablishes a purposeful narrative situation and maintains a coherent controlling effect, tension, question, or change from beginning to end.Establishes a clear narrative situation and sustains a coherent sequence with an understandable purpose or effect.Contains a recognizable situation but may wander, shift purpose, or include events/details that do not clearly contribute.Lacks a coherent narrative situation or purpose; events are fragmentary, confusing, or largely unrelated.
Character & DetailUses specific action, sensory detail, interiority, and/or dialogue to reveal character and make important moments vivid without unnecessary explanation.Uses relevant details and character actions/dialogue that help the reader understand people, setting, and important moments.Includes some concrete detail, but characterization may rely heavily on labels, exposition, generic description, or inconsistent dialogue.Provides minimal or confusing characterization/detail; important events are asserted without enough concrete development.
Structure & PacingControls scene, summary, chronology, flashback, transition, and pacing deliberately to emphasize consequential moments and shape reader expectation.Uses a logical sequence and generally effective pacing, slowing for important moments and moving efficiently through less important material.Sequence is mostly understandable, but pacing may rush key scenes, overextend minor moments, or use transitions inconsistently.Sequence or pacing makes the narrative difficult to follow; important events are missing, misplaced, or undeveloped.
Language & VoiceUses distinctive, controlled voice; precise diction; varied syntax; imagery and figurative language where purposeful; dialogue and tone fit character and situation.Uses clear, appropriate voice and diction with some purposeful sentence variety, imagery, or dialogue.Voice is inconsistent or generic; language may be repetitive, vague, overly explained, or occasionally mismatched to the scene.Language is frequently unclear or inappropriate to the narrative situation, limiting the reader's ability to enter the story.
Revision & ConventionsRevision evidence shows substantive rethinking of scene, structure, character, language, or ending; editing produces a polished text while preserving intentional voice.Revision improves meaningful aspects of the narrative and conventions are generally controlled; remaining errors do not distract from meaning.Revision is mostly surface-level or incomplete; recurring convention issues or formatting problems sometimes interrupt reading.Little meaningful revision is evident; convention problems frequently obscure meaning or the final text remains incomplete.
Scoring: 20 points possible. A score of 16/20 equals 80% and meets the course mastery threshold when this rubric is the defined authoritative evidence. Originality, polish, or dramatic intensity cannot substitute for coherent craft evidence and revision.
