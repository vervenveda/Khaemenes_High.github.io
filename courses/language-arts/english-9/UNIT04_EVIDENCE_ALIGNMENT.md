# English 9 · Unit 04 Evidence Alignment
## Poetry, Voice & Form

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 04 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Notice → Interpret → Compare → Compose → Defend**

Grade 9 keeps the evidence trail visible: learners name the poetic feature, describe its effect, compare alternatives, and then apply the same craft deliberately in their own writing.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 10 · Image, Diction & Figurative Language
1. **Notice:** Identify one image, diction choice, or figure of speech that changes the emotional or sensory experience of the poem. Describe the literal detail before interpreting it.
2. **Interpret:** Explain how one image or figurative comparison contributes to tone or meaning. Separate what the poem states from what you infer.
3. **Compare:** Compare two word or image choices that could have produced different effects. Why is the poet’s actual choice more precise, surprising, or powerful?
4. **Compose:** Write a brief image-rich passage or free-verse stanza that creates a specific mood without naming the mood directly. Annotate the craft choices.
5. **Defend:** Make one claim about how imagery, diction, or figurative language shapes meaning. Support it with two details and name one reasonable alternative reading.

### Week 11 · Sound, Rhythm & Form
1. **Notice:** Mark one sound, rhythm, rhyme, repetition, line break, or formal pattern. Read it aloud and describe what changes when the pattern is heard rather than only seen.
2. **Interpret:** Explain how one sound or structural pattern creates emphasis, expectation, pace, or contrast.
3. **Compare:** Compare two possible lineations, rhythms, or formal choices. Which better supports the poem’s purpose, and what evidence supports your judgment?
4. **Compose:** Draft a short formal or rhythmically controlled poem. Identify the rule you chose and where you intentionally followed or broke it.
5. **Defend:** Explain how one formal decision in your own poem affects a reader. Cite the exact line or pattern and describe the intended effect.

### Week 12 · Comparison, Translation & Performance
1. **Notice:** Compare two translations or two performances of the same or similar poem. List what remains stable and what changes.
2. **Interpret:** Choose one translation or performance decision and explain how it changes tone, emphasis, rhythm, image, or cultural meaning.
3. **Compare:** Evaluate two versions using explicit criteria rather than preference alone. Preserve uncertainty where the original language or performance context limits certainty.
4. **Perform:** Prepare an oral reading that uses pace, pause, emphasis, and tone as interpretive evidence. Annotate the choices before performing.
5. **Defend:** Present your poetry portfolio with a brief defense: strongest poem, strongest revision, most important craft choice, and one limitation or next step.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 04 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 10–12. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior Unit evidence, qualification, comparison, and method-selection habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 04 methods;
- a cumulative Unit 04 transfer, performance, rhetorical defense, or audit task on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, and approved authentic works. Course-created practice texts remain clearly identified as instructional examples. Exact source, edition, translator, speaker, historical-context, and provenance records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 04 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, transfer, comparison, counterreading, composition, ethical evaluation, defense, and exit-audit prompts only.

## Release criterion

Unit 04 is ready when:
1. all 15 instructional days receive the grade-specific evidence layer;
2. the grade-level scaffold fade is preserved;
3. authentic-source custody remains visible;
4. no duplicate generic prompts appear;
5. JavaScript parses cleanly;
6. storage/assessment authority remains unchanged;
7. the unit landing page communicates the learning progression.
