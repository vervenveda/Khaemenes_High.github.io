Unit 12 · Week 36

Cumulative Examination & Oral Defense

How can a student independently demonstrate, explain, and defend what has been learned?

Examination: Apply Grade 9 reading, analysis, evidence, language, research judgment, and writing skills to unfamiliar literary and informational material.
Defense: Use the completed portfolio to make evidence-based claims about growth, transfer, mastery, independence, revision, and next steps under authentic questioning.

Final Targets

By the end of English 9, the student can…

  • Read unfamiliar literary and informational texts closely without depending on prior classroom discussion of those texts.
  • Develop defensible interpretations and arguments supported by precise evidence and reasoning.
  • Analyze language, structure, rhetoric, perspective, context, evidence quality, and relationships among ideas as the task requires.
  • Distinguish textual evidence, source information, interpretation, inference, and unsupported assertion.
  • Write independently with purposeful organization, style, sentence control, conventions, and revision/editing judgment.
  • Evaluate unfamiliar source material for relevance, provenance, claim fit, limitations, and responsible use.
  • Transfer Grade 9 skills to a new task rather than reproducing a memorized answer from a previous unit.
  • Use portfolio artifacts to support precise claims about completion, improvement, mastery, transfer, and independence.
  • Respond to unfamiliar oral-defense questions by locating evidence, thinking aloud when appropriate, revising an overbroad claim, and acknowledging uncertainty honestly.
  • Explain a consequential revision decision and what it reveals about current reading/writing judgment.
  • Identify a remaining growth area and formulate a specific English 10 transition goal.
  • Use accurately: defense, evidence, growth, transfer, mastery, independence.
  • Earn at least 80% on every defined mastery assessment required for course completion, with targeted corrective learning and reassessment below 80%.

Five-Day Final Demonstration

Transfer → examine → prepare → defend → transition.

Day 1 · Cold-Read Transfer Laboratory

Can the Skill Travel?

Work with unfamiliar literary and informational passages under conditions that require independent application rather than recall of a previously taught interpretation.

  • Annotate for central ideas, textual patterns, language/structure, evidence, rhetoric, and uncertainty as appropriate.
  • Develop at least one interpretation that could be challenged and defend it with precise textual evidence.
  • Compare how different texts or modes develop related ideas when the prompt requires synthesis.
  • Identify what the text establishes directly and what the reader is inferring.
  • Use the experience to identify—not rehearse answers for—the skills most important on the cumulative examination.
Day 2 · Cumulative Examination

Independent Application

Complete the cumulative final through unfamiliar material and tasks designed to sample Grade 9 transfer across reading, evidence, language, writing, and source judgment.

  • Assessment conditions and permitted supports must be stated before the examination begins.
  • Students may not use Eiren, PROSE, generative assistance, answer keys, or unapproved collaboration during independently scored portions unless the assessment explicitly defines a permitted accommodation/support.
  • Preserve responses and scoring evidence.
  • Any defined examination component below 80% enters targeted corrective learning and reassessment rather than automatic course completion.
  • Reassessment should measure the same competency with fresh or meaningfully changed material rather than simply repeating memorized answers.
Day 3 · Portfolio Defense Preparation

Build an Evidence Map, Not a Script

Prepare claims and artifact locations while leaving room for genuine questioning.

  • Select claims about growth, transfer, mastery, independence, revision, research judgment, and/or communication as appropriate.
  • For each claim, identify artifact, exact evidence location, what it demonstrates, and a limitation or counterexample.
  • Prepare one consequential revision story: problem → evidence/feedback → decision → effect → what the student now understands.
  • Prepare one remaining-growth claim and a specific next-step goal.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately and complete the scored cumulative editing/defense-language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Oral Defense

The Portfolio Must Survive Unfamiliar Questions

Present a concise opening claim set, then respond to examiner questions that require evidence retrieval and reasoning rather than recitation.

  • Point to artifacts instead of answering only from memory.
  • Explain why the evidence supports the claim and what it does not prove.
  • Respond to at least one unfamiliar or follow-up question.
  • Revise an overbroad claim when the evidence does not support it.
  • Say “I don't know yet” when appropriate, then explain how the question could be investigated.
  • Preserve the defense rubric/record and examiner questions.
Day 5 · Reassessment, Final Reflection & English 10 Bridge

Finish With Evidence, Then Look Forward

Complete required targeted reassessment, finalize the portfolio, and write the course transition reflection.

  • Resolve any defined mastery assessment still below 80% through corrective learning and reassessment.
  • Finalize portfolio organization and preserve final course evidence.
  • Identify two competencies ready for increased challenge in English 10.
  • Identify one competency that still requires deliberate practice and name a concrete strategy.
  • Write a final reflection distinguishing confidence from evidence of competence.

Sovereign Oral-Defense Game

The Evidence Hot Seat

Students practice defending claims without memorizing a speech. The examiner changes the question; the evidence must still hold.

Round 1 · Point

Make one narrow claim about learning and point to the exact artifact evidence that supports it.

Round 2 · Pressure

The examiner introduces a limitation, counterexample, or unfamiliar follow-up: “Does this show mastery or only improvement?” “Where else did the skill transfer?” “What evidence would weaken your claim?”

Round 3 · Recalibrate

Keep, narrow, qualify, or revise the claim. Changing a claim because the evidence demands it counts as stronger reasoning—not failure.

Assessment Integrity & Support Boundary

Mentoring prepares the student; it does not impersonate independent mastery.

Before independently scored examination or oral-defense portions, students may use course lessons, portfolio evidence, approved study materials, teacher feedback, Eiren mentoring, PROSE, and other approved tools for preparation. During independently scored portions, only supports explicitly permitted by the assessment or required accommodations may be used. The record should distinguish preparation support from independent performance.

Reassessment is not punishment. A score below 80% identifies a competency requiring additional instruction. The student receives targeted corrective learning and then demonstrates the competency again using fresh or meaningfully changed evidence.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren becomes the rehearsal examiner—then steps out of the assessed performance.

Before the defense, bring portfolio claims, evidence locations, revision stories, likely weaknesses, and English 10 goals. Eiren can ask unpredictable practice questions, challenge inflated claims, request counterevidence, and help the student recognize where an answer depends on memory rather than an artifact.

Practice with Eiren
Try: “Ask me five oral-defense questions I have not rehearsed.” · “Challenge my claim that this artifact proves independence.” · “Make me locate evidence instead of giving a polished speech.” · “Ask what I would do differently now and why.” Eiren cannot provide answers during independently scored examination/defense portions, fabricate portfolio evidence, impersonate the student, certify mastery, assign verified course scores, or override the 80% gate.
