Unit 02 · Week 06

Comparative Myth Synthesis

How can two traditions illuminate one another without being flattened into sameness?

Reading: Paired myths with attention to textual pattern, historical/cultural context, and meaningful difference.
Writing: Build a nuanced comparative thesis, organize evidence by analytical criteria, and assemble the canonical Unit 2 mastery evidence.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Synthesize evidence from two mythic traditions around a meaningful analytical criterion rather than merely listing similarities and differences.
  • Write a comparative thesis that contains both connection and distinction.
  • Use context carefully to qualify interpretation without treating a culture as uniform or timeless.
  • Analyze transformation, archetype, motif, symbol, or allusion while preserving textual nuance.
  • Build a visual archetype map that communicates evidence and reasoning rather than decoration.
  • Demonstrate close-reading transfer on an unrehearsed passage.
  • Complete all four canonical Unit 2 evidence components at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Learning Cycle

Synthesis asks what becomes visible when texts are placed in relationship.

Day 1 · Re-enter the Evidence

Choose a Meaningful Criterion

Review Week 4 and Week 5 notes. Choose one analytical criterion that can reveal both connection and difference.

  • Collect at least three relevant details from each text.
  • Reject one comparison that is merely superficial.
  • Write a provisional “both / however / therefore” synthesis statement.
  • Identify one contextual question that could change the interpretation.
Day 2 · Synthesis Laboratory

Map Relationship, Not Sameness

Use an evidence matrix to test the provisional thesis. Pair evidence because details illuminate the same analytical question—not simply because they look alike.

  • Map claim → Text A evidence → Text B evidence → relationship → warrant.
  • Use Evidence Studio to preserve source/evidence relationships when helpful.
  • Mark where the texts converge, diverge, complicate, or transform the criterion.
  • Add one counterexample that prevents an overly universal claim.
Day 3 · Visual Logic & Revision

Make Comparison Visible

Construct the visual archetype map as an analytical model, then use it to test whether the essay's comparison preserves both pattern and difference.

  • Build a visual map showing pattern, evidence, difference, and context.
  • Label every connection so the visual communicates an argument.
  • Identify one unsupported or overly broad connection and repair it.
  • Preserve the change for the revision reflection.
Day 4 · Comparative Essay Studio

Draft by Idea, Not Text-by-Text

Draft the comparative literary analysis in PROSE. Organize body sections around analytical criteria, integrating both texts into the same line of reasoning.

  • Write a thesis with a defensible relationship and meaningful distinction.
  • Contextualize evidence from both texts.
  • Explain warrants instead of assuming similarity proves the claim.
  • Preserve planning and at least one substantive revision.
Day 5 · Unit 2 Mastery Assembly

Analyze Freshly, Defend, Demonstrate

Complete and review the four canonical Unit 2 components: comparative literary analysis (55%), fresh close-reading transfer (25%), visual archetype map (10%), and revision reflection (10%).

  • The transfer passage must be unrehearsed and scored independently of a memorized classroom interpretation.
  • Every required component must reach at least 80%.
  • The weighted Unit 2 result must also reach at least 80%.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the missed competency, complete targeted corrective learning, and reassess with revised or fresh evidence as appropriate.
