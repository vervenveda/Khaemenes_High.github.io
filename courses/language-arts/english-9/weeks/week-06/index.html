Unit 02 · Week 06

Comparative Myth Synthesis

How can two traditions illuminate one another without being flattened into sameness?

Reading: Paired myths with attention to textual pattern, historical/cultural context, and meaningful difference.
Writing: Build a nuanced comparative thesis, organize evidence by analytical criteria, and complete the Unit 2 synthesis.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Synthesize evidence from two mythic traditions around a meaningful analytical criterion rather than merely listing similarities and differences.
  • Write a comparative thesis that contains both connection and distinction.
  • Use context carefully to qualify interpretation without treating a culture as uniform or timeless.
  • Analyze transformation, archetype, motif, symbol, or allusion while preserving textual nuance.
  • Use parallel structure and comparison/contrast signals to make relationships between ideas clear.
  • Use accurately: synthesis, transformation, allusion, context, nuance, universality.
  • Complete the comparative literary analysis, visual archetype map, and defined Unit 2 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Learning Cycle

Synthesis asks what becomes visible when texts are placed in relationship.

Day 1 · Re-enter the Evidence

Choose a Meaningful Criterion

Review Week 4 and Week 5 notes. Instead of beginning with “both stories are similar,” choose one analytical criterion that can reveal both connection and difference: origins, duty, transformation, hospitality, power, mortality, knowledge, sacrifice, or another text-supported idea.

  • Collect at least three relevant details from each text.
  • Reject one comparison that is merely superficial.
  • Write a provisional “both / however / therefore” synthesis statement.
  • Identify one contextual question that could change the interpretation.
Day 2 · Synthesis Laboratory

Map Relationship, Not Sameness

Use an evidence matrix to test the provisional thesis. Each row should pair evidence because the details illuminate the same analytical question—not simply because they look alike.

  • Map claim → Text A evidence → Text B evidence → relationship → warrant.
  • Use Evidence & Citation Studio to preserve source/evidence relationships when helpful.
  • Mark where the texts converge, diverge, complicate, or transform the criterion.
  • Add one counterexample that prevents an overly universal claim.
Day 3 · Language & Visual Logic

Make Comparison Visible

Study parallel structure and comparison signals such as similarly, whereas, although, in contrast, while, yet, and however. Then construct the visual archetype map as an analytical model rather than decoration.

  • Repair ten faulty or unclear comparative sentences.
  • Revise five sentences from the essay plan for parallel structure.
  • Build a visual map showing pattern, evidence, difference, and context.
  • Label every connection so the visual communicates an argument.
  • Complete the scored language/vocabulary check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Comparative Essay Studio

Draft by Idea, Not Text-by-Text

Draft the comparative literary analysis in PROSE. Organize body sections around analytical criteria whenever appropriate, integrating both texts into the same line of reasoning.

  • Write a thesis with a defensible relationship and meaningful distinction.
  • Contextualize evidence from both texts.
  • Explain warrants instead of assuming similarity proves the claim.
  • Use transitions to show the exact relationship between ideas.
  • Preserve the planning state and at least one substantive revision.
Day 5 · Unit 2 Defense & Mastery

Qualify, Defend, Demonstrate

Present the visual map and defend the essay's synthesis. Then complete the defined Unit 2 assessment evidence. Unit mastery requires 80% or higher.

  • Identify the strongest cross-text relationship in the essay.
  • Explain one place where context required qualification.
  • Respond to a challenge or alternative interpretation.
  • Revise one overgeneralized or under-explained sentence.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the missed skill, complete corrective learning, and retry before Unit 3.
