Weekly Targets

Day 26Context & biasRL.9-10.6SL.9-10.1

Context Before Comparison

Today’s Question: How can my own expectations distort a cross-cultural comparison?

Learning Target

I can distinguish textual evidence, contextual information, interpretation, and assumption before I compare traditions.

I Do · Four-Column Discipline

Model one observation through four categories: Text says → Context establishes → I infer → I need to verify. An interpretation may be strong even when a contextual question remains open; it becomes weak when an assumption is presented as fact.

We Do · The Universality Test

Test the sentence “All cultures tell hero stories because every culture values individual courage.” Identify what could be supported, what is overgeneralized, and what evidence would be needed. Revise it into a narrower scholarly claim.

You Do · Context Record

Select two Unit 2 traditions. For each, record: genre/form, cultural or historical setting, transmission form, central conflict/question, one pattern, and one context fact you must not erase during comparison.

Prove It

Write 120–160 words explaining one assumption a careless reader might bring to your chosen pair and how you will control for it.

Optional Reinforcement

ELA-BIAS-PERSPECTIVE-002. Optional. Autoplay off. The written lesson is the required source while transcript/caption verification remains incomplete.

ELA-CULTURAL-SIMILARITIES-001. Use to notice connections, then apply the lesson’s caution against flattening difference.

Day 27Comparative criteriaRL.9-10.2W.9-10.9

Compare What Matters

Today’s Question: What makes a comparison analytical instead of merely observational?

Power

Who may act, command, refuse, or rule—and at what cost?

Duty

What obligations bind the character to family, guest, ruler, community, deity, or future?

Limits

What can humans control, and what remains beyond control?

Transformation

What changes, what resists change, and why does that matter?

Learning Target

I can choose a meaningful criterion and organize evidence from two texts around that shared question.

I Do · Surface vs. Analytical Comparison

Surface: both heroes travel. Analytical: both journeys test responsibility, but one text measures responsibility through changing kingship while another repeatedly tests identity, hospitality, and return. The second claim gives the comparison a criterion and preserves difference.

We Do · Evidence Matrix

Practice with Gilgamesh and Odysseus using four rows: cultural expectation, hero’s capacity, limit/failure, consequence. In each row, add a final column: So what does the difference reveal?

You Do

Create your own four-row matrix for the pair you plan to use in the Unit 2 essay. Each row must contain evidence from both texts and one sentence of comparative reasoning.

Language Lab · Comparison Signals

Use signals precisely: similarly, whereas, by contrast, unlike, both, although, while, in each text, however. Do not use “on the other hand” when the relationship is not actually contrastive.

Optional Reinforcement

Day 28SynthesisW.9-10.2L.9-10.1

Make the Texts Interact

Today’s Question: How does synthesis create an idea that neither summary produces alone?

Practice Text A · The Borrowed Flame

When the valley’s winter fires failed, Ilyra climbed to the ridge where lightning had split an ancient pine. She carried one ember home in a clay bowl. The villagers praised her courage and demanded that the fire remain in her house so it could never be lost again. Ilyra refused. She divided the ember among twelve hearths and taught each household how to bank coals beneath ash. “A gift kept in one room,” she said, “is only another kind of hunger.”

Practice Text B · The Sealed Well

After three dry seasons, Tarek found water beneath a stone court no one had opened for generations. The council ordered a wall built around the well and appointed Tarek its keeper. At first he measured every cup, fearing the water would vanish. When travelers arrived from the eastern road, the council told him to turn them away. Tarek opened the gate instead. By dawn the well had not emptied, but the council’s certainty had: they now had to decide whether survival meant guarding what remained or becoming a community worth surviving.

Learning Target

I can synthesize two texts by using each to sharpen, complicate, or revise an idea about the other.

I Do · Stacking vs. Synthesis

Stacking: Text A is about sharing. Text B is also about sharing. Synthesis: both texts connect survival to shared resources, yet Text A treats distribution as practical wisdom while Text B turns sharing into a political test of who belongs. The relationship produces a larger idea about community.

We Do · Comparative Thesis Formula

Although both texts ___, Text A ___ whereas Text B ___; this difference suggests ___. Use the frame only to expose the logic. Rewrite it naturally afterward.

You Do

Write two possible comparative theses about the practice texts. One must focus on responsibility; the other must focus on power or belonging. Choose the stronger thesis and explain why it is more arguable and specific.

Language Lab · Parallel Structure

Parallel ideas should use parallel grammar: “to protect resources, to define belonging, and to distribute responsibility,” not “to protect resources, defining belonging, and responsibility is distributed.” Revise three sentences from your matrix.

Optional Reinforcement

ELA-SYNTHESIS-001. Optional reinforcement. Written instruction and practice on this page remain required.

Synthesis Test

A strong sentence should answer: What do I understand after putting these texts together that I did not understand as clearly from either text alone?

Day 29Unit essay draftW.9-10.2W.9-10.5

Build the Unit Essay

Today’s Question: How do I organize a comparison around ideas rather than around one text at a time?

Learning Target

I can plan and draft a comparative literary analysis in which each body section develops one shared criterion using both texts.

Assessment Pair

Default: use The Epic of Gilgamesh and The Odyssey from the assigned course reading/edition. A teacher may approve another Unit 2 pair if both texts have sufficient evidence and context. Identify the edition or translation when quoting exact wording.

Body Architecture · Point-by-Point
  1. Introduction: context + comparative thesis.
  2. Criterion 1: Text A + Text B + synthesis.
  3. Criterion 2: Text A + Text B + synthesis.
  4. Criterion 3: complication, limit, or meaningful difference.
  5. Conclusion: what the comparison reveals without claiming false universality.
Required Draft

Write 700–900 words. Use precise evidence from both texts, at least three moments of explicit synthesis, and a source/translation note. Integrate quotations when your assigned copy is available; paraphrase accurately when exact wording is not necessary.

Revision Pass

Highlight each paragraph in three categories: evidence from A, evidence from B, comparative reasoning. Any paragraph containing only one category is incomplete. Then check parallel structure and comparison signals.

PROSE Rule

Plan first. Draft second. PROSE third.
Preserve your evidence matrix and initial thesis before moving the draft into PROSE. Save Draft 1 before revising.

Unit 02 Scholarly Demonstration

Today’s Question: Can I compare two traditions with precision, context, evidence, and intellectual fairness?

Learning Target

I can defend a comparative interpretation that recognizes a meaningful connection while preserving cultural and textual difference.

Submit · Part A

Comparative literary analysis, 700–900 words. Include a defensible thesis, evidence from both texts, point-by-point synthesis, at least one meaningful complication or difference, accurate attribution, and controlled language.

Submit · Part B

Visual archetype/pattern map. Include 4–6 patterns. For each: pattern name, Text A expression, Text B expression, similarity, difference, possible cultural meaning, and evidence. The map may show that a shared pattern performs different work.

Submit · Part C

Defense, approximately 2 minutes spoken or 180–250 words written. Answer: What comparison in your essay is strongest? What difference did you initially underestimate? What evidence made you revise or qualify your thinking?

Source / Translation Note

List the editions, translations, course packets, or Arshif context pages used. If no exact quotations are used from a source, say so. Contextual research should support rather than replace literary evidence.

Corrective Learning

8/10 = mastery. Below 8: identify the lowest rubric row, complete a targeted correction, revise that dimension, and resubmit. The standard does not change.
