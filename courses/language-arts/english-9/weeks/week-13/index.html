Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Read dramatic dialogue and stage directions as evidence while recognizing that performance adds interpretive choices not fully specified by the page.
  • Trace conflict through objectives, obstacles, tactics, entrances, exits, silence, proximity, and shifts in power.
  • Distinguish spoken text from subtext and support a subtext interpretation with language and situation.
  • Track differences among character knowledge, audience knowledge, and dramatic irony.
  • Write concise, playable stage directions using verbals and precise action rather than novelistic explanation.
  • Use accurately: scene, act, stage direction, soliloquy, aside, dramatic irony.
  • Complete the director's notebook, scene analysis, and defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Stagecraft Laboratory

Page → objective → space → interpretation → defense.

Day 1 · Page to Stage

What Is Written—and What Must Be Chosen?

Separate explicit textual information from staging choices a director or actor must supply. Mark dialogue, stage directions, entrances/exits, pauses, structural turns, conflict, objectives, and one question the script leaves open.

Day 2 · Blocking & Power Lab

Space Makes an Argument

Create two contrasting blocking plans for the same exchange. Test position, distance, movement, stillness, level, entrance, and exit. Every major movement must have a textual clue or interpretive warrant.

Day 3 · Subtext, Silence & Language Lab

Characters Do Not Always Say What They Mean

Annotate spoken text → objective → possible subtext → evidence. Rewrite vague stage directions as concise playable actions, use the six weekly terms accurately, and meet the 80% scored drama/language check.

Day 4 · Director's Notebook Studio

Build an Interpretation the Text Can Support

Develop the director concept, map objectives/obstacles/tactics/turns/blocking/audience knowledge, and connect at least three staging choices to evidence. AuraInk may be used to sketch stage space, movement paths, symbolic spatial relationships, or a visual concept before translating the sketch back into written staging rationale.

Day 5 · Table Read & Director's Defense

Test the Interpretation in Performance

Table-read or stage the passage, compare intended and observed effects, revise one weak staging warrant, and preserve annotations, stage maps, language evidence, director notebook, scene analysis, feedback, and reflection.

Sovereign Stagecraft Game

Same Words, Different Stage

Round 1 · Distance

Play the exchange far apart, then close together. Record changes in intimacy, threat, authority, or vulnerability.

Round 2 · Movement

Give one character movement while the other remains still, then reverse. Explain which arrangement best fits the script.

Round 3 · Silence

Place a defensible pause at two moments and compare how subtext or audience expectation changes.
