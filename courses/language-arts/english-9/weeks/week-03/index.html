Revision, Style & Academic Integrity

What changes make writing clearer, stronger, and more trustworthy?

Reading: Before-and-after drafts, paraphrases, summaries, quotations, and source notes.
Writing: Revise for focus, coherence, sentence control, attribution, citation, and intellectual honesty.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Distinguish revision from editing and explain why substantial revision improves meaning.
  • Paraphrase and summarize source material without distorting the source or disguising borrowed ideas.
  • Integrate quotations and attribution so a reader can tell which ideas belong to the writer and which come from a source.
  • Repair fragments, run-ons, and comma splices and use coordination deliberately.
  • Explain and apply the vocabulary: paraphrase, summary, plagiarism, attribution, coherence, syntax.
  • Complete the Unit 1 mastery task and conventions check at 80% or higher before Unit 2 progression.

Five-Day Learning Cycle

Revision is thinking again—not merely fixing commas.

Each day produces evidence that feeds the Unit 1 mastery task.

Day 1 · Revision Laboratory

Diagnose Before You Rewrite

Place a short first draft beside its revised version. Identify changes to claim, organization, evidence, explanation, word choice, and sentence structure. Label each change revision or editing, then explain which change most improves meaning.

  • Return to one paragraph from Week 1 or 2.
  • Write a one-sentence purpose statement for the paragraph.
  • Underline anything that does not serve that purpose.
  • Move, cut, combine, or expand at least three places.
  • Save the before/after pair and a revision rationale.
Day 2 · Integrity Laboratory

Quote, Paraphrase, Summarize

Work from one short source passage. Create three faithful representations: one brief quotation, one paraphrase, and one summary. Compare each against the original for accuracy, independence of wording, and attribution.

  • Highlight ideas that still belong to the source even when wording changes.
  • Use Evidence & Citation Studio to record the source, claim, and evidence relationship.
  • Identify one paraphrase that is too close to the original and repair it.
  • Add a signal phrase or attribution where the reader needs ownership clarified.
  • Save the source record and corrected examples.
Day 3 · Language Laboratory

Control the Sentence

Study fragments, run-ons, comma splices, and coordination as meaning problems. Diagnose ten sentences, repair each error, then revise five sentences from your own writing for clarity and rhythm.

  • Explain why each fragment is incomplete.
  • Repair run-ons in more than one valid way.
  • Use a coordinating conjunction only when the relationship between ideas is clear.
  • Read revisions aloud to test syntax and flow.
  • Score the conventions practice; reteach and retry if below 80%.
Day 4 · Editorial Studio

Build a Trustworthy Revision

Open a Week 1 or Week 2 composition in PROSE. Revise in passes: purpose and focus first, organization second, evidence and reasoning third, attribution fourth, sentence style fifth, conventions last.

  • Preserve the earlier draft before revising.
  • Make at least one substantive change in three different revision categories.
  • Check every quotation, paraphrase, and borrowed idea for clear attribution.
  • Write a short editorial note naming the strongest revision and why it matters.
  • Export or preserve the revised work for the portfolio.
Day 5 · Unit 1 Mastery

Demonstrate, Review, Retry

Complete the Unit 1 mastery task: an evidence-based analytical paragraph, revised diagnostic writing, and a conventions/integrity check. Mastery requires 80% or higher on defined scored evidence.

  • Complete the assessment without mentor-generated answers.
  • If the score is below 80%, identify the missed skill rather than merely repeating the same attempt.
  • Use the matching lesson, PROSE revision evidence, or Eiren conference for corrective learning.
  • Retry with new or corrected evidence.
  • Advance only after the mastery record shows 80% or higher.

Conference on a decision, not a blank page.

Before opening Eiren, identify one revision or integrity problem yourself. Bring the relevant sentence, paragraph, source note, or revision choice and ask for help examining your reasoning.

Open Eiren
