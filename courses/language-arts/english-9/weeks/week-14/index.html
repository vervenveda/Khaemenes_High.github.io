Course-created practice text

Evidence available to every learner

VOICE OF THE CITY: The gate must remain closed until sunrise.
NIA: My brother is outside it.
CAPTAIN: And three hundred families are inside it.
NIA: A rule that cannot see a person is blind.
CAPTAIN: A guard who sees only one person abandons the city.
[Neither moves. Beyond the gate, someone knocks three times.]
NIA: If I obey, I betray him.
CAPTAIN: If I open it, I may betray everyone.
VOICE OF THE CITY: Dawn is two hours away.

This original instructional text may be annotated, printed, performed and revised inside the course.

Day 66Competing Duties

Competing Duties

Identify a tragic conflict in which more than one obligation has moral force.

Learning target

Identify a tragic conflict in which more than one obligation has moral force.

Learn and model

Read the paired position statements. List each speaker’s claim, duty, feared consequence, assumption, and missing perspective. Avoid labeling one side “good” before testing its reasoning.

Independent evidence

Write a neutral 120-word summary of both positions.

Student response · handwriting or typing

Evidence check

  • Claim answers today’s target
  • Specific words or stage actions are cited
  • Reasoning explains how evidence supports the claim
  • A limitation or alternate reading is considered

Language focus: subordination and logical relationships.

Authority and Conscience

Day 68Syntax Shows Relationship

Syntax Shows Relationship

Use subordination to express cause, condition, concession, and consequence precisely.

Learning target

Use subordination to express cause, condition, concession, and consequence precisely.

Learn and model

Revise short choppy statements using because, although, unless, while, if, and therefore. Check that the conjunction states the relationship you actually mean.

Independent evidence

Revise eight sentences from the argument map and annotate the logical relationship in each.

Day 69Consequences Are Not Proof

Consequences Are Not Proof

Evaluate decisions without assuming that a bad outcome proves a decision was irrational.

Learning target

Evaluate decisions without assuming that a bad outcome proves a decision was irrational.

Learn and model

Trace decision → intended result → actual result → intervening forces. Identify what the character could reasonably know at the time.

Independent evidence

Draft a thesis and two analytical paragraphs evaluating one decision fairly.
