Publication Studio

What responsibilities accompany publishing work for others to read?

Reading: Student submissions, editorial notes, source/credit records, permissions or consent records where applicable, accessibility findings, and final proofs.
Publishing: Move work through editorial review, author revision, clearance, accessibility QA, proofreading, preflight, publication, presentation, and post-publication reflection.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Move a submission through a visible editorial workflow instead of treating publication as a single upload action.
  • Distinguish editorial approval, author approval, source verification, permission/consent status, accessibility readiness, and final proof approval.
  • Use version labels or another reliable version-control convention so the team knows which file is current and what changed.
  • Proofread systematically in passes for text, names, titles, credits, links/references, captions, formatting, consistency, and final display rather than relying on one casual reread.
  • Verify attribution and preserve unresolved rights/permission questions instead of representing them as cleared.
  • Respect privacy and consent boundaries for student names, images, recordings, personal information, and contributor material before public-facing publication.
  • Run an accessibility preflight that checks reading order, headings, text alternatives, captions/transcripts where needed, non-color-dependent meaning, readable presentation, and restrained motion.
  • Publish a masthead/editorial statement that accurately credits roles and explains purpose, curation, process, and responsibility.
  • Respond to launch/presentation feedback without silently rewriting the historical published record; document corrections or later versions when necessary.
  • Use accurately: proof, masthead, attribution, permission, version, publication.
  • Complete the literary magazine, digital exhibit, or themed publication; editorial statement; QA records; reflection; and defined Unit 11 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Publication Pipeline

Review → clear → proof → publish → account.

Day 1 · Submission Desk & Editorial Review

Nothing Enters the Final Issue by Accident

Inventory every proposed contribution and move each through a documented editorial decision.

  • Record title/identifier, contributor, genre/mode, version, status, and assigned editor.
  • Check fit against the Week 31 audience, scope, curation principle, and editorial standards.
  • Return substantive changes to the author for consultation/approval rather than silently rewriting the piece.
  • Record accept, revise/resubmit, hold, or decline decisions with instructional reasons rather than popularity.
  • Preserve the submission and editorial trail.
Day 2 · Clearance & Accessibility Desk

Publication Permission Is a Separate Question From Editorial Quality

Clear source, attribution, permission/consent, privacy, and accessibility issues before final proof.

  • Verify names, quotations, factual claims, captions, credits, and source references appropriate to the publication.
  • Confirm rights/permission basis for third-party material; unresolved status remains unresolved.
  • Check consent/privacy needs for identifiable student images, recordings, personal information, or contributor material where applicable.
  • Run accessibility checks for headings/reading order, text alternatives, captions/transcripts, color-independent meaning, readable presentation, and motion.
  • Do not publish material that fails a required clearance boundary.
Day 3 · Proofreading & Version Control Lab

The Final Proof Is a System, Not a Feeling

Freeze a candidate proof and inspect it in separate passes so one type of error does not hide another.

  • Pass 1: body text, spelling, punctuation, grammar, and accidental omissions/duplication.
  • Pass 2: names, titles, quotations, dates, credits, captions, links/references, and masthead.
  • Pass 3: house style, headings, spacing, typography, sequence, page/screen consistency, and final display.
  • Use explicit version labels so no one edits an obsolete file.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately and complete the scored proofreading/publication-language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Preflight & Publication

Stop the Press Before the Reader Has To

Run a final preflight, resolve blocking defects, and publish only the approved version.

  • Confirm correct version and complete contents.
  • Confirm no placeholder text, missing assets, broken internal navigation, or accidental private/internal notes are visible.
  • Confirm credits, attribution, permissions/consent status, accessibility essentials, and author approvals.
  • Confirm the masthead accurately represents editorial/contributor roles.
  • Publish or present through the approved public-facing channel without exposing credentials, protected endpoints, repository secrets, infrastructure topology, or network mapping.
Day 5 · Launch, Editorial Statement & After-Action Review

Publication Creates Responsibility After Release Too

Present the publication, collect audience response, and write the editorial statement and reflection.

  • Explain audience, purpose, curation principle, major design/editorial choices, accessibility approach, and contributor responsibilities.
  • Distinguish preference feedback from genuine errors, accessibility barriers, attribution problems, or factual corrections.
  • Document post-publication corrections transparently when needed rather than silently pretending the first version never existed.
  • Identify one editorial decision the team would change in a future issue and why.
  • Preserve final publication, launch evidence, editorial statement, correction log if needed, and reflection.

Sovereign Publication Game

The Preflight Tower

A publication climbs only when every floor passes inspection. One unresolved blocking defect sends it back to the correct desk.

Floor 1 · Content

Correct version? Complete pieces? Author approvals? No placeholder copy or accidental editorial notes? If not, return to editorial review.

Floor 2 · Trust

Sources, names, quotations, credits, attribution, permission/consent, privacy, and accessibility status accounted for? If not, return to clearance.

Floor 3 · Reader

Reading order, hierarchy, navigation, captions, alternatives, formatting, and final display work as intended? Only then may the publication leave the tower.

Open Eiren
