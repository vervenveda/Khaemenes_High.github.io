Publication studio · Rights, attribution and durable release

Submission packet. A student magazine article includes an original essay, a photograph taken by a classmate, a chart adapted from a course simulation, and a quotation from a public speech. The draft currently labels only the essay author.

Publication problem. A polished page can still be academically weak if readers cannot distinguish original work, borrowed material, simulated evidence, and externally sourced material. Publication therefore requires both editorial quality and provenance.

Worked model. The article should credit the classmate’s photograph with permission status, label the chart as adapted from a course simulation, identify the quoted speaker and source locator, and preserve the student author’s own byline. If permission cannot be verified, replace the asset rather than hiding the uncertainty.

Resilient-format check. A publication should remain understandable when printed, viewed on a narrow screen, opened without decorative media, or archived later. Essential meaning cannot depend on hover effects, color alone, or an unlabeled external link.

Editorial review. Separate four passes: factual/source verification, structure and audience, sentence/style editing, and final proof. A last-minute spelling pass is not a substitute for editorial review.

Transfer challenge. Prepare a release checklist showing authorship, permissions, source labels, accessibility, format testing, and one unresolved issue that must be fixed before publication.

The packet is a fictional course-created publication scenario.

Day 1

Audience boundary

Direct instruction

Publication can be evaluator-only or family-only. Public release is optional and requires adult review and permission.

Apply and preserve

Choose the audience and remove unnecessary identifying details.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 2

Rights and attribution

Direct instruction

Credit identifies a source; permission or a license determines whether reuse is allowed. Do not assume found images are free.

Apply and preserve

Build an asset log with creator, source, reuse basis and required credit; replace uncertain assets.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 3

Editorial review

Direct instruction

Feedback checks accuracy, clarity, accessibility and audience fit. The writer should record reasoned decisions.

Apply and preserve

Obtain feedback on three questions. Explain why you accept, modify or decline each suggestion.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 4

Resilient formats

Direct instruction

A readable text or print version preserves meaning when an interactive format is unavailable. Export does not mean public posting.

Apply and preserve

Prepare an editable draft and print/text edition. Check headings, citations and diagrams.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 5

Close the record

Direct instruction

A publication package preserves work, credits, revision history and an explicit release decision. Local submission is sufficient.

Apply and preserve

Submit the package and a 250-word editorial reflection; keep public distribution adult-approved.

Week 33 tools, games and return path

Evidence-Based English Architecture · Unit 11

Publication Studio · Retrieval & Transfer

Grade 9 turns composition into authentic publication by making audience, curation, visual rhetoric, accessibility, source credit, collaborative editing, and editorial judgment visible.

Day 1 · Curate

Finalize the publication sequence so the pieces build a coherent experience rather than simply appear in submission order.

Day 2 · Edit Collaboratively

Respond to peer/editor feedback by accepting, modifying, or rejecting changes with a reason tied to audience and purpose.

Day 3 · Quality Check

Check links, credits, spelling, captions, media playback, accessibility, layout, and consistency before release.

Day 4 · Publish

Release or simulate release to the intended audience and gather one piece of audience-response evidence.

Day 5 · Reflect

Complete an editorial statement: publication purpose, strongest piece, strongest collaboration, audience response, ethical/source decision, and next improvement.

Mastery boundary: These prompts strengthen publication, drama analysis, performance, sustained reading, critical-lens use, seminar leadership, accessibility, and reflection only. They do not change assessment answers, thresholds, learner-record authority, or completion rules.
