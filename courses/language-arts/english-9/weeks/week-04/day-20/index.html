Cultural-Function Analysis

Today’s Question: Can I explain what work a myth performs using pattern, symbol, and evidence?

Learning Target

I can independently read an unfamiliar course-created myth, identify a defensible cultural function, and support my interpretation with precise evidence and reasoning.

Transfer Text · The River’s Promise

Original course myth created for assessment practice; it does not represent a real cultural tradition.

In the first years of the valley, the river changed its path whenever people argued about who owned its water. One season it ran beside the eastern fields. The next it bent toward the western orchards. Each village built walls to keep the water close, and each wall pushed the river farther away.

A young mason named Aro discovered a stone beneath the riverbed marked with a circle split into four open gates. His grandmother told him the mark was older than any village boundary. “The river remembers the whole valley,” she said. “We remember only the bank where we stand.”

Aro carried the stone to the four villages. Each council demanded that he place it on their side of the river as proof that the water belonged there. Instead, he set the stone in the dry center of the valley and broke every wall he had helped build.

That night the river flooded. It crossed fields, filled old channels, and ruined three storehouses. By morning, however, water stood in all four village wells for the first time in years.

The councils ordered Aro to rebuild the walls. He refused. “A wall can hold water for a season,” he said, “but it can also teach us to forget where the river must go.” The villages argued until winter. When spring came, they built gates instead of walls.

Independent Process
  1. Annotate repetition, contrast, choice, consequence, and symbol.
  2. Write one interpretive question.
  3. Choose one primary cultural function: explain, preserve, model, warn, connect, or question.
  4. Form a defensible claim.
  5. Select at least two precise pieces of evidence.
  6. Explain how one pattern and one symbol contribute to meaning.
  7. Write 300–450 words.
  8. Complete the pattern map.
Pattern Map

Create four labeled boxes: Recurring Situation · Repeated Motif · Central Symbol · Cultural Function. Each box needs evidence and a one-sentence explanation.
