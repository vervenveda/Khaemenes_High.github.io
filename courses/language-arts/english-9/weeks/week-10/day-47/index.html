Day 47SpeakerVoice

Poet, Speaker & Persona

Today’s Question: Who is speaking inside a poem, and what evidence tells me who that voice is?

Learning Target

I can distinguish the poet from the poem’s speaker and analyze the speaker’s perspective, attitude, and limits using textual evidence.

Original Course Poem · Borrowed Coat

I still wear your coat in weather that does not quite deserve it. The sleeves cover half my hands. One pocket holds a train receipt from a city I have never seen. When people say it suits me, I say thank you. I do not tell them I am practicing the weight of leaving.

I Do · Do Not Collapse Author Into Speaker

The poet is the real-world writer. The speaker is the voice constructed inside the poem. A speaker may resemble the poet, but readers should not assume biography from first-person pronouns alone. Analyze what the voice says, notices, withholds, exaggerates, or cannot know.

We Do · Voice Evidence

What can we infer about this speaker from the phrases does not quite deserve it, I say thank you, and practicing the weight of leaving? Which inference is strongest? Which remains uncertain?

Language Lab · Tone Is Not Mood

Tone is the speaker’s or writer’s attitude as created through language. Mood describes an emotional atmosphere or reader experience. Avoid one-word labeling without evidence. Instead write: The speaker sounds restrained because…

You Do

Write 180–240 words analyzing the speaker’s attitude toward the coat and toward leaving. Use at least two precise word-level observations. End by naming one thing the poem does not allow you to know for certain about the poet.

Think About It

Why might a poet invent a speaker who sees the world differently from the poet?
