Day 49CompositionW.9-10.3d

Build Two Poems From Deliberate Choices

Today’s Question: How can changing speaker, image, or tone change the poem itself?

Learning Target

I can draft two original poems from deliberate constraints, then revise at least one meaningful craft choice in each.

Launch · Begin With Pressure, Not Decoration

Choose something concrete: an object, place, action, sound, texture, or recurring image. Then give it pressure: memory, conflict, uncertainty, desire, change, distance, responsibility, belonging, loss, wonder, or another tension. Do not begin by deciding, “I need a metaphor.” Begin by deciding what experience deserves attention.

Poem 1 · Image-Led Free Verse

Write 12–20 lines. Include at least three concrete images, precise verbs, and one figurative move that changes understanding. Do not name the central emotion in the first draft. Let image and action carry it.

Poem 2 · Change the Lens

Write 8–16 lines about the same subject or image field, but change at least one controlling condition: speaker, distance, tone, time, addressee, or structure. The purpose is not to make a “better” poem; it is to discover what changes when the lens changes.

Revision Pass
  1. Circle the most generic noun or verb; replace it if precision improves the poem.
  2. Underline one image doing real work; strengthen or reposition it.
  3. Box one figurative phrase. Keep it only if it changes understanding.
  4. Read aloud. Mark one place where rhythm or line break changes emphasis.
  5. Preserve Draft 1 before the meaningful revision.
Studio Rule

Choose any route below. Tools are equal options, not levels. You may also use plain paper or a standard text editor. Writer’s Grove may generate constraints only. It may not generate your poem. PROSE may preserve and revise a draft only after you have made the intellectual and creative decisions.

Use handwriting if physical spacing, line breaks, arrows, marginal notes, or revision marks help you think. Preserve the intellectual work in your portfolio afterward.
