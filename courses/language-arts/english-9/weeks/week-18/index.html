Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Design a speech around a clear rhetorical situation, defensible claim, logical architecture, and audience needs.
  • Select, verify, explain, and orally attribute evidence rather than dropping facts into a script without context.
  • Use parallelism, repetition, anaphora, antithesis, cadence, and strategic sentence length when they serve meaning.
  • Write transitions and signposts for listeners who cannot reread the previous paragraph.
  • Rehearse pace, pause, emphasis, volume, articulation, gesture, posture, and eye contact purposefully.
  • Distinguish memorization, reading, and prepared speaking choices without treating one delivery mode as universally superior.
  • Evaluate delivery for clarity and rhetorical effect rather than personality, accent, disability, or conformity to one speaking style.
  • Use accurately: anaphora, antithesis, cadence, delivery, emphasis, gesture.
  • Complete the four-to-six-minute speech, rhetorical reflection, and defined Unit 6 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Public Voice Laboratory

Architect → compose → hear → rehearse → deliver.

Day 1 · Speech Architecture

Design for a Listener's Memory

Return to the rhetorical situation from Week 16 and reasoning work from Week 17. Build a speech architecture before polishing sentences.

  • State audience, purpose, exigence, central claim, and ethical constraints.
  • Design an opening that earns attention without misleading the audience.
  • Sequence reasons/evidence so each section prepares the next.
  • Plan listener signposts, transitions, and a conclusion that does more than repeat the introduction.
  • Estimate timing before drafting to keep the final speech within four to six minutes.
Day 2 · Evidence for the Ear

Listeners Need Context Before the Number

Draft the evidence-bearing sections. Spoken evidence should be understandable, attributable, and connected to the claim in real time.

  • Verify factual claims and preserve source records.
  • Introduce source identity/context when relevant before or near the evidence.
  • Translate dense statistics into comprehensible comparisons without distorting them.
  • Explain the warrant connecting evidence to the speech's claim.
  • Draft in PROSE and flag any sentence that is difficult to understand aloud.
Day 3 · Sound & Language Laboratory

Write With the Mouth and Ear

Study parallelism, repetition, anaphora, antithesis, cadence, emphasis, and sentence-length variation. Use them selectively rather than decorating every paragraph.

  • Read the entire draft aloud and mark breath, stumble, ambiguity, and dead spots.
  • Revise one passage using parallel structure.
  • Test purposeful repetition against unnecessary repetition.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored rhetoric/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Rehearsal Studio

Delivery Is Part of Meaning

Rehearse multiple delivery choices and use evidence from timing and listeners to revise both script and performance.

  • Time a complete run; revise rather than simply speaking unnaturally fast to fit the limit.
  • Test two versions of one key passage using different pace, pause, or emphasis.
  • Use a listener to identify where meaning became unclear or attention dropped.
  • Practice gesture and movement only where they support communication.
  • Use Eiren for questioning/rehearsal feedback, never for scripting the assessed speech.
Day 5 · Public Voice Colloquium

Deliver, Analyze, Defend

Deliver the four-to-six-minute speech and complete the rhetorical self-analysis. The assessment evaluates evidence, reasoning, rhetorical design, clarity, and purposeful delivery.

  • Deliver through an accessible approved mode appropriate to the course setting.
  • Identify one rhetorical choice that worked as intended and support that judgment with evidence from the performance.
  • Identify one place where delivery or structure changed the audience's access to the argument.
  • Explain one substantive revision from earlier draft to final speech.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the weak criterion, complete corrective learning, and reassess before Unit 7.

Sovereign Delivery Game

The Voice Console

No external application is required. Students hold the words constant and adjust one delivery variable at a time to discover what the voice contributes.

Control 1 · Pace & Pause

Deliver the same passage once quickly and once with deliberate pauses. Identify where comprehension or emphasis changes, then design a third version intentionally.

Control 2 · Emphasis

Shift stress among key words without changing the sentence. Listeners explain how the implied meaning changes and which version best serves the rhetorical purpose.

Control 3 · Gesture & Stillness

Perform once with unnecessary movement and once with purposeful stillness/limited gesture. Decide what supports meaning rather than rewarding theatricality for its own sake.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren is the rehearsal room, not the ghostwriter.

Bring the outline, source record, draft, timed rehearsal notes, or rhetorical self-analysis first. Eiren can act as a questioning audience, flag unclear reasoning, and ask what delivery choice best serves the intended meaning.

Open Eiren
Try: “Stop me when a listener would need more context.” · “Ask what evidence supports my strongest claim.” · “Challenge a transition that does not actually show the reasoning.” · “Ask why I am using repetition here.” Eiren cannot write or deliver the assessed speech, fabricate sources, script answers, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
