Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Design a speech around a clear rhetorical situation, defensible claim, logical architecture, and audience needs.
  • Select, verify, explain, and orally attribute evidence rather than dropping facts into a script without context.
  • Use parallelism, repetition, anaphora, antithesis, cadence, and strategic sentence length when they serve meaning.
  • Rehearse pace, pause, emphasis, volume, articulation, gesture, posture, or accessible equivalents purposefully.
  • Evaluate delivery for clarity and rhetorical effect rather than personality, accent, disability, or conformity to one speaking style.
  • Complete the rhetorical self-analysis and preserve source/provenance evidence.
  • Complete all four canonical Unit 6 evidence components at 80% or higher.

Canonical Unit 6 Evidence

  • 20% rhetorical-situation analysis
  • 25% reasoning map & repair
  • 40% evidence-based speech

Five-Day Public Voice Laboratory

Architect → compose → hear → rehearse → demonstrate.

Day 1 · Speech Architecture

Design for a Listener's Memory

Return to the rhetorical situation from Week 16 and reasoning work from Week 17. Build a speech architecture before polishing sentences.

  • State audience, purpose, exigence, central claim, and ethical constraints.
  • Sequence reasons and evidence so each section prepares the next.
  • Plan listener signposts, transitions, and a conclusion that does more than repeat the introduction.
  • Estimate timing before drafting.
Day 2 · Evidence for the Ear

Listeners Need Context Before the Number

Draft the evidence-bearing sections. Spoken evidence should be understandable, attributable, verified, and connected to the claim in real time.

  • Preserve source/provenance records.
  • Introduce source identity/context when relevant.
  • Translate dense statistics without distortion.
  • Explain the warrant connecting evidence to the claim.
Day 3 · Sound & Language Laboratory

Write With the Mouth and Ear

Study parallelism, repetition, anaphora, antithesis, cadence, emphasis, and sentence-length variation. Use them selectively rather than decoratively.

  • Read the draft aloud or use an accessible equivalent.
  • Mark breath/load, stumble, ambiguity, and dead spots.
  • Revise one passage using purposeful parallel structure or repetition.
  • Treat the rhetoric/language check as a corrective checkpoint, not the Unit 6 mastery score.
Day 4 · Rehearsal Studio

Delivery Is Part of Meaning

Rehearse multiple delivery choices and use evidence from timing and listeners to revise both script and performance.

  • Time a complete run and revise rather than simply rushing.
  • Test two versions of one key passage.
  • Use a listener to identify where meaning became unclear.
  • Use Eiren for questioning/rehearsal feedback, never for scripting the assessed speech.
Day 5 · Unit 6 Mastery Assembly

Deliver, Analyze, Defend, Reflect

Assemble the four canonical components: rhetorical-situation analysis (20%), reasoning map/repair (25%), evidence-based speech (40%), and rhetorical self-analysis/source record (15%).

  • Every required component must reach at least 80%.
  • The weighted Unit 6 result must also reach at least 80%.
  • Political or civic agreement cannot affect the score.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the weak competency, complete targeted corrective learning, and reassess with revised or meaningfully changed evidence.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren is the rehearsal room, not the ghostwriter.

Bring the outline, source record, reasoning map, draft, timed rehearsal notes, or rhetorical self-analysis first. Eiren can act as a questioning audience, flag unclear reasoning, and ask what delivery choice best serves the intended meaning.

Open Eiren
Eiren cannot choose the student's position, write or deliver the assessed speech, fabricate sources, script scored answers, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
