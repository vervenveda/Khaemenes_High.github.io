Seminar & Dramatic Interpretation

How can interpretation remain creative while accountable to evidence?

Reading: Key scenes, critical questions, defensible alternate stagings, and a fresh dramatic passage.
Writing, Discussion & Performance: Revise the drama analysis, participate in evidence-based seminar, stage and defend an interpretation, then demonstrate unfamiliar-scene transfer.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Develop a defensible dramatic interpretation and distinguish it from unsupported invention.
  • Compare a counterreading fairly and explain what evidence strengthens or weakens each interpretation.
  • Participate in seminar by citing evidence, asking genuine questions, listening accurately, building on ideas, challenging reasoning respectfully, and revising a position when warranted.
  • Translate an interpretive claim into playable blocking, voice, movement, pause, and spatial choices.
  • Explain a staged-interpretation rationale with explicit script evidence.
  • Analyze an unfamiliar dramatic scene independently.
  • Complete all four canonical Unit 5 evidence components at 80% or higher.

Canonical Unit 5 Evidence

  • 40% dramatic literary analysis
  • 20% Socratic seminar
  • 25% staged-interpretation rationale
  • 15% unfamiliar-scene transfer

Five-Day Interpretation & Seminar Laboratory

Question → counterread → discuss → stage → transfer.

Day 1 · Interpretive Question Workshop

Questions That Can Sustain Inquiry

Convert textual tensions into questions with multiple evidence-based answers, attach passages, write a provisional answer, and identify evidence that could change it.

Day 2 · Counterreading Laboratory

More Than One Reading Can Be Defensible

State Interpretation A and Counterreading B fairly, identify shared evidence both must explain, compare inference load, and revise the literary analysis to address the strongest counterreading.

Day 3 · Seminar & Listening Lab

Listen to Think, Not Merely to Reply

Complete the reviewed Socratic seminar evidence. Paraphrase accurately, cite the script, ask information-seeking questions, build or challenge responsibly, and record where another interpretation changes or complicates your own.

Day 4 · Staging Interpretation Studio

Make the Reading Visible

Create and test a staging claim and blocking map. Connect movement, stillness, proximity, pause, pace, vocal emphasis, and visual emphasis to the script, then defend the revised staging rationale after feedback or an alternate version.

Day 5 · Unit 5 Mastery Assembly

Interpret, Stage, Transfer, Defend

Assemble the four canonical components: dramatic literary analysis (40%), Socratic seminar (20%), staged-interpretation rationale (25%), and unfamiliar-scene transfer (15%).

  • The transfer scene must be unrehearsed and independently interpreted.
  • Every required component must reach at least 80%.
  • The weighted Unit 5 result must also reach at least 80%.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the missed competency, complete targeted corrective learning, and reassess with revised or fresh evidence as appropriate.
