Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Develop a defensible dramatic interpretation and distinguish it from unsupported invention.
  • Compare a counterreading fairly and explain what evidence would strengthen or weaken each interpretation.
  • Participate in seminar by citing evidence, asking genuine questions, building on ideas, challenging reasoning respectfully, and revising a position when warranted.
  • Use formal register without confusing formality with inflated vocabulary or artificial speech.
  • Translate an interpretive claim into playable blocking, voice, movement, pause, and spatial choices.
  • Use accurately: interpretation, staging, blocking, seminar, counterreading, evidence.
  • Complete the final drama essay, seminar evidence, staged interpretation, reflection, and defined Unit 5 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Interpretation & Seminar Laboratory

Question → counterread → discuss → stage → defend.

Day 1 · Interpretive Question Workshop

Questions That Can Sustain Inquiry

Return to key scenes and convert observations into questions that permit multiple evidence-based answers rather than trivia or predetermined conclusions.

  • Draft three interpretive questions beginning from genuine textual tension.
  • Reject questions answerable by simple recall.
  • Attach two passages that make the strongest question worth discussing.
  • Write a provisional answer and identify what evidence could change it.
Day 2 · Counterreading Laboratory

More Than One Reading Can Be Defensible

Construct a serious counterreading of a key scene or character. The purpose is not artificial “both sides” balance; it is to test whether the preferred interpretation accounts for the strongest competing evidence.

  • State Interpretation A and its strongest evidence.
  • State Counterreading B as fairly and specifically as possible.
  • Identify evidence both readings must explain.
  • Mark where one reading requires more inference than the other.
  • Revise the drama essay to acknowledge and answer the strongest counterreading.
Day 3 · Seminar & Language Lab

Listen to Think, Not Merely to Reply

Practice formal register and discussion moves, then conduct the Socratic seminar. Students should make their reasoning traceable to the group.

  • Use moves such as: “The passage suggests…,” “I want to complicate that claim…,” “What evidence would distinguish those readings?” and “I am revising my view because…”.
  • Track one idea that another participant genuinely changed or complicated.
  • Revise five informal, vague, or inflated statements into precise formal register.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored seminar/language knowledge check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Staging Interpretation Studio

Make the Reading Visible

Translate the interpretive claim into a low-resource staged scene. Every major performance choice should either emerge from textual evidence or be identified honestly as a production choice.

  • Create a staging claim and blocking map.
  • Assign movement, stillness, proximity, pause, pace, and vocal emphasis intentionally.
  • Use Evidence Studio to connect at least three performance choices to the script.
  • Rehearse two versions of one consequential moment and select the stronger interpretation.
  • Use observer feedback to revise one staging decision.
Day 5 · Dramatic Colloquium & Unit Defense

Interpret, Stage, Defend, Revise

Present the staged interpretation and final analysis, then complete the defined Unit 5 assessment evidence.

  • Perform or present the selected scene with the staging rationale.
  • Defend one creative choice using precise textual evidence.
  • Respond to one counterreading without caricaturing it.
  • Explain one substantive revision to the final drama essay.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the weak criterion, complete corrective learning, and reassess before Unit 6.

Sovereign Seminar Game

The Evidence Relay

This discussion game rewards listening, connection, and textual accountability rather than speed or domination.

Relay 1 · Build

Speaker A makes a claim with evidence. Speaker B must accurately paraphrase the claim before adding a new piece of evidence or reasoning.

Relay 2 · Complicate

Speaker C introduces counterevidence or a counterreading without dismissive language. The group identifies what the original interpretation must now explain.

Relay 3 · Revise

The original speaker may defend, qualify, or revise the claim. The point is awarded for making the reasoning more precise—not for refusing to change one's mind.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren prepares the student for questions, not scripted answers.

Bring the seminar question, provisional interpretation, counterreading, staging plan, or revised essay first. Eiren can simulate respectful challenge and expose evidence gaps before the assessed discussion.
