Unit 05 · Drama, Choice & Consequence · Week 15

Seminar and Dramatic Interpretation

How can interpretation remain creative while accountable to evidence?

This five-day sequence teaches the required concepts directly, provides a local course-created text, and supports either handwriting or typing. External tools are not required.

Course-created practice text

Evidence available to every learner

Use either the Week 13 station scene or the Week 14 gate scene. A new interpretation must preserve the printed dialogue. Performance choices may change pace, emphasis, blocking, gesture, silence, or focal point, but the student must explain how each major choice follows from evidence.

Day 71Interpretation Has Boundaries

Interpretation Has Boundaries

Distinguish an imaginative possibility from an evidence-supported interpretation.

Learning target

Distinguish an imaginative possibility from an evidence-supported interpretation.

Learn and model

Rank four readings of the course scene from strongly supported to contradicted. For each, identify the warrant connecting evidence to the claim.

Independent evidence

Write one defensible claim, one plausible counterreading, and one interpretation the text cannot support.

Student response · handwriting or typing

Evidence check

  • Claim answers today’s target
  • Specific words or stage actions are cited
  • Reasoning explains how evidence supports the claim
  • A limitation or alternate reading is considered

Language focus: formal register and accountable discussion moves.

Day 72Prepare the Evidence Seminar

Prepare the Evidence Seminar

Enter discussion with organized evidence rather than a memorized speech.

Learning target

Enter discussion with organized evidence rather than a memorized speech.

Learn and model

Prepare three claims, six precise references, two genuine questions, and one possible revision. Practice paraphrasing another speaker before agreeing or challenging.

Independent evidence

Day 74Revise the Interpretation

Revise the Interpretation

Use new evidence and counterarguments to improve a written or performed reading.

Learning target

Learn and model
