Evidence laboratory · Cedar Route 8 proposal

Source A · ridership snapshot. A school transportation committee reports that 31 of 52 surveyed riders would use a later Route 8 departure at least twice each week. The survey was distributed only to current riders; students who stopped using the route were not contacted.

Source B · safety memorandum. The transportation office records three late-evening pedestrian incidents near the present stop during the previous semester. The memorandum does not establish that a later bus would prevent those incidents, but it identifies a safety condition relevant to route planning.

Source C · cost estimate. A preliminary estimate places the added monthly operating cost between $1,800 and $2,500, depending on driver availability. The estimate is unsigned and marked “planning only.”

Competing claims. Advocate: “The survey proves a later bus is necessary.” Skeptic: “The cost range makes the proposal irresponsible.” Both claims overreach. The survey supports demand among current riders, while the cost estimate signals uncertainty rather than impossibility.

Worked model. A responsible rebuttal concedes what the opposing evidence genuinely shows, tests the warrant connecting that evidence to the conclusion, and then narrows the claim. Ethical persuasion does not hide uncertainty, ridicule an opponent, or convert a planning estimate into a fact.

Transfer challenge. Draft one concession, one rebuttal, and one qualified recommendation using at least two sources. Label the limit of each source.

Evidence-Based English Architecture · Unit 10

Evidence, Rebuttal & Ethical Persuasion · Retrieval & Transfer

Grade 9 formalizes civic argument by requiring fair issue framing, evidence testing, accurate counterclaims, respectful rebuttal, and revision after debate.

Day 1 · Test Evidence

Evaluate evidence for relevance, reliability, sufficiency, date, and whether it actually supports the claim being made.

Day 2 · Compare

Rank two pieces of evidence for the same point and explain which is stronger and why.

Day 3 · Rebut

Respond to a counterclaim by conceding what is valid before explaining where the reasoning or evidence remains weaker.

Day 4 · Audit Ethics

Identify any emotional appeal, framing choice, or omission that risks manipulating rather than informing the audience.

Day 5 · Deliberate

Revise one argument passage so evidence, rebuttal, tone, and ethical persuasion work together.

Mastery boundary: These prompts strengthen argument, research writing, publication, audience adaptation, accessibility, professional communication, and reflection only. They do not change assessment answers, thresholds, learner-record authority, or completion rules.
