Day 116 · Required lesson

Read the data boundary

Distinguish observations, calculations, comparisons, inferences, and unknowns.

Direct instruction and model

A table records 261 visits, not necessarily 261 different students. The data describe ten pilot days and cannot prove long-term impact.

Independent evidence

Write three supported observations, two cautious inferences, and three conclusions the table cannot support. Cite exact values and units.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 117 · Required lesson

Choose an honest display

Match table, bar chart, line graph, or proportional display to the question.

Direct instruction and model

A table supports lookup; bars compare categories; a line emphasizes ordered change. Scales, baselines, labels, and aggregation alter interpretation.

Independent evidence

Sketch two possible displays. Select one and defend its form, axis or category choices, scale, units, and ordering.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 118 · Required lesson

Write titles, captions and annotations

Make each text element perform a distinct job.

Direct instruction and model

A title states the subject; labels identify variables and units; a caption gives context and boundary; an annotation directs attention to a defensible feature.

Independent evidence

Draft a title, complete labels, 40–70-word caption, and two annotations. Remove any causal language the data cannot justify.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 119 · Required lesson

Design for access

Ensure meaning survives without color, tiny text, hovering, or vision.

Direct instruction and model

Use readable contrast, direct labels, patterns or shapes in addition to color, logical reading order, and a concise text alternative that reports the important relationship.

Independent evidence

Create the visual and a text equivalent. Check contrast, type size, color independence, reading order, units, and print legibility.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 120 · Required lesson

Publish and defend

Integrate visual evidence with prose without duplicating every number.

Direct instruction and model

The findings brief should state the question, principal pattern, transportation concern, limitations, and next evidence needed. The graphic supports—not replaces—the reasoning.

Independent evidence

Submit the accessible infographic, findings brief, data-check sheet, accessibility check, revision note, and 150-word design defense.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?
