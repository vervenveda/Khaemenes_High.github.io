Unit 08 · Week 24

Data, Visuals & Accessible Information

How should words and visuals divide the work of explanation?

Reading: Charts, tables, diagrams, maps, captions, infographics, and accessible design examples.
Writing & Design: Interpret verified data, expose visual choices, write useful captions and alt text, create an accessible infographic, and explain what the evidence does—and does not—show.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Read a data display by identifying title, source, population/sample, variables, units, time frame, scale, categories, legend, and missing context where available.
  • Separate what a visual directly shows from an interpretation or causal claim added by the viewer.
  • Recognize how axis range, baseline, aggregation, category boundaries, omitted values, scale, area, perspective, and visual emphasis can affect interpretation.
  • Use numbers and units consistently and avoid false precision.
  • Write captions that orient the reader and explain relevance without merely repeating labels.
  • Write concise, purpose-aware alt text that communicates essential visual information and avoids unnecessary decorative description.
  • Use hierarchy, spacing, typography, labels, and redundant cues so meaning does not depend on color alone.
  • Attribute data and visual sources clearly and preserve provenance.
  • Design words and visuals so each medium does useful work rather than duplicating everything.
  • Use accurately: visual rhetoric, hierarchy, caption, alt text, accessibility, attribution.
  • Complete the accessible infographic, explanatory article, visual/source audit, and defined Unit 8 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Visual Information Laboratory

Read → interrogate → translate → design → accessibility-test.

Day 1 · Visual Autopsy

What Does the Display Actually Show?

Inspect contrasting charts, tables, diagrams, maps, or infographics before accepting the accompanying interpretation.

  • Record source, variables, units, population/sample, time period, categories, scale, and legend where supplied.
  • Write three observations limited to what the display directly supports.
  • Separate observation from inference and causal explanation.
  • Identify one piece of context needed for responsible interpretation.
  • Preserve an annotated visual audit.
Day 2 · Visual Rhetoric Stress Test

Same Data, Different Impression

Examine how design decisions can change salience or apparent magnitude without changing the underlying values.

  • Compare alternate axis ranges/baselines or category groupings where appropriate.
  • Inspect whether area, perspective, icon size, map scale, or aggregation exaggerates or conceals differences.
  • Ask what values, categories, uncertainty, or context are omitted.
  • Redesign one display more transparently without claiming there is always one uniquely neutral visualization.
  • Explain which interpretation remains justified after the redesign.
Day 3 · Caption, Alt Text & Numbers Lab

Translate Without Distorting

Practice moving information among visual, caption, alt text, and prose while preserving meaning.

  • Write a caption that identifies what matters and why the visual is present.
  • Write alt text that communicates essential content according to the visual's purpose.
  • Use consistent units, terminology, decimal precision, and number formatting.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored visual/language/accessibility check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Accessible Infographic Studio

Design a Second Path Into the Explanation

Create the Unit 8 infographic using verified data/information and the explanatory work developed across Weeks 22–24.

  • Choose a clear reading order and information hierarchy.
  • Make labels readable and terminology consistent.
  • Do not rely on color alone to distinguish essential categories or states.
  • Credit data, images, and source material appropriately.
  • Pair the visual with meaningful caption/alt-text records and preserve the design/source trail.
Day 5 · Access & Interpretation Audit

Can the Meaning Survive Without the Decoration?

Test the article/infographic with another reader or through a structured self-audit. The reviewer should be able to identify the central message, evidence, hierarchy, source, and limitations.

  • Review the infographic without relying on color distinctions alone.
  • Review the alt text without viewing the image and identify missing essential information.
  • Trace one number or visual claim back to its source record.
  • Use peer, teacher, or Eiren challenge to revise one misleading, inaccessible, or redundant element.
  • Preserve audit evidence, revision, final article/infographic, and reflection.

Sovereign Visual Game

The Data Lens

Students learn how framing changes perception while the underlying evidence remains constant.

Lens 1 · Raw Record

Begin with a small verified data table. Write only statements directly supported by the values. Mark any statement requiring outside context or causal evidence.

Lens 2 · Two Frames

Represent the same values in two defensible visual forms or scales. Compare what each makes easy to notice, difficult to notice, or potentially misleading.

Lens 3 · Access Translation

Describe the essential information without seeing the visual layout: title/purpose, major pattern, important values or relationships, and meaningful limitation. Compare this with the alt text and revise.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren separates what the visual shows from what the writer claims.

Bring the data/source record, visual, caption, alt text, infographic plan, or interpretation first. Eiren can challenge causal overreach, hidden denominators, unclear units, misleading emphasis, redundant prose, and inaccessible information hierarchy.

Open Eiren
Try: “Make me distinguish observation from inference.” · “Ask what denominator or comparison my claim requires.” · “Read my alt text as if you cannot see the image and tell me what essential meaning is missing.” · “Challenge any sentence that says the data prove more than they show.” Eiren cannot invent data, certify accessibility compliance, fabricate sources, create the assessed infographic, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
