Day 126 · Required lesson

Track an arc through choices

Build a change claim from separated moments, not adjectives.

Direct instruction and model

An arc may show change, revelation, resistance to change, or deterioration. Compare an early choice, pressure point, turning point, and later consequence.

Core-text evidence

Create a four-point arc using precise core-text locators. For each point, record action, pressure, motive inference, consequence, and alternative interpretation.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 127 · Required lesson

Trace motif without symbol hunting

Establish repetition before assigning meaning.

Direct instruction and model

A motif is a recurring image, phrase, object, situation, or contrast. Its meaning comes from changing contexts, not from a universal symbol dictionary.

Core-text evidence

Trace one candidate motif across at least four separated appearances. Record context, association, change, and what would disconfirm your interpretation.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 128 · Required lesson

Move from topic to theme

Write a contestable insight the text develops.

Direct instruction and model

“Power” is a topic. A theme statement expresses a qualified idea about power and must be supported across the work without pretending it is the only meaning.

Core-text evidence

Draft three theme statements. Reject one as topic-only, one as too absolute, and refine the strongest with a because-clause linking pattern and consequence.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 129 · Required lesson

Synthesize character and motif

Explain how formal patterns interact.

Direct instruction and model

Strong analysis asks how a recurring detail changes around a character’s decisions and how that interaction develops an idea. Evidence must come from more than one region of the text.

Core-text evidence

Write a two-paragraph synthesis using three locators. Integrate evidence, explain warrants, and address one contradiction or counterexample.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 130 · Required lesson

Defend the pattern

Test the claim against the whole fiction cycle read so far.

Direct instruction and model

A credible interpretation explains major evidence and acknowledges resistant details. Quantity of quotations cannot replace reasoning.

Core-text evidence

Submit the evidence map, theme analysis, reading log, counterevidence note, and a 150–200 word reflection on how the interpretation changed.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?
