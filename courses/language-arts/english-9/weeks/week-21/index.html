Unit 07 · Week 21

Synthesis, Citation & Research Reporting

How do researchers create a new understanding from multiple sources?

Reading: Source clusters containing complementary, conflicting, qualified, and incomplete evidence.
Writing: Build a 900–1,200-word research report through synthesis, summary, paraphrase, quotation, citation, analysis, revision, and transparent source use.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Synthesize sources by organizing ideas around relationships, patterns, tensions, gaps, and questions rather than writing one-source-at-a-time summaries.
  • Distinguish summary, paraphrase, quotation, common knowledge, and original analysis.
  • Paraphrase from understanding while preserving the source's meaning, scope, qualification, and attribution.
  • Integrate quotations grammatically and rhetorically; avoid dangling quotations and quotation dumping.
  • Preserve citation/provenance information from research notes through final draft.
  • Represent disagreement and uncertainty among sources accurately instead of forcing false consensus.
  • Use citation as intellectual traceability—not decoration added after drafting.
  • Prevent plagiarism through note separation, provenance records, attribution, drafting practice, and verification rather than fear-based rules alone.
  • Use accurately: synthesis, citation, bibliography, paraphrase, integration, plagiarism.
  • Complete the annotated bibliography, source audit, 900–1,200-word research report, and defined Unit 7 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Synthesis Laboratory

Map → integrate → draft → audit → defend.

Day 1 · Source Conversation Map

Sources Do Not Stand in Separate Piles

Return to the Week 19 question and Week 20 source audits. Build a synthesis matrix organized by ideas, subquestions, evidence, agreements, disagreements, limitations, and gaps.

  • Place source names across one dimension and research ideas/subquestions across the other.
  • Record what each source contributes—not merely its topic.
  • Mark agreement, tension, contradiction, qualification, missing evidence, and shared source lineage.
  • Draft one synthesis statement that accurately represents at least two sources.
  • Identify one gap the available evidence cannot responsibly answer.
Day 2 · Paraphrase & Quotation Laboratory

Preserve Meaning While Changing Form

Practice summary, paraphrase, selective quotation, and source integration. The goal is accurate representation plus clear authorship boundaries.

  • Read a passage, set it aside, explain its meaning from understanding, then verify the paraphrase against the original.
  • Preserve qualifications, uncertainty, technical meaning, and scope.
  • Choose quotation only when exact language matters.
  • Integrate quotations into the writer's syntax and analysis instead of leaving them dangling.
  • Check that every borrowed idea, wording, statistic, or distinctive finding is traceable where citation is required.
Day 3 · Citation & Language Lab

Attribution Is Part of the Sentence

Study signal phrases, grammatical quotation integration, citation placement, bibliography/source records, and the language of synthesis.

  • Revise source-by-source sentences into relationship-driven synthesis.
  • Use signal phrases that identify relevant source authority without credential inflation.
  • Correct dangling quotations, ambiguous pronouns, and unclear source boundaries.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored synthesis/citation/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Research Report Studio

The Researcher Must Add Structure and Understanding

Draft and substantially revise the 900–1,200-word report in PROSE. Organize around the research question and evidence relationships—not around the order sources were discovered.

  • Write a focused introduction that establishes the inquiry and necessary context.
  • Develop sections around ideas, patterns, relationships, or subquestions.
  • Integrate evidence with explanation of what it contributes and how it relates to other evidence.
  • Represent conflicting evidence and uncertainty honestly.
  • Complete a source-boundary audit before finalizing the draft.
Day 5 · Research Defense & Unit Colloquium

Can Another Reader Trace the Knowledge?

Submit the annotated bibliography, source audit, final research report, and research trail. Defend one synthesis decision and one citation/provenance decision.

  • Show how the final report changed from the initial Week 19 question or search plan.
  • Trace one important sentence backward from final report → note/evidence record → source.
  • Explain why one source was used for a limited purpose rather than treated as universally credible.
  • Use peer, teacher, or Eiren challenge to revise one synthesis overstatement or unclear source boundary.
  • Below 80%: diagnose the weak criterion, complete corrective learning, revise, and reassess before Unit 8.

Sovereign Synthesis Game

The Source Loom

Students weave evidence by idea rather than stacking source summaries. No external application is required.

Thread 1 · Evidence Cards

Create cards containing one claim/evidence contribution per source with provenance attached. Never detach the evidence from its source identity.

Thread 2 · Relationship Weave

Group cards by agreement, contrast, qualification, cause, example, chronology, uncertainty, or another defensible relationship. A card may participate in more than one relationship.

Thread 3 · New Pattern

Write a sentence or paragraph explaining what becomes visible only after the sources are considered together. If the sources do not support a new conclusion, state the uncertainty instead of manufacturing synthesis.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren tests the seams between sources.

Bring the synthesis matrix, paraphrase, integrated quotation, report section, bibliography, or source-boundary audit first. Eiren can ask whether a source actually supports the sentence, whether two sources genuinely agree, and whether attribution is clear.

Open Eiren
Try: “Make me trace this sentence back to its source.” · “Ask whether my paraphrase preserves the original qualification.” · “Challenge me if I am forcing these sources to agree.” · “Find where the reader could confuse my analysis with the source's claim.” Eiren cannot write the assessed report, fabricate citations, invent source content, disguise plagiarism, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
