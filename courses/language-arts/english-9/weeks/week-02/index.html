Unit 01 · Week 02

Close Reading, Claims & Evidence

How does a careful reader move from what a text says to what the evidence supports?

Reading: Short literary and informational passages with layered details.
Writing: Build claim–evidence–reasoning paragraphs with precise quotations and explicit warrants.

Weekly Targets

By Friday, show the reasoning—not just the answer.

  • Distinguish explicit detail from reasonable inference and identify the evidence that makes the inference defensible.
  • Write a focused claim and connect evidence to it with a clear warrant.
  • Compare evidence for relevance, precision, and strength.
  • Recognize subjects, predicates, phrases, independent clauses, and dependent clauses; use that knowledge to improve clarity.
  • Use accurately: claim, inference, context, warrant, connotation, precision.
  • Preserve two polished evidence paragraphs, an annotation sample, language-lab evidence, and reflection.

Five Distinct Daily Lessons

Observe → infer → test → explain → defend.

Day 1 · Observation Before Inference

What is actually on the page?

Read one short literary passage and one informational passage. Mark observable details first, then write three inferences beside the details that support them.

  • Label notes D for detail or I for inference.
  • Reject one inference that outruns the evidence.
  • Artifact: annotated detail→inference trail.
Day 2 · Evidence Laboratory

Which evidence earns its place?

Turn the strongest inference into a claim. Enter the claim and three candidate pieces of evidence in Evidence Studio, then rank them for relevance, precision, and explanatory power.

  • Record source context/location.
  • Identify one interesting but insufficient piece.
  • Write a one-sentence warrant for the strongest evidence.
Day 3 · Language Laboratory

Build sentences that reveal reasoning.

Study subjects/predicates, phrases/clauses, and independent/dependent clauses. Diagnose six sentences from your notes and revise at least two so the evidence-to-warrant relationship becomes clearer.

Day 4 · Composition Studio

Make the quotation work.

Draft two claim–evidence–reasoning paragraphs in PROSE—one literary, one informational. Integrate quotations grammatically and explain how each supports the claim.

Day 5 · Defense & Transfer

Can your reasoning survive a challenge?

Ask what a careful reader might question. Revise weak reasoning, then explain the detail→inference→claim→evidence→warrant chain without relying on definitions.
