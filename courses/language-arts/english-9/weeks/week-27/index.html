Unit 09 · Week 27

Critical Perspectives & Literary Analysis

How can a critical lens clarify a text without replacing the text?

Reading: Complete the novel; revisit longitudinal evidence; examine carefully selected contextual or critical perspectives after substantial primary-text reading.
Writing: Develop, draft, challenge, conference, and substantially revise a 1,200–1,500-word literary analysis grounded primarily in the novel.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Use a critical perspective as a set of questions that directs attention—not as a predetermined answer imposed on the novel.
  • Distinguish primary textual evidence, historical/cultural context, and secondary interpretation.
  • Compare plausible readings of the same passage and evaluate which interpretation better accounts for evidence, context, and counterevidence.
  • Develop a thesis that is specific, contestable, qualified where necessary, and capable of organizing a sustained literary argument.
  • Build paragraphs around analytical work rather than quotation accumulation or plot summary.
  • Use secondary criticism, when assigned or appropriate, as conversation/counterreading without allowing it to replace direct engagement with the novel.
  • Use cohesion, transitions, reference chains, paragraph sequencing, and precise analytical verbs to make relationships among ideas explicit.
  • Revise in response to a serious counterreading rather than merely proofreading the first interpretation.
  • Defend reading knowledge independently through a conference using passages and longitudinal records.
  • Use accurately: lens, interpretation, context, thesis, counterreading, synthesis.
  • Complete the 1,200–1,500-word literary analysis, independent reading conference, and defined Unit 9 scored evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Literary Inquiry Laboratory

Lens → compare → architect → counterread → defend.

Day 1 · Lens Laboratory

A Lens Changes the Questions, Not the Words on the Page

Revisit one significant passage through two different critical question sets. The exact lenses may vary with the novel and instructional purpose.

  • Begin with what the passage establishes before applying a lens.
  • Ask how each perspective redirects attention toward features such as power, class, gender, psychology, form, history, environment, ethics, or another relevant dimension.
  • Record what each lens illuminates and what it may leave less visible.
  • Reject interpretations that require evidence the passage or novel does not provide.
  • Preserve the two-lens comparison.
Day 2 · Interpretation Tribunal

Two Readings Enter; Evidence Decides What Survives

Develop two plausible interpretations of a passage, character decision, motif, conflict, or ending and test both against the whole novel.

  • State each interpretation fairly before evaluating it.
  • Identify the strongest evidence supporting each.
  • Identify the passage each reading has the hardest time explaining.
  • Distinguish disagreement from factual misreading.
  • Reach a provisional judgment: stronger, weaker, differently scoped, or genuinely unresolved.
Day 3 · Thesis Architecture & Cohesion Lab

A Thesis Must Organize More Than One Paragraph

Build the argument architecture before drafting the full analysis.

  • Write a contestable thesis that answers an interpretive problem rather than announcing a topic.
  • Map each major section to a distinct analytical job.
  • Use precise verbs such as complicates, exposes, reframes, contrasts, develops, qualifies, destabilizes, or reinforces only when the evidence warrants them.
  • Revise transitions so they express actual relationships rather than merely saying “another example.”
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored thesis/cohesion/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Counterreading Revision Studio

Make the Argument Meet Its Strongest Objection

Draft in PROSE, then expose the argument to a serious counterreading from a peer, teacher, Eiren, or assigned critical perspective.

  • Identify the strongest reasonable challenge—not a deliberately weak objection.
  • Determine whether the challenge requires rebuttal, qualification, concession, reframing, or thesis revision.
  • Use secondary criticism only with clear attribution and source boundaries.
  • Preserve the pre-challenge draft and the substantive revision.
  • Complete the 1,200–1,500-word analysis without padding the word count with plot summary.
Day 5 · Independent Reading Defense

The Novel Must Still Be in the Room

Complete an independent reading conference using the novel and the student's longitudinal records. Defend the analysis through direct textual knowledge.

  • Locate and explain one passage central to the thesis.
  • Connect it to an earlier or later passage without relying on a secondary summary.
  • Explain one interpretation that changed during the unit and why.
  • Respond to one unfamiliar but text-grounded question about the selected novel.
  • Preserve final analysis, conference evidence, revision trail, and reflection.

Sovereign Interpretation Game

The Lens Prism

The text is the light source; a lens changes what becomes prominent. It cannot manufacture light that was never there.

Face 1 · Text First

Write observations about a selected passage before naming any critical perspective. Separate textual features from interpretation.

Face 2 · Rotate the Prism

Apply two question sets to the same passage. Record which details become newly important and which claims remain unsupported.

Face 3 · White-Light Test

Remove the lens labels. Can the interpretation still be defended from the novel? If the argument disappears without theoretical vocabulary, return to the text and rebuild.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren becomes the counterreader—not the ghostwriter.

Bring the novel passage, thesis, evidence map, lens comparison, paragraph, or counterreading first. Eiren can challenge scope, identify evidence the thesis has difficulty explaining, test cohesion, and ask whether theoretical language is doing work the textual evidence cannot support.

Open Eiren
Try: “Give my thesis the strongest text-grounded counterreading you can.” · “Ask which passage my argument has the hardest time explaining.” · “Remove my lens terminology and test whether the interpretation still stands.” · “Challenge a paragraph if it summarizes plot instead of analyzing language, structure, pattern, or choice.” Eiren cannot invent quotations or page locations, replace the novel with summaries, impose a required political/critical interpretation, write the assessed analysis, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
