Day 131 · Required lesson

Learn four lens protocols

Treat lenses as question sets, not answer machines.

Direct instruction and model

Formal analysis asks how language and structure work; contextual analysis uses verified historical or cultural evidence; reader-response examines the reader-text transaction; a power lens examines authority, access, constraint, and resistance.

Core-text evidence

Apply two lenses to the calibration ending. Record what each reveals, what evidence it needs, and what it risks overlooking.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 132 · Required lesson

Build competing interpretations

Let the same passage support more than one defensible reading.

Direct instruction and model

A counterreading is not a weak claim invented to lose. It uses genuine textual evidence and exposes where the preferred interpretation needs qualification.

Core-text evidence

Choose one consequential core-text passage. Draft two plausible interpretations, cite evidence for each, and identify the hinge detail that makes one more persuasive.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 133 · Required lesson

Design the thesis and evidence architecture

Make the essay’s reasoning visible before drafting.

Direct instruction and model

A literary thesis names an interpretive claim and a line of reasoning. Body sections should advance that reasoning, not march through plot order or one quotation at a time.

Core-text evidence

Create a thesis, three-part reasoning map, six locators from across the fiction cycle, and one counterreading placement. Explain why each item belongs.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 134 · Required lesson

Draft, revise, and audit

Revise analysis before polishing sentences.

Direct instruction and model

Audit claim–evidence–warrant chains, quotation accuracy, plot-summary proportion, lens limits, counterreading fairness, transitions, and citation consistency. Preserve a before-and-after passage.

Core-text evidence

Draft 1,200–1,500 words. Complete the audit, receive evaluator or conference feedback, and make one structural and one sentence-level revision.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 135 · Required lesson

Independent reading defense

Demonstrate whole-text knowledge and defend the finished interpretation.

Direct instruction and model

The evaluator may ask for an unfamiliar locator, a turning point, motif development, counterevidence, lens limitation, and the revision that most improved reasoning.

Core-text evidence

Submit essay, reading record, evidence map, draft, feedback, revision record, and reflection. Complete the conference in an accessible response mode.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?
