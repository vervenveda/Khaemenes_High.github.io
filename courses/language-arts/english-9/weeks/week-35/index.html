Unit 12 · Week 35

Capstone Synthesis Project

How can one project integrate literary, informational, research, argument, creative, and multimodal modes without becoming a scrapbook?

Reading & Research: Student-selected literary and informational texts plus verified sources connected by a significant inquiry.
Creating: Build one coherent capstone in which analysis, research, argument, creative transformation, and multimodal choices answer the same inquiry and illuminate one another.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Develop a significant, researchable inquiry that cannot be answered adequately by one quotation, one source, or a simple yes/no response.
  • Select literary and informational texts because each contributes a distinct perspective, method, context, or form of evidence.
  • Build a source/evidence trail that distinguishes primary text evidence, contextual information, research findings, interpretation, and the student's own inference.
  • Use literary analysis to investigate how form, language, character, structure, imagery, rhetoric, or another textual feature contributes to the inquiry.
  • Synthesize research sources by relationship—agreement, tension, qualification, context, cause, consequence, limitation—not by writing one source summary after another.
  • Make a defensible argument or interpretive claim that emerges from the inquiry rather than deciding the conclusion first and collecting supporting material afterward.
  • Create an original creative transformation that makes an intellectually necessary contribution to the inquiry and is accompanied by an artist/author rationale.
  • Use multimodal elements only when they add meaning, evidence, accessibility, orientation, or interpretation.
  • Control style across analytical, research, argumentative, reflective, and creative sections while preserving appropriate genre differences.
  • Explain how the components interact so the project functions as one intellectual architecture rather than several unrelated assignments bound together.
  • Use accurately: synthesis, inquiry, methodology, audience, integration, originality.
  • Complete inquiry proposal, source/evidence map, analytical/research/argument work, creative transformation, integration map, final capstone, process record, and defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Capstone Laboratory

Question → investigate → connect → create → integrate.

Day 1 · Inquiry Architecture

Build a Question Big Enough to Need Synthesis

Design the capstone around a meaningful inquiry and identify what kinds of evidence and thinking will actually be required.

  • Generate several candidate questions from Grade 9 themes, texts, problems, or interests.
  • Reject questions answerable by a definition, fact lookup, or predetermined opinion.
  • Define scope, key terms, audience, purpose, and what remains genuinely uncertain.
  • Draft a methodology map: what will literary analysis reveal, what must research establish, what might argument decide, and what can creative work explore differently?
  • Write a proposal explaining why the components need one another.
Day 2 · Source Constellation & Synthesis

Sources Must Speak to One Another

Build a source constellation instead of a stack of summaries.

  • Annotate literary evidence separately from contextual/research evidence.
  • Verify provenance, relevance, limitations, and claim fit for research sources.
  • Map relationships: supports, complicates, contradicts, contextualizes, narrows, extends, or leaves unanswered.
  • Identify where evidence is insufficient and conduct targeted additional research rather than padding the bibliography.
  • Preserve source locations and citation information in Evidence Studio.
Day 3 · Argument, Style & Integration Lab

One Inquiry, Different Intellectual Jobs

Draft the analytical/research/argument spine and test whether each section performs a distinct job while serving the same central inquiry.

  • Develop a working synthesis claim that can still change as evidence develops.
  • Use literary analysis rather than plot summary.
  • Integrate research by idea/relationship rather than source-by-source reporting.
  • Address meaningful tension, counterevidence, ambiguity, or limitation.
  • Shift style intentionally across genres without losing conceptual continuity.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately and complete the scored synthesis/research/style/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Creative Transformation Laboratory

Creation Must Think, Not Decorate

Create an original component—such as a short narrative, poem, dramatic monologue, visual-text composition, audio script, exhibit concept, or another appropriate form—that investigates an aspect of the inquiry unavailable through exposition alone.

  • Identify the precise intellectual job of the creative component before producing it.
  • Do not fictionalize research findings and present them as evidence.
  • Do not impersonate a real person's undocumented inner thoughts as historical fact.
  • Preserve attribution/rights boundaries for incorporated material.
  • Write an artist/author rationale explaining choices, connection to evidence/texts, and what the creative form reveals.
Day 5 · Integration Defense & Final Assembly

Remove Anything That Cannot Explain Why It Belongs

Assemble the capstone and defend its architecture to a peer, teacher, or Eiren before finalizing.

  • Trace the inquiry through every component.
  • Identify what each component contributes that another component cannot do as well.
  • Remove or redesign decorative/redundant sections.
  • Check transitions and cross-references so components genuinely interact.
  • Complete source, citation, attribution, accessibility, and final editorial checks.
  • Preserve proposal, methodology, research trail, drafts, creative rationale, feedback, revision record, final capstone, and reflection.

Sovereign Synthesis Game

The Constellation Test

A capstone is not a pile of stars. Students must draw and defend the lines that make the constellation.

Node 1 · Contribution

For every text, source, section, or creative element, finish: “This belongs because it contributes ___ to the inquiry that the other components do not.”

Node 2 · Connection

Draw at least one meaningful relationship to another node: supports, complicates, contradicts, contextualizes, transforms, qualifies, or raises a new question.

Node 3 · Cut Test

Remove the component mentally. If the project's reasoning and meaning remain unchanged, the component is probably decorative, redundant, or insufficiently integrated.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren becomes the capstone architecture examiner.

Bring the inquiry, methodology map, source constellation, working synthesis claim, creative-component plan, integration map, or draft first. Eiren can challenge whether the question is genuinely open, whether research is being synthesized, whether a creative component thinks rather than decorates, and whether the parts actually depend on one another.

Open Eiren
Try: “Which part of my inquiry is still just a fact question?” · “Make me explain the relationship between these two sources.” · “What intellectual work does my creative component perform?” · “Which section could I remove without changing the capstone—and what does that reveal?” Eiren cannot choose the student's conclusion, fabricate sources or quotations, invent undocumented perspectives, write assessed capstone components for the student, turn creative work into factual evidence, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
