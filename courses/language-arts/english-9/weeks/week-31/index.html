Composition studio · Emergency heat advisory

Communication problem. A school must explain a two-day heat advisory to three audiences: students, families, and staff supervising outdoor activities. The underlying facts are the same, but the users’ immediate decisions differ.

Verified facts. Outdoor practice moves indoors after 1:00 p.m.; water stations remain open all day; students may carry refillable bottles; families should check the transportation update before dismissal; staff must document any schedule relocation. The notice does not authorize students to leave campus early.

Genre choices. A phone alert needs immediate action and brevity. A family webpage needs context, dates, links, and accessibility. A hallway poster needs large hierarchy and very few decisions. An audio announcement needs language that can be understood without seeing a screen.

Worked model. Effective multimodal composition does not merely repeat the same paragraph in several formats. It coordinates modes so that words, headings, icons, spacing, audio, and links each carry a useful part of the message without creating contradictions.

Prototype test. A first-time reader should be able to answer: What changed? When? What must I do? Where can I verify details? What information is still unresolved?

Transfer challenge. Create two versions for different audiences and explain three design choices in a short rationale. Include one accessibility improvement and one fact you deliberately chose not to infer.

Evidence-Based English Architecture · Unit 11

Audience, Genre & Multimodal Composition · Retrieval & Transfer

Grade 9 turns composition into authentic publication by making audience, curation, visual rhetoric, accessibility, source credit, collaborative editing, and editorial judgment visible.

Day 1 · Define Audience

Identify the real or simulated publication audience, purpose, genre expectations, and what action or experience the publication should create.

Day 2 · Curate

Choose texts, images, audio, or other media that fit the theme and audience for a reason beyond personal preference.

Day 3 · Compose

Draft or revise one piece so words, image, sound, layout, or sequence share the communication work deliberately.

Day 4 · Test

Ask a peer to identify the intended audience, message, and strongest/weakest design choice without explanation from the creator.

Day 5 · Reflect

Write an editorial rationale explaining audience, genre, multimodal choices, source credit, and one revision still needed.

Mastery boundary: These prompts strengthen publication, drama analysis, performance, sustained reading, critical-lens use, seminar leadership, accessibility, and reflection only. They do not change assessment answers, thresholds, learner-record authority, or completion rules.
