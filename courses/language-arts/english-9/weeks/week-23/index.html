Week 23 technical packet

Shared facts and constraints

Operational requirements

  1. Visitor presents a student ID or approved temporary pass.
  2. Staff records arrival time and chosen work area; no sensitive notes belong in the public sign-in view.
  3. Visitor stores food, reviews the area expectation, and confirms transportation home.
  4. Collaborative-room use requires a reservation when capacity reaches twelve.
  5. At departure, visitor checks out and returns borrowed equipment.

Exception cards

  • ID forgotten but identity can be confirmed by an authorized school record.
  • Quiet room is full.
  • A visitor needs an accessible route or communication format.
  • The final bus leaves before the visitor’s planned departure.
  • The sign-in device is unavailable.

Day 111 · Required lesson

Read procedures as systems

Identify goal, user, prerequisites, inputs, sequence, decision points, outputs, and verification.

Direct instruction and model

A procedure is a model of action. Missing prerequisites or hidden decisions cause failure even when every sentence is grammatical.

Independent evidence

Annotate the operational requirements using the seven system elements. Identify three details a first-time user still needs.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 112 · Required lesson

Write precise actions

Use imperative verbs, concrete nouns, quantities, locations, and observable results.

Direct instruction and model

“Handle the form correctly” is vague. “Enter the arrival time in the check-in field, then confirm the student’s work area” is testable.

Independent evidence

Rewrite five vague directions. For each, add an observable success condition without inventing unavailable policy.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 113 · Required lesson

Design decisions and exceptions

Use conditional language for branches without burying the normal path.

Direct instruction and model

Write the normal sequence first; place “if/then” branches at the exact decision point. Safety and accessibility exceptions must be visible, not footnotes.

Independent evidence

Integrate all five exception cards into a decision table: condition, action, responsible person, and verification.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 114 · Required lesson

Run a usability test

Test the document, not the reader.

Direct instruction and model

A tester thinks aloud while following only the written instructions. The observer records hesitation, error, question, and recovery without coaching.

Independent evidence

Conduct or simulate two test runs. Record task completion, unclear step, error type, time or step count, and the revision the evidence supports.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?

Day 115 · Required lesson

Revise for reproducibility

Separate verified instructions from assumptions and unresolved policy.

Direct instruction and model

Do not conceal missing authority with confident wording. Label unresolved decisions and route them to an evaluator or authorized staff member.

Independent evidence

Submit the procedure, decision table, two test records, before-and-after revision excerpt, and a short limitation statement.

Usability check

  • Is every fact accurate?
  • Is the structure visible?
  • Can the intended reader act?
  • Are exceptions and limits disclosed?
  • Does meaning survive print and assistive reading?
