Unit 07 · Week 20

Credibility, Bias & Lateral Reading

How can a researcher investigate a source instead of merely reading it?

Reading: Web pages, articles, data displays, source histories, about pages, citations, and corroborating coverage.
Research & Writing: Investigate provenance, expertise, incentives, evidence, date, context, corroboration, uncertainty, and limitations before deciding how a source can responsibly be used.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Investigate who created or published a source, what expertise is relevant, when it appeared, and what provenance can be established.
  • Use lateral reading: leave the source, investigate the source/claim elsewhere, then return with context.
  • Examine ownership, funding, sponsorship, mission, incentives, affiliations, and intended audience without treating any one factor as automatic disqualification.
  • Evaluate the evidence supporting a particular claim separately from the overall reputation of the source.
  • Corroborate important claims across genuinely independent evidence rather than counting copied reports as multiple confirmations.
  • Trace quotations, images, statistics, and claims toward their original context when practical.
  • Recognize missing context, cherry-picking, manipulated framing, outdated information, and uncertainty.
  • Use hedging, certainty, and attribution language proportional to the evidence.
  • Use accurately: credibility, bias, corroboration, provenance, currency, expertise.
  • Complete the three-source credibility audit and defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Source Forensics Laboratory

Identify → leave → trace → corroborate → judge use.

Day 1 · Provenance Before Trust

What Is This Source—and Who Is Behind It?

Select one research source and build its provenance record before making a credibility judgment.

  • Identify author/creator, publisher/platform, publication date, source type, and stated purpose where available.
  • Investigate relevant expertise rather than relying on titles alone.
  • Record ownership, sponsorship, funding, affiliation, or mission when discoverable and relevant.
  • Distinguish what is known, inferred, unknown, and not relevant to the current claim.
  • Preserve the source location and access date in the source ledger.
Day 2 · Lateral Reading Lab

Leave the Page

Do not let a source describe itself unchallenged. Open independent pathways to learn what others report about the author, publisher, organization, event, data, or claim.

  • Search the source/organization rather than only the topic.
  • Compare self-description with independent descriptions.
  • Trace one citation, statistic, quotation, or linked claim toward its originating evidence.
  • Identify whether apparently separate reports are actually repeating the same upstream source.
  • Return to the original page and revise the initial credibility judgment.
Day 3 · Certainty & Attribution Lab

Write Only as Strongly as the Evidence Allows

Practice language that distinguishes established fact, source claim, inference, possibility, uncertainty, and dispute.

  • Revise absolute statements when evidence supports only a qualified conclusion.
  • Replace vague attribution such as “experts say” with specific, meaningful attribution.
  • Distinguish “the source reports” from “the evidence establishes.”
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored source/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Corroboration & Context Studio

Independent Agreement Is Stronger Than Repetition

Audit three sources connected to the Week 19 research question. Compare not just conclusions but evidence lineage, context, and limitations.

  • Identify which claims the sources actually share and which they do not.
  • Determine whether sources are independent or derive from the same report/data/press release.
  • Inspect dates and ask whether currency matters for this claim.
  • Check whether quotations, images, graphs, or statistics have lost relevant context.
  • Draft the three-source credibility audit in PROSE, including limits on how each source may responsibly be used.
Day 5 · Source Defense Board

Credible for What?

Present the three-source audit. Avoid assigning a single universal “credible/not credible” stamp when usefulness depends on the specific claim and research purpose.

  • Defend one source as useful for a specific purpose and identify its limitations.
  • Explain one bias/incentive without claiming that bias alone proves falsehood.
  • Show one claim that became stronger, weaker, or more uncertain after lateral reading.
  • Use peer, teacher, or Eiren challenge to revise one credibility judgment.
  • Preserve provenance records, lateral-reading trail, language check, audit, challenge notes, and reflection.

Sovereign Source Game

The Source Detective Board

The objective is not to “catch bias.” Students build an evidence board showing what can actually be established about a source and its claims.

Board 1 · Identity

Create cards for creator, publisher, date, source type, expertise, mission, funding/sponsorship, and intended audience. Mark each card known, inferred, unknown, or irrelevant.

Board 2 · Lineage

Choose one quotation, statistic, image, or claim and trace it backward. Draw arrows between pages that merely repeat one another until the earliest practical source/evidence is located.

Board 3 · Corroboration

Add independent evidence. If three pages all depend on one underlying source, count the lineage honestly as one evidence stream—not three confirmations.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren asks “How do you know?” one layer deeper.

Bring a source, provenance record, lateral-reading trail, credibility judgment, or attribution sentence first. Eiren can expose reputation shortcuts, unsupported bias claims, missing lineage, and certainty stronger than the evidence.

Open Eiren
Try: “Ask what independent evidence supports this claim.” · “Challenge me if I am using ‘biased’ as a substitute for analysis.” · “Ask whether these three reports are actually independent.” · “Make me separate what I know about the source from what I know about this specific claim.” Eiren cannot certify a source as true by authority alone, fabricate provenance, invent corroboration, write the assessed audit, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
