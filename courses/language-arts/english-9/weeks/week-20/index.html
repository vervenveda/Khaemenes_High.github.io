ProReSources / Evidence & PROSELearning Arcade

Local research simulation · Rivermark Learning Annex

Common case file

Decision: Rivermark must decide whether to convert an unused municipal annex into an evening study center for students ages 13–18.

Background brief: The annex has four rooms, basic internet wiring, and a bus stop nearby. Renovation, supervision, accessibility, transportation, operating cost, demand, and neighborhood impact all require investigation. No conclusion is supplied.

Evidence boundary: Rivermark and every document in this packet are course-created simulations. Students may cite them for this instructional case but must never present them as evidence about a real community.

Week 20 evidence packet

Read before drawing conclusions

Source A · Planning Office memo

Dated 12 March 2026; signed by analyst Mira Chen. Reports that 163 of 240 voluntary online survey respondents supported the annex. The survey link appeared on the city youth-services page for ten days. Raw questions are attached, but respondent demographics are not.

Source B · StudyNow vendor brief

Undated. Claims “learning hubs raise achievement by 40%.” It links to no study and offers Rivermark a renovation package. The company biography names designers but no education researcher.

Source C · Transit operations table

Dated 28 February 2026; issued by Rivermark Transit. Shows the nearby route ends at 7:10 p.m. on weekdays. The proposed center would close at 9:00 p.m. The table documents schedules, not student travel behavior.

Source D · Residents Association letter

Dated 18 March 2026; signed by 31 residents. Predicts noise and parking problems but includes no measurement. It accurately notes that the annex lot has twelve spaces.

Day 96 · Required lesson

Read vertically, then laterally

Distinguish what a page says from what can be learned about its producer.

Direct instruction and model

Vertical reading examines claims and evidence on the source. Lateral reading leaves the source to investigate author, institution, funding, reputation, and independent coverage.

Independent evidence

For Sources A and B, record two vertical observations and four lateral checks you would perform. State what each check could reveal.

Research integrity check

  • What is known?
  • What is inferred?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Which source supports the statement?
  • What limitation must be disclosed?

Day 97 · Required lesson

Trace claims to evidence

Follow numbers and quotations to their original support.

Direct instruction and model

A precise number can still be weak if the sample, measure, comparison, or origin is hidden. Trace the “40%” claim and the 163/240 survey result.

Independent evidence

Complete a claim-trace chain: claim → original evidence → method → missing information → justified conclusion.

Research integrity check

  • What is known?
  • What is inferred?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Which source supports the statement?
  • What limitation must be disclosed?

Day 98 · Required lesson

Bias without dismissal

Identify perspective and incentive without treating bias as an automatic disqualifier.

Direct instruction and model

Every source has a position. The question is how that position shapes selection, framing, omission, and interpretation—and whether usable evidence remains.

Independent evidence

Write balanced audit notes for Sources B and D: one relevant contribution, one incentive or perspective, one limitation, and one responsible use.

Research integrity check

  • What is known?
  • What is inferred?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Which source supports the statement?
  • What limitation must be disclosed?

Day 99 · Required lesson

Corroborate and contextualize

Compare independent sources that address the same part of a claim.

Direct instruction and model

Corroboration requires genuine overlap. Source C can test transportation feasibility but cannot confirm demand. Source D can identify concerns but cannot measure their probability.

Independent evidence

Build a four-source matrix: claim addressed, evidence supplied, evidence missing, corroborating source needed, and present confidence.

Research integrity check

  • What is known?
  • What is inferred?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Which source supports the statement?
  • What limitation must be disclosed?

Day 100 · Required lesson

Issue a source verdict

Use calibrated language rather than “good source” or “bad source.”

Direct instruction and model

Verdicts should be claim-specific: reliable for a narrow fact, useful with qualification, lead only, or unusable for the stated claim.

Independent evidence

Submit four claim-specific verdicts and a 200–250 word memo explaining what the current packet can and cannot establish.

Research integrity check

  • What is known?
  • What is inferred?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Which source supports the statement?
  • What limitation must be disclosed?
