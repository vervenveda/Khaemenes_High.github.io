Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Distinguish direct characterization from indirect characterization through action, dialogue, thought, appearance, relationships, and response to pressure.
  • Analyze how physical and social setting shape conflict, options, atmosphere, and character behavior.
  • Trace a character's motivation through a sequence of choices and consequences.
  • Make a defensible claim about what a pressured choice reveals and support it with precise textual evidence.
  • Use modifiers deliberately and replace vague verbs with precise verbs in analytical and creative writing.
  • Use accurately: protagonist, antagonist, motivation, conflict, atmosphere, characterization.
  • Complete defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Learning Cycle

Character becomes visible under pressure.

Day 1 · Character Evidence Lab

Observe Before Judging

Read a short-fiction selection and collect evidence before labeling the protagonist. Track what the character says, does, thinks, notices, avoids, and chooses.

  • Sort examples into direct and indirect characterization.
  • Create an evidence → trait → warrant chart.
  • Write two competing character claims and test which is better supported.
  • Identify one detail that complicates the stronger claim.
Day 2 · Pressure Map

Setting Is an Active Force

Map the physical and social conditions surrounding the character. Ask what choices would disappear or change if the setting changed.

  • Identify concrete setting details and the atmosphere they produce.
  • Trace external and internal conflict separately.
  • Build a setting → pressure → choice → consequence chain.
  • Write a claim explaining what the decisive choice reveals about a value or motivation.
Day 3 · Language Precision Lab

Make Sentences Carry the Action

Study misplaced/dangling modifiers and precise verb choice using sentences drawn from character and setting analysis.

  • Diagnose and repair ten modifier problems.
  • Replace vague verbs in ten sentences and explain how meaning changes.
  • Revise five sentences from the student's own analysis.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored language/vocabulary check at 80% or higher; correct, review, and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Story Engineering Studio

Create Pressure, Then Reveal Character

Plan and draft an original scene with a decisive turn. The setting must constrain or intensify the conflict, and characterization must emerge primarily through behavior rather than explanation.

  • Define character motivation and a competing pressure.
  • Design three setting details that affect the action.
  • Draft the decisive choice and its immediate consequence.
  • Use PROSE to revise for precise verbs, modifier placement, dialogue/action balance, and unnecessary explanation.
  • Preserve the planning state and one meaningful revision.
Day 5 · Analysis ↔ Creation Defense

Prove the Craft Choice

Submit the character analysis and original scene. Explain how the creative choices apply a technique observed in the week's reading without copying its content.

  • Defend the strongest analytical claim with evidence.
  • Identify the moment of maximum pressure in the original scene.
  • Explain what the protagonist's choice reveals without relying on a direct label.
  • Use peer, teacher, or Eiren challenge to revise one weak inference or craft choice.
  • Preserve analysis, scene, language evidence, and reflection as Unit 3 evidence.
