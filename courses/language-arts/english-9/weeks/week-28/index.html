Issue laboratory · Rivermark evening study pilot

Source A · visitor survey. During a ten-day pilot, 40 current visitors were asked about later study-room hours: 24 requested later hours, 10 preferred the existing schedule, and 6 reported no preference. The survey did not include students who could not already reach the room, families, transport providers, or staff who would cover the added time.

Source B · operations note. Extending closing time to 9:00 p.m. would require two additional staff-hours each evening. The note does not identify the wage rate, funding source, security coverage, or whether staffing can be sustained beyond the pilot.

Source C · access note. The last scheduled bus leaves at 7:40 p.m. One student representative argues that a 9:00 p.m. closing is justified because “most students asked for it.” Another asks that transport and staffing be investigated before any permanent change.

Worked model. The strongest defensible claim is narrower than either speaker’s first position: the survey shows demand among current visitors, but it does not establish system-wide demand or feasibility. A fair recommendation is to investigate transport and staffing while testing a limited supervised extension. A fair counterclaim is that delaying action may prolong an already documented access need.

Transfer challenge. Rewrite the issue as a decision question, identify three stakeholders, define two fair criteria, and state one piece of evidence that could change your position.

Day 1

Define the decision

Direct instruction

A topic names a subject; an issue asks what a decision-maker should do under stated constraints.

Apply and preserve

Rewrite “school technology” as two different decisions. Name affected people and needed evidence.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 2

Fact, value and policy

Direct instruction

Facts describe what is; values specify judgment criteria; policies recommend action. A policy depends on both facts and values.

Apply and preserve

Classify six annex claims. Identify an unsupported fact and a value disagreement.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 3

Fair criteria

Direct instruction

Define standards before choosing a favored option and apply them consistently. Access, cost and usability require observable indicators.

Apply and preserve

Compare extending hours with improving daytime access using three defined criteria.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 4

Strong counterclaims

Direct instruction

Represent an alternative in language its supporter could recognize. A weak invented opponent does not test your position.

Apply and preserve

Write a 150-word case against your preferred option; identify evidence that could change your mind.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Day 5

Qualified thesis

Direct instruction

State scope and conditions. Always and never require much stronger evidence than a limited claim.

Apply and preserve

Submit a 450–600-word issue brief, criteria table, two-sided map and conditional thesis.

Check: Answer the task, cite a precise detail, explain the connection and disclose a limitation.

Evidence-Based English Architecture · Unit 10

Issue Framing & Counterclaims · Retrieval & Transfer

Grade 9 formalizes civic argument by requiring fair issue framing, evidence testing, accurate counterclaims, respectful rebuttal, and revision after debate.

Day 1 · Frame Issue

Define the issue narrowly enough to argue responsibly. Distinguish the question, stakeholders, key terms, and what evidence would matter.

Day 2 · Map Claims

Identify the main claims, assumptions, and values on more than one side without reducing either side to a slogan.

Day 3 · Represent Opposition

Write the strongest reasonable version of an opposing position so a supporter of that view would recognize it as fair.

Day 4 · Test Scope

Identify where a claim is too broad, too certain, or dependent on an unstated assumption. Revise the wording.

Day 5 · Deliberate

State a provisional position that includes the strongest counterclaim and one unresolved question.

Mastery boundary: These prompts strengthen argument, research writing, publication, audience adaptation, accessibility, professional communication, and reflection only. They do not change assessment answers, thresholds, learner-record authority, or completion rules.
