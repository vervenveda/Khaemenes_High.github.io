Unit 10 · Week 28

Issue Framing & Counterclaims

How does the way an issue is framed influence possible conclusions?

Reading: Balanced issue sets with definitions, histories, stakeholder views, claims, and evidence.
Writing: Define the issue before taking a side, map stakeholders and assumptions, represent serious counterclaims fairly, calibrate certainty, and produce an issue brief with a provisional thesis.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Distinguish a broad topic from an arguable issue and an answerable argument question.
  • Identify how definitions, boundaries, time frames, comparison groups, causal assumptions, and omitted alternatives shape an issue before evidence is weighed.
  • Separate a source's explicit claim from assumptions or warrants the reader must infer.
  • Map stakeholders without treating a stakeholder group as internally uniform.
  • Represent a counterclaim in language its proponents could reasonably recognize before responding to it.
  • Distinguish common ground from false agreement and identify genuine points of disagreement.
  • Use qualifiers and modal verbs to calibrate claims to evidence rather than to make weak writing sound sophisticated.
  • Recognize loaded wording, false binaries, presuppositions, and definitions that quietly predetermine conclusions.
  • Revise the framing of an issue when new stakeholders, evidence, or legitimate alternatives reveal that the original question was too narrow or biased.
  • Use accurately: stakeholder, framing, claim, counterclaim, qualification, common ground.
  • Complete a framing audit, stakeholder/counterclaim map, issue brief, provisional thesis, and defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Civic Reasoning Laboratory

Frame → map → calibrate → steelman → brief.

Day 1 · Framing Autopsy

What Has the Question Already Decided?

Compare multiple descriptions or questions about the same issue before adopting any position.

  • Underline definitions and boundary choices.
  • Identify the time frame, population, comparison, and assumed alternatives.
  • Mark loaded terms, presuppositions, or false binaries.
  • Rewrite one slanted or prematurely narrow question in a form that leaves more than one conclusion genuinely possible.
  • Preserve the original and revised frames.
Day 2 · Stakeholder & Assumption Map

Who Is Affected, and What Is Being Assumed?

Map relevant stakeholders and trace claims back to explicit reasons, evidence, assumptions, and values.

  • Do not assume all members of a stakeholder category share one position.
  • Separate direct interests from claimed representation of another group's interests.
  • Identify at least one unstated assumption behind each major argument where the text supports the inference.
  • Distinguish factual disagreement, value disagreement, definition disagreement, and policy disagreement.
  • Identify genuine common ground without erasing the remaining conflict.
Day 3 · Certainty Calibration Lab

How Strongly May the Evidence Let You Speak?

Study qualifiers and modal verbs as tools for matching claim strength to evidence.

  • Compare may, can, tends to, often, likely, should, must, always, proves, and similar certainty signals in context.
  • Revise overclaims downward when evidence is limited.
  • Strengthen unjustifiably timid claims when evidence is robust.
  • Explain how qualification changes scope—not merely tone.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored framing/language check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Steelman Exchange

Earn the Right to Rebut

Before arguing against a serious opposing view, reconstruct it as fairly and strongly as the available source set permits.

  • State the counterclaim without mockery, loaded labels, or strategic omissions.
  • Include its strongest relevant reason/evidence rather than its weakest example.
  • Identify what the position gets right or what legitimate concern it addresses.
  • Ask a peer, teacher, or Eiren whether the representation is recognizable and fair.
  • Revise before drafting any rebuttal.
Day 5 · Issue Brief & Provisional Thesis

Take a Position Without Pretending Inquiry Is Over

Draft a concise issue brief in PROSE that defines the question, establishes necessary context, maps major positions, and ends with a provisional thesis.

  • State what is inside and outside the scope of the brief.
  • Represent at least one serious counterclaim fairly.
  • Distinguish sourced fact from interpretation or recommendation.
  • Use calibrated qualifiers where uncertainty remains.
  • Identify what evidence could cause the provisional thesis to change.
  • Preserve framing audit, source trail, counterclaim map, issue brief, feedback, revision, and reflection.

Sovereign Argument Game

The Frame Forge

Students discover how the same public question can be distorted before anyone begins arguing about evidence.

Forge 1 · Neutral Core

Start with a broad issue and write the smallest factual description of the disagreement you can support. No persuasive adjectives.

Forge 2 · Frame Shift

Create two differently framed but recognizable questions by changing scope, definition, time horizon, comparison, or stakeholder emphasis. Identify what each frame makes easier to notice and easier to ignore.

Forge 3 · Bias Alarm

Inspect both questions for loaded premises, false choices, excluded stakeholders, or built-in conclusions. Repair the stronger defects and explain why the revised question is more suitable for inquiry.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren becomes the fairness examiner.

Bring the issue question, source excerpt, stakeholder map, counterclaim, or provisional thesis first. Eiren can expose loaded framing, hidden assumptions, missing stakeholders, false binaries, overclaiming, and weak representations of opposition.

Open Eiren
Try: “What has my question already assumed?” · “Would a serious opponent recognize this description of their position?” · “Find the strongest version of the counterclaim supported by my source set.” · “Which word in my thesis claims more certainty than my evidence earns?” Eiren cannot choose a political/civic position for the student, fabricate stakeholder views, invent sources, manufacture false balance, write the assessed issue brief, assign verified scores, or override mastery.
