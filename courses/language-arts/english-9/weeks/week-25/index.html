Unit 09 · Week 25

Launching a Sustained Novel Study

How can readers maintain attention, questions, and evidence across a long text?

Reading: Opening chapters, a teacher-approved full novel, carefully selected context, and a realistic reading calendar.
Thinking: Build a durable mental model of people, places, chronology, questions, vocabulary, predictions, and emerging patterns without turning every page into a worksheet.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Create a realistic reading plan based on the actual edition/length, available days, and appropriate checkpoints rather than assuming every reader has the same pace.
  • Sustain meaningful reading intervals and adjust the plan from observed pace without treating reading speed as the learning goal.
  • Annotate selectively so notes preserve important questions, changes, evidence, and patterns without destroying reading flow.
  • Maintain a working chronology and distinguish story events from the order in which a narrator reveals them.
  • Track characters, relationships, locations, conflicts, and unresolved questions across separated reading sessions.
  • Treat early interpretations as hypotheses that may strengthen, change, or fail as new evidence appears.
  • Calibrate predictions by recording evidence before reading onward and revisiting them afterward.
  • Use morphology and context together to infer unfamiliar vocabulary, then verify when precision matters.
  • Use historical/cultural context to clarify the novel without allowing context to predetermine what the text must mean.
  • Use accurately: motif, context, annotation, chronology, pattern, hypothesis.
  • Complete the reading plan, opening evidence map, context note, first reading conference, and defined scored checks at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Sustained Reading Laboratory

Plan → enter → map → recalibrate → confer.

Day 1 · Reading Expedition Plan

Build a Calendar That Can Survive Reality

Inspect the selected edition, major divisions, available reading days, and Unit 9 deadlines. Build a first-pass reading calendar with checkpoints rather than a brittle page quota.

  • Record edition and useful location system: page, chapter, section, or other stable marker.
  • Estimate an initial pace, then label it provisional.
  • Create catch-up/recovery space instead of scheduling every day at maximum load.
  • Define what evidence will be preserved at checkpoints.
  • Begin reading and record actual—not imagined—pace.
Day 2 · Opening World Map

Who, Where, When, and What Is Still Unclear?

Continue the opening reading while building a lightweight mental-model map.

  • Track only characters/relationships that currently matter.
  • Record locations and chronology when they affect understanding.
  • Separate what the narrator/text establishes from what the reader currently infers.
  • Record unresolved questions rather than forcing premature answers.
  • Save evidence markers for details likely to matter later.
Day 3 · Vocabulary & Morphology Lab

Infer First; Verify When It Matters

Use roots, prefixes, suffixes, syntax, nearby context, and word relationships to infer unfamiliar language from the actual novel.

  • Break selected words into meaningful morphological parts where possible.
  • Write a context-based meaning hypothesis before consulting a reference.
  • Verify high-value words and record how the hypothesis changed.
  • Distinguish dictionary meaning from the meaning activated in this passage.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored vocabulary/morphology check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Prediction Calibration Lab

Prediction Is a Testable Reading Hypothesis

Pause at a meaningful point before reading onward. Make one prediction and record the textual evidence that makes it plausible.

  • State the prediction before discovering the outcome.
  • Assign confidence: low, medium, or high—and explain why.
  • Read onward without editing the original prediction.
  • Classify the result: supported, partly supported, contradicted, or still unresolved.
  • Explain which evidence was informative and which clue was over-weighted.
Day 5 · First Reading Conference

Show the Reader's Thinking Trail

Conduct a brief teacher, peer, or Eiren-supported conference using the novel and reading record—not a plot summary downloaded from elsewhere.

  • Explain one current question and the evidence that generated it.
  • Trace one character/relationship or chronology change across at least two reading locations where the text supports it.
  • Present one hypothesis that changed because of new evidence.
  • Compare planned pace with observed pace and adjust the calendar responsibly.
  • Preserve the reading plan, map, context note, morphology work, prediction record, conference evidence, and reflection.

Sovereign Reading Game

The Long-Book Memory Palace

No external application is required. The game strengthens retrieval across reading sessions without rewarding trivia memorization.

Room 1 · People & Places

Close the book. Reconstruct the currently important characters, relationships, and locations from memory. Reopen the text and correct only what the evidence shows was wrong or missing.

Room 2 · Chronology Chain

Arrange five to eight important events in story chronology, then compare that order with the order in which the narration revealed them. Note any meaningful difference.

Room 3 · Question Lanterns

Carry three unresolved questions forward. At each checkpoint, mark each as answered, deepened, replaced, or still open. Evidence—not impatience—determines when a question closes.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren conducts a reading conference without replacing the reading.

Bring the novel passage, location marker, question, prediction, context note, or reading map first. Eiren can ask for evidence, test memory, challenge premature patterns, and help distinguish text from inference.

Open Eiren
Try: “Quiz my mental model without giving me the answers first.” · “Ask me what in the text made me predict this.” · “Challenge this pattern if I only have one occurrence.” · “Make me separate historical context from evidence inside the novel.” Eiren cannot replace assigned reading with summaries, fabricate passages or page numbers, reveal later plot events during a spoiler-limited conference, write the assessed literary analysis, assign verified scores, or override mastery.

80% Weekly Mastery Contract

Sustained reading is measured by comprehension and evidence—not raw speed.

Required evidence includes the adaptive reading calendar, opening-world map, selective annotations/evidence bookmarks, context note where appropriate, morphology/vocabulary check, prediction calibration record, first reading conference, and reflection. Every defined scored check requires at least 80%; below 80% triggers targeted corrective learning and retry.
