Full novel study and shared calibration

The Lamp Room is a short local calibration cycle, not a full novel. A complete teacher-approved novel remains required for the original novel-study outcome. Agree on a lawful, accessible edition with the evaluator; record title, author, edition and stable chapter/page or audio locators. Do not award full novel-study credit for the short packet alone.

Week 25: begin the complete novel and establish a realistic reading plan. Week 26: track choices, motifs and themes across the middle reading. Week 27: finish the complete novel, synthesize evidence from beginning, middle and end, and submit the 1,200–1,500-word analysis and independent reading conference. Adjust the reading schedule with the evaluator rather than substituting summaries.

If access is not yet ready, start the local calibration tasks while the evaluator arranges the text; record the novel requirement as pending.

Day 121 · Required lesson

Verify access and edition

Establish the exact text, format, and locator system.

Direct instruction and model

Different editions and translations may divide or word a sustained fiction cycle differently. Citation requires a stable locator: page plus chapter, chapter plus section, or audio time plus chapter.

Core-text evidence

Complete the access preflight. Compare two possible locator systems and explain which will let another reader find your evidence.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 122 · Required lesson

Map the reading load

Create a realistic schedule from verified length and available days.

Direct instruction and model

Divide total pages, chapters, or audio minutes across six reading-and-analysis sessions, reserving three sessions for synthesis and revision. Build recovery space rather than assuming identical daily speed.

Core-text evidence

Record total length, six session targets, checkpoint dates, and a recovery plan. The evaluator verifies feasibility.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 123 · Required lesson

Annotate for retrieval

Use a small code that makes evidence findable later.

Direct instruction and model

Tag turning point, question, pattern, character choice, setting pressure, unfamiliar word, and possible theme. Every note includes a locator and a brief reason—not highlighting alone.

Core-text evidence

Annotate the calibration passage, then record five core-text annotations from the opening section with locators and reasons.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 124 · Required lesson

Learn vocabulary in context

Combine context, morphology, reference checking, and sentence testing.

Direct instruction and model

A plausible guess is provisional. Separate prefix, base or root, and suffix; test the meaning in context; verify when needed; then reread the sentence.

Core-text evidence

Choose five consequential core-text words. Record context, word parts where useful, provisional meaning, verified meaning, and effect on the passage.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?

Day 125 · Required lesson

Opening checkpoint conference

Demonstrate reading through evidence rather than page claims.

Direct instruction and model

A conference tests orientation, comprehension, questions, evidence retrieval, and a next-step plan. Accessible spoken, typed, signed, or supported modes use the same intellectual standard.

Core-text evidence

Present the text and edition, locate three passages promptly, explain one developing question, correct one initial inference, and set the next reading target.

Whole-text check

  • Is the locator retrievable?
  • What happens before and after?
  • Is this evidence or inference?
  • What pattern across the book matters?
  • What resists the interpretation?
