Day 37ReliabilityEvidence Cross-Check

Can We Trust the Narrator?

Today’s Question: What evidence allows a reader to question a narrator without simply calling that narrator a liar?

Learning Target

I can evaluate narrator reliability by comparing claims with actions, omissions, contradictions, motives, and other textual evidence.

I Do · Reliability Is a Spectrum

A narrator may be accurate about events but mistaken about motives; honest but limited; self-protective; biased; confused; exaggerating; or deliberately deceptive. The analytical task is not “trust/don’t trust.” It is: Which parts of the account deserve confidence, and which require verification?

The Photograph in the Drawer, Part II

I still wasn’t hiding the photograph. Hiding means you do not want something found. I wanted Mina to have it back; I just needed the right moment to explain why I had taken it.

When she asked whether anyone had seen me near the display, I remembered the cabinet key in my pocket and moved my hand away from it.

“Sam?” she said.

“I was carrying programs.” That was true.

The leak widened. Water struck the empty frame and ran down the wall in clear lines. Ms. Ortiz told everyone to move the remaining work. I could have opened the drawer then. Instead, I waited until the room had nearly emptied.

Mina was the last person left besides me.

I unlocked the cabinet and placed the photograph on the table.

She looked at the dry picture, then at the wet wall, then at me.

“You saved it,” she said.

I nodded too quickly.

“Then why did you say you didn’t know where it was?”

I had spent twenty minutes preparing an answer to a different question.

You Do

Write 180–240 words answering: Is Sam unreliable about facts, motives, or both? Use at least three pieces of evidence and distinguish between what Sam says happened and how Sam interprets the behavior.

Prove It

Add one sentence beginning: “The strongest reason to trust Sam is…” and another beginning: “The strongest reason to question Sam is…”

Important Distinction

Bias is not automatically dishonesty. A narrator can sincerely interpret events through a distorted or incomplete perspective.
