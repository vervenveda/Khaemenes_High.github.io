Day 38StructureRL.9-10.5

Structure Changes Meaning

Today’s Question: How does changing the order of events change what the reader expects, suspects, or understands?

Learning Target

I can distinguish linear and non-linear structure and explain how flashback, foreshadowing, and pacing control information.

I Do · Structure Is Information Timing
  • Linear: events are primarily presented in chronological order.
  • Non-linear: the narration rearranges chronology through flashback, frames, jumps, parallel timelines, or delayed explanation.
  • Flashback: moves backward to reveal a past event relevant to the present.
  • Foreshadowing: places a clue or signal that gains meaning when a later event occurs.
  • Pacing: controls how much narrative time and attention an event receives.

Version A · Chronological

Sam sees the leak, removes Mina’s photograph, puts it in the drawer, lies about knowing where it is, waits for the room to empty, and finally returns it.

Version B · Non-Linear Opening

“Then why did you say you didn’t know where it was?” Mina asked.

Twenty minutes earlier, before the photograph disappeared, I had been the first person to notice the leak.

We Do

Compare the two openings. In Version A, when does suspicion begin? In Version B, what question does the reader carry into the flashback? Which version makes Sam’s earlier choices feel more consequential?

Foreshadowing Test

Return to Part I: “I had the cabinet key in my pocket. I could feel its square metal edge against my hand.” Explain how this detail functions differently before and after the reader learns Sam’s full behavior.

You Do

Reorder six key events from The Photograph in the Drawer into a purposeful non-linear sequence. Then write 160–220 words explaining why your first event should appear first and what expectation or question it creates.
