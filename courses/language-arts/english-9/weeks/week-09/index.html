Narrative Workshop

What makes a short story purposeful rather than merely eventful?

Reading: Mentor openings, dialogue passages, turning points, endings, and revision models.
Writing: Design, draft, workshop, revise, proof, and defend an original short story whose craft choices serve a purpose.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Design a short story around character pressure, consequential choice, and change rather than a chain of unrelated events.
  • Control exposition, scene, pacing, dialogue, subtext, climax, and denouement deliberately.
  • Use peer/mentor feedback as evidence for revision without surrendering authorship.
  • Apply dialogue punctuation and sentence rhythm accurately and purposefully.
  • Explain specific craft choices in a concise commentary using evidence from the student's own story.
  • Use accurately: scene, exposition, climax, denouement, subtext, revision.
  • Complete defined scored language and Unit 3 assessment evidence at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Narrative Workshop

Architecture → draft → feedback → revision → defense.

Day 1 · Story Architecture

Pressure Creates Story

Study mentor openings and turning points. Build an original story architecture around a character who wants something, encounters meaningful pressure, makes a consequential choice, and reaches a changed condition.

  • Define character want, obstacle, stakes, and pressure.
  • Sketch beginning state → escalation → consequential choice → aftermath.
  • Choose point of view and identify what information it permits or withholds.
  • Draft an opening that creates a question rather than explaining everything.
Day 2 · Scene & Dialogue Studio

Let Characters Want Different Things

Draft the central scene. Dialogue should carry action, conflict, relationship, or subtext rather than simply deliver information the characters already know.

  • Give each speaker an immediate objective.
  • Use action beats and setting details selectively.
  • Practice dialogue punctuation in context.
  • Read the scene aloud or use an approved read-aloud tool to hear rhythm and unnatural exposition.
  • Complete the scored dialogue/conventions check at 80% or higher; correct and retry below 80%.
Day 3 · Workshop & Diagnostic Feedback

Readers Report Effects

Complete the draft before workshop. Reviewers diagnose the reading experience rather than rewriting the story for the author.

  • Reviewer marks: where attention increased, where confusion began, where stakes became clear, and where pacing slowed.
  • Author asks one craft question before receiving feedback.
  • Separate reader observation from reader prescription.
  • Use Eiren only after the author's own review to pressure-test a craft problem.
  • Create a revision plan naming the three highest-impact changes.
Day 4 · PROSE Revision Laboratory

Revise Structure Before Polish

Use PROSE for a multi-pass revision. Preserve the earlier draft so substantive changes remain visible.

  • Pass 1: purpose—does the story center on meaningful pressure and choice?
  • Pass 2: structure—cut, move, expand, or compress scenes and exposition.
  • Pass 3: character/POV—strengthen motivation, information access, and consequence.
  • Pass 4: language—dialogue, sentence rhythm, verbs, details, punctuation.
  • Record at least three before → after revisions with reasons.
Day 5 · Publication, Commentary & Defense

Explain the Craft

Proofread the final story and write a concise craft commentary. Then complete the defined Unit 3 assessment evidence.

  • Identify one deliberate choice in character, structure, perspective, dialogue, or pacing.
  • Cite a specific passage from the student's story and explain its intended effect.
  • Describe the most substantive revision and why it improved the work.
  • Preserve the planning map, draft, feedback, revision evidence, final story, and commentary.
  • Unit 3 requires 80% or higher before Unit 4 progression.

Eiren · Language Arts Mentor

Eiren is a conference partner, not a ghostwriter.

The student brings a completed scene, draft, feedback question, or revision dilemma. Eiren asks questions that help the student diagnose the craft problem.

Open Eiren
Useful prompts: “Ask me questions that test whether my climax grows from the character's choices.” · “Where might a reader need more information, and where am I over-explaining?” · “Help me compare two revision options without writing the replacement for me.” Eiren cannot generate the assessed story, assign the verified score, or override the 80% gate.
