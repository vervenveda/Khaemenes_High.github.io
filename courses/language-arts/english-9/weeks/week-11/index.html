Unit 04 · Week 11

Sound, Rhythm & Form

How do sound and structure create expectation, disruption, and emphasis?

Reading: Sonnets, ballads, odes, haiku, blank verse, and free verse.
Writing: Hear and map rhythmic/structural choices, then compose and revise a poem in a deliberate form.

Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Hear and mark recurring stress, rhythm, rhyme, pause, lineation, and stanza patterns where they are meaningfully present.
  • Explain how form creates expectation and how variation or disruption can create emphasis.
  • Distinguish meter from rhythm and avoid forcing irregular poems into a false metrical pattern.
  • Analyze enjambment, caesura, stanza, rhyme, and volta by effect rather than terminology alone.
  • Use syntax, inversion, and sentence placement purposefully in original poetry.
  • Use accurately: meter, rhyme, stanza, caesura, enjambment, volta.
  • Compose a formal/constraint-based poem with craft explanation and reach 80% or higher on defined scored checks.

Five-Day Sound Laboratory

Hear → map → disrupt → compose → perform.

Day 1 · Ear Before Label

Hear the Pattern First

Read several poems aloud before naming their forms. Mark where the voice naturally stresses, pauses, accelerates, slows, or crosses a line ending.

  • Read each selection silently and aloud.
  • Tap or clap a recurring pulse only where one can actually be heard.
  • Mark pauses created by syntax and punctuation separately from line breaks.
  • Write one observation about how sound changed the experience of meaning.
Day 2 · Form & Expectation Lab

Patterns Teach the Reader What to Expect

Compare poems using different forms. Map stanza shape, rhyme or non-rhyme, line length, meter/rhythm, and any turn or volta. Then identify a place where the poet fulfills or violates an established expectation.

  • Create a structure map for two poems.
  • Use Evidence Studio to connect a formal pattern to a specific interpretive effect.
  • Distinguish observed pattern from assumed form.
  • Explain why one structural disruption matters.
Day 3 · Line-Break & Syntax Laboratory

The Same Words Can Move Differently

Experiment with enjambment, caesura, inversion, and syntax. Re-lineate the same short passage several ways and read every version aloud.

  • Complete the Rhythm Switchboard challenge below.
  • Repair ten unclear or accidental inversions/syntax problems.
  • Revise five lines so syntax and line break cooperate—or deliberately resist one another.
  • Use all six weekly vocabulary terms accurately.
  • Complete the scored language/form check at 80% or higher; review and retry below 80%.
Day 4 · Constraint Composition Studio

Form Is a Creative Pressure

Choose an approved received form or teacher-defined constraint. Draft a poem that uses the constraint as a thinking device rather than filling a template mechanically.

  • State the chosen formal constraint before drafting.
  • Draft in PROSE and preserve the first complete version.
  • Mark one place where the form created a useful discovery.
  • Revise one place where obedience to form damaged meaning or natural language.
  • Read aloud and make at least one revision based on sound.
Day 5 · Performance & Craft Defense

Score the Page With the Voice

Perform the poem and submit a concise craft explanation. Performance choices should arise from the poem's syntax, lineation, sound, and meaning—not theatrical decoration alone.

  • Annotate intended stress, pause, pace, and one tonal shift.
  • Perform live or through an approved course method.
  • Explain one formal choice and one deliberate departure/variation.
  • Use peer, teacher, or Eiren questioning to test the craft explanation.
  • Preserve structure analysis, language check, draft, revision evidence, final poem, performance notes, and reflection.

Sovereign Rhythm Game

Rhythm Switchboard

No external application is required. Students manipulate one short line to discover how structure alters emphasis.

Switch 1 · Pause

Speak the line normally, then insert a strong internal pause in two different locations. Which word gains emphasis each time?

Switch 2 · Break

Write the same words using three different line breaks. Read each version without inventing pauses that the syntax does not support. What expectation does each break create?

Switch 3 · Stress

Shift vocal stress among three content words. A partner describes how the implied meaning changes. Then decide which performance is best supported by context.

Eiren tests whether the sound claim survives the page.
