Learning Target

Poem 4 · Voice & Cadence

Write an original 16–28 line poem designed to work both silently and aloud. It must contain a meaningful turn or shift. Your lineation, punctuation, sound pattern, repetition, pace, or silence should serve that turn.

No device quota. You do not need alliteration, rhyme, caesura, metaphor, refrain, and enjambment in one poem. Choose only the craft that earns its place.

Rehearse

Select one poem from the four-poem portfolio for formal reading. Rehearse at least three times, changing only one variable each time: pace, emphasis, or pause. Keep notes about what improved and what became distorted.

Accessible Performance

The intellectual standard is interpretation through voice, timing, emphasis, and phrasing. A student who uses AAC, text-to-speech, recorded delivery, or another approved accessible communication mode may demonstrate the same interpretive decisions. The student still authors the performance map.

Do not confuse volume with expression. A strong reading can be quiet, restrained, slow, conversational, or highly rhythmic if those choices are justified by the poem.
