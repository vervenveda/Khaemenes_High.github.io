Weekly Targets

By the end of this week, the student can…

  • Compare translations through diction, image, syntax, lineation, sound, ambiguity, and tone.
  • Distinguish observable translation differences from unsupported claims about intent.
  • Use original-language evidence responsibly and treat machine translation as hypothesis, not authority.
  • Explain how performance choices create interpretation.
  • Curate and substantively revise a four-poem portfolio.
  • Analyze an unfamiliar poem independently from precise textual evidence.
  • Complete all four canonical Unit 4 components at 80% or higher.

Five-Day Translation, Performance & Mastery Laboratory

Compare → investigate → rehearse → curate → transfer.

Day 1 · Translation Microscope

Difference Before Judgment

Read two approved translations of the same poem and record observable differences before judging effect.

  • Align corresponding passages.
  • Mark diction, syntax, image, punctuation, lineation, sound, and ambiguity differences.
  • Write a provisional claim about one consequential translation choice.
Day 2 · Multilingual Inquiry

Translation Is a Series of Choices

Investigate one consequential word, phrase, image, or structural feature with appropriate language support and traceable references.

  • Use ARSHIF or approved references when context matters.
  • Use the Sovereign Interlingual Translator to generate hypotheses only.
  • Verify consequential literary choices against trusted evidence.
Day 3 · Cadence & Performance Lab

A Reading Is an Interpretation

Prepare contrasting oral or approved accessible interpretations, varying pace, pause, emphasis, phrasing, or another appropriate delivery feature while remaining accountable to the poem.

  • Annotate syntax, line breaks, punctuation, stress, and possible breath points.
  • Explain what each version foregrounds.
  • Revise the performance plan from listener evidence.
Day 4 · Portfolio Curation

Revision Across Four Poems

Use PROSE to make final substantive revisions while preserving earlier versions.

  • Revise for image, diction, sound, form, syntax, structure, or intentional deviation.
  • Complete the comparative poetry/translation analysis.
  • Prepare the selected performance piece and rationale.
Day 5 · Unit 4 Mastery Assembly

Interpret, Perform, Transfer

Assemble the four canonical components and complete fresh unfamiliar-poetry transfer.

  • The transfer poem must be unrehearsed and independently interpreted.
  • Each required component must reach at least 80%.
  • The weighted Unit 4 result must also reach at least 80%.
  • Below 80%: repair the specific missed competency and reassess with revised or fresh evidence as appropriate.

Sovereign Translation Game

Three Doors, One Line

Use a teacher-prepared source note and several plausible renderings to test what is preserved or sacrificed.

Door 1 · Literal

Choose the rendering that best preserves literal information and name what it sacrifices.

Door 2 · Music

Choose the rendering that best preserves sound, rhythm, repetition, or compactness and identify the tradeoff.

Door 3 · Context

Choose the rendering that best fits surrounding imagery, speaker, or tone and defend the choice with context.
