Compare Without Erasing the Source

How can two translations illuminate a poem without pretending they are interchangeable?

Learning Target

I can compare two authentic translations using named criteria and accurate attribution.

I Do · Attribution Before Interpretation

Record: poet, source language when known, translator A, translator B, edition/source, date if available, and access location. Translation wording belongs to the translator as well as to the source tradition.

You Do · ARSHIF Research Route

Use the Poetry Hall to locate a poem available in two lawful English translations or a dossier that identifies multiple versions. Complete the matrix and write a 250–350 word comparative paragraph.

Required claim form: “Translation A emphasizes ___, while Translation B preserves/creates ___; this difference matters because ___.”

Think About It

“More literal,” “more beautiful,” and “more accurate” are not self-explanatory judgments. What criterion would make each defensible?

Research in ARSHIF
Cultural terms require care. A translator may preserve a source-language term, gloss it, approximate it, or adapt it. Each option can reveal something and conceal something.
