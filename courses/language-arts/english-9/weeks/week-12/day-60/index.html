Part B · Comparative Translation Analysis

Write 500–700 words comparing two English translations of one poem. Use an authentic pair from the Poetry Hall when accessible. Identify translators and source information. Develop a defensible claim about at least two dimensions: image/diction, syntax/lineation, sound/rhythm, ambiguity, cultural reference, tone, or implied speaker.

Fallback: if an authentic lawful pair is not accessible, use the Day 56 course simulation and explicitly label it a simulation. Analyze versioning choices; do not claim historical or linguistic accuracy.

Part C · Formal Oral Reading

Perform one portfolio poem in approximately 2–3 minutes including a brief introduction. Submit the annotated performance map showing breath, emphasis, pace, and the poem’s turn. Your introduction should name the poem and one interpretive choice—not explain the whole poem in advance.

Part D · Portfolio Reflection

Write 200–300 words: Which revision across the four poems most changed meaning? Which performance choice most changed meaning? What do you now understand about the relationship among page, sound, reader, and interpretation?
