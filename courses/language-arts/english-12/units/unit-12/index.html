Essential Question

What body of evidence demonstrates independent readiness for college, career, civic life, and lifelong literacy?

Learning Architecture

Complete a senior capstone, curate and defend a standards-based portfolio, revise representative work, complete a cumulative final, document corrections, and prepare a postsecondary literacy plan.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English IV portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Senior capstone, graduation portfolio, oral defense, cumulative final examination, corrections, and postsecondary literacy plan.

Senior required reading · A Letter to the Future Reader

Original Academy scholarly microtext, included offline.

At the graduation conference, Noor described the reading strategies that had helped with conflicting documents. She presented annotated drafts, noted an unresolved question, and explained how she would assess claims beyond school.

Senior response: Cite two precise details, articulate a defensible thesis, develop a substantial counterinterpretation, and explain what additional sources would be needed to advance the inquiry. Revisit during Weeks 34–36; preserve your draft and reflection in the existing portfolio. Full-length literature remains part of the course.
