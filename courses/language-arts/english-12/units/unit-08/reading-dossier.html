WEEK 22 · UNIT 08

Language, Register & Institutional Power

Source A · Interview transcript

A student says, “At home I say it one way, with my friends another, and in presentations I slow down and choose words people expect. None of those voices is fake. They answer different rooms.”

Source B · Policy excerpt

A fictional internship handbook requires “professional English at all times” but does not define whether the rule concerns clarity, grammar, accent, vocabulary, dialect, or audience. Supervisors interpret the phrase differently.

Source C · Research caution

Language varieties are systematic forms of communication. A careful analysis distinguishes register shift, dialect, multilingual practice, code-switching and audience design without assuming that one community variety signals lower intelligence or weaker reasoning.

Worked synthesis

The interview frames shifting language as audience competence, while the policy uses an undefined standard that can concentrate interpretive power in supervisors. A defensible thesis can evaluate the policy’s ambiguity without claiming that every workplace expectation is discriminatory.

Independent task

Write a 500-word analysis that identifies one linguistic choice, one institutional assumption, one counterargument and one revision to the policy that improves clarity without erasing legitimate audience expectations.

WEEK 23 · UNIT 08

Visual Rhetoric, Editing & Interface

Media sequence

A fictional documentary opens with a 12-second aerial shot of a crowded shoreline, cuts to a close-up interview, overlays a statistic without naming its source, then shifts to quiet music while displaying damaged buildings.

Interface version

The same material appears on a webpage with the interview transcript, an interactive map, the unsourced statistic in large type and the methodology in a collapsed section at the bottom.

Worked analysis

The documentary controls sequence and timing; the webpage gives readers more navigation control but can still create hierarchy that privileges the dramatic number over its methodology. Visual prominence is an argument about attention even when the underlying words do not change.

Independent task

Create an evidence table for image, sound, sequence, typography, interface and source visibility. Redesign one element to improve transparency and accessibility, then explain what persuasive effect changes.

WEEK 24 · UNIT 08

Global-Issue Comparative Oral

Literary excerpt · course-created

A narrator watches floodwater reach the front step of a family home and remembers that an older relative once described the river as “the boundary that kept us safe.” The image now reverses: the boundary crosses the threshold.

Nonliterary source · course-created

A planning brief reports that three neighborhoods face increased seasonal flooding, but its map aggregates households into large zones and does not show renter displacement, insurance access or individual adaptation costs.

Global-issue boundary

A focused global issue should be specific enough to analyze through authorial and presentational choices—for example, how communities narrate risk and belonging under environmental change. The student should not claim that two texts represent all countries or communities.

Worked oral structure

Frame the issue in one sentence, analyze a precise literary choice, analyze a precise nonliterary choice, compare how each shapes public understanding, state a limitation and answer a plausible audience question.

Independent task

Prepare an 8–10 minute oral outline with exact evidence, transitions, source record, one counterinterpretation and two anticipated questions. Preserve a written transcript or equivalent accessible record.
