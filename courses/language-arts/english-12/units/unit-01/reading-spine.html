WEEK 01 · SATIRICAL ESSAY

A Modest Proposal

Author: Jonathan Swift · Published: 1729

Assigned span: Complete essay

Reading focus: satire; persona; quantitative rhetoric; ethical interpretation

Evidence task: Separate Swift's literal persona from the essay's satirical target. Audit one numerical claim for rhetorical function and explain what evidence would be needed to treat it as empirical rather than satirical.

Source custody: First published 1729. Public domain.

WEEK 02 · PHILOSOPHICAL ARGUMENT

On Liberty, Chapter II: Of the Liberty of Thought and Discussion

Author: John Stuart Mill · Published: 1859

Assigned span: Chapter II, with teacher-selected passages for close reading

Reading focus: argument architecture; counterargument; qualification; falsifiability

Evidence task: Map Mill's argument as claims, concessions, and consequences. Identify one assumption that must hold for the argument to work and one plausible challenge.

Source custody: On Liberty, first published 1859; public-domain fourth edition also available. Public-domain edition.

WEEK 03 · EXTENDED ESSAY

A Room of One's Own

Author: Virginia Woolf · Published: 1929

Assigned span: Chapter I; optional continuation into Chapter III for advanced pathway

Reading focus: method; evidence limits; narrative essay voice; authorship and intellectual independence

Evidence task: Track how Woolf moves between scene, hypothetical reasoning, evidence, and qualification. Write a method note explaining which parts are observation, inference, example, or argument.

Source custody: Hogarth Press / Harcourt, 1929. Published in 1929; entered the U.S. public domain January 1, 2025.
