WEEK 25 · UNIT 09

Search Strategy, Source Taxonomy & Method

Research problem

A senior asks: “How do public heat-alert messages affect protective behavior among adolescents?” A search for “heat warnings students” produces news stories, government guidance, school policies and research studies—different source types answering different questions.

Method contrast

A survey can measure reported behavior or attitudes; an experiment may test message framing under controlled conditions; an observational study can identify associations; an interview can reveal reasoning and experience. These methods do not license the same conclusions.

Search-log model

Record database/search location, exact query, filters, date, useful result, rejected result and next-query decision. A reproducible search trail makes research choices visible and helps identify blind spots.

Worked limitation

If the strongest study samples university students, a senior researching younger adolescents should not silently generalize across age groups. The limitation belongs in the research design, not hidden in a footnote.

Independent task

Create a methods plan and search log for your question. Identify at least four source types, two databases/archives or credible search environments, inclusion/exclusion criteria and three limitations that affect what you can conclude.

WEEK 26 · UNIT 09

Literature Review: Map the Conversation

Study A · fictional

A controlled message-framing experiment finds that concrete action steps improve recall compared with a general warning.

Study B · fictional

Interviews with adolescents suggest family routines and transportation constraints can prevent recommended protective actions even when the warning is understood.

Study C · fictional

A school-district observational report finds fewer outdoor-activity incidents after a new alert protocol, but the report cannot isolate the effect of messaging from schedule changes.

Worked synthesis

A literature review should not announce that all three sources “prove alerts work.” Study A addresses recall, Study B addresses implementation barriers, and Study C reports an association within a bundled policy change. Together they suggest that comprehension, feasibility and institutional procedure are distinct parts of the problem.

Gap/significance model

A defensible gap might be: existing evidence distinguishes message comprehension from behavioral feasibility, but fewer sources examine how adolescent transportation or family constraints interact with school alert design.

Independent task

Build a synthesis matrix organized by question, method, population, finding, limitation and relationship to other sources. Draft a literature-review section organized around themes or disagreements—not a source-by-source list.

WEEK 27 · UNIT 09

Original Argument, Citation Audit & Peer Review

Draft claim

“Heat alerts should include concrete actions because clear instructions improve safety.”

Challenge

The claim overstates the evidence if the research measured recall rather than safety outcomes. A stronger claim might argue that concrete instructions can improve message comprehension while implementation still depends on feasible options and institutional support.

Citation audit

For every sourced sentence, ask: Is the source locator traceable? Does the wording match the source’s level of certainty? Is a quotation necessary? Is paraphrase genuinely rewritten? Does the citation support the entire sentence or only one clause?

Peer-review boundary

A reviewer should identify reasoning gaps, evidence mismatches, organization problems and unanswered counterarguments without rewriting the author’s paper. Preserve the author’s decisions and document substantive revisions.

Independent task

Draft a substantial academic section with original claim, synthesis, counterargument and limitations. Run a citation audit, receive peer/evaluator feedback, and produce a revision memo naming at least three substantive changes and why they strengthen the argument.
