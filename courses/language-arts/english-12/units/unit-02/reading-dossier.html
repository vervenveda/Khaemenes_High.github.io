WEEK 04 · UNIT 02

Epic, Myth, Archetype & Allusion

Original practice foundation tale · The Borrowed Fire

A city forbade household fires during a year of drought. An apprentice baker stole one coal from the governor’s kitchen to relight the communal oven. The bread saved stored grain from spoiling, but the copied fire spread to three unauthorized hearths. Years later, public murals showed the apprentice either holding a torch above the city or hiding a coal inside a sleeve.

Senior model

Foundational stories persist partly because later audiences reinterpret them. Track action, consequence and later allusion separately. An adaptation may celebrate what an older narrative treats ambivalently, or expose costs that the heroic pattern hides.

Independent task

Pair one approved foundational narrative with a later adaptation. Record exact edition/translation and adaptation source. Analyze one retained pattern, one transformed value and one contextual reason for the change without claiming authorial intention beyond evidence.

WEEK 05 · UNIT 02

Tragedy, Character & Moral Choice

Original practice dramatic scene

JUDGE: You knew the bridge inspection was incomplete. / ENGINEER: I knew the report was incomplete. I did not know the bridge would fail. / JUDGE: Yet you signed it. / ENGINEER: I signed what they required before the storm arrived. / [Silence. The engineer removes the company badge.]

Tragic reasoning

Tragedy often resists easy moral sorting. Analyze choice under constraint, knowledge, responsibility, reversal, recognition, public/private duty and consequence. Performance choices may shift sympathy without changing the textual facts.

Independent task

Using the approved complete tragedy or substantial selection, map a character’s choices at three turning points. Include knowledge available at each point, alternatives, consequences, and one competing ethical interpretation.

WEEK 06 · UNIT 02

Comparative Foundations & Contemporary Adaptation

Adaptation problem

A modern retelling may change setting, medium, narrator, ending or social position while preserving a structural conflict. The question is not whether it is ‘faithful’ in the abstract, but what the transformation makes newly visible or less visible.

Comparative model

Organize by transformed feature: authority, heroism, gender, community, fate, evidence, voice, audience. Support claims with exact moments from both the foundational work and adaptation.

Independent task

Prepare a comparative evidence map with at least four paired moments, source custody for both works, one contextual source, one counterreading and a limitations statement. Draft a thesis about transformation rather than simple similarity.
