Unit 05

Argument, Logic, Media & Information Literacy

Compare competing arguments, test evidence, detect fallacies, interpret quantitative information, evaluate news and digital media, and construct fair, evidence-based positions.

Begin Week 13Unit OverviewUnit Assessment
OverviewWeeksAssessmentStandards

Essential Question

How can citizens distinguish sound reasoning from manipulation in a complex information environment?

Learning Architecture

Compare competing arguments, test evidence, detect fallacies, interpret quantitative information, evaluate news and digital media, and construct fair, evidence-based positions.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English IV portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Multi-source issue dossier, argument comparison, media-verification lab, deliberative seminar, and formal civic argument.

Three-Week Sequence

Read, evaluate, compose, revise, and demonstrate.
