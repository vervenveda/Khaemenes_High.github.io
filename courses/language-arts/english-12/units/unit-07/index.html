Unit 07

Modern & Contemporary World Literature

OverviewWeeksAssessmentStandards

Essential Question

How do modern and contemporary writers represent identity, memory, displacement, power, and social change?

Learning Architecture

Analyze globally diverse short fiction, memoir, essays, translated literature, and postcolonial or diasporic voices with attention to perspective, structure, historical context, and translation.

Universal Course Expectations

  • Use complex literary and informational texts from varied periods, cultures, disciplines, and media.
  • Distinguish evidence, inference, interpretation, evaluation, and synthesis.
  • Write through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing, publication, and reflection.
  • Practice oral communication, active listening, intellectual fairness, and responsible source use.
  • Preserve major products and process evidence in the English IV portfolio.

Unit Record

Length: Three weeks

Major assessment: Comparative global-literature essay and seminar connecting form, context, perspective, and universal themes.

Three-Week Sequence

Read, evaluate, compose, revise, and demonstrate.

Week 19

Modern World Short Fiction & Perspective

perspective verbs, structural analysis, chronology, and interpretive qualification

Week 20

Postcolonial, Diasporic & Displacement Narratives

How do writers represent the relationship among language, place, history, migration, identity, and power?

Open Week

Translation, Adaptation & Intertextuality

What changes when a text crosses language, medium, culture, period, or audience?

translator attribution, comparison, register, connotation, and evidence of transformation
