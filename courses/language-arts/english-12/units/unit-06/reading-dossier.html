WEEK 16 · ORIGINAL FICTIONAL SOURCE SET

The Incomplete Survey — Evidence Without Forced Agreement

Constructed senior inquiry scenario · not empirical findings

FICTIONAL CIVIC SURVEY BRIEF: In the invented township of Vale Harbor, a library committee surveyed fifty visitors who attended a weekday program. Thirty-four respondents said longer hours would help them. The committee's summary called this a mandate from “most residents.” Yet the sampling frame covered current weekday visitors, not every resident, and offered little access to people who worked when the program took place. The briefing included no estimate of the number of eligible residents or of potential nonresponse among people unable to visit.

FICTIONAL PERSONAL TESTIMONY: A resident named Nura described difficulty reaching the library after work and argued for evening hours. Another witness, Tomas, praised the present afternoon schedule because it allowed his children to attend before dinner. Neither witness could supply a complete townwide trend. Their experiences are meaningful but not interchangeable with the visitor survey. One speaks about a barrier; the other about an existing benefit. A careful writer must represent both without presenting their disagreement as a contest to determine who is telling the truth.

FICTIONAL BUDGET NOTE: The library director estimated that extending Thursday hours would require changing one staff shift, and the note asked for a review of transportation access, wage agreements and operating costs. It did not give an approved budget or predict how many new users would attend. An editorial draft announced, “The survey proves that extended hours serve everyone.” A research reviewer rewrote that sentence: “Most respondents in a limited survey favored extended hours, while other sources indicate that scheduling changes could benefit some groups and inconvenience others.” The narrower statement preserved useful evidence and made its limits explicit.

Explicit instruction

Source-based synthesis compares what different methods reveal. The survey's denominator is fifty sampled visitors, not all residents. Analyze agreement, conflict, missing perspectives, relevance, source provenance and the epistemic scope of testimony. Synthesis can establish a nuanced conclusion without forcing uniformity or averaging incomparable evidence.

Worked senior analysis

Thirty-four of fifty surveyed weekday visitors equals 68% of that survey sample; it is not a population estimate without representative sampling. A defensible synthesis joins the measured interest with Nura's access issue, Tomas's concern and the incomplete budget note, then recommends targeted outreach and transparent constraints.

Guided and independent research

  1. Develop an evidence matrix with source, population, claim, denominator and limitation.
  2. Write a 200-word synthesis organized around access and constraints, citing all three sources responsibly.
  3. Explain why neither visitor percentage nor two testimonies establishes a community mandate.

Misconception check: A synthesis does not turn a convenience sample into a representative population estimate.

Reading self-check · Week 16

Does 34 out of 50 prove 68% of all residents prefer longer hours?

Reveal the model response

No. It is 68% of the fifty surveyed visitors, with no demonstrated representativeness of the entire township.

Teacher guidance

Require attention to sampling frame, testimonial voice and cost uncertainty; do not use fictional numbers as actual library statistics.

WEEK 17 · ORIGINAL FICTIONAL SOURCE SET

The Research Prospectus — Scope, Sources and Ethical Questions

Constructed senior inquiry scenario · not empirical findings

FICTIONAL STUDENT PROPOSAL, FIRST DRAFT: “My research will prove that every community should keep public libraries open all night.” The learner, Sera, lists a fictional local news story, one opinion column and the Vale Harbor survey but has not checked the original survey method. The question already assumes the conclusion and spans budgets, worker safety, transit, disability access and public demand in every community. Sera's mentor responds that a source list without publication dates, authorship and methodological notes is not yet an annotated bibliography.

SERA'S SECOND DRAFT asks: “How might one additional evening library session each week affect access for employed adult learners in Vale Harbor during a twelve-week pilot?” The question names a population, one operational change, a location, a timespan and an outcome that can be described. It still requires defining “access,” specifying what data may ethically be gathered, and recognizing that employed learners are not the only relevant group. The project can begin as a research design without claiming that the pilot has taken place.

A RESEARCHER'S SOURCE PLAN proposes reviewing the library's actual, authorized schedule, interviewing participants voluntarily with informed consent, comparing possible cost scenarios from verified budget documents, and searching scholarly studies on library access in similar settings. The proposal records the source's author, date, method, population, relevant claim and limitations. One annotation explains why the weekday visitor survey can help formulate hypotheses but is inadequate as a townwide estimate. A second note explains that student-made mock documents must never be included as authentic primary research.

Explicit instruction

A feasible research question is focused, researchable, contestable and ethically appropriate. An annotation is more than a summary; identify provenance, method, credibility, relevance and limitation. Avoid reverse-engineering sources to prove an answer chosen in advance. Maintain a difference between a proposed method, verified evidence and speculative prediction.

Worked senior analysis

Broad thesis-first topic: 'All libraries should open all night.' Researchable question: 'What barriers and potential benefits should a twelve-week evening-session pilot measure among employed adult learners in one community?' The latter can guide data collection without presupposing an outcome.

Guided and independent research

  1. Revise the first research claim into a narrow question naming population, timeframe, variable and evidence needed.
  2. Create three **provisional** annotated bibliography records as source-search plans; do not invent published authors or URLs.
  3. Write a brief ethics note covering consent, privacy, accessibility and risk of excluding underrepresented participants.

Misconception check: An impressive list of source titles is not a credible bibliography if sources were never verified.

Reading self-check · Week 17

Why isn't a fictional practice survey an acceptable authentic citation in a serious research bibliography?

Reveal the model response

Because it is invented instructional material and cannot substantiate an empirical claim about real people.

Teacher guidance

Assess annotations for source verification and intellectual fairness. Use actual library/catalog databases if available, but keep a no-login local alternative plan.

WEEK 18 · ORIGINAL FICTIONAL SOURCE SET

Midyear Oral Defense — Revising a Claim Without Erasing Uncertainty

Constructed senior inquiry scenario · not empirical findings

FICTIONAL SENIOR PORTFOLIO EXCERPT: Sera's first essay claimed that extending library hours would unquestionably improve education for the whole township. A subsequent draft distinguished the observed interest of weekday visitors from uncounted residents, discussed two contrasting testimonials and revised the thesis into a conditional recommendation for a limited pilot. Sera preserved the old version and annotated why the change mattered: the initial conclusion had used an unrepresentative sample as though it described everyone.

DURING A MIDYEAR CONFERENCE, the teacher asks what Sera learned that would transfer to a new research topic. Sera replies: “I now ask who produced each source, who could participate, what the denominator means and which interpretation the evidence can support. I also ask which people were absent and whether my question silently assumes a favorable outcome.” A peer challenges Sera's confidence in the pilot design. She acknowledges that a proposed study has no empirical results yet; even a good method cannot guarantee its prediction.

SERA'S REVISION PLAN names three artifacts: an initial annotated reading, a midterm-source synthesis and a research prospectus. For each she identifies a concrete intellectual change rather than a cosmetic improvement. She lists future work on literature-review synthesis, proper citation, oral reasoning and accessible presentation. The course's official midterm examination is administered and evaluated separately under its existing authority. The fictional conference rehearses the process of explaining growth and gaps; it must not supply exam solutions or replace teacher review.

Explicit instruction

Senior readiness is evidenced by transferable methods, responsible source handling, independent composition and a defensible research question. A midyear conference should allow challenge and revision without converting every uncertainty into an untestable opinion. Separate formative practice, authentic portfolio evidence and the controlled midterm.

Worked senior analysis

Weak defense: 'My new thesis is right because I found studies.' Improved defense: 'My revised conclusion is proportionate to observed data and named limitations; I would need a representative design and verified source methods to make a broader claim.' A qualified response can still recommend a precise next step.

Guided and independent research

  1. Prepare a three-artifact portfolio defense identifying initial reasoning, revision and independent transfer.
  2. Record two methodological strengths, two unresolved questions and one concrete research follow-up.
  3. Practice responding to a fair objection in a two-minute oral or accessible equivalent presentation; preserve feedback in the original course records.

Misconception check: Formative reading checks and rehearsal conferences are not alternative routes to a secured midterm score.

Reading self-check · Week 18

Does admitting that a proposed study has no results undermine the validity of the research plan?

Reveal the model response

No. It accurately separates the quality of a research design from evidence about outcomes that have not yet been observed.

Teacher guidance

Evaluate senior synthesis, ethical source use and oral argument against official rubrics; preserve student ownership and existing midterm security.
