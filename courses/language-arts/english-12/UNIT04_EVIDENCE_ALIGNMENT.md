# English 12 · Unit 04 Evidence Alignment
## Rhetoric, Public Discourse & Ethical Persuasion

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 04 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Set Inquiry → Choose Method → Test Effect → Defend → Audit**

Grade 12 places the burden of inquiry, method choice, ethical judgment, audience analysis, and falsification on the learner.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 10 · Rhetorical Situation, Purpose & Ethical Appeals
1. **Set Inquiry:** Frame an inquiry about how audience, exigence, credibility, emotion, logic, and timing interact in a specific public text. Define what evidence would count as effectiveness and what would count as manipulation.
2. **Choose Method:** Select a rhetorical framework or combination of frameworks and justify why it fits the text, audience, and historical moment.
3. **Test Effect:** Test whether the persuasive strategy works equally well for intended, resistant, and unintended audiences.
4. **Defend:** Defend an ethical judgment about one persuasive choice using textual evidence and a clearly stated standard.
5. **Audit:** State the claim, method, strongest evidence, ethical standard, and one condition that would cause you to revise your judgment.

### Week 11 · Rhetorical Analysis Across Speeches & Essays
1. **Set Inquiry:** Formulate a comparative question that requires evaluating relationship, difference, and context rather than merely listing similarities.
2. **Choose Method:** Choose the comparison criteria—structure, evidence, credibility, emotional appeal, framing, timing, or another defensible set—and explain why those criteria matter.
3. **Test Effect:** Apply the same criteria to multiple texts and note where the framework produces an unfair or incomplete comparison.
4. **Defend:** Defend which text is more effective for its own rhetorical situation, not which one you personally agree with.
5. **Audit:** Revise the comparison to distinguish effectiveness, ethical quality, factual reliability, and personal agreement as separate judgments.

### Week 12 · Public Speaking, Debate & Civic Discourse
1. **Set Inquiry:** Define the public question you will address and identify what a fair, informed audience would need to know before accepting your position.
2. **Choose Method:** Choose an argument structure and delivery strategy appropriate to the audience, stakes, evidence, and likely objections.
3. **Test Effect:** Stress-test the argument against the strongest opposing evidence, not the easiest objection. Revise claims that overstate certainty.
4. **Defend:** Deliver or rehearse the argument while responding accurately and respectfully to counterclaims. Track where rhetorical force risks exceeding evidentiary support.
5. **Audit:** Complete a civic-discourse audit: strongest evidence, weakest assumption, fair representation of opposition, ethical risk, audience effect, and next revision.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 04 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 10–12. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior Unit evidence, qualification, comparison, and method-selection habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 04 methods;
- a cumulative Unit 04 transfer, performance, rhetorical defense, or audit task on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, and approved authentic works. Course-created practice texts remain clearly identified as instructional examples. Exact source, edition, translator, speaker, historical-context, and provenance records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 04 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, transfer, comparison, counterreading, composition, ethical evaluation, defense, and exit-audit prompts only.
