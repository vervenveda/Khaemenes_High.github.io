# English 12 · Unit 09 Evidence Alignment
## Advanced Research & Academic Writing

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 09 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Design Method → Map Scholarship → Identify Gap → Build Original Claim → Audit Limitations**

Grade 12 treats research as an independent scholarly process: method choice, literature review, gap identification, original argument, citation discipline, peer review, and explicit limitation analysis.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 25 · Advanced Search, Databases & Research Methods
1. **Design Method:** Define the research problem, disciplinary or interdisciplinary approach, source types, search databases, and criteria for inclusion/exclusion.
2. **Search Strategically:** Use advanced search operators, citation trails, subject headings, reference lists, and database filters to move beyond general web search.
3. **Evaluate Method:** Ask how source availability, method, database coverage, language, date range, or sampling may shape what can be concluded.
4. **Refine:** Revise the research design when the evidence landscape reveals feasibility, ethical, or scope problems.
5. **Audit Limitations:** Produce a methods note stating what the chosen design can answer, what it cannot answer, and one likely source of bias or blind spot.

### Week 26 · Literature Review & Synthesis of Scholarship
1. **Map Scholarship:** Group scholarship by argument, method, evidence base, school of thought, chronology, or debate rather than summarizing source by source.
2. **Compare:** Identify substantive agreement, disagreement, methodological difference, and unresolved tension across sources.
3. **Identify Gap:** Distinguish a genuine gap, contradiction, underexamined relationship, or unresolved question from a topic that has simply been studied less often.
4. **Test Significance:** Explain why the gap matters and what kind of evidence could responsibly address it.
5. **Audit Limitations:** Draft a literature-review synthesis that shows the state of the conversation, the proposed contribution, and the boundaries of the claim.

### Week 27 · Academic Argument Draft, Citation & Peer Review
1. **Build Original Claim:** State a claim that contributes analysis or synthesis beyond repeating the literature review and define its scope.
2. **Develop:** Integrate evidence, reasoning, counterargument, and source attribution so the paper’s logic remains visible.
3. **Peer Review:** Use criteria focused on argument, evidence, method, organization, citation, and limitation rather than surface preference.
4. **Revise:** Respond to peer review by accepting, rejecting, or modifying feedback with a reason tied to the research question and evidence.
5. **Audit Limitations:** Complete a draft audit: strongest contribution, weakest inferential step, source limitation, counterevidence, citation risk, and next revision.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 09 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 25–27. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, synthesis, source evaluation, contextualization, research, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 09 methods;
- a cumulative literary-analysis, research, synthesis, scholarly-argument, or limitation audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved research sources. Exact source, author, publication date, edition, translator, database/archive location, research method, and provenance remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 09 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds sustained-reading tracking, research design, source evaluation, lateral verification, synthesis, citation, scholarly integration, peer review, qualification, and exit-audit prompts only.
