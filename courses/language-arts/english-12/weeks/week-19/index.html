Monday · Day 1

Launch, Context & Independent Reading

Objective: Frame Modern World Short Fiction & Perspective and build the cultural, historical, disciplinary, source, and vocabulary knowledge required for independent senior-level work.

Mini-Lesson

Introduce the essential question: “How do modern writers use perspective, fragmentation, memory, and uncertainty to represent changing worlds?” Model how an advanced Grade 12 reader previews genre, context, source conditions, audience, complexity, uncertainty, and vocabulary: modernism, fragmentation, focalization, memory, uncertainty.

Guided Practice

Preview the text/source family: Modern or contemporary short fiction from multiple regions, including translated work where possible. Complete a notice–wonder–context–source routine and identify how standards ELA.12.R.1.1, ELA.12.R.1.3, CCSS.RL.11-12.3 will be demonstrated.

Independent Work

Create a context and vocabulary record, establish a reading or research plan, and write an initial 10–12 sentence response that includes a claim, evidence, qualification, and an honest uncertainty.

Evidence to Preserve

Context record, vocabulary applications, source preview, reading/research plan, and initial response

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Locate a credible comparative text, translation note, historical artifact, data source, criticism, or disciplinary perspective that complicates the essential question.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading, Evidence & Evaluation Lab

Objective: Apply advanced reading and evaluation to evaluate author choices, context, perspective, structure, and influence through precise textual evidence.

Mini-Lesson

Model a multi-pass analysis: comprehension; structure and author choices; evidence and source conditions; context and assumptions; then evaluation of function, significance, effectiveness, limitations, and alternative interpretations.

Guided Practice

Annotate at least ten purposeful details. Classify evidence by standard, function, context, reliability, and relationship to other evidence. Apply the language focus: perspective verbs, structural analysis, chronology, and interpretive qualification.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence matrix: observation → quotation, detail, or data → accurate paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance → limitation or counterreading. Rank the evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated source, evidence matrix, defensible interpretation, source evaluation, and limitation or counterreading

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Develop and test a plausible counterinterpretation, alternate line of reasoning, or competing explanation using different evidence.

Wednesday · Day 3

Senior Composition & Craft Studio

Objective: Transform reading, research, or discussion into the week’s major product: Perspective and structure analysis with comparative seminar notes.

Mini-Lesson

Analyze a mentor product for rhetorical situation or interpretive problem, controlling claim, line of reasoning, organization, evidence integration, commentary, source ethics, style, audience, accessibility, and perspective verbs, structural analysis, chronology, and interpretive qualification.

Guided Practice

Plan with a nuanced claim or controlling idea, evidence sequence, reasoning notes, counterpoint, audience and medium choices, accessibility check, and authorship or source-use record. Draft one demanding section through modeling or collaborative rehearsal.

Independent Work

Draft the major product: Perspective and structure analysis with comparative seminar notes. Highlight the strongest synthesis or evaluation, identify one gap in evidence or reasoning, and document one ethical, source, technology, or accessibility decision.

Evidence to Preserve

Plan, substantial draft, evidence integration, source or authorship record, and self-identified revision targets

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Recast a section for a substantially different audience, medium, culture, discipline, or purpose and explain the consequences of each change.

Thursday · Day 4

Seminar, Conference & Substantive Revision

Objective: Use collaborative inquiry, intellectual fairness, and revision to improve precision, coherence, evidence, style, ethics, accessibility, and audience impact.

Mini-Lesson

Practice advanced response: represent another claim accurately, identify a strength, test evidence and assumptions, ask a genuine question, identify a reasoning gap, and recommend a precise revision tied to purpose and audience.

Guided Practice

Conduct a seminar, workshop, or conference around “How do modern writers use perspective, fragmentation, memory, and uncertainty to represent changing worlds?” Use multiple pieces of evidence, paraphrase another perspective fairly, and distinguish disagreement about evidence, reasoning, definitions, values, methods, or consequences.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product substantially. Complete a revision memo documenting at least five meaningful changes, the feedback or evidence that prompted them, and the effect on the reader, listener, or viewer.

Evidence to Preserve

Seminar record, feedback, revised product, source/ethics/accessibility check, and revision memo

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Respond to the strongest counterargument or counterinterpretation and state what evidence could legitimately change the conclusion.

Friday · Day 5

Transfer, Mastery Check, Corrections & Portfolio

Objective: Demonstrate mastery on unfamiliar material, complete error analysis, preserve evidence, and decide whether to advance or repeat targeted practice.

Mini-Lesson

Review standards language, vocabulary, and a common misconception. Model corrections as reasoning: identify the tested skill, diagnose the error, cite the governing evidence or rule, show corrected reasoning, and name a prevention strategy.

Guided Practice

Complete retrieval practice and a transfer task using an unfamiliar passage, source, data display, audience, or scenario. Identify one priority before the quiz.

Independent Work

Take the weekly quiz. Complete corrections for missed items. File the strongest artifact and process evidence in the portfolio. Write a 5–7 sentence reflection connecting mastery evidence to the essential question, senior readiness, and the next goal.

Evidence to Preserve

Quiz score, corrections, transfer task, portfolio artifact, reflection, and next-step decision

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Create one rigorous original question with four plausible choices, one definitive answer, an explanation, and the standard or skill assessed.

Friday Mastery Check

Weekly Quiz

Answer every question. The best score is retained. A score below 80% signals targeted review, corrections, and a retake.
