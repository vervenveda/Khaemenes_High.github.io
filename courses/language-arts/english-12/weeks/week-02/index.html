Monday senior reading · analysis task

Day 1 · Original short-short story

The Record and Its Silence

A signed agreement stated that the dispute had been settled. No representative from one affected community had signed it. A historian could document the formal decision without treating its language as proof of universal consent.

Evidence question: Assess the difference between institutional authority and comprehensive evidence.

Monday · Day 1

Launch, Context & Independent Reading

Objective: Frame Reading at the High End of Text Complexity and build the cultural, historical, disciplinary, source, and vocabulary knowledge required for independent senior-level work.

Mini-Lesson

Introduce the essential question: “How do independent readers manage uncertainty, density, unfamiliar structures, and competing interpretations?” Model how an advanced Grade 12 reader previews genre, context, source conditions, audience, complexity, uncertainty, and vocabulary: complexity, ambiguity, inference, structure, qualification.

Guided Practice

Preview the text/source family: Demanding literature and literary nonfiction selected from the high end of the Grade 11–CCR complexity band. Complete a notice–wonder–context–source routine and identify how standards CCSS.RL.11-12.10, CCSS.RI.11-12.10, ELA.12.R.1.1 will be demonstrated.

Independent Work

Create a context and vocabulary record, establish a reading or research plan, and write an initial 10–12 sentence response that includes a claim, evidence, qualification, and an honest uncertainty.

Evidence to Preserve

Context record, vocabulary applications, source preview, reading/research plan, and initial response

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Locate a credible comparative text, translation note, historical artifact, data source, criticism, or disciplinary perspective that complicates the essential question.

Tuesday senior reading · analysis task

Day 2 · Original short-short story

A Translator's Question

One translation rendered a line as 'I remain'; another used 'I belong.' Each suggested a different relationship to place. Since the student could not inspect the original wording, she discussed the effects rather than declaring one version definitive.

Evidence question: Evaluate interpretation under incomplete linguistic evidence.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading, Evidence & Evaluation Lab

Objective: Apply advanced reading and evaluation to read independently, document uncertainty, reason from evidence, revise transparently, and preserve authorship evidence.

Mini-Lesson

Model a multi-pass analysis: comprehension; structure and author choices; evidence and source conditions; context and assumptions; then evaluation of function, significance, effectiveness, limitations, and alternative interpretations.

Guided Practice

Annotate at least ten purposeful details. Classify evidence by standard, function, context, reliability, and relationship to other evidence. Apply the language focus: precise paraphrase, inference verbs, embedded quotation, qualification, and commentary.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence matrix: observation → quotation, detail, or data → accurate paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance → limitation or counterreading. Rank the evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated source, evidence matrix, defensible interpretation, source evaluation, and limitation or counterreading

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Develop and test a plausible counterinterpretation, alternate line of reasoning, or competing explanation using different evidence.

Wednesday senior reading · analysis task

Day 3 · Original short-short story

The Unnamed Audience

A speech promised opportunity to every citizen but offered examples only from established merchants. The critic noted the contrast between inclusive language and narrow evidence while avoiding claims about the speaker's private intentions.

Evidence question: Analyze rhetorical inclusiveness and the limits of motive attribution.

Wednesday · Day 3

Senior Composition & Craft Studio

Objective: Transform reading, research, or discussion into the week’s major product: Multi-pass annotation, uncertainty log, evidence matrix, and defensible close-reading paragraph.

Mini-Lesson

Analyze a mentor product for rhetorical situation or interpretive problem, controlling claim, line of reasoning, organization, evidence integration, commentary, source ethics, style, audience, accessibility, and precise paraphrase, inference verbs, embedded quotation, qualification, and commentary.

Guided Practice

Plan with a nuanced claim or controlling idea, evidence sequence, reasoning notes, counterpoint, audience and medium choices, accessibility check, and authorship or source-use record. Draft one demanding section through modeling or collaborative rehearsal.

Independent Work

Draft the major product: Multi-pass annotation, uncertainty log, evidence matrix, and defensible close-reading paragraph. Highlight the strongest synthesis or evaluation, identify one gap in evidence or reasoning, and document one ethical, source, technology, or accessibility decision.

Evidence to Preserve

Plan, substantial draft, evidence integration, source or authorship record, and self-identified revision targets

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Recast a section for a substantially different audience, medium, culture, discipline, or purpose and explain the consequences of each change.

Thursday senior reading · analysis task

Day 4 · Original short-short story

The Hidden Denominator

An abstract announced that nine participants improved. The appendix revealed that twenty had enrolled and only ten completed the final task. The researcher asked what could be inferred about completers and what remained unknown about the rest.

Evidence question: Explain why omitted denominators affect a research claim.

Thursday · Day 4

Seminar, Conference & Substantive Revision

Objective: Use collaborative inquiry, intellectual fairness, and revision to improve precision, coherence, evidence, style, ethics, accessibility, and audience impact.

Mini-Lesson

Practice advanced response: represent another claim accurately, identify a strength, test evidence and assumptions, ask a genuine question, identify a reasoning gap, and recommend a precise revision tied to purpose and audience.

Guided Practice

Conduct a seminar, workshop, or conference around “How do independent readers manage uncertainty, density, unfamiliar structures, and competing interpretations?” Use multiple pieces of evidence, paraphrase another perspective fairly, and distinguish disagreement about evidence, reasoning, definitions, values, methods, or consequences.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product substantially. Complete a revision memo documenting at least five meaningful changes, the feedback or evidence that prompted them, and the effect on the reader, listener, or viewer.

Evidence to Preserve

Seminar record, feedback, revised product, source/ethics/accessibility check, and revision memo

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Respond to the strongest counterargument or counterinterpretation and state what evidence could legitimately change the conclusion.

At a seminar, Inez defended a thesis until a classmate introduced a contradictory letter. She kept her central question but revised the conclusion to acknowledge competing motives. Her revision recorded intellectual movement rather than defeat.

Evidence question: Defend a nuanced interpretation using the new evidence.

Friday · Day 5

Transfer, Mastery Check, Corrections & Portfolio

Objective: Demonstrate mastery on unfamiliar material, complete error analysis, preserve evidence, and decide whether to advance or repeat targeted practice.

Mini-Lesson

Review standards language, vocabulary, and a common misconception. Model corrections as reasoning: identify the tested skill, diagnose the error, cite the governing evidence or rule, show corrected reasoning, and name a prevention strategy.

Guided Practice

Complete retrieval practice and a transfer task using an unfamiliar passage, source, data display, audience, or scenario. Identify one priority before the quiz.

Independent Work

Take the weekly quiz. Complete corrections for missed items. File the strongest artifact and process evidence in the portfolio. Write a 5–7 sentence reflection connecting mastery evidence to the essential question, senior readiness, and the next goal.

Evidence to Preserve

Quiz score, corrections, transfer task, portfolio artifact, reflection, and next-step decision

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Create one rigorous original question with four plausible choices, one definitive answer, an explanation, and the standard or skill assessed.

Friday Mastery Check

Weekly Quiz

Answer every question. The best score is retained. A score below 80% signals targeted review, corrections, and a retake.
