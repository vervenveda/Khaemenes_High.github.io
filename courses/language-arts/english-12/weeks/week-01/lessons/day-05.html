Independent Scholarly Defense and Revision

Defend a defensible interpretation by articulating evidence, limitations, counterargument, and an achievable next inquiry.

Weekly Classroom & Saved WorkCourse HomePrevious Day

Read the original passage

For her final seminar, Sana placed an early paragraph beside her revision. The first had offered certainty; the second named evidence, counterexamples, and missing testimony. A classmate asked whether she now believed nothing could be known. She answered that uncertainty did not end inquiry: it made the standards of proof visible. Her conclusion identified a next question worth studying.

Annotate at least two phrases. Distinguish facts within the story from unavailable source documents.

Explicit instruction

A defense is more than speaking confidently. It explains which observations support a thesis, which alternatives were considered, and where the argument's limits lie. Intellectual humility does not require skepticism about everything. It means calibrating the breadth of a conclusion to the evidence. When a challenge reveals a gap, respond by narrowing a claim or proposing responsible additional investigation, not by claiming certainty that the sources cannot supply.

Vocabulary: scholarly defense; counterargument; qualification; revision.

Worked reasoning model

A model defense has four parts: claim (“the official account is institutionally authoritative yet incomplete”); basis (its signatories and missing representation); limitation (other consultation records may exist); and next investigation (locate contemporaneous community accounts). A critic might note that nonrepresentation alone does not prove disagreement. A strong response concedes that point without abandoning the narrower claim.

Guided practice

What part of a thesis survives after you admit an important limitation? Identify one possible objection and a reply supported by the original story.

Independent evidence

  1. Prepare a two-minute mini-seminar statement using claim, two evidence references, objection, response, and limitation.
  2. Show an initial and revised thesis side by side; annotate exactly what changed and why.
  3. Finish with a research question and two plausible source types that could answer it.

Misconception check

Acknowledging uncertainty is compatible with clearly stating what is presently supported.

Low-stakes concept check

What is an appropriate response when an objection exposes unsupported breadth in a thesis?

Reveal model response

Revise the scope of the thesis, identify the supporting evidence that remains, and record the additional evidence needed for a broader conclusion.

Unscored support; does not modify any assessment or completion records.

Teacher notes

Offer feedback on evidence, reasoning, and intellectual honesty rather than rhetorical confidence alone. Require students to retain their original draft in the existing portfolio.

Portfolio return
