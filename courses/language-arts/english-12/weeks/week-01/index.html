Open monday Deep Lesson · Model & Independent Practice

Day 1 · Original short-short story

The Definitive Record

A curator called a single official memorandum the definitive account of an old agreement. Sana examined the signatures and discovered that several affected communities had no representatives at the meeting. The document established the terms adopted by officials, but not how everyone experienced those terms. Her essay began to separate institutional power from historical completeness.

Close-reading question: Analyze the limits of official evidence.

Monday · Day 1

Launch, Context & Independent Reading

Objective: Frame Senior Diagnostic & Independent Scholar Habits and build the cultural, historical, disciplinary, source, and vocabulary knowledge required for independent senior-level work.

Mini-Lesson

Introduce the essential question: “What evidence reveals a student’s present strengths, needs, habits, and readiness for advanced senior English?” Model how an advanced Grade 12 reader previews genre, context, source conditions, audience, complexity, uncertainty, and vocabulary: diagnostic, independence, evidence, metacognition, integrity.

Guided Practice

Preview the text/source family: A high-complexity literary passage, an informational or civic passage, an informational graphic, and a short source set. Complete a notice–wonder–context–source routine and identify how standards ELA.12.R.3.2, ELA.12.C.1.5, CCSS.RL.11-12.1 will be demonstrated.

Independent Work

Create a context and vocabulary record, establish a reading or research plan, and write an initial 10–12 sentence response that includes a claim, evidence, qualification, and an honest uncertainty.

Evidence to Preserve

Context record, vocabulary applications, source preview, reading/research plan, and initial response

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Locate a credible comparative text, translation note, historical artifact, data source, criticism, or disciplinary perspective that complicates the essential question.

Open tuesday Deep Lesson · Model & Independent Practice

Day 2 · Original short-short story

The Translation Choice

A poem's last phrase appeared as 'I remain' in one translation and 'I belong' in another. One version suggested endurance; the other suggested acceptance. Keira could not consult the original language, so she refused to declare either translation uniquely correct. She compared each effect and named the linguistic evidence she still needed.

Close-reading question: Assess how translation can change interpretation.

Annotate two precise details and preserve a written response in your existing lesson workspace.

Tuesday · Day 2

Close Reading, Evidence & Evaluation Lab

Objective: Apply advanced reading and evaluation to read independently, document uncertainty, reason from evidence, revise transparently, and preserve authorship evidence.

Mini-Lesson

Model a multi-pass analysis: comprehension; structure and author choices; evidence and source conditions; context and assumptions; then evaluation of function, significance, effectiveness, limitations, and alternative interpretations.

Guided Practice

Annotate at least ten purposeful details. Classify evidence by standard, function, context, reliability, and relationship to other evidence. Apply the language focus: sentence boundaries, analytical precision, evidence commentary, academic tone, and honest self-assessment.

Independent Work

Complete an evidence matrix: observation → quotation, detail, or data → accurate paraphrase → inference → reasoning → significance → limitation or counterreading. Rank the evidence and justify the ranking.

Evidence to Preserve

Annotated source, evidence matrix, defensible interpretation, source evaluation, and limitation or counterreading

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Develop and test a plausible counterinterpretation, alternate line of reasoning, or competing explanation using different evidence.

Open wednesday Deep Lesson · Model & Independent Practice

Day 3 · Original short-short story

The Two Audiences

A public official promised a quieter square to residents and unrestricted access to merchants. The applause sounded unanimous until a reporter requested an actual delivery schedule. Each group had heard a reassuring possibility in the same words. Tomas argued that the speech's ambiguity brought listeners together while postponing a decision about costs.

Close-reading question: Identify rhetoric that conceals a policy trade-off.

Annotate two precise details and preserve a written response in your existing lesson workspace.

Wednesday · Day 3

Senior Composition & Craft Studio

Objective: Transform reading, research, or discussion into the week’s major product: Diagnostic literary analysis, rhetorical reading response, convention edit, source-use audit, and senior growth plan.

Mini-Lesson

Analyze a mentor product for rhetorical situation or interpretive problem, controlling claim, line of reasoning, organization, evidence integration, commentary, source ethics, style, audience, accessibility, and sentence boundaries, analytical precision, evidence commentary, academic tone, and honest self-assessment.

Guided Practice

Plan with a nuanced claim or controlling idea, evidence sequence, reasoning notes, counterpoint, audience and medium choices, accessibility check, and authorship or source-use record. Draft one demanding section through modeling or collaborative rehearsal.

Independent Work

Draft the major product: Diagnostic literary analysis, rhetorical reading response, convention edit, source-use audit, and senior growth plan. Highlight the strongest synthesis or evaluation, identify one gap in evidence or reasoning, and document one ethical, source, technology, or accessibility decision.

Evidence to Preserve

Plan, substantial draft, evidence integration, source or authorship record, and self-identified revision targets

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Recast a section for a substantially different audience, medium, culture, discipline, or purpose and explain the consequences of each change.

Open thursday Deep Lesson · Model & Independent Practice

Day 4 · Original short-short story

The Unreliable Abstract

A research summary claimed a new program improved literacy for every participant. A footnote showed that several students had not completed the final assessment. The author changed the abstract to describe outcomes only for those with recorded results and added an attrition limitation. The claim became narrower, but the research became more useful to readers deciding what to investigate next.

Close-reading question: Explain how omitted data changes a research claim.

Thursday · Day 4

Seminar, Conference & Substantive Revision

Objective: Use collaborative inquiry, intellectual fairness, and revision to improve precision, coherence, evidence, style, ethics, accessibility, and audience impact.

Mini-Lesson

Practice advanced response: represent another claim accurately, identify a strength, test evidence and assumptions, ask a genuine question, identify a reasoning gap, and recommend a precise revision tied to purpose and audience.

Guided Practice

Conduct a seminar, workshop, or conference around “What evidence reveals a student’s present strengths, needs, habits, and readiness for advanced senior English?” Use multiple pieces of evidence, paraphrase another perspective fairly, and distinguish disagreement about evidence, reasoning, definitions, values, methods, or consequences.

Independent Work

Revise the Wednesday product substantially. Complete a revision memo documenting at least five meaningful changes, the feedback or evidence that prompted them, and the effect on the reader, listener, or viewer.

Evidence to Preserve

Seminar record, feedback, revised product, source/ethics/accessibility check, and revision memo

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Respond to the strongest counterargument or counterinterpretation and state what evidence could legitimately change the conclusion.

Day 5 · Original short-short story

An Honest Defense

For her final seminar, Sana placed an early paragraph beside her revision. The first had offered certainty; the second named evidence, counterexamples, and missing testimony. A classmate asked whether she now believed nothing could be known. She answered that uncertainty did not end inquiry: it made the standards of proof visible. Her conclusion identified a next question worth studying.

Close-reading question: Write a claim about scholarly growth grounded in the narrative.

Friday · Day 5

Transfer, Mastery Check, Corrections & Portfolio

Objective: Demonstrate mastery on unfamiliar material, complete error analysis, preserve evidence, and decide whether to advance or repeat targeted practice.

Mini-Lesson

Review standards language, vocabulary, and a common misconception. Model corrections as reasoning: identify the tested skill, diagnose the error, cite the governing evidence or rule, show corrected reasoning, and name a prevention strategy.

Guided Practice

Complete retrieval practice and a transfer task using an unfamiliar passage, source, data display, audience, or scenario. Identify one priority before the quiz.

Independent Work

Take the weekly quiz. Complete corrections for missed items. File the strongest artifact and process evidence in the portfolio. Write a 5–7 sentence reflection connecting mastery evidence to the essential question, senior readiness, and the next goal.

Evidence to Preserve

Quiz score, corrections, transfer task, portfolio artifact, reflection, and next-step decision

Support and access options

Provide accessible text formats, audio or text-to-speech, vocabulary and context supports, chunking, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, reduced visual load, or an equivalent pathway while preserving the same reasoning and evidence target.

Extension

Create one rigorous original question with four plausible choices, one definitive answer, an explanation, and the standard or skill assessed.

Quiz Reading · Original Short Story

A Source That Disagrees

An official report described an agreement as a complete resolution. Interviews conducted later revealed different experiences of its enforcement. The interviews could not reproduce the original negotiations, and the agreement could not settle how the decision affected individuals.

Evidence question: Develop a precise claim that can incorporate both kinds of evidence without flattening their limitations.
