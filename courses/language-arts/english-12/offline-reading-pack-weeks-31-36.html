WEEK 31 · UNIT 11

Complete-Work Pattern Tracking

Long-form model

In a fictional novel, a locked garden appears in chapters 2, 8, 15 and 24. Its function changes from exclusion, to secrecy, to refuge, to a place deliberately left open in the ending.

Senior reading method

Track recurrence and transformation across the whole work. A motif claim should explain what changes in context, character relationship and structural position rather than merely counting appearances.

Worked claim

The garden’s movement from locked boundary to open shared space parallels a shift in the protagonist’s understanding of control, but the final openness may also create vulnerability rather than simple resolution.

Independent task

For the approved complete work, select one motif and one structural pattern. Build an evidence trail from at least four separated locations and identify one detail that complicates your initial thesis.

WEEK 32 · UNIT 11

Critical Lenses Without Formula

Lens problem

A critical lens is a set of questions, not a machine that produces one answer. A gender, class, historical, formalist, postcolonial or psychological lens may illuminate different evidence while also leaving other aspects less visible.

Worked comparison

A class-based reading may focus on who controls access to the garden; a formalist reading may focus on repetition, threshold imagery and structural placement. These readings can coexist, conflict or reveal different scales of meaning.

Method boundary

Do not force every detail to prove the lens. State what the lens helps explain, what evidence resists it and what a competing interpretation sees differently.

Independent task

Apply two lenses to the same passage or motif. Write a 500–700 word comparison including evidence, explanatory gain, limitation and a counterreading.

WEEK 33 · UNIT 11

Independent Seminar & Oral Defense

Seminar architecture

A rigorous seminar begins with a contestable question, uses exact textual evidence, invites disagreement, distinguishes clarification from rebuttal and tracks unanswered questions.

Leadership model

The leader should not dominate speaking time. Prepare opening evidence, two follow-up questions, one counterinterpretation and a method for returning the discussion to the text when claims become general.

Oral defense

After the seminar, defend a literary argument by explaining evidence choices, interpretive method, limitations and one point you revised after discussion.

Independent task

Prepare a seminar packet, 1,200–1,800 word literary argument or equivalent substantial draft, oral-defense outline and reflection documenting one genuine change in interpretation.

WEEK 34 · UNIT 12

Senior Capstone: Inquiry, Creation & Contribution

Capstone architecture

A strong senior capstone defines audience, need, research question or design challenge, evidence/source base, product, process record, ethical constraints and criteria for success.

Contribution principle

The project does not need to solve a huge problem. A meaningful contribution may be a well-researched guide, analysis, exhibit, prototype, public resource, creative-critical work or other approved product that serves a defined audience.

Worked model

A public-facing resource about local heat safety should distinguish research evidence, practical recommendations, unresolved limitations and source provenance rather than presenting a polished design as proof of effectiveness.

Independent task

Prepare a capstone abstract, evidence/source map, process log, audience test and limitations statement. Identify one substantive revision made because of research or audience feedback.

WEEK 35 · UNIT 12

Graduation Portfolio & Oral Defense

Portfolio principle

The graduation portfolio should show mastery and growth across reading, writing, research, speaking, revision, publication and independent judgment—not simply showcase attractive final products.

Defense structure

State a claim about readiness, present evidence, explain one revision decision, acknowledge a limitation, answer a counterquestion and connect the work to postsecondary demands.

Ethical documentation

Clearly identify collaboration, feedback, tools, source use and any generated or adapted media. Do not allow assistance to obscure authorship or decision-making.

Independent task

Prepare a standards-annotated portfolio, revised representative artifact, 8–10 minute defense outline and question bank with evidence-based answers.

WEEK 36 · UNIT 12

Cumulative Final & Postsecondary Literacy Plan

Final boundary

The cumulative final and timed writing remain authoritative assessments. This dossier offers transfer practice only and must not provide answers to the live final.

Postsecondary transfer

College, career and civic contexts require different reading speeds, source environments, document conventions and collaboration expectations. The plan should name where each skill will be used next.

Corrections model

Separate content misunderstanding, evidence misuse, reasoning error, citation/source issue, organization problem and language-control issue. A correction should name both cause and future prevention strategy.

Independent task

Complete the final and corrections record under course rules, then create a postsecondary literacy plan with three concrete habits, evidence from the portfolio, and a method for reviewing progress after graduation.
