# English 12 · Unit 07 Evidence Alignment
## Modern & Contemporary World Literature

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 07 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Frame Context → Analyze Perspective → Trace Power → Test Transformation → Audit Interpretation**

Grade 12 places contextual framing, perspective, translation, cultural power, adaptation, and interpretive uncertainty under independent learner control.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 19 · Modern World Short Fiction & Perspective
1. **Frame Context:** Identify the social, political, cultural, or historical pressure relevant to the text without assuming it fully determines meaning.
2. **Analyze Perspective:** Examine how fragmentation, memory, unreliable perspective, chronology, or uncertainty shapes what the reader can know.
3. **Trace Power:** Identify who controls narration, memory, interpretation, or silence and how that affects representation.
4. **Test:** Develop an alternative reading that treats ambiguity as structural rather than accidental. Identify what evidence separates the readings.
5. **Audit Interpretation:** State the interpretation, strongest evidence, contextual dependency, unresolved ambiguity, and one condition that would change your reading.

### Week 20 · Postcolonial, Diasporic & Displacement Narratives
1. **Frame Context:** Identify the specific histories of migration, colonialism, displacement, language, or identity relevant to the text; avoid treating these categories as interchangeable.
2. **Analyze Perspective:** Examine who speaks, who is spoken for, and how language or narrative position mediates belonging and exclusion.
3. **Trace Power:** Track how institutions, borders, memory, language, class, race, or cultural authority shape character choices and representation.
4. **Counter:** Test a reading centered on individual psychology against one centered on historical or structural power. Where does each overreach?
5. **Audit Interpretation:** Produce a calibrated claim that distinguishes textual evidence, historical context, theoretical inference, and uncertainty.

### Week 21 · Translation, Adaptation & Intertextuality
1. **Frame Relationship:** Define the relationship among source text, translation, adaptation, allusion, or intertext before evaluating what changed.
2. **Analyze Transformation:** Identify what is preserved, revised, omitted, amplified, or resisted across language, medium, culture, period, or audience.
3. **Trace Power:** Ask who gains or loses interpretive authority in the transformation and what cultural assumptions travel with the new version.
4. **Test:** Distinguish evidence of influence from resemblance or coincidence. Identify what would count as stronger proof of relationship.
5. **Audit Interpretation:** Conclude with a defensible transformation claim, its strongest evidence, its cultural or linguistic limitation, and what remains unknowable.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 07 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 19–21. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, synthesis, interpretation, rhetoric, research, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 07 methods;
- a cumulative research, comparative, contextual, translation, or interpretation audit on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, approved authentic works, and approved research sources. Exact source, author, publication date, edition, translator, archive/database location, historical context, and provenance remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 07 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, research, lateral reading, ambiguity testing, contextualization, cross-cultural comparison, translation/intertextual analysis, synthesis, and exit-audit prompts only.
