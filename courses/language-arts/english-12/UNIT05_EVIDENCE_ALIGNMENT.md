# English 12 · Unit 05 Evidence Alignment
## Argument, Logic, Media & Information Literacy

**Upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Unit 05 propagation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade-level rhythm

**Define Claim → Verify Evidence → Stress-Test Logic → Audit Media → Calibrate Judgment**

Grade 12 places source verification, quantitative reasoning, logical validity, media context, uncertainty, and intellectual fairness under the learner’s direct control.

## Three-week architecture

### Week 13 · Argument Architecture: Claims, Evidence & Warrants
1. **Define Claim:** State the argument’s main claim, subordinate claims, warrants, and scope conditions. Distinguish what is asserted from what must be true for the reasoning to work.
2. **Verify Evidence:** Check whether each major piece of evidence is relevant, sufficient, and actually supports the claim being made.
3. **Stress-Test Logic:** Identify one inferential step that could fail. Test the argument under a changed assumption, missing datum, or alternative causal explanation.
4. **Counter:** Construct the strongest logically coherent counterargument using the same evidence set where possible.
5. **Calibrate Judgment:** Rate the argument’s validity, evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, and scope separately rather than collapsing them into a single agree/disagree judgment.

### Week 14 · Fallacies, Counterargument & Intellectual Fairness
1. **Define Claim:** Identify the exact proposition under dispute before naming any fallacy. Separate a flawed inference from a conclusion that is merely unpopular.
2. **Verify Evidence:** Check whether the supposed counterevidence is relevant, representative, current, and proportionate to the claim it challenges.
3. **Stress-Test Logic:** Test for common failures such as false dilemma, causal overreach, equivocation, hasty generalization, cherry-picking, or circularity using the actual reasoning chain.
4. **Counter:** Steelman the opposing position before critiquing it. Record what evidence would genuinely strengthen that side.
5. **Calibrate Judgment:** Write a fairness audit: strongest opposing point, strongest reply, remaining uncertainty, and any claim you narrowed after testing.

### Week 15 · Media Literacy, Misinformation & Quantitative Evidence
1. **Define Claim:** Extract the exact factual or causal claim from a news item, post, image, chart, or video before evaluating the surrounding presentation.
2. **Verify Evidence:** Trace source, date, author or institution, original data or document, and whether the media item accurately represents the primary evidence.
3. **Stress-Test Logic:** Check denominators, baselines, sample size, axes, correlation/causation, omitted context, and whether visual framing changes the apparent conclusion.
4. **Audit Media:** Compare at least two independent representations of the same claim. Separate verified fact, interpretation, uncertainty, and unsupported amplification.
5. **Calibrate Judgment:** Issue a verification verdict with confidence level, strongest supporting evidence, strongest unresolved concern, and what new evidence would change the verdict.

## Spacing and retrieval

The Unit 05 evidence layer uses a 15-day map across Weeks 13–15. Retrieval deliberately returns to:
- prior-unit evidence, interpretation, structure, rhetoric, and qualification habits;
- prior-day concepts;
- approximately one-week-delayed concepts;
- earlier Unit 05 methods;
- a cumulative Unit 05 defense, deliberation, verification, or calibrated-judgment task on the final instructional day.

Each prompt is tied to the actual intellectual work of the day rather than a generic recall routine.

## Reading/source custody

Required reading remains in the existing unit/week pages, reading dossier, locally embedded Academy texts, and approved authentic works. Course-created practice texts remain clearly identified as instructional examples. Exact source, edition, author/speaker, publication date, historical context, data provenance, and translation records remain required where applicable.

## Mastery boundary

The Unit 05 evidence layer:
- writes no learner data;
- changes no storage keys;
- changes no quiz answers or thresholds;
- changes no assessment authority;
- does not convert completion into mastery;
- adds retrieval, transfer, fair representation, counterargument, verification, ethical evaluation, performance, deliberation, and exit-audit prompts only.
