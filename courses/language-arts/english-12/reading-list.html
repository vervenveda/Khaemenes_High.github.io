Flexible · Rigorous · Globally Inclusive

Senior Reading & Source Selection Guide

Select demanding, age-appropriate texts that satisfy the advanced skills while representing varied periods, regions, cultures, genres, viewpoints, disciplines, and media.

The course does not impose one commercial anthology, political viewpoint, religious viewpoint, or exclusive canon. The instructor must document the chosen texts, complexity, context, accessibility, copyright status, alternatives, and relationship to the weekly standards.

Recommended Full-Length Minimum

  • One foundational epic, tragedy, sacred/classical narrative, or substantial equivalent
  • One complete drama from a different period or cultural tradition
  • One modern or contemporary world novel, memoir, or narrative nonfiction work
  • One independently selected long-form novel or drama for senior seminar

A substantial collection or approved accessible equivalent may substitute when documented, while preserving the same analytical demand.

Global & Historical Breadth

  • Texts from at least three historical periods
  • Authors or traditions connected to at least three countries or regions and at least two continents
  • At least two works in translation or studied with translation context
  • Indigenous, diasporic, postcolonial, multilingual, or otherwise globally situated perspectives
  • Meaningful competing interpretations and viewpoints

Required Forms

  • Short fiction, poetry, complete drama, and long-form prose
  • Speeches, essays, public letters, civic documents, journalism, criticism, memoir, and research writing
  • Scientific, technical, historical, economic, environmental, health, or social-science texts
  • Visual, film, audio, digital, and quantitative sources
  • Primary, secondary, scholarly, archival, and public-facing sources

Complexity & Independence

  • Include literature and literary nonfiction at the high end of the Grade 11–CCR band
  • Use qualitative complexity: structure, language, knowledge demands, ambiguity, and purpose
  • Increase independent reading, annotation, scheduling, and seminar responsibility across the year
  • Provide accessible formats without lowering the reasoning and evidence target
  • Document supports and gradual release of scaffolding

Source & Media Integrity

  • Verify authorship, publication, provenance, expertise, evidence, funding, date, and corrections
  • Teach lateral reading and corroboration
  • Interpret scale, variables, uncertainty, and limitations in graphics and data
  • Distinguish quotation, paraphrase, summary, synthesis, and original contribution
  • Document permissions, licenses, citations, and responsible technology or AI use

Selection Review

  • Standards and essential-question fit
  • Literary, rhetorical, disciplinary, and cultural value
  • Complexity, maturity, accessibility, and student readiness
  • Historical and cultural context; avoidance of tokenism or unsupported generalization
  • Family, school, umbrella, scholarship, and jurisdiction requirements
  • Alternative pathways that retain equivalent rigor
