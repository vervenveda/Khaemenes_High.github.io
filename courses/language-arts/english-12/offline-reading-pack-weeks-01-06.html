Week 1

Day 1 · Original short-short story

The Definitive Record

A curator called a single official memorandum the definitive account of an old agreement. Sana examined the signatures and discovered that several affected communities had no representatives at the meeting. The document established the terms adopted by officials, but not how everyone experienced those terms. Her essay began to separate institutional power from historical completeness.

Close-reading question: Analyze the limits of official evidence.

The Translation Choice

A poem's last phrase appeared as 'I remain' in one translation and 'I belong' in another. One version suggested endurance; the other suggested acceptance. Keira could not consult the original language, so she refused to declare either translation uniquely correct. She compared each effect and named the linguistic evidence she still needed.

Close-reading question: Assess how translation can change interpretation.

Each group had heard a reassuring possibility in the same words. Tomas argued that the speech's ambiguity brought listeners together while postponing a decision about costs.

Day 4 · Original short-short story

The Unreliable Abstract

A research summary claimed a new program improved literacy for every participant. A footnote showed that several students had not completed the final assessment. The author changed the abstract to describe outcomes only for those with recorded results and added an attrition limitation. The claim became narrower, but the research became more useful to readers deciding what to investigate next.

Close-reading question: Explain how omitted data changes a research claim.

An Honest Defense

For her final seminar, Sana placed an early paragraph beside her revision. The first had offered certainty; the second named evidence, counterexamples, and missing testimony. A classmate asked whether she now believed nothing could be known. She answered that uncertainty did not end inquiry: it made the standards of proof visible. Her conclusion identified a next question worth studying.
