WEEK 07 · UNIT 03

Poetic Speaker, Situation & Form

Original practice poem

The station clock refuses / to choose a side of midnight. / I hold one ticket / printed with yesterday’s date / and wait for a train / whose name keeps changing.

Senior model

Distinguish poet from speaker. Identify situation, audience, form, lineation and structural turn before assigning theme. Form is not a container added after meaning; it shapes pacing, emphasis and ambiguity.

Independent task

Compare two approved authentic poems from different periods/traditions, including a translation when appropriate. Record poet, translator, edition/source and exact poem. Analyze speaker, situation, form and one structural turn.

Return to Week 07 classroom and saved work

WEEK 08 · UNIT 03

Figurative Language, Sound, Ambiguity & Image

Original practice poem

Rain writes one sentence / across the warehouse roof— / too loud to quote, / too steady to ignore.

Ambiguity model

An image can support more than one interpretation, but not every interpretation is equally supported. Sound, syntax, line break and figurative pattern create constraints. A strong reading names both evidence and uncertainty.

Independent task

Perform an unseen-poem analysis on an approved poem. Build an evidence matrix with image, sound, syntax, ambiguity and counterinterpretation. State what the text supports and what remains unresolved.

Return to Week 08 classroom and saved work

WEEK 09 · UNIT 03

Major Poets in Historical Context

Context boundary

Historical context should illuminate a poem rather than replace close reading. A date, movement or political event is useful only when a credible source connects it to the language, publication conditions or interpretive problem.

Worked model

Do not write ‘the poem means X because the poet lived during Y.’ Instead: identify a textual feature, add verified context, explain what the context helps clarify, then return to the poem and acknowledge what the context cannot prove.

Independent task

Compare two approved poems by major poets from different periods or regions. Record source custody and one credible contextual source for each. Draft a comparative claim that integrates—but does not overstate—historical context.

WEEK 10 · UNIT 04

Rhetorical Situation, Purpose & Ethical Appeals

Original public statement

We can publish the full inspection report today, including names of witnesses, or release a redacted version while privacy review continues. Transparency matters; so does the safety of people who gave evidence under confidentiality.

Senior model

Ethical persuasion requires more than effectiveness. Analyze accuracy, omission, audience vulnerability, evidence quality, privacy, uncertainty and the consequences of framing.

Independent task

Use an approved speech, letter, testimony or civic document. Record full source custody, then evaluate purpose, audience, evidence, ethical appeal and one legitimate competing obligation.

Return to Week 10 classroom and saved work

WEEK 11 · UNIT 04

Rhetorical Analysis Across Texts

Comparative problem

Two speakers advocate the same policy. One addresses lawmakers with cost and legal precedent; another addresses affected families with testimony and practical consequences.

Model

Cross-text rhetorical analysis should compare choices in relation to audience and occasion, not rank texts by which uses more devices. Ask why different strategies are reasonable—or misleading—under different constraints.

Independent task

Compare two approved speeches/essays with differing audiences or occasions. Build a strategy/audience/evidence/ethics matrix and draft a timed rhetorical analysis with one counterinterpretation.

Return to Week 11 classroom and saved work

WEEK 12 · UNIT 04

Public Speaking, Debate & Civic Discourse

Practice issue brief

A fictional city proposes closing one downtown street to private cars on weekends. Evidence includes pedestrian counts, emergency-access concerns, business surveys with mixed response rates, and transit-capacity limits.

Civic model

Responsible public discourse distinguishes facts, projections, values and assumptions. Debate should answer the strongest opposing evidence and state what additional information could change a recommendation.

Independent task

Prepare an evidence-based speech or debate response using an approved real issue brief or transparent classroom simulation. Record source custody, identify uncertainty, anticipate questions, and write a revision memo after the question period.
