# English 12 · Unit 01 Evidence Alignment
## Senior Scholar Foundations & Independent Literacy

**Audit/upgrade:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Canonical Grade 12 implementation of the Khaemenes Academy Evidence-Based English Architecture v1.

## Grade 12 instructional difference

English 12 preserves the stable Academy cognitive rhythm while shifting planning authority to the learner. Seniors are expected to set subgoals, choose analytical or research methods, justify source and evidence decisions, distinguish known from unknown, state what evidence would change a conclusion, document authorship/tool use, and defend revision choices.

The stable senior rhythm is:

**Retrieve → Set Goal → Choose Method → Test Evidence → Qualify → Defend → Audit → Reflect**

## Fifteen-step senior retrieval map

1. W1D1: retrieve prior qualification/source-scope habits; set an inquiry goal for official authority vs representation.
2. W1D2: retrieve source limits; choose a method for evaluating translation uncertainty without inventing source-language facts.
3. W1D3: retrieve ambiguity/audience; define what evidence would make a policy claim operationally testable.
4. W1D4: retrieve scope/denominator; determine what statistical claim is supportable and what causal claim is not.
5. W1D5: retrieve all four prior methods; defend a thesis, answer an objection, preserve uncertainty, propose next sources.
6. W2D1: delayed retrieval of source authority; identify which method best addresses a complex archival record.
7. W2D2: retrieve translation limits; choose evidence needed to distinguish ambiguity from error.
8. W2D3: retrieve audience/operational definition; evaluate an unnamed or underspecified audience.
9. W2D4: retrieve denominator/attrition; audit a quantitative claim and propose a stronger comparison design.
10. W2D5: cumulative transfer; revise an interpretation after evidence changes and state what would still alter the conclusion.
11. W3D1: retrieve statistical/source audit habits; verify an edited abstract against its underlying evidence.
12. W3D2: retrieve primary-source verification; audit a digital summary and document correction.
13. W3D3: retrieve syntactic qualification; preserve conditional scope and modality.
14. W3D4: retrieve translation humility; compare alternatives while naming missing linguistic evidence.
15. W3D5: cumulative senior audit; defend a revised synthesis, authorship record, verification trail, uncertainty statement, and next research target.

## Senior autonomy requirements

A senior evidence prompt should increasingly require the learner to decide:
- what question matters;
- what method is appropriate;
- what source has authority for which claim;
- what evidence is missing;
- what would change the conclusion;
- what part of the work was assisted by a tool;
- what verification was performed;
- why the final revision is more defensible.

Supports remain available, but they are optional pathways rather than the default intellectual script.

## Mastery boundary

Completion controls record workflow only. Objective checks support retrieval and correction. Senior mastery requires the actual analytical/research artifact, source-use or authorship record where relevant, meaningful revision, and evaluator judgment.

## Promotion criterion

The senior blueprint is ready when all fifteen instructional days:
1. begin with retrieval without notes;
2. require a learner-set goal or methodological choice;
3. distinguish source authority from claim scope;
4. require explicit uncertainty or falsification conditions;
5. preserve independent evidence and revision history;
6. require transparent authorship/tool-use records where relevant;
7. end with an audit of reasoning rather than a simple confidence rating.
