English IV Teacher Guide

Deliver, differentiate, assess, and document a senior course built around independent literacy, advanced analysis, consequential communication, capstone inquiry, and postsecondary transfer.

Recommended weekly rhythm

  1. Monday: context, vocabulary, complexity preview, reading or research plan.
  2. Tuesday: advanced close reading, evidence matrix, source evaluation, counterreading.
  3. Wednesday: senior composition, research, craft, or production studio.
  4. Thursday: seminar, conference, intellectual-fairness check, and substantive revision.
  5. Friday: unfamiliar transfer task, quiz, corrections, portfolio reflection, and next-step decision.

Instructional time and credit

Plan approximately 150–180 documented hours for one full high-school credit unless the student’s jurisdiction or program requires another measure. Record direct instruction, supervised workshop, seminar, assessment, research, presentation, and qualifying independent reading according to local rules.

Assessment model

  • Weekly quizzes: 25%
  • Unit assessments: 25%
  • Major projects and portfolio products: 25%
  • Midterm: 10%
  • Final: 15%

Automatically scored questions are mastery checks, not substitutes for teacher-scored analysis, research, narrative, oral communication, capstone work, and portfolio evidence.

Advanced senior expectations

  • Independent reading at the upper end of secondary complexity
  • Nuanced theses, qualified claims, counterinterpretations, and limitations
  • Literary, rhetorical, visual, quantitative, and cross-text evidence
  • Multiple sources and competing scholarly or public interpretations
  • Sustained research, academic integrity, transparent authorship, and responsible technology use
  • Long-form reading, timed writing, seminar leadership, accessible publication, capstone, and oral defense

Mastery and retakes

The portal retains best quiz and assessment scores. Require corrections and targeted review before a retake. Preserve the original attempt, correction reasoning, revised work, and best score so progress is visible rather than erased.

Required records

  • Annual calendar and instructional-time record
  • Approved reading and source list
  • Reading log and independent seminar record
  • Gradebook and scoring rubrics
  • Drafts, feedback, revisions, and source-use records
  • Research paper and capstone process evidence
  • Speaking, multimedia, portfolio, and oral-defense evidence
  • Midterm, final, corrections, completion certificate, and transcript support

Access and differentiation

Use accessible text formats, audio, chunking, vocabulary and context supports, guided annotation, oral rehearsal, assistive technology, extended time, alternative response modes, or reduced visual load while preserving the same reasoning, evidence, and communication target.

Advanced extensions

Use additional long-form texts, independent criticism, original data collection where ethical, public presentation, publication, AP-style timed analysis, or interdisciplinary capstone work. Do not label the course AP, IB, Cambridge, dual-enrollment, or accredited without separate authorization.
