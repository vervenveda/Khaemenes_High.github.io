How We Study History

Students are taught to distinguish evidence from interpretation, ideals from implementation, correlation from causation, and similarity from equivalence. Difficult histories are not erased because they are uncomfortable. Claims are examined through identifiable sources, chronology, geography, competing perspectives, uncertainty, and corroboration.

Across the four years, the pathway moves from foundational historical inquiry to independent research and public-facing argument. Mastery is demonstrated through knowledge, evidence analysis, geographic and quantitative reasoning, comparison, causation, and written argument—not vocabulary recognition alone.

The four grade-level doors above are the department's canonical student entry points. Electives and interdisciplinary studies have their own hall so the core pathway remains clear.
