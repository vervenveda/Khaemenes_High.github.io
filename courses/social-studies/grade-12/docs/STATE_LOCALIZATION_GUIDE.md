# Grade 12 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule
Grade 12 Global Politics, International Relations & Senior Research Honors is nationally/globally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. The comparative-politics, international-relations, international-law, human-rights, development, security, technology, public-health, research, and capstone spine remains canonical.

Localization changes or adds jurisdiction-specific evidence; it does not lower the Academy's 80% mastery requirements or replace the global core.

## Required state/local overlays
- Compare the learner's **state constitution, legislature, executive institutions, courts, and local governments** with federal and international cases when institutionally relevant.
- Use the state's **election administration, ballot-access rules, districting/representation structures, public records, and official election data** for nonpartisan methods work where appropriate.
- Include **state/local economic, demographic, labor, migration, environmental, public-health, infrastructure, and budget data** in selected data laboratories.
- Map jurisdiction-specific **required instruction and civic-literacy requirements** into the existing course rather than creating a competing course sequence.
- Permit the senior capstone to use **state/local policy, institutions, archives, courts, agencies, or public data** when the research question is academically suitable and nonpartisan.

## What must not change
The 36-week senior sequence, 180 lessons/equivalent competencies, 108 principal assignments, weekly 80% mastery, semester/overall 80% requirements, major-assessment thresholds, ten capstone checkpoints, thesis, oral defense, final, academic-integrity review, and evaluator verification remain Academy requirements.

## Safeguards
Localization must remain nonpartisan, evidence-based, and privacy-protective. Do not require disclosure of political affiliation, immigration/citizenship status, religion, health information, family finances, or other sensitive personal information. Use official/public records and de-identified or teacher-curated data where possible.
