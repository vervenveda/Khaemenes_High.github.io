## Week 25 · Protection, migration and statelessness
**1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees, Article 1(A)(2)** — Defines refugee status with conditions concerning persecution and location outside country of nationality or former habitual residence. **Article 33** — establishes the principle of non-refoulement, subject to the instrument's text and legal qualifications.
Source: UNHCR https://www.unhcr.org/refugee-convention
Exercise: Compare a fictional applicant, an internally displaced citizen and an economic migrant; distinguish legal definitions from a decision on any individual case. Assess evidence needed before classification.

## Week 26 · Development and inequality
**UNDP Human Development Index** — Combines indicators of long and healthy life, knowledge and a decent standard of living. It is a summary measure, not a direct test of political freedom, within-country inequality or environmental impacts.
Source: UNDP https://hdr.undp.org/data-center/human-development-index
Exercise: Explain why higher average income with lower schooling or life expectancy can complicate a development ranking. State limitations and the need for disaggregated statistics.

## Week 27 · Trade and sovereign debt
**WTO Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World Trade Organization (1994)** — Founding treaty that provides an institutional framework for the multilateral trading system; its annexed agreements and dispute-settlement arrangements require distinct examination. A WTO commitment is not inferred solely from the word 'free trade'.
Source: WTO https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/04-wto_e.htm
Exercise: Compare the legal scope of a fictional tariff claim with the actual instrument's institutional role. Separately evaluate household impacts and supply-chain risks.

## Week 28 · Climate and environmental justice
**Paris Agreement (2015), Articles 2, 4 and 7** — Addresses temperature goals, nationally determined contributions and adaptation. National targets, delivered emissions changes, and communities' climate vulnerability are distinct empirical matters.
Source: UNFCCC https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement
Exercise: Compare a fictional pair of mitigation policies with different distributional impacts and an adaptation plan, distinguishing the agreement's goals from observed national outcomes.
