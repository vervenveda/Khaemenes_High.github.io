# Khaemenes Academy · Grade 12 · Senior capstone source and evidence dossier · Weeks 33–36

This locally readable Academy guide cites authentic foundational sources. It is not a substitute for the full original instruments or for the student's own independently reviewed primary-source record.

## Week 33 — Evidence and findings
**United Nations Charter (1945), Article 18**: important General Assembly questions have a two-thirds majority rule among members present and voting. Research task: compute an illustrative vote result, state the denominator, then examine how procedural definitions affect an empirical findings table. Source: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text

## Week 34 — Thesis and independent review
**Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), Articles 7 and 10**: addresses equality before the law and rights related to a fair hearing. Research task: distinguish provisions in the source from observed legal access; specify competing findings, methodology, data limits and a genuine counterargument. Source: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

## Week 35 — Public communication
**United Nations Charter (1945), Article 33**: identifies peaceful methods for settlement of disputes. Public briefing task: explain at least two methods and their legal/institutional limitations using accessible, nonpartisan language; explicitly differentiate text, inference and advocacy. Source: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text

## Week 36 — Final defense and academic integrity
**International Court of Justice Statute (1945), Article 36**: contentious jurisdiction depends on specific legal bases and conditions; a claim about a treaty does not alone establish that the ICJ has jurisdiction. Defense task: substantiate a claim, state an alternative interpretation, cite a primary text, and answer the examiner's methodological question. Source: https://www.icj-cij.org/statute

## Required evidence convention
For each final work product: Academy heading; student name/date (kept within private learner records, not this shared dossier); grade/week/lesson; inquiry; provenance and citations; vocabulary; calculations and observations; reasoned claim, limitations and counterclaim; cross-disciplinary reflection; and educator review. A machine-graded weekly quiz does not replace human evaluation of a thesis or oral defense.

These are original educational paraphrases. The primary texts are not reproduced or certified offline as complete documents.
