# National Portability Crosswalk

## Purpose
This document explains why the course can be reviewed as a rigorous one-credit senior social-studies course across jurisdictions. It does not guarantee acceptance by every state, district, school, college, scholarship, or accreditor.

## Portable evidence
| Review concern | Course evidence |
|---|---|
| Recognizable credit | 36-week, 1.0-credit honors course with 150 documented hours |
| Academic scope | Comparative government, international relations, law, rights, economics, geography, global issues, and research |
| Reading and writing | Complex source analysis; argument, explanatory writing, sustained research, citation, revision |
| Quantitative literacy | Election, survey, development, trade, debt, conflict, climate, and public-health data |
| Civic learning | Institutions, rights, participation, engagement, public communication, and nonpartisan deliberation |
| Assessment | 36 mastery quizzes, 108 assignments, major performances, two cumulative exams, thesis, defense |
| Mastery | Weekly 80% threshold; semester, overall, thesis, defense, and final minimums |
| Integrity | Source verification, plagiarism rules, data integrity, AI disclosure, oral defense |
| Verification | Attendance/hours, gradebook, portfolio, checkpoint record, evaluator sign-off, transcript record |

## National benchmark strands
- NCSS C3 Inquiry Arc: questions, disciplinary tools, sources/evidence, communication/informed action.
- Grades 11–12 disciplinary literacy: precise evidence, source integration, argument, research, revision, independent reading.
- Comparative politics: concepts, country comparison, quantitative analysis, source analysis, and argumentation.
- Global politics: power, sovereignty, rights, development, peace/conflict, and engagement.
- Research capstone: student-generated question, ethical method, sustained evidence, paper, presentation, oral defense.

## Local review checklist
Before issuing or transferring credit, verify:
- current social-studies credit requirements;
- any state-mandated course or assessment;
- homeschool notice/evaluation/portfolio rules;
- minimum attendance or instructional-hour rules;
- approved course-title and coding practices;
- scholarship and college-admission expectations;
- whether an umbrella or cover school must approve the course.

Use `STATE_AND_DC_REVIEW_WORKSHEET.csv` as a review log. It intentionally does not make static legal claims that may become outdated.


## Weekly standards evidence
Each weekly record lists C3 inquiry, disciplinary civics/economics/geography/history strands, grades 11–12 literacy, comparative-government skills, research skills, and NCSS thematic strands as appropriate.


## State/localization requirement
The course must receive a state/local overlay for jurisdiction-specific government, election administration, courts, public records, economic/demographic data, required instruction, and capstone evidence. Localization cannot replace the global comparative/IR core or reduce Academy mastery requirements. See `docs/STATE_LOCALIZATION_GUIDE.md`.
