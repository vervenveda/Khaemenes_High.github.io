About Global Politics, International Relations & Senior Research Honors

This senior capstone course integrates comparative politics, international relations, global law, human rights, development, political economy, security, technology governance, and independent social-science research. Students are expected to evaluate institutions and evidence across countries and global systems, document uncertainty and source limitations, conduct sustained ethical research, and defend a rigorous conclusion in writing and through oral presentation.

Course Features

Comparative Politics & Institutions Students compare regimes, constitutions, executives, legislatures, courts, parties, elections, public opinion, civil society, media systems, and country cases without erasing historical context.
International Relations, Law & Human Rights Foreign policy, diplomacy, international organizations, treaties, courts, humanitarian principles, migration, citizenship, and human rights connect power with global legal and ethical questions.
Political Economy, Security & Technology Development, inequality, trade, debt, climate, conflict, terrorism, nuclear risk, cyber conflict, public health, AI, surveillance, and digital rights develop interdisciplinary global reasoning.
Senior Thesis & Oral Defense Students complete a sustained research project with source verification, methodology, capstone checkpoints, documented findings and limitations, a 4,000–5,000-word thesis, public communication, oral defense, and final portfolio review.
