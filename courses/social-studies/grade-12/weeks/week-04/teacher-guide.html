Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 4 · Political Ideologies and Models of Justice

Essential question: How do political ideologies define freedom, equality, authority, property, obligation, and justice?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Liberalism, conservatism, socialism, nationalism, feminism, environmentalism, religious political thought, and plural models of justice.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Selections from Locke, Burke, Mill, Marx, Rawls, Indigenous political thought, and contemporary voices. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Political Ideologies and Models of Justice and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Selections from Locke, Burke, Mill, Marx, Rawls, Indigenous political thought, and contemporary voices.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Comparative Ideology and Justice Matrix through evidence-based deliberation.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Ideology means a structured set of ideas about society, power, rights, and political change.
  2. 2. C — Liberty means freedom from unjust restraint and the capacity to make meaningful choices.
  3. 3. C — Equality means a principle concerning equal status, rights, opportunity, treatment, or outcomes.
  4. 4. A — Justice means a standard for fair institutions, rights, duties, procedures, and distributions.
  5. 5. D — Pluralism means a system in which multiple groups, values, and centers of influence coexist.
  6. 6. D — Public good means a condition or benefit understood to serve the community as a whole.
  7. 7. B — Ideologies prioritize values differently rather than simply supporting or opposing government.
  8. 8. D — The same policy may be defended through different principles.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to Selections from Locke, Burke, Mill, Marx, Rawls, Indigenous political thought, and contemporary voices, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How do political ideologies define freedom, equality, authority, property, obligation, and justice?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
