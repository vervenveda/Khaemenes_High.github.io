Week 25 · Migration, Refugees, Citizenship, and Statelessness

Essential question: How should states balance borders, sovereignty, labor needs, family unity, humanitarian protection, and equal dignity?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Voluntary and forced migration, refugee law, asylum, internally displaced persons, citizenship, statelessness, diaspora, remittances, integration, detention, and burden sharing.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Refugee Convention materials, UNHCR data, migration statistics, and national policies. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. D — Refugee means a person outside their country with a well-founded fear of persecution on protected grounds.
  2. 2. B — Asylum means protection granted by a state to a qualifying person seeking refuge.
  3. 3. B — Internally displaced person means a person forced from home who has not crossed an international border.
  4. 4. A — Statelessness means the condition of not being recognized as a national by any state under its law.
  5. 5. D — Non-refoulement means the principle against returning a person to serious threats of persecution or harm.
  6. 6. B — Remittance means money transferred by migrants to people or communities in another place.
  7. 7. A — Migrant, refugee, asylum-seeker, displaced person, and stateless person are not interchangeable.
  8. 8. A — Migration has demographic, economic, cultural, security, and human consequences.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to Refugee Convention materials, UNHCR data, migration statistics, and national policies, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How should states balance borders, sovereignty, labor needs, family unity, humanitarian protection, and equal dignity?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
