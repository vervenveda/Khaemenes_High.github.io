Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 20 · Foreign Policy and National Interest

Essential question: How should governments define national interest when security, prosperity, values, law, and domestic politics conflict?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Foreign-policy decision making, executive and legislative roles, bureaucratic politics, public opinion, strategic culture, interests, values, and unintended consequences.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Declassified decision records, executive statements, legislative debates, and outcome data. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Foreign Policy and National Interest and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Declassified decision records, executive statements, legislative debates, and outcome data.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Foreign-Policy Decision Analysis through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. C — National interest means objectives a government identifies as important to national security, prosperity, influence, or values.
  2. 2. D — Foreign policy means a government’s strategy and actions toward external actors and conditions.
  3. 3. D — Strategic culture means historically shaped assumptions about security, force, risk, and international role.
  4. 4. D — Bureaucratic politics means policy outcomes shaped by bargaining among organizations with different missions and interests.
  5. 5. D — Credible commitment means an assurance others believe will be honored despite future incentives to defect.
  6. 6. C — Unintended consequence means an outcome not sought or anticipated by a decision maker.
  7. 7. A — National interest is debated rather than objectively self-defining.
  8. 8. B — Decision makers operate under uncertainty and time pressure.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to Declassified decision records, executive statements, legislative debates, and outcome data, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How should governments define national interest when security, prosperity, values, law, and domestic politics conflict?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
