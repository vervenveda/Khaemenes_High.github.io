Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 28 · Climate, Resources, and Environmental Justice

Essential question: How can political institutions govern environmental risks that cross borders, generations, and unequal communities?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Climate science-policy interface, common but differentiated responsibilities, adaptation, mitigation, energy transition, resource conflict, environmental justice, Indigenous sovereignty, and treaty design.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to IPCC summaries, climate agreements, emissions data, adaptation plans, and environmental-justice cases. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Climate, Resources, and Environmental Justice and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from IPCC summaries, climate agreements, emissions data, adaptation plans, and environmental-justice cases.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Environmental Governance Case Study through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Mitigation means action reducing greenhouse-gas emissions or increasing removal.
  2. 2. C — Adaptation means adjustment that reduces vulnerability to actual or expected climate effects.
  3. 3. B — Environmental justice means fair distribution of environmental benefits, burdens, participation, and remedies.
  4. 4. C — Common-pool resource means a difficult-to-exclude resource vulnerable to overuse.
  5. 5. C — Intergenerational equity means fair consideration of present and future generations.
  6. 6. A — Loss and damage means severe climate impacts that cannot be fully avoided through mitigation or adaptation.
  7. 7. B — Environmental harms and capacities are distributed unequally.
  8. 8. A — Scientific evidence informs but does not determine policy choices.
  9. 9. D — Trace claims to IPCC summaries, climate agreements, emissions data, adaptation plans, and environmental-justice cases, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How can political institutions govern environmental risks that cross borders, generations, and unequal communities?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
