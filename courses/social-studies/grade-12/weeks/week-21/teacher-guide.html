Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 21 · Diplomacy, Negotiation, and Statecraft

Essential question: How can actors negotiate durable agreements when interests, power, information, and trust differ?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Diplomacy, bargaining, mediation, signaling, leverage, reservation points, issue linkage, verification, and implementation.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Treaty negotiations, peace processes, trade bargaining, and diplomatic communications. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Diplomacy, Negotiation, and Statecraft and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Treaty negotiations, peace processes, trade bargaining, and diplomatic communications.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Diplomatic Negotiation Simulation through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. B — Diplomacy means management of relations through communication, representation, negotiation, and agreement.
  2. 2. B — Reservation point means the least favorable deal an actor will accept before walking away.
  3. 3. B — Leverage means a resource or condition that improves bargaining influence.
  4. 4. D — Mediation means assistance by a third party seeking to facilitate agreement.
  5. 5. B — Verification means procedures used to determine whether commitments are being honored.
  6. 6. A — Issue linkage means connecting separate issues to create trades, pressure, or broader agreement.
  7. 7. B — Negotiation is not the same as compromise at any price.
  8. 8. B — Durable agreements address incentives, monitoring, enforcement, and change.
  9. 9. C — Trace claims to Treaty negotiations, peace processes, trade bargaining, and diplomatic communications, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How can actors negotiate durable agreements when interests, power, information, and trust differ?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
