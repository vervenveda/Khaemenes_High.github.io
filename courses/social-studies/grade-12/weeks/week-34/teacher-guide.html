Week 34 · Thesis Drafting and Peer Review

Essential question: How can revision improve reasoning, evidence, organization, fairness, and academic integrity?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Thesis architecture, literature review, methods, findings, analysis, counterargument, implications, citations, peer review, revision memo, and final editing.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Complete draft, rubric, citation record, peer feedback, and version history. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Literature review means a synthesis of relevant scholarship and evidence showing what is known and disputed.
  2. 2. A — Counterargument means a serious alternative claim or interpretation addressed by an argument.
  3. 3. A — Coherence means logical connection among question, method, evidence, findings, and conclusion.
  4. 4. D — Peer review means structured evaluation by another reader using shared criteria.
  5. 5. C — Revision memo means a record explaining changes made in response to evidence and feedback.
  6. 6. C — Citation audit means a check that claims, quotations, data, and references are accurate and complete.
  7. 7. D — Revision is a reasoning process, not only proofreading.
  8. 8. D — A thesis must distinguish evidence, analysis, inference, and recommendation.
  9. 9. C — Trace claims to Complete draft, rubric, citation record, peer feedback, and version history, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How can revision improve reasoning, evidence, organization, fairness, and academic integrity?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
