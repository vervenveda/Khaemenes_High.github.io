Week 23 · International Law, Treaties, and Courts

Essential question: How does international law create obligations without a single global sovereign?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Treaties, custom, general principles, jurisdiction, state responsibility, international courts, international criminal law, humanitarian law, and enforcement.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to UN Charter, Vienna Convention principles, ICJ and ICC materials, and Geneva law. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Treaty means a written international agreement governed by international law.
  2. 2. C — Customary international law means a general and consistent state practice accepted as legally required.
  3. 3. D — Jurisdiction means legal authority to hear a matter, regulate conduct, or enforce law.
  4. 4. D — State responsibility means legal consequences arising when a state breaches an international obligation.
  5. 5. A — Complementarity means the principle that international criminal jurisdiction supplements rather than automatically replaces national courts.
  6. 6. A — Humanitarian law means rules governing conduct during armed conflict and protection of persons.
  7. 7. B — International law relies on consent, reciprocity, institutions, domestic implementation, and legitimacy.
  8. 8. C — Legal obligation and political enforcement are distinct.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to UN Charter, Vienna Convention principles, ICJ and ICC materials, and Geneva law, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How does international law create obligations without a single global sovereign?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
