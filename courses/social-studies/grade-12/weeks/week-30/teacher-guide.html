Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 30 · Security, Terrorism, Nuclear Risk, and Cyber Conflict

Essential question: How should societies address severe security threats while preserving law, evidence, proportionality, and human rights?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Traditional and human security, terrorism, radicalization, counterterrorism, deterrence, proliferation, arms control, cyber operations, attribution, resilience, and emergency power.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Security Council records, arms-control treaties, threat assessments, court cases, and cyber incident reports. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Security, Terrorism, Nuclear Risk, and Cyber Conflict and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Security Council records, arms-control treaties, threat assessments, court cases, and cyber incident reports.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Global Security Council Simulation through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. B — Human security means protection of people from severe threats to life, livelihood, dignity, and freedom.
  2. 2. A — Terrorism means political violence targeting or threatening civilians to create fear and influence a wider audience.
  3. 3. A — Deterrence means discouraging action by credibly threatening unacceptable costs.
  4. 4. B — Proliferation means spread of weapons, materials, technology, or capabilities.
  5. 5. B — Cyber attribution means identification of actors responsible for a digital operation.
  6. 6. D — Resilience means capacity to withstand, adapt to, and recover from disruption.
  7. 7. B — Security terms require precise definitions and evidence.
  8. 8. B — Countermeasures can produce backlash, rights violations, or strategic adaptation.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to Security Council records, arms-control treaties, threat assessments, court cases, and cyber incident reports, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How should societies address severe security threats while preserving law, evidence, proportionality, and human rights?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
