Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 2 · Evidence, Methodology, and Academic Integrity

Essential question: What procedures make research credible, ethical, reproducible, and honest?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Source authority, triangulation, research ethics, citation, plagiarism prevention, disclosure of AI assistance, and data integrity.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Stanford Civic Online Reasoning, research ethics protocols, and citation standards. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Evidence, Methodology, and Academic Integrity and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Stanford Civic Online Reasoning, research ethics protocols, and citation standards.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Verified Source Dossier and Academic Integrity Agreement through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. D — Authority means the expertise, responsibility, or firsthand access that makes a source relevant.
  2. 2. D — Corroboration means independent evidence that supports, qualifies, or contradicts a claim.
  3. 3. A — Triangulation means using different sources or methods to examine the same question.
  4. 4. D — Plagiarism means presenting another person’s words, ideas, or work as one’s own.
  5. 5. C — Fabrication means inventing evidence, data, sources, quotations, or results.
  6. 6. B — Disclosure means a transparent statement of assistance, methods, conflicts, or limitations.
  7. 7. A — Credibility depends on authority, method, evidence, transparency, and corroboration rather than appearance alone.
  8. 8. C — Primary sources are not automatically accurate or complete; they require context and evaluation.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to Stanford Civic Online Reasoning, research ethics protocols, and citation standards, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. B — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

What procedures make research credible, ethical, reproducible, and honest?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
