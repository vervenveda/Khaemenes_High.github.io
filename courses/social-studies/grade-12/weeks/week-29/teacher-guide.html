Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 29 · War, Civil Conflict, and Peacebuilding

Essential question: Why do conflicts begin, persist, end, and sometimes recur?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Interstate war, civil war, grievance, opportunity, security dilemmas, commitment problems, identity, external intervention, mediation, ceasefires, peace agreements, justice, and reconstruction.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Peace agreements, conflict datasets, mediation records, and post-conflict evaluations. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of War, Civil Conflict, and Peacebuilding and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Peace agreements, conflict datasets, mediation records, and post-conflict evaluations.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Conflict Early-Warning and Peacebuilding Analysis through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. C — Civil war means sustained organized armed conflict within a state.
  2. 2. D — Commitment problem means inability to credibly promise future compliance when power or incentives may change.
  3. 3. B — Ceasefire means an agreement to halt fighting, temporarily or permanently.
  4. 4. D — Peacebuilding means long-term work to address institutions and relationships that sustain peace.
  5. 5. B — Spoiler means an actor using violence or obstruction to undermine a peace process.
  6. 6. B — Reconciliation means processes seeking to rebuild relationships, acknowledgment, and coexistence after harm.
  7. 7. B — Conflict explanations should not reduce violence to ancient hatred.
  8. 8. A — Peace agreements must address security, institutions, resources, and implementation.
  9. 9. D — Trace claims to Peace agreements, conflict datasets, mediation records, and post-conflict evaluations, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

Why do conflicts begin, persist, end, and sometimes recur?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
