Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 33 · Capstone Evidence and Findings

Essential question: Can the student transform a documented body of evidence into transparent, appropriately qualified findings?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Coding, quantitative and qualitative analysis, pattern identification, triangulation, negative cases, uncertainty, tables, figures, findings, and limitations.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Student evidence corpus, analysis log, data tables, and methods record. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Capstone Evidence and Findings and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Student evidence corpus, analysis log, data tables, and methods record.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Capstone Findings and Limitations Portfolio through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. B — Finding means a conclusion directly derived from analyzed evidence.
  2. 2. C — Coding means systematic labeling of qualitative evidence for comparison and analysis.
  3. 3. D — Negative case means evidence that does not fit an emerging explanation.
  4. 4. B — Sensitivity analysis means testing whether a finding changes under alternative assumptions or measures.
  5. 5. D — Confidence means the justified degree of certainty attached to a conclusion.
  6. 6. B — Research limitation means a boundary or weakness affecting interpretation or generalization.
  7. 7. A — Findings must answer the research question rather than merely summarize sources.
  8. 8. B — Negative and contradictory evidence should be examined, not hidden.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to Student evidence corpus, analysis log, data tables, and methods record, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. B — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

Can the student transform a documented body of evidence into transparent, appropriately qualified findings?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
