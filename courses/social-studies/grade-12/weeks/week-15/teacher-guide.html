Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 15 · China and Russia

Essential question: How do major authoritarian states maintain control while adapting institutions, markets, nationalism, and technology?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

One-party rule, party-state institutions, personalism, federal form, managed elections, censorship, state capitalism, nationalism, and coercive capacity.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Constitutions, party documents, election records, economic data, and information-control studies. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of China and Russia and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Constitutions, party documents, election records, economic data, and information-control studies.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the China–Russia Authority and State-Capacity Analysis through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Assignment Scoring Guidance

  • Accuracy and source authority: 8
  • Evidence and citation: 8
  • Reasoning and corroboration: 8
  • Limitations and clarity: 6

  • Disciplinary accuracy: 10

Do not accept uncited claims, fabricated evidence, or a response that substitutes political identity for analysis. Return incomplete work for correction.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. C — Party-state means a system in which a ruling party is interwoven with state institutions.
  2. 2. B — Democratic centralism means a Leninist principle combining internal discussion with enforced unity after a decision.
  3. 3. A — Personalism means concentration of authority around an individual leader rather than autonomous institutions.
  4. 4. C — Managed election means an election whose competition or information environment is substantially controlled.
  5. 5. B — State capitalism means market activity combined with extensive state ownership, direction, or strategic control.
  6. 6. D — Censorship means suppression, restriction, or manipulation of access to information.
  7. 7. B — Authoritarian resilience depends on institutions, performance, adaptation, and elite management as well as repression.
  8. 8. A — China and Russia organize authority differently despite both being authoritarian.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to Constitutions, party documents, election records, economic data, and information-control studies, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. B — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How do major authoritarian states maintain control while adapting institutions, markets, nationalism, and technology?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
