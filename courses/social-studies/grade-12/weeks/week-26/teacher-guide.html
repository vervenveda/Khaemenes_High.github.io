Week 26 · Development, Poverty, and Global Inequality

Essential question: How should development be measured, explained, financed, and evaluated?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

GDP, human development, poverty measures, capability, institutions, colonial legacies, gender, health, education, infrastructure, aid, and sustainable development.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to UNDP, World Bank, national statistics, Sustainable Development Goals, and program evaluations. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Development, Poverty, and Global Inequality and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from UNDP, World Bank, national statistics, Sustainable Development Goals, and program evaluations.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. C — Development means long-term improvement in capabilities, institutions, living conditions, and opportunity.
  2. 2. D — Human development means expansion of people’s capabilities, health, education, and meaningful choices.
  3. 3. D — Absolute poverty means deprivation measured against a fixed minimum standard.
  4. 4. A — Relative poverty means deprivation measured in relation to prevailing standards in a society.
  5. 5. D — Dependency means a relationship in which economic structures constrain autonomous development.
  6. 6. D — Sustainable development means meeting present needs without undermining future generations’ capacity.
  7. 7. C — No single indicator captures development.
  8. 8. A — Growth can reduce poverty while increasing inequality or environmental pressure.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to UNDP, World Bank, national statistics, Sustainable Development Goals, and program evaluations, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How should development be measured, explained, financed, and evaluated?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
