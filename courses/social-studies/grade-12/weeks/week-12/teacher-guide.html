Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 12 · Media, Propaganda, and Political Communication

Essential question: How can citizens evaluate political information when governments, parties, platforms, journalists, and networks compete to shape attention?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Press systems, censorship, ownership, agenda setting, framing, propaganda, disinformation, algorithms, verification, and information resilience.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to The Verifier, press-freedom reports, platform transparency records, and selected propaganda artifacts. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Media, Propaganda, and Political Communication and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from The Verifier, press-freedom reports, platform transparency records, and selected propaganda artifacts.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Media Verification Investigation through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Assignment Scoring Guidance

Source Authority and Corroboration Record

  • Accuracy and source authority: 8
  • Evidence and citation: 8
  • Reasoning and corroboration: 8
  • Limitations and clarity: 6

Do not accept uncited claims, fabricated evidence, or a response that substitutes political identity for analysis. Return incomplete work for correction.

Comparative Data or Case Laboratory

  • Method and comparability: 8
  • Evidence display: 8

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Propaganda means strategic communication designed to shape belief or behavior, often selectively or deceptively.
  2. 2. C — Disinformation means false or manipulated information spread deliberately.
  3. 3. A — Misinformation means false or inaccurate information shared without necessarily intending deception.
  4. 4. B — Agenda setting means influence over which issues receive public attention.
  5. 5. A — Framing means selection and presentation that shape how an issue is interpreted.
  6. 6. A — Verification means testing a claim through source tracing, corroboration, context, and evidence.
  7. 7. D — Freedom of expression and reliable information are related but not identical.
  8. 8. D — Propaganda often mixes truth, omission, emotion, identity, and repetition.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to The Verifier, press-freedom reports, platform transparency records, and selected propaganda artifacts, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How can citizens evaluate political information when governments, parties, platforms, journalists, and networks compete to shape attention?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
