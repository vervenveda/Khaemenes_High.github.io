Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 13 · United Kingdom and Mexico

Essential question: How do two electoral democracies with different constitutional histories distribute power, representation, and accountability?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

UK parliamentary sovereignty, devolution, constitutional conventions, first-past-the-post; Mexico’s federal presidency, multiparty competition, electoral reform, and state capacity.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to UK Parliament, Electoral Commission, Mexico’s Constitution, INE records, and comparative data. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of United Kingdom and Mexico and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from UK Parliament, Electoral Commission, Mexico’s Constitution, INE records, and comparative data.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the United Kingdom–Mexico Structured Comparison through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Parliamentary sovereignty means the principle that Parliament is the supreme lawmaking authority in the UK constitutional order.
  2. 2. A — Constitutional convention means a politically binding practice not ordinarily enforced as a legal rule.
  3. 3. C — Devolution settlement means the statutory distribution of powers to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.
  4. 4. A — Sexenio means mexico’s six-year presidential term without immediate reelection.
  5. 5. B — Clientelism means exchange of targeted benefits for political support.
  6. 6. A — Electoral reform means change to voting, representation, administration, or competition rules.
  7. 7. C — The UK’s uncodified constitution still contains legal rules, institutions, and conventions.
  8. 8. D — Mexico’s transition illustrates how electoral reform can transform dominant-party rule.
  9. 9. D — Trace claims to UK Parliament, Electoral Commission, Mexico’s Constitution, INE records, and comparative data, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. C — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How do two electoral democracies with different constitutional histories distribute power, representation, and accountability?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
