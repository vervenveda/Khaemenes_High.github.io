Week 6 · Constitutions, Federalism, and Unitary States

Essential question: How do constitutional design and territorial organization distribute power and manage diversity?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Written and unwritten constitutions, federalism, devolution, unitary government, amendment, emergency power, and constitutional endurance.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to United States, United Kingdom, India, Nigeria, Mexico, and South Africa constitutional structures. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. A — Constitution means the foundational rules organizing government, authority, rights, and amendment.
  2. 2. B — Federalism means constitutional division of authority between national and regional governments.
  3. 3. C — Unitary state means a state in which ultimate constitutional authority rests with the central government.
  4. 4. A — Devolution means transfer of authority from a central government to regional or local bodies.
  5. 5. D — Entrenchment means protection that makes a constitutional rule harder to change than ordinary law.
  6. 6. A — Constitutionalism means the principle that government is limited and structured by higher law.
  7. 7. A — Constitutions distribute power as well as announce ideals.
  8. 8. C — Federal and unitary systems can each be centralized or decentralized in practice.
  9. 9. B — Trace claims to United States, United Kingdom, India, Nigeria, Mexico, and South Africa constitutional structures, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

How do constitutional design and territorial organization distribute power and manage diversity?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
