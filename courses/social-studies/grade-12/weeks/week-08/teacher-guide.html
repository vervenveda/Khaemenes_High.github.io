Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 8 · Courts, Rule of Law, and Accountability

Essential question: When can courts constrain political power, and what makes judicial independence credible?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Judicial review, constitutional courts, common and civil law traditions, appointment, tenure, access to justice, corruption control, and transitional justice.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Constitutional court decisions from multiple legal traditions and anti-corruption institutions. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Courts, Rule of Law, and Accountability and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Constitutional court decisions from multiple legal traditions and anti-corruption institutions.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Comparative Legal Case Brief through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. B — Judicial review means court evaluation of whether government action complies with higher law.
  2. 2. D — Judicial independence means the capacity of courts to decide cases without improper political control.
  3. 3. A — Precedent means an earlier decision used to guide later legal reasoning.
  4. 4. D — Civil law means a legal tradition centered on comprehensive codes and enacted law.
  5. 5. A — Common law means a legal tradition in which judicial decisions and precedent have major importance.
  6. 6. C — Transitional justice means processes addressing serious abuses during movement from conflict or repression.
  7. 7. D — Courts require public, institutional, and professional support to make independence effective.
  8. 8. A — Judicial power can protect rights or entrench elite authority.
  9. 9. D — Trace claims to Constitutional court decisions from multiple legal traditions and anti-corruption institutions, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. A — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

When can courts constrain political power, and what makes judicial independence credible?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
