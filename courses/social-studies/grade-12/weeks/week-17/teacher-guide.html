Khaemenes High · Grade 12 Teacher Guide

Week 17 · Comparative Political Systems Synthesis

Essential question: Which concepts best explain similarities and differences across political systems without erasing history and context?

Teacher PortalStudent Packet

Instructional Purpose

Most-similar and most-different systems, case selection, typologies, causal inference, institutional comparison, and evidence qualification.

Evidence standard

Require claims to be traceable to Country portfolios from Weeks 13–16 and cross-national governance datasets. Grade accuracy, authority, evidence, reasoning, comparison, limitation, citation, and revision—not viewpoint.

Daily Guidance

Monday · Concept and Inquiry Laboratory

Define the core concepts of Comparative Political Systems Synthesis and frame a researchable response to the essential question.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Tuesday · Primary and Authoritative Source Laboratory

Evaluate evidence from Country portfolios from Weeks 13–16 and cross-national governance datasets.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Wednesday · Comparative Data and Case Laboratory

Compare at least two cases or data series using common concepts and transparent methods.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Thursday · Argument, Seminar, and Performance Studio

Develop the Comparative Political Systems Portfolio through evidence-based deliberation.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Friday · Mastery, Correction, and Capstone Checkpoint

Demonstrate weekly mastery and document correction or capstone progress.

Look for: explicit concepts, source evaluation, evidence-to-claim reasoning, counterargument, and revision.

Minimum time: 50 minutes.

Mastery Quiz Answer Key

  1. 1. C — Comparative method means systematic comparison used to describe, classify, explain, or evaluate cases.
  2. 2. A — Case selection means the reasoned choice of cases appropriate to a research question.
  3. 3. C — Most-similar design means comparison of similar cases with different outcomes.
  4. 4. B — Most-different design means comparison of different cases sharing an outcome or relationship.
  5. 5. B — Typology means a classification system based on multiple dimensions.
  6. 6. D — Causal inference means reasoning about whether and how one factor contributes to an outcome.
  7. 7. A — Comparison requires common concepts and attention to case-specific context.
  8. 8. A — Selecting only convenient cases creates biased conclusions.
  9. 9. A — Trace claims to Country portfolios from Weeks 13–16 and cross-national governance datasets, evaluate authority and context, corroborate material facts, and state limitations.
  10. 10. D — Define the terms, compare evidence, explain reasoning, address a serious alternative, and name a revision condition.

Constructed Response

Which concepts best explain similarities and differences across political systems without erasing history and context?

Scoring: 10 points: precise claim (2), accurate concepts (2), two specific pieces of evidence (2), reasoning (2), counterargument or limitation (1), revision condition (1).

Supports Without Lowering the Standard

  • Provide vocabulary previews and audio access.
  • Chunk complex sources while preserving essential evidence.
  • Permit speech-to-text, graphic organizers, and extended time.
  • Use additional practice and correction before reassessment.
  • Require the same final claim, evidence, reasoning, and integrity standard.
