Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The United States and Soviet Union entered the postwar period with different political systems, security experiences, and visions for Europe. Soviet control in Eastern Europe and American economic and strategic initiatives intensified mutual suspicion.

People, Power, and Systems

Containment took varied forms: the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin airlift, NATO, support for allies, nuclear planning, and war in Korea. The Chinese Revolution and Korean War made the rivalry truly global.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare George Kennan’s analysis, the Truman Doctrine, a Soviet response, Marshall Plan data, and testimony from loyalty investigations. They distinguish communism as an ideology, specific communist governments, and domestic political accusation.

Why It Matters

The Cold War shaped foreign policy, military spending, science, education, immigration, and civil liberties for decades. Studying it requires attention to both communist repression and the dangers of using national-security claims to suppress lawful dissent.

Source Study: Competing Visions of the Postwar World

Analyze containment, Soviet security arguments, Marshall Plan evidence, a loyalty-board case, and Korean War maps.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

Cold WarA prolonged geopolitical conflict short of direct full-scale war between the United States and Soviet Union.
containmentA strategy aimed at limiting the expansion of Soviet influence or communism.
Truman DoctrineA policy pledging support to governments resisting coercion or armed minorities.
Marshall PlanU.S. economic assistance for European recovery.
NATOThe North Atlantic Treaty Organization, a collective-defense alliance formed in 1949.
McCarthyismAccusatory anticommunism associated with unsupported or reckless charges of disloyalty.
  • The Cold War grew from ideological conflict, security fears, wartime decisions, and competing interpretations of postwar order.
  • Containment combined diplomacy, economic aid, alliances, intelligence, military force, and nuclear deterrence.
  • Domestic anticommunism addressed real espionage concerns but also produced violations of due process and free expression.
