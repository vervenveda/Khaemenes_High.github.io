# Standards Crosswalk

## Course identity
The recommended Grade 10 sequence is **United States History Honors with integrated constitutional government and civic literacy**. Florida identifies United States History as a one-credit graduation requirement and United States Government as a separate half-credit requirement; therefore this package does not merge or mislabel the credits.

## Florida alignment
The course is organized around the Florida American History strands:
- SS.912.A.1 — historical inquiry and research
- SS.912.A.2 — Civil War and Reconstruction
- SS.912.A.3 — industrialization, urbanization, immigration, labor, and the late nineteenth century
- SS.912.A.4 — changing U.S. role in world affairs through World War I
- SS.912.A.5 — the 1920s and Great Depression
- SS.912.A.6 — World War II
- SS.912.A.7 — postwar United States and contemporary movements

Required instruction is intentionally embedded: founding documents and constitutional principles; African American history; Holocaust education; Asian American and Pacific Islander history; Hispanic and women’s contributions; Florida history; veterans and wartime service; history of communism; voting, civil government, free enterprise, and media literacy.

## C3 Framework
Every week uses the Inquiry Arc:
1. Develop questions and plan inquiries.
2. Apply historical, civic, economic, and geographic concepts.
3. Evaluate sources and use evidence.
4. Communicate conclusions and take informed, nonpartisan civic action.

## Grades 9–10 disciplinary literacy
Repeated targets include:
- RH.9-10.1 — cite specific textual evidence.
- RH.9-10.2 — determine central ideas.
- RH.9-10.6 — compare points of view.
- RH.9-10.7 — integrate quantitative/technical and qualitative analysis.
- RH.9-10.9 — compare primary and secondary sources.
- WHST.9-10.1 — write discipline-specific arguments.
- WHST.9-10.2 — write informative explanations.
- WHST.9-10.7 and 9 — conduct research and draw evidence from sources.

## Honors and international overlays
AP United States History supplies an honors skill overlay: developments and processes, sourcing, claims and evidence, contextualization, comparison, causation, continuity and change, and argumentation. This is not a claim of AP Course Audit authorization.

Cambridge IGCSE History supplies an international evidence-analysis overlay: knowledge and understanding, explanation, source evaluation, and structured historical argument. This is not a claim that completion alone enters a student for a Cambridge examination.

## Universal-course limitation
There is no single national Grade 10 social-studies curriculum that automatically satisfies every state, country, district, accreditor, IB programme, Cambridge centre, or examination board. This crosswalk is designed to make local review transparent rather than to make an unsupported universal-acceptance guarantee.


## State/localization requirement
Educators must add a state/local overlay without replacing the national U.S. History spine. Use state/local sources for Indigenous history, settlement/colonization, constitutional development, slavery/emancipation/Reconstruction, migration, labor/industry, civil rights, elections, government, public records, and contemporary history where appropriate. See `STATE_LOCALIZATION_GUIDE.md`.
