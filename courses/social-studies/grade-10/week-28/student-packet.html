Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Modern conservatism developed over decades through business organizations, religious activism, anticommunism, tax movements, suburban growth, southern political change, and criticism of federal programs. Ronald Reagan presented government as a problem while maintaining or expanding major parts of the state.

People, Power, and Systems

Tax cuts, deregulation, monetary restraint, union conflict, military buildup, and changes in social policy marked the era. Inflation fell and growth resumed after a severe recession, but inequality, debt, and deindustrialization remained important.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare conservative speeches, policy data, labor testimony, Soviet reform documents, dissident accounts, and diplomatic records. They resist attributing the Cold War’s end to one leader or one policy.

Why It Matters

Communist governments in Eastern Europe lost authority amid economic stagnation, reform, civil resistance, national movements, and reduced Soviet willingness to intervene. The Soviet Union dissolved in 1991, ending the bipolar system but not global conflict.

Source Study: Why Did the Cold War End?

Compare Reagan, Gorbachev, Eastern European dissidents, economic evidence, and diplomatic agreements.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

New RightA coalition of conservative activists emphasizing markets, anticommunism, social issues, and limited government.
supply-side economicsThe view that lower taxes and regulation can encourage investment and production.
deregulationReduction or removal of government rules governing an industry.
Strategic Defense InitiativeA proposed missile-defense research program announced in 1983.
glasnostSoviet policy of greater openness under Mikhail Gorbachev.
perestroikaSoviet restructuring reforms under Gorbachev.
  • Conservative growth reflected long-term organizing, economic dissatisfaction, social conflict, and regional change.
  • Reagan-era policy reduced some taxes and regulations while military spending and deficits increased.
  • The Cold War ended through interacting economic, political, social, national, diplomatic, and leadership factors.
