Week 14 · American Expansion and the Spanish-American War

Archive: National Archives · Treaty of Paris (1898)

A treaty is evidence of an agreement between governments, not evidence that people living in transferred territories consented to their new status.

Historical inquiry and evidence

  1. Identify territories addressed in the treaty and explain how it altered jurisdiction.
  2. What sources from Puerto Rico, Guam, Cuba, or the Philippines would reveal local perspectives?
  3. Compare the vocabulary of sovereignty in this treaty with American claims about self-government.

Week 15 · World War I and the Home Front

Archive: National Archives · Zimmermann Telegram

The intercepted telegram is evidence of German diplomatic intentions in 1917. It must be situated alongside unrestricted submarine warfare and other factors affecting U.S. entry into war.

Historical inquiry and evidence

  1. Who wrote this message, to whom, and for what strategic purpose?
  2. What cannot be concluded from a single diplomatic telegram about the motives of every American?
  3. Explain how wartime mobilization and security debates changed federal power.

Week 16 · The 1920s: Mass Culture, Prosperity, and Conflict

Archive: Library of Congress · Consumer Advertising

Advertisements reveal what sellers hoped buyers would desire, not how affordable products were for every household. Compare promotional imagery against wage, credit, or farm-income evidence.

Historical inquiry and evidence

  1. Identify a persuasive technique used by a period advertisement.
  2. What household evidence would test a claim of widespread prosperity?
  3. Explain how consumer culture could expand while economic insecurity persisted.

Week 17 · The Great Depression and the Crisis of Confidence

Archive: Library of Congress · Dust Bowl Migration

Migrant testimony, photographs, and government records show overlapping economic and environmental pressures; experiences of displaced families varied across regions.

Historical inquiry and evidence

  1. Distinguish the Depression's banking and employment crises from environmental pressures in the Plains.
  2. What can a photograph document, and what can it not establish without context?
  3. Use two kinds of evidence to explain why hardship fell unevenly across communities.

Week 18 · The New Deal and the Changing Federal Government

Archive: National Archives · Social Security Act (1935)

The law established a federal framework for social insurance and assistance. Its scope, early exclusions, and implementation must be examined rather than assuming universal coverage.

Historical inquiry and evidence

  1. Identify a purpose of the Act from its provisions.
  2. Who may have been excluded or underserved under early program rules and labor arrangements?
  3. What economic evidence could help evaluate the Act's impact over time?
