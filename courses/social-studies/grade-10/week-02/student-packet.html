Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

European voyages connected the Americas, Africa, and Europe in a sustained Atlantic system. Crops such as maize and potatoes changed diets abroad, while horses, livestock, and invasive species altered American environments. Epidemic diseases caused catastrophic Indigenous population loss, but Native nations continued to negotiate, resist, relocate, and adapt.

People, Power, and Systems

Spanish, French, Dutch, and English empires pursued different combinations of trade, conversion, extraction, and settlement. Colonists depended on Indigenous knowledge and alliances even as imperial expansion threatened Native sovereignty. In English colonies, labor systems shifted from mixed forms of servitude toward legally defined hereditary racial slavery.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a colonial charter, a Native diplomatic speech, a plantation law, and an Atlantic trade map. They examine legal language, audience, and economic incentives. A law can reveal official power while failing to show how fully people obeyed or resisted it.

Why It Matters

The colonial period established patterns of land conflict, cultural exchange, forced migration, regional diversity, and contested authority. These patterns shaped later arguments about liberty, citizenship, federal power, race, and national expansion.

Source Study: Charters, Covenants, and Native Diplomacy

Analyze a colonial charter and an Indigenous diplomatic address. Identify competing claims to land, authority, and mutual obligation.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

Columbian ExchangeThe movement of plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between hemispheres after 1492.
sovereigntyThe authority of a people or government to govern itself.
settler colonialismColonization aimed at permanent settlement and control of Indigenous land.
mercantilismAn economic policy linking colonies to the wealth and power of an imperial state.
indentured servitudeLabor performed for a fixed term in exchange for passage, support, or debt repayment.
racial slaveryA hereditary system of forced labor organized through racial categories.
  • Disease and ecological exchange transformed societies across the Atlantic.
  • European colonies developed differently because of geography, labor systems, imperial policy, and Indigenous resistance.
  • Racial slavery emerged through law, economic demand, and political choices rather than as an inevitable institution.
