Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

During the 1930s, many Americans opposed another European war, while the administration watched expansion by Germany, Italy, and Japan with growing concern. Neutrality laws attempted to reduce entanglement, but aid policies gradually shifted toward countries resisting Axis aggression.

People, Power, and Systems

Japan’s expansion in Asia and U.S. economic pressure deepened conflict. The attack on Pearl Harbor brought declarations of war, and Germany and Italy then declared war on the United States. Mobilization required coordination of industry, labor, science, finance, and military manpower on a national scale.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a neutrality speech, Roosevelt’s Four Freedoms, Lend-Lease arguments, diplomatic evidence, and accounts of Pearl Harbor. They avoid treating entry as either completely sudden or predetermined.

Why It Matters

The shift from neutrality to war shows how public opinion, alliance, economics, intelligence, and security interact. Wartime fear also contributed to the forced removal and incarceration of Japanese Americans, a policy later formally acknowledged as unjust.

Source Study: From Neutrality to War

Analyze neutrality arguments, the Four Freedoms, Lend-Lease, diplomatic communications, and Pearl Harbor testimony.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

isolationismA preference for limiting political or military involvement abroad.
Neutrality Acts1930s laws intended to restrict U.S. involvement in foreign wars.
Lend-LeaseA program supplying defense materials to countries considered vital to U.S. security.
arsenal of democracyThe idea that U.S. industrial production would support nations fighting aggression.
Pearl HarborThe Japanese attack on the U.S. Pacific Fleet in Hawaiʻi on December 7, 1941.
internmentConfinement of people, often without ordinary criminal process, during wartime.
  • Neutrality reflected memories of World War I as well as strategic and domestic political calculations.
  • Aid to the Allies increasingly connected U.S. security to the outcome of the war before formal entry.
  • Pearl Harbor produced rapid mobilization and wartime policies that tested constitutional rights.
