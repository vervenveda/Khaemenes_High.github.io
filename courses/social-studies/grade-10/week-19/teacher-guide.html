Week 19 Teacher Guide

World War II: From Neutrality to Global War

Teacher Portal
Essential Question: Why did the United States move from neutrality to full participation in World War II?

Standards: SS.912.A.6; Florida history · D2.His.1.9-12 · D3.1.9-12 · D4.1.9-12 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.WHST.9-10.1

Instructional Emphasis

  • Neutrality reflected memories of World War I as well as strategic and domestic political calculations.
  • Aid to the Allies increasingly connected U.S. security to the outcome of the war before formal entry.
  • Pearl Harbor produced rapid mobilization and wartime policies that tested constitutional rights.

Differentiation: Use vocabulary preview, chunked texts, oral processing, accessible maps, extended time, and alternative response modes. Honors students should source independently, write a stronger counterclaim, and compare a second interpretation.

Assignment Guidance

Assignment 1 · Source Lab — From Neutrality to War

Teacher note: Award evidence credit for specific details and source reasoning, not summary alone.

Exemplar characteristics: A full-credit response identifies the origin and purpose of the sources, uses at least two details, and explains how the evidence bears on: Why did the United States move from neutrality to full participation in World War II? It also states one limitation.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Reasoning: Explains how evidence supports the claim and addresses complexity or limitation.

Accuracy: Uses historical, geographic, economic, and civic concepts correctly.

Communication: Organizes ideas clearly with appropriate academic language.

Assignment 2 · Atlas and Data Investigation

Teacher note: Check labels, legend, scale or units, and whether the student avoids claiming that correlation alone proves causation.

Exemplar characteristics: A full-credit product uses accurate labels and units, identifies a meaningful pattern, proposes an evidence-based explanation, and names what the visualization cannot prove.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Reasoning: Explains how evidence supports the claim and addresses complexity or limitation.

Accuracy: Uses historical, geographic, economic, and civic concepts correctly.

Communication: Organizes ideas clearly with appropriate academic language.

Assignment 3 · Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

Teacher note: Score evidence and reasoning rather than the political, moral, or policy position selected.

Exemplar characteristics: Reasonable conclusions may differ. Full credit directly answers “Could the United States have remained neutral without endangering its security or democratic commitments?,” uses accurate evidence, explains reasoning, and addresses a substantial counterargument.

Constructed Response

Using at least two specific pieces of evidence, answer: Why did the United States move from neutrality to full participation in World War II?

A full-credit response states a defensible claim, uses two accurate details, explains how each supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
