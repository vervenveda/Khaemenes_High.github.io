Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The Soviet collapse removed the organizing framework of containment but left questions about U.S. leadership. The Gulf War, interventions in the Balkans, and debates over Rwanda reflected differing views of force, alliances, and humanitarian responsibility.

People, Power, and Systems

Domestically, the 1990s saw economic expansion, deficit reduction, welfare reform, crime legislation, technology growth, and intense conflict over social issues. Republicans won Congress in 1994, and divided government became a central feature.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare presidential and congressional policy arguments, economic data, welfare participant testimony, media coverage, and impeachment documents. They separate constitutional standards from partisan preference.

Why It Matters

The decade’s apparent stability masked changing party coalitions, information systems, inequality, and global security risks. Institutional conflict and media fragmentation would intensify in the next century.

Source Study: Prosperity Without Consensus

Analyze policy speeches, congressional contracts, welfare reform, economic evidence, and impeachment arguments.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

divided governmentControl of the executive and legislative branches by different political parties.
culture warPolitical conflict over social values, identity, religion, and public institutions.
welfare reformChanges to public-assistance programs emphasizing time limits, work, and state administration.
impeachmentA formal legislative accusation that may lead to removal of an official.
humanitarian interventionUse of diplomatic, economic, or military power to prevent or respond to severe human suffering.
partisan polarizationIncreasing ideological distance and hostility between political parties.
  • Post-Cold War policy mixed international intervention, market-oriented policy, and domestic ideological conflict.
  • Economic growth and low unemployment did not eliminate poverty, inequality, or regional dislocation.
  • Media change, partisan strategy, institutional conflict, and social issues contributed to polarization.
