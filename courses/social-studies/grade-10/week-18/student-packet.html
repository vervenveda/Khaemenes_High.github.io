Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Franklin Roosevelt’s administration acted rapidly to stabilize banks, provide jobs, support agriculture, regulate finance, and restore confidence. Programs such as the CCC, WPA, TVA, FDIC, SEC, and Social Security created lasting institutions and infrastructure.

People, Power, and Systems

Labor protections supported union growth, while public works employed millions. Yet agricultural and domestic workers—disproportionately Black and Latino—were excluded from major early protections. Some programs reinforced local segregation or displaced communities.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a fireside chat, a critic from the left, a critic from the right, a Supreme Court decision, and program data. They distinguish criticism that the New Deal did too much from criticism that it did too little.

Why It Matters

The New Deal did not eliminate depression-era unemployment, but it changed federal responsibility and created institutions that continue to shape economic life. Its constitutional controversies remain important to debates over executive power and the regulatory state.

Source Study: Relief, Recovery, Reform, and Constitutional Power

Compare Roosevelt, Huey Long, a conservative critic, a Supreme Court opinion, and participant testimony.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

New DealThe programs and reforms of Franklin D. Roosevelt’s administration responding to the Depression.
recoveryPolicies intended to restore production and employment.
reformStructural change designed to reduce future risk or abuse.
Social SecurityA federal social-insurance system established in 1935.
collective bargainingNegotiation between employees and employers through representatives.
court-packing planRoosevelt’s unsuccessful proposal to add Supreme Court justices under certain conditions.
  • New Deal programs addressed relief, recovery, and reform through experimentation rather than one unified blueprint.
  • Federal authority expanded in banking, labor relations, agriculture, infrastructure, and social insurance.
  • New Deal benefits were substantial but uneven, and the Depression did not fully end until wartime mobilization.
