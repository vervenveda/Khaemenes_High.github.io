Week 18 Teacher Guide

The New Deal and the Changing Federal Government

Teacher Portal
Essential Question: How did the New Deal redefine the relationship among citizens, markets, states, and the federal government?

Standards: SS.912.A.5; Florida history · D2.His.1.9-12 · D3.1.9-12 · D4.1.9-12 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.WHST.9-10.1

Instructional Emphasis

  • New Deal programs addressed relief, recovery, and reform through experimentation rather than one unified blueprint.
  • Federal authority expanded in banking, labor relations, agriculture, infrastructure, and social insurance.
  • New Deal benefits were substantial but uneven, and the Depression did not fully end until wartime mobilization.

Differentiation: Use vocabulary preview, chunked texts, oral processing, accessible maps, extended time, and alternative response modes. Honors students should source independently, write a stronger counterclaim, and compare a second interpretation.

Assignment Guidance

Assignment 1 · Source Lab — Relief, Recovery, Reform, and Constitutional Power

Teacher note: Award evidence credit for specific details and source reasoning, not summary alone.

Exemplar characteristics: A full-credit response identifies the origin and purpose of the sources, uses at least two details, and explains how the evidence bears on: How did the New Deal redefine the relationship among citizens, markets, states, and the federal government? It also states one limitation.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Reasoning: Explains how evidence supports the claim and addresses complexity or limitation.

Accuracy: Uses historical, geographic, economic, and civic concepts correctly.

Communication: Organizes ideas clearly with appropriate academic language.

Constructed Response

Using at least two specific pieces of evidence, answer: How did the New Deal redefine the relationship among citizens, markets, states, and the federal government?

A full-credit response states a defensible claim, uses two accurate details, explains how each supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
