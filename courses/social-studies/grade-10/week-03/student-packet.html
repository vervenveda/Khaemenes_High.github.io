Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The Seven Years’ War expanded British territory and debt, leading Parliament to impose new taxes and controls. Many colonists objected not only to cost but to authority: could Parliament tax people who lacked direct representation there? Protest escalated through petitions, boycotts, crowd action, propaganda, and eventually armed conflict.

People, Power, and Systems

The Declaration of Independence announced universal principles while the new states retained slavery, property restrictions, and unequal legal status. Enslaved people pursued freedom through military service, escape, petitions, and resistance. Native nations often viewed both Britain and the colonies through the question of territorial survival.

Reading the Evidence

Students source the Declaration, a Loyalist argument, a petition by enslaved people, and an image of political protest. They distinguish stated ideals from political practice and avoid assuming that one document represents every participant.

Why It Matters

The Revolution created an independent republic and a language of rights later used by abolitionists, women’s-rights advocates, workers, and civil-rights movements. It also left unresolved conflicts over slavery, Indigenous sovereignty, representation, and the balance between liberty and public order.

Source Study: Four Claims About Independence

Compare the Declaration of Independence, a Loyalist response, an enslaved petition for freedom, and a Haudenosaunee diplomatic position.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

republicanismThe belief that government should serve the public good through representatives and civic virtue.
natural rightsRights understood as inherent to human beings rather than granted by rulers.
popular sovereigntyThe principle that legitimate government rests on the consent of the governed.
boycottCollective refusal to buy goods or participate in activity as political pressure.
loyalistA colonist who remained loyal to Britain during the American Revolution.
emancipationRelease from slavery or another form of legal bondage.
  • Imperial war and taxation disputes intensified a constitutional crisis.
  • Revolutionary rhetoric drew on natural rights while political participation remained restricted.
  • The war involved Patriots, Loyalists, Native nations, enslaved people, women, and international powers with differing goals.
