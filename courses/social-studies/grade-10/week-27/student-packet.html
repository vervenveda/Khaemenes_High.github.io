Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The Watergate break-in led investigators toward campaign misconduct, obstruction, misuse of agencies, and secret recording. Courts required the release of evidence, congressional hearings informed the public, and President Richard Nixon resigned when support collapsed.

People, Power, and Systems

The decade also brought oil shocks, inflation, deindustrialization pressures, changing global competition, and unemployment. These problems did not share a single cause, and policies to reduce inflation could worsen unemployment.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare the Nixon tapes ruling, congressional articles, investigative reporting, inflation data, and environmental testimony. They analyze how institutions corroborated one another and where they depended on individual choices.

Why It Matters

New environmental laws addressed air, water, species protection, and toxic hazards. Florida’s open-government tradition and growth debates provide a local lens on transparency, land use, water, and development.

Source Study: Accountability in a Constitutional System

Analyze United States v. Nixon, congressional evidence, journalism, economic data, and environmental advocacy.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

WatergateThe political scandal involving the Nixon administration and abuses of power.
executive privilegeA claimed presidential power to withhold certain internal communications.
stagflationThe combination of high inflation, weak growth, and unemployment.
oil shockA sudden disruption or price increase in petroleum supply.
Environmental Protection AgencyThe federal agency established in 1970 to implement environmental law.
Sunshine LawA law requiring specified government meetings or records to be open to the public.
  • Watergate demonstrated the roles of courts, Congress, journalism, elections, and internal whistleblowers in accountability.
  • Energy shocks, global competition, productivity change, and inflation weakened the postwar economic pattern.
  • Environmental and consumer movements translated public concern into institutions and regulation.
