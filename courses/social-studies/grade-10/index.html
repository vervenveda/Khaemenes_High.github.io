About United States History Honors

United States History Honors studies the American story from Indigenous nations and Atlantic encounters through constitutional development, expansion, Civil War and Reconstruction, industrialization, world wars, civil rights, the Cold War, globalization, and contemporary civic questions. Students compare evidence and interpretation while connecting historical events to geography, economics, institutions, rights, public policy, and constitutional change.

Course Features

Historical Development & Change Students investigate colonization, founding, expansion, sectional conflict, Reconstruction, industrialization, reform, war, civil rights, political realignment, and contemporary change across a coherent chronology.
Constitution & Civic Literacy Federalism, courts, rights, elections, institutions, public policy, amendment, civic pressure, and constitutional interpretation connect historical study to responsible citizenship.
Evidence, Geography & Economics Maps, data, source provenance, competing interpretations, markets, labor, inequality, migration, technology, and economic change strengthen historical and quantitative reasoning.
Media, Synthesis & Civic Capstone Students evaluate contested recent evidence, media claims, historical distance, pluralism, constitutional rights, and public argument before completing a final civic and historical synthesis.
