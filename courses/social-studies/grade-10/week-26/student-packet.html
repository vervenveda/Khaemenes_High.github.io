Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Vietnamese independence struggles developed through resistance to French colonial rule, Japanese occupation, and postwar conflict. U.S. leaders interpreted events through containment and feared the strategic and political consequences of communist victory.

People, Power, and Systems

Advisers, covert action, and aid expanded before large-scale combat troops arrived. The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution gave President Johnson broad authority, and bombing and troop levels increased. Military power did not resolve the political legitimacy and local-war dimensions of the conflict.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare presidential speeches, the Gulf of Tonkin record, soldier accounts, Vietnamese perspectives, protest arguments, and casualty and polling data. They distinguish opposition to a war from opposition to servicemembers.

Why It Matters

After the Tet Offensive, the credibility gap widened. Withdrawal under Vietnamization and the 1973 peace agreement did not prevent the fall of South Vietnam in 1975. The war reshaped trust, congressional oversight, veteran experience, and U.S. foreign policy.

Source Study: Escalation and the Credibility Gap

Analyze presidential justifications, congressional authority, Vietnamese perspectives, military testimony, protest, and polling.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

domino theoryThe belief that communist victory in one country would encourage nearby countries to follow.
Gulf of Tonkin ResolutionA 1964 congressional resolution granting broad authority for military action in Southeast Asia.
guerrilla warfareIrregular warfare using mobility, local knowledge, and dispersed forces.
credibility gapDistrust caused by differences between official statements and observed reality.
VietnamizationA policy shifting more combat responsibility to South Vietnamese forces.
War Powers ResolutionA 1973 law intended to clarify limits on presidential military deployment.
  • Vietnam emerged from colonialism, nationalism, civil conflict, Cold War ideology, and great-power intervention.
  • Presidents escalated incrementally through assumptions about credibility and containment.
  • The draft, casualties, media, official secrecy, protest, and battlefield developments transformed public debate.
