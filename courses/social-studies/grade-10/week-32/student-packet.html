Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Rising home prices, expanding mortgage credit, securitization, leverage, and weak underwriting created vulnerability. When housing prices fell and defaults rose, financial institutions could not easily value or absorb losses, credit froze, and the recession deepened.

People, Power, and Systems

Federal Reserve action, bank support, fiscal stimulus, auto-industry intervention, and later regulation aimed to prevent collapse and restore demand. Critics disagreed about whether interventions were necessary, insufficient, unfair, or poorly designed.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a mortgage document, regulator testimony, a homeowner account, financial data, and arguments for and against intervention. They trace mechanisms rather than treating 'Wall Street' or borrowers as a single cause.

Why It Matters

The recovery eventually restored employment and output, but many households lost homes and wealth. The period also saw the election of the first Black president, healthcare reform, Tea Party activism, Occupy Wall Street, and changing party coalitions.

Source Study: From Mortgage to Systemic Crisis

Analyze lending evidence, financial-sector testimony, homeowner experience, bailout arguments, and recovery data.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

subprime mortgageA home loan made to a borrower with higher assessed credit risk.
securitizationPooling financial assets and selling claims on their cash flows.
systemic riskRisk that failure in one part of a system can destabilize the whole system.
bailoutGovernment support intended to prevent collapse of an institution or system.
foreclosureLegal loss of property after failure to meet mortgage obligations.
Affordable Care ActA 2010 federal health law expanding coverage and regulating insurance markets.
  • Housing, leverage, complex securities, weak risk controls, and interconnected finance contributed to the crisis.
  • Emergency interventions stabilized major institutions but created disputes about fairness, accountability, and moral hazard.
  • Recovery reduced unemployment over time while wealth loss and regional inequality shaped political reaction.
