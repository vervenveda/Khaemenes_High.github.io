Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Manufacturing employment declined in many older industrial regions as automation, trade, corporate restructuring, and global supply chains changed production. Service, finance, healthcare, logistics, and technology sectors expanded, but gains and losses were geographically uneven.

People, Power, and Systems

Immigration transformed cities, suburbs, schools, agriculture, small business, and cultural life. New arrivals from Latin America, Asia, the Caribbean, Africa, and the Middle East joined long-established communities and internal migrants.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a trade agreement argument, a displaced-worker account, an immigrant entrepreneur’s testimony, technology advertising, and labor-market data. They identify whether a source addresses national totals, regional effects, or individual experience.

Why It Matters

The early Internet lowered barriers to communication and knowledge but access differed by income and location. Florida became a major gateway for migration, tourism, trade, aerospace, services, and Latin American and Caribbean connections.

Source Study: Winners, Losers, and Measures of Globalization

Analyze trade debates, worker testimony, immigrant experience, corporate evidence, and digital-access data.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

globalizationThe intensification of cross-border economic, cultural, political, and technological connections.
deindustrializationThe decline or relocation of manufacturing employment and capacity in a region.
information economyEconomic activity centered on data, communication, services, and knowledge.
free-trade agreementA treaty reducing barriers to trade among participating countries.
digital divideUnequal access to computers, connectivity, and digital skills.
remittanceMoney sent by migrants to family or communities elsewhere.
  • Trade, automation, finance, logistics, and corporate strategy transformed employment and production.
  • Immigration after 1965 reshaped communities, culture, entrepreneurship, labor, and politics.
  • Digital networks expanded access to information while creating new inequalities and forms of concentration.
