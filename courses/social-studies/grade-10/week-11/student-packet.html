Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Large corporations reorganized steel, oil, railroads, meatpacking, communications, and finance. Productivity and consumer markets grew, but wealth and decision-making became concentrated. Courts and governments often protected contracts and property while gradually developing new regulatory approaches.

People, Power, and Systems

Millions of immigrants arrived from Europe, Asia, the Caribbean, and Latin America, joining Black and white rural migrants in expanding cities. Ethnic institutions, mutual-aid societies, newspapers, unions, churches, and businesses helped communities adapt. Nativist laws and violence restricted Chinese immigration and targeted other newcomers.

Reading the Evidence

Students analyze a factory photograph, a worker testimony, a corporate defense, a union platform, and wage and price data. They compare the value and limits of narrative and quantitative evidence.

Why It Matters

Industrial America created unprecedented output and infrastructure while producing hazardous work, urban crowding, labor conflict, and environmental damage. Debates over regulation, unions, immigration, and corporate power remain central to modern economic citizenship.

Source Study: Who Should Govern the Workplace?

Compare worker testimony, a corporate statement, a labor-platform excerpt, a political cartoon, and strike reporting.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

corporationA legally recognized organization that can own property, enter contracts, and limit investor liability.
monopolyDominant control of a market by one firm or coordinated group.
vertical integrationControl of multiple stages of production and distribution.
collective bargainingNegotiation between workers and employers through representatives.
strikeOrganized refusal to work in order to press demands.
nativismPolitical or social hostility toward immigrants or perceived outsiders.
  • Industrial growth depended on technology, finance, natural resources, government policy, management, and labor.
  • Immigrants and internal migrants built industrial cities while facing discrimination and dangerous conditions.
  • Labor conflict raised enduring questions about property rights, public order, bargaining power, and regulation.
