Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The modern civil-rights movement grew from generations of resistance, institution building, litigation, journalism, labor organizing, and wartime activism. Black veterans and community leaders challenged the contradiction between democratic war aims and segregation.

People, Power, and Systems

Brown v. Board declared segregated public schools inherently unequal, overturning the constitutional foundation of school segregation. Resistance was immediate. In Montgomery, a broad local network sustained a year-long boycott after Rosa Parks’s arrest, with women organizers and neighborhood institutions performing essential work.

Reading the Evidence

Students analyze Brown, local organizing records, a boycott leaflet, photographs, and executive action at Little Rock. They identify the distinct roles of courts, local activism, media, and the presidency.

Why It Matters

These campaigns show that landmark decisions matter most when communities can organize to implement them. They also reveal the costs borne by individuals who challenge entrenched systems and the importance of nonviolent discipline, legal strategy, and collective support.

Source Study: Law Meets Local Organizing

Compare Brown, a Montgomery organizing document, a student account from Little Rock, and federal enforcement evidence.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

civil disobediencePublic, nonviolent violation of law to protest injustice.
NAACPThe National Association for the Advancement of Colored People.
Brown v. Board of EducationThe 1954 Supreme Court decision rejecting state-mandated school segregation.
Montgomery Bus BoycottThe 1955–1956 mass protest against segregated buses in Montgomery, Alabama.
mass meetingA large community gathering used to deliberate and organize.
Little Rock NineNine Black students who integrated Central High School in Little Rock in 1957.
  • Black activism before and after World War II created legal, organizational, and moral foundations for the movement.
  • Brown established a constitutional principle, but implementation required sustained local and federal action.
  • Women, students, churches, lawyers, workers, and local organizations were central movement strategists.
