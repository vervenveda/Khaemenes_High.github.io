Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

President Woodrow Wilson initially declared neutrality, but American trade and finance increasingly favored the Allies. German submarine warfare, the Zimmermann Telegram, and strategic concerns contributed to the declaration of war in 1917.

People, Power, and Systems

The federal government organized a draft, sold bonds, coordinated production, regulated railroads, and used propaganda. Women entered new occupations, Black migrants moved to industrial cities, and Mexican migration supported labor demand. Military service remained segregated, and racial conflict intensified in several cities.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare Wilson’s war message, an antiwar speech, a Committee on Public Information poster, a soldier letter, and a Supreme Court decision on wartime speech. They assess how emergency claims change standards of acceptable dissent.

Why It Matters

The war elevated the United States in world affairs but the Senate rejected membership in the League of Nations. The conflict also sharpened debates about civil liberties, citizenship, internationalism, and whether democratic ideals were applied consistently at home.

Source Study: War Aims and Wartime Liberty

Analyze Wilson’s war message, Senator La Follette’s dissent, propaganda, and Schenck v. United States.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

neutralityA policy of not taking part in a war between other states.
unrestricted submarine warfareSubmarine attacks without traditional warning or search procedures.
Selective ServiceThe system used to draft men for military service.
Great MigrationThe movement of millions of Black Americans from the South to other regions.
Espionage ActA 1917 law penalizing interference with military operations and certain national-defense information.
self-determinationThe principle that peoples should have a meaningful role in choosing their political status.
  • The U.S. entered World War I after a prolonged neutrality debate shaped by security, commerce, and submarine warfare.
  • Mobilization expanded federal management of finance, industry, labor, information, and military service.
  • Wartime demands opened opportunities while also producing repression, racial violence, and contested civil liberties.
