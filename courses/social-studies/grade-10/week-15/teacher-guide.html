Week 15 Teacher Guide

World War I and the Home Front

Teacher Portal
Essential Question: How did total war expand federal power and redefine citizenship, dissent, and America’s world role?

Standards: SS.912.A.4; Florida history · D2.His.1.9-12 · D3.1.9-12 · D4.1.9-12 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7 · CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.WHST.9-10.1

Instructional Emphasis

  • The U.S. entered World War I after a prolonged neutrality debate shaped by security, commerce, and submarine warfare.
  • Mobilization expanded federal management of finance, industry, labor, information, and military service.
  • Wartime demands opened opportunities while also producing repression, racial violence, and contested civil liberties.

Differentiation: Use vocabulary preview, chunked texts, oral processing, accessible maps, extended time, and alternative response modes. Honors students should source independently, write a stronger counterclaim, and compare a second interpretation.

Assignment Guidance

Assignment 1 · Source Lab — War Aims and Wartime Liberty

Teacher note: Award evidence credit for specific details and source reasoning, not summary alone.

Exemplar characteristics: A full-credit response identifies the origin and purpose of the sources, uses at least two details, and explains how the evidence bears on: How did total war expand federal power and redefine citizenship, dissent, and America’s world role? It also states one limitation.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Reasoning: Explains how evidence supports the claim and addresses complexity or limitation.

Accuracy: Uses historical, geographic, economic, and civic concepts correctly.

Communication: Organizes ideas clearly with appropriate academic language.

Assignment 2 · Atlas and Data Investigation

Teacher note: Check labels, legend, scale or units, and whether the student avoids claiming that correlation alone proves causation.

Exemplar characteristics: A full-credit product uses accurate labels and units, identifies a meaningful pattern, proposes an evidence-based explanation, and names what the visualization cannot prove.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Reasoning: Explains how evidence supports the claim and addresses complexity or limitation.

Accuracy: Uses historical, geographic, economic, and civic concepts correctly.

Communication: Organizes ideas clearly with appropriate academic language.

Assignment 3 · Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

Teacher note: Score evidence and reasoning rather than the political, moral, or policy position selected.

Exemplar characteristics: Reasonable conclusions may differ. Full credit directly answers “Should civil liberties be narrowed during war, and who should decide when the emergency has ended?,” uses accurate evidence, explains reasoning, and addresses a substantial counterargument.

Evidence: Uses accurate, relevant evidence and identifies its source.

Constructed Response

Using at least two specific pieces of evidence, answer: How did total war expand federal power and redefine citizenship, dissent, and America’s world role?

A full-credit response states a defensible claim, uses two accurate details, explains how each supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
