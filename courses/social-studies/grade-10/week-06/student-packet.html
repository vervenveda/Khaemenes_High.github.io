Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

During the first half of the nineteenth century, the United States expanded across Indigenous homelands and former Mexican territory. Supporters invoked security, agriculture, republican opportunity, and Manifest Destiny. Opponents questioned war aims, slavery expansion, constitutional authority, and the treatment of Native and Mexican communities.

People, Power, and Systems

The Indian Removal Act and state pressure led to coerced treaties and forced migrations, including the Cherokee Trail of Tears. Supreme Court decisions recognized elements of tribal political status, but enforcement depended on political institutions willing to respect those rulings.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare a removal treaty, a Native petition, a presidential war message, an antiwar speech, and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo. They distinguish legal text from implementation and assess how translation, coercion, and unequal military power affected agreements.

Why It Matters

Territorial growth shaped modern borders, federal-tribal relations, citizenship, land ownership, and sectional politics. Studying borderlands also reveals communities whose histories do not fit a simple east-to-west national story.

Source Study: Law, War, and Territorial Claims

Analyze Worcester v. Georgia, a Cherokee petition, Polk’s war message, and an antiwar congressional speech.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

Manifest DestinyThe nineteenth-century belief that U.S. continental expansion was justified or inevitable.
Indian RemovalFederal and state policies forcing Indigenous peoples from eastern homelands.
annexationFormal incorporation of territory into a state.
cessionTransfer of territory, often through treaty.
sovereigntyIndependent governing authority.
borderlandsRegions where peoples, empires, and legal systems overlap and interact.
  • Expansion resulted from migration, diplomacy, purchase, war, and forced removal.
  • The Supreme Court, executive branch, states, and Native nations contested authority over Indigenous lands.
  • The Mexican-American War expanded U.S. territory and intensified conflict over slavery.
