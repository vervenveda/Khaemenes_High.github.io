Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The 1920s brought rapid growth in automobiles, household appliances, broadcasting, advertising, chain stores, and installment buying. Productivity increased and urban lifestyles gained visibility, but farmers, many workers, and racial minorities often experienced less prosperity.

People, Power, and Systems

The Harlem Renaissance transformed American literature, music, visual art, and political thought. Women’s political participation grew after the Nineteenth Amendment, and young urban culture challenged older conventions. At the same time, the revived Ku Klux Klan, immigration quotas, and racial violence reflected organized exclusion.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare an advertisement, a Harlem Renaissance text, an immigration-law excerpt, a religious-modernist debate, and economic data. They identify whether a source describes aspiration, representation, or typical experience.

Why It Matters

The decade matters because mass media and consumer culture changed how Americans imagined national identity. It also reveals how prosperity can coexist with structural weakness and how cultural innovation can emerge under discrimination.

Source Study: Selling and Contesting Modern America

Compare advertising, Harlem Renaissance writing, immigration restriction, and cultural-conflict evidence.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

consumer creditBorrowing used to purchase goods and services.
mass cultureWidely shared entertainment and consumer practices distributed through national media.
Harlem RenaissanceA major flowering of Black arts, literature, music, and thought centered in Harlem and beyond.
quota systemImmigration limits based on numerical or national-origin rules.
fundamentalismA religious movement emphasizing strict adherence to foundational beliefs or texts.
Red ScareA period of intense fear of radicalism or communism.
  • Automobiles, radio, advertising, movies, and consumer credit helped create a national mass culture.
  • The decade produced artistic innovation and expanded freedom for some while nativism and racial violence persisted.
  • Economic growth rested on uneven income, speculative finance, agricultural distress, and consumer debt.
