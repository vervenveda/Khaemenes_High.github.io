Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The term Gilded Age describes a period whose surface prosperity concealed corruption, inequality, and social conflict. National parties competed closely, while local machines connected immigrants and working-class residents to jobs and services in exchange for loyalty.

People, Power, and Systems

Southern states used violence, poll taxes, literacy tests, white primaries, and segregation law to exclude Black citizens from political power. The Supreme Court’s Plessy v. Ferguson decision allowed state-mandated segregation under the doctrine of 'separate but equal,' although facilities and rights were profoundly unequal.

Reading the Evidence

Students analyze a segregation law, Black journalism, a Populist platform, machine-era testimony, and election data. They examine whether reform movements challenged racial hierarchy consistently or selectively.

Why It Matters

Farmers and workers organized against debt, freight rates, and concentrated finance. Populism influenced later reforms even though the movement fractured. The era shows how formal elections can coexist with unequal access to the ballot and unequal influence over policy.

Source Study: Democracy Under Constraint

Compare a Jim Crow law, an Ida B. Wells anti-lynching argument, the Omaha Platform, and a political-machine defense.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

Jim CrowA system of laws and customs enforcing racial segregation and white supremacy.
disfranchisementThe removal or obstruction of voting rights.
poll taxA tax used in some states as a condition for voting.
PopulismA late nineteenth-century movement seeking political and economic reform for farmers and workers.
political machineAn organization that exchanges services or patronage for political support.
antitrustGovernment action intended to prevent or limit monopolistic practices.
  • After Reconstruction, southern states constructed systems of segregation and voter suppression.
  • Political machines provided services and opportunity while also practicing patronage and corruption.
  • Farmers’ alliances and Populists challenged railroad, credit, currency, and political systems.

Wednesday · Map, Measure, and Model
