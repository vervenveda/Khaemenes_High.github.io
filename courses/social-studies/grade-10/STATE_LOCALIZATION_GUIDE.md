# Grade 10 U.S. History Honors · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule
The national U.S. History sequence is canonical and **state-localizable by requirement**. State/local history and evidence should deepen the course, not fragment it into separate state editions or lower the Academy's 80% mastery requirements.

## Required overlays
- Name specific Indigenous nations/homelands connected to the learner's state/region and use current tribal/nation sources where possible.
- Connect colonization, slavery, emancipation, Reconstruction, westward expansion, industrialization, immigration/migration, civil rights, war/home-front history, and recent history to relevant state/local cases.
- Use the state constitution, state courts/legislature/executive institutions, election administration, archives, historic sites, public records, demographic/economic data, and local journalism as source laboratories when appropriate.
- Include state/local African American history, labor history, migration, entrepreneurship, public service, and civil-rights institutions.
- Permit the civic capstone to use a safe, nonpartisan state/local issue supported by public evidence.

## Non-negotiable national core
The national chronology, constitutional development, historical reasoning, source evaluation, geography, economics, civics, media literacy, 36-week sequence, assignments, midterm/final expectations, and 80% mastery gates remain fixed.

## Privacy and nonpartisanship
Do not require disclosure of political affiliation, immigration/citizenship status, religion, family finances, or private family history. Local civic work must remain evidence-based and nonpartisan.
