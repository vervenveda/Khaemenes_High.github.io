Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Progressivism included many movements rather than one program. Reformers investigated tenements and factories, promoted food and drug regulation, expanded municipal services, protected natural resources, and challenged corporate concentration. States adopted direct-democracy tools, and constitutional amendments changed taxation, Senate elections, prohibition, and women’s suffrage.

People, Power, and Systems

Women’s clubs, settlement houses, labor organizations, Black civic groups, and journalists built reform networks. Figures such as Ida B. Wells, W. E. B. Du Bois, Jane Addams, Florence Kelley, and Mary Church Terrell linked local organizing to national debate.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare investigative journalism, a city reform report, a suffrage argument, and evidence about eugenic policy. They evaluate the benefits and dangers of expert authority when affected communities lack decision-making power.

Why It Matters

Progressive reforms helped create the modern regulatory state and broadened participation, but they did not eliminate racial violence, labor exploitation, or exclusion. Reform must therefore be assessed by goals, methods, implementation, and distribution of benefits.

Source Study: Reformers and the Regulated Society

Analyze a muckraking excerpt, a settlement-house report, a suffrage speech, and a coercive reform law.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

ProgressivismA diverse reform movement seeking responses to industrial and urban problems.
muckrakerA journalist who investigated corruption or harmful conditions.
initiativeA process allowing voters to propose laws.
referendumA direct vote on a proposed law or policy.
recallA process for removing an elected official before the end of a term.
social insurancePublic programs that protect people against economic risks.
  • Progressives pursued regulation, public health, conservation, political reform, and social welfare through government action.
  • Women, Black reformers, immigrants, labor activists, and professionals shaped reform agendas but faced unequal access.
  • Some Progressive policies relied on coercive assumptions about race, disability, immigration, and expertise.
