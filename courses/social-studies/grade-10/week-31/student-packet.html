Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

On September 11, 2001, al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked four aircraft, killing nearly three thousand people. The attacks produced national mourning, emergency response, military mobilization, and a broad reassessment of domestic and international security.

People, Power, and Systems

Congress authorized force against those responsible, and the United States invaded Afghanistan. New agencies, surveillance authorities, detention practices, and security procedures expanded executive capacity. Muslim, Arab, Sikh, and South Asian communities also experienced hate crimes and suspicion.

Reading the Evidence

Students compare the 2001 authorization, security legislation, a civil-liberties challenge, intelligence claims concerning Iraq, and later investigative findings. They practice distinguishing evidence available at the time from evidence learned later.

Why It Matters

The Iraq invasion removed Saddam Hussein but led to occupation, insurgency, sectarian conflict, and long-term regional consequences. The era demonstrates the importance of congressional oversight, intelligence reliability, legal limits, and care in assigning collective blame.

Source Study: Security Claims and Evidence Standards

Analyze the 2001 AUMF, Patriot Act provisions, civil-liberties arguments, Iraq intelligence claims, and later commission findings.

Vocabulary and Key Understandings

terrorismViolence or threat of violence against civilians intended to achieve political aims.
Authorization for Use of Military ForceA congressional authorization permitting specified presidential military action.
Patriot ActA 2001 law expanding and revising surveillance, investigation, and information-sharing authorities.
preemptionActing against a perceived threat before an attack occurs.
insurgencyAn organized rebellion against an established authority.
habeas corpusJudicial protection against unlawful detention.
  • The September 11 attacks transformed security policy, foreign relations, immigration, surveillance, and public life.
  • Military action in Afghanistan targeted al-Qaeda and the Taliban government that sheltered it.
  • The Iraq War was justified through claims about weapons and security that require careful evaluation against later evidence.
