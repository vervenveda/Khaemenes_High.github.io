# Grade 9 Global Studies Honors · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule
Grade 9 remains a global history, geography, civics, economics, and media-literacy course, but it is **state-localizable by requirement**. State/local evidence should create meaningful bridges to the learner's jurisdiction without displacing the global sequence.

## Required overlays
- Use the learner's state/local **archives, museums, tribal/nation sources, public records, government sites, election data, census/economic data, and civic institutions** as evidence laboratories where appropriate.
- Connect global themes—migration, trade, industrialization, imperialism, conflict, human rights, media, globalization, and government—to state/local examples when they clarify scale and interdependence.
- Include specific Indigenous nations/homelands connected to the state/region when relevant; preserve present-day continuity and sovereignty.
- Compare state/local constitutional or governmental institutions with global systems when doing so strengthens—not replaces—comparative understanding.
- Permit the civic capstone to use a safe, evidence-ready, nonpartisan state/local issue.

## Non-negotiable core
The 36-week global sequence, five lessons and three assignments per week, weekly 80% quiz mastery, constructed-response evaluation, midterm/final, disciplinary literacy, and civic-action expectations remain fixed.

## Safeguards
Localization is nonpartisan and privacy-protective. Do not require disclosure of political affiliation, immigration/citizenship status, religion, family finances, health information, or private family history.
