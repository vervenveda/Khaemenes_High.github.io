# Standards Crosswalk — Grade 9 Global Studies Honors

**Review date:** August 5, 2026

There is no single national Grade 9 social studies sequence. This course therefore uses a broad standards architecture and must be adapted to local credit and required-instruction rules.

## National and professional frameworks
- NCSS College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework: inquiry questions, disciplinary concepts, evidence evaluation, communication, and informed action.
- Common Core Literacy in History/Social Studies, Grades 9–10: evidence citation, central ideas, point of view, quantitative/visual integration, argument writing, research, and evidence use.
- UNESCO Media and Information Literacy: access, analysis, evaluation, ethical use, content production, and civic participation.

## State breadth checks
### Florida
The course addresses world history, geography, economics, civic literacy, primary-source analysis, Holocaust education, human rights, and the development and consequences of communist systems. Florida’s official source for individual standards and course descriptions remains CPALMS and the Florida Department of Education.

### New York
The curriculum includes stimulus-based multiple choice, constructed responses, document analysis, enduring issues, global history, geography, and evidence-based essays consistent with the skill architecture of Global History and Geography.

### Texas
The course covers World History Studies themes including chronology, geography, government, economics, culture, science and technology, citizenship, and social studies skills.

### California
The program reflects the California History–Social Science Framework’s emphasis on inquiry, source evidence, historical and geographic analysis, civic learning, literacy, and inclusive perspectives.

## Coursewide C3 emphasis
- D1.5.9-12: source selection for inquiry
- D2.His.1.9-12: context
- D2.His.14.9-12: complex causes and effects
- D2.Geo.4.9-12: human and physical systems
- D2.Eco.1.9-12: incentives and choices
- D2.Civ.10.9-12: interests and perspectives in civic life
- D3.1.9-12: source origin, authority, structure, context, and corroboration
- D4.1.9-12: evidence-based arguments
- D4.6.9-12: interdisciplinary understanding of public problems

## Coursewide literacy emphasis
- RH.9-10.1: specific textual evidence
- RH.9-10.2: central ideas and accurate summary
- RH.9-10.6: point-of-view comparison
- RH.9-10.7: quantitative and qualitative integration
- WHST.9-10.1: discipline-specific arguments
- WHST.9-10.7: research
- WHST.9-10.9: evidence from informational texts

## Important limitation
Breadth and rigor do not equal automatic approval. A parent, teacher, district, umbrella school, or evaluator must verify local course naming, credit, required topics, hours, attendance, assessment, portfolio, and transcript requirements.


## State/localization requirement
The global-history course retains its international sequence. Educators must add a jurisdiction-specific state/local overlay for civic institutions, elections, public records, state history connections, Indigenous nations, economic/demographic data, and required instruction where appropriate.
