Week 26: Industrial Revolution

Essential Question: Did industrialization improve human life, and for whom?

Instructional Throughline

Historical conditions → industrial systems → productivity and labor → urban/environmental consequences → reform and collective action → model → evidence → qualified judgment.

Standards

  • D1.5.9-12 — Determine the kinds of sources helpful in answering compelling and supporting questions.
  • D2.His.1.9-12 — Evaluate how historical events and developments were shaped by unique circumstances and broader contexts.
  • D3.1.9-12 — Gather relevant information from multiple sources using origin, authority, structure, context, and corroborative value.
  • D2.Eco.1.9-12 — Analyze how incentives influence choices and behavior.
  • D4.1.9-12 — Construct arguments using precise claims and evidence while acknowledging counterclaims.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 — Cite specific textual evidence.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7 — Integrate quantitative/technical and qualitative analysis.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.WHST.9-10.1 — Write evidence-based arguments.

Simulation Instruction

Use the sequence predict → change one variable → observe → record → explain → identify limitation → corroborate. Students should understand that simulator output demonstrates the behavior of model assumptions; it does not independently prove historical causation.

Growth Simulator: students examine investment, depreciation, population growth, technology, steady-state capital, and output per worker. Require three controlled runs.

Supply & Demand Simulator: students manipulate demand/supply strength and record equilibrium price and quantity across five runs. Require an explanation of one observed change and one limitation.

Mastery & Corrections

The weekly gate remains 80%. A student below mastery should complete the academy correction workflow: identify the reasoning error, state the corrected reasoning with evidence/rule, and explain how the strategy transfers to a similar question before retrying.

Constructed Response

Prompt: Using at least three specific pieces of historical evidence and, if used, one result from a Week 26 simulation, answer: Did industrialization improve human life, and for whom? Explain one limitation of your evidence or model.

  • 5: Qualified claim; three accurate pieces of evidence; clear relationships; appropriate simulation use/rejection; limitation.
  • 4: Defensible claim; three relevant pieces of evidence; explanation and limitation mostly clear.
  • 3: Claim and two relevant pieces of evidence with some explanation.
  • 2: General claim with limited/weak evidence.
  • 1: Minimal or unsupported response.
  • 0: No relevant response.
