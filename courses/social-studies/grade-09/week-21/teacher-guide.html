Week 21: Land-Based Empires, 1450–1750

Essential Question: How did large empires govern religious, linguistic, and ethnic diversity without unlimited resources?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Grade comparative state-capacity reasoning, source analysis, implementation evidence, local negotiation, and qualification—not vocabulary recall.

Core Safeguards

  • Do not let “gunpowder empire” become a technology-only explanation. Firearms mattered alongside taxation, logistics, administration, alliances, food supply, skilled labor, and local intermediaries.
  • Official imperial claims, court images, sacred edicts, and monumental architecture communicate legitimacy but do not prove uniform implementation.
  • Terms such as millet, zamindar, absolutism, tolerance, and frontier require historical context; avoid treating them as identical across empires.
  • Accommodation and plural governance should not be equated automatically with modern equality, citizenship, or religious liberty.
  • Central revenue assessments are not the same thing as actual collection. Require provincial/local corroboration.
  • Maps of imperial territory simplify frontiers, mixed populations, negotiated authority, and changes over time.

Reading protocol: creator/institution → date/period → genre → audience → claim → evidence → limitation → corroboration → essential-question connection.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

ARSHIF: Build a balanced imperial source set across at least three empires and more than one source category.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Track official claim, implementation evidence, intermediary role, local response, value, limitation, and corroboration.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Map capitals, revenue zones, frontiers, routes, religious/linguistic regions, and contested borderlands. Label central claims separately from demonstrated administrative reach.

PROSE: 850–1150 words: How did large empires govern diversity without unlimited resources? Require comparison across at least three empires, one implementation gap, one local/intermediary mechanism, and one qualification concerning tolerance or coercion.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require a defensible comparative claim; at least three specific pieces of evidence from at least three source categories; evidence from at least three empires or imperial contexts; one mechanism of negotiated rule or intermediary governance; and one qualification about coercion, implementation, tolerance, or frontier limits.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the failed reasoning category, reopen the relevant reading/source/Atlas case, explain why the original reasoning failed, correct with evidence, and demonstrate transfer on a new imperial example before reassessment.
