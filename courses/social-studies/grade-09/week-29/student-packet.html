Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggered a crisis, but war became continental and global because leaders made choices within alliance systems, military timetables, imperial rivalries, and nationalist politics. Colonized peoples supplied soldiers, labor, food, and resources.

People, Power, and Systems

Machine guns, artillery, submarines, poison gas, railways, and mass conscription changed warfare. Civilians faced blockade, occupation, hunger, forced migration, censorship, and propaganda. The war was fought in Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and the oceans.

Reading the Evidence

In Russia, military failure and social crisis helped overthrow the monarchy. Bolsheviks promised peace, land, and worker power, then fought a civil war and built a one-party state. The revolution inspired supporters and opponents worldwide.

Why It Matters

Peace settlements redrew borders and created mandates while often applying self-determination selectively. Wartime violence and unresolved grievances shaped later conflicts.

Source Study: Soldier Letters, Propaganda, and Peace Terms

Compare a soldier letter, a home-front poster, a colonial perspective, and a peace proposal. Identify audience and the gap between experience and official language.

Vocabulary

militarismThe elevation of military power and values in national policy.
alliance systemAgreements among states to cooperate, often in defense or war.
total warWar mobilizing economies, civilians, propaganda, and state power on a broad scale.
armisticeAn agreement to stop fighting.
BolshevikA member of the revolutionary party that seized power in Russia in 1917.
self-determinationThe principle that peoples should have a meaningful role in choosing their political status.

Key Understandings

  • World War I resulted from interacting causes including alliance commitments, imperial rivalry, nationalism, militarism, and crisis decisions.
  • Industrial technology increased destructive capacity while states mobilized soldiers, workers, colonies, finance, and information.
  • War pressures contributed to revolutions and the collapse of Russian, Ottoman, German, and Austro-Hungarian imperial structures.
