Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

In the nineteenth century, European states expanded formal control across much of Africa and Asia while the United States and Japan also built empires. Industrial production created demand for raw materials and markets, but prestige, strategy, missionary activity, and racial ideology also mattered.

People, Power, and Systems

Colonial regimes used direct and indirect rule, concessions, taxation, forced labor, schools, and legal categories. Borders often ignored existing political and cultural relationships. Local elites sometimes collaborated to protect influence, while others resisted or shifted strategies over time.

Reading the Evidence

Imperial propaganda portrayed conquest as civilization. Anti-imperial voices exposed violence, economic extraction, hypocrisy, and cultural destruction. Photographs, maps, treaties, company records, and oral histories reveal both official claims and local experience.

Why It Matters

Imperial legacies include borders, languages, infrastructure, economic dependency, legal systems, migration, and movements for national liberation. These outcomes were neither uniform nor inevitable.

Source Study: Imperial Propaganda and Countervoices

Analyze an imperial map or advertisement beside an African, Asian, Pacific, or European anti-imperial statement. Identify visual and verbal strategies.

Vocabulary

imperialismThe extension of political, economic, or cultural control over other territories or peoples.
colonialismDirect settlement or administration of a territory by an outside power.
Social DarwinismThe misuse of evolutionary concepts to justify hierarchy, racism, and imperial competition.
sphere of influenceAn area where an outside power claims special economic or political privileges.
indirect ruleColonial administration conducted partly through existing local authorities.
anti-imperialismOpposition to empire based on self-determination, equality, economics, religion, or other principles.

Key Understandings

  • Industrial weapons and transport aided empire, but conquest also depended on local alliances, finance, medicine, and political divisions.
  • Colonial rule reorganized land, labor, borders, education, law, and trade for imperial priorities.
  • Resistance ranged from armed conflict to legal petitions, religious movements, labor action, cultural preservation, and intellectual critique.

Choose the best answer. Ten objective questions = 20 points. Mastery threshold = 80% on the objective section.

1. A European power gains access to a resource region, builds a railway to a port, and imposes new taxes. Which interpretation is strongest?

2. An imperial advertisement describes conquest as a civilizing mission. What should a historian do first?

10. A map shows resistance movements clustered near transport corridors and resource zones. What can the map establish most directly?
