Weekly Reading

State formation and political change

Rome expanded from a city-state through republican government and eventually imperial rule. Civil conflict and concentration of authority transformed Roman politics, but some republican institutions and offices continued in altered forms. In East Asia, Qin unification preceded the Han dynasty, which developed and adapted imperial institutions across a large territory.

Administration from center to locality

Roman government used law, taxation, provincial administration, military institutions, officials, and local elites. Han government used imperial officials, commanderies and kingdoms, household registration, taxation, legal administration, and relationships with local elites. Later Chinese examination systems should not simply be projected backward onto the Han period.

Networks and frontiers

Roads, sea lanes, rivers, defenses, and exchange corridors moved soldiers, officials, messages, taxes, and goods. The Silk Roads were connected networks involving many regions and intermediaries rather than one road directly linking Rome and the Han capital. Infrastructure increased imperial reach, but control remained uneven.

Membership and cultural integration

Roman citizenship was a changing legal status that expanded over centuries, while social inequality remained. Han political and social membership followed different institutions and categories. Romanization is best understood as uneven spread and local adaptation of Roman language, law, institutions, and practices—not the automatic replacement of local cultures.

Transformation rather than disappearance

Neither empire simply vanished. Han rule ended and political division followed, but institutions and traditions continued in changed forms. Western Roman imperial government fragmented in the fifth century while the eastern Roman Empire continued. Warfare, fiscal strain, succession struggles, disease, migration, frontier pressures, elite competition, and local adaptation interacted differently in each case.

Vocabulary

republicA political order in which public authority operates through civic institutions rather than hereditary monarchy alone.
imperial bureaucracyA network of officials and administrative institutions used to govern an empire.
citizenshipA recognized legal or political status carrying particular rights and obligations; its meaning changes across societies and periods.
RomanizationThe uneven spread, negotiation, and local adaptation of Roman language, law, customs, and institutions.
Silk RoadsConnected overland exchange networks linking regions of East, Central, South, and Southwest Asia and the Mediterranean world through many intermediaries.
continuity and changeHistorical reasoning that identifies what persisted, what transformed, and why.

Daily Lessons

Monday · From Republics and Warring States to Empires

Create parallel Rome and Qin-Han timelines. Identify three turning points for each and label each continuity, change, or both.

Tuesday · Law, Office, Taxation, and Administration

Analyze Roman and Han administrative evidence. Ask how orders, revenue, information, and personnel moved from central institutions to local communities.

Wednesday · Roads, Frontiers, Exchange, and Uneven Integration

Map Roman roads and sea lanes beside Han roads, rivers, frontier defenses, and exchange corridors. Explain what moved through each network and what the map cannot prove.

Thursday · Citizenship, Elites, Cultural Integration, and Imperial Legitimacy

Build a comparison matrix for legal status, local elites, service obligations, culture, law, and access to authority. Do not assume Roman citizenship and Han membership were equivalent.

Friday · Transformation, Fragmentation, and Legacy

Create a causal web separating long-term pressures, triggers, feedback loops, political fragmentation, and continuities. Then complete the mastery assessment.

3 · Durable Rule Argument

Which mattered most for durable imperial rule: law/status, officials, military infrastructure, or cooperation with local elites? Use evidence from both empires and address a serious counterclaim.
