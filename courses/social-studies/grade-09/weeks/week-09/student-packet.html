Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

The Mauryan Empire unified much of South Asia through military power, taxation, provincial government, roads, and diplomacy. Emperor Ashoka later sponsored inscriptions presenting an ethic of restraint, welfare, and religious respect after a destructive war. These edicts reveal both political communication and royal self-presentation.

People, Power, and Systems

After Mauryan rule, South Asia remained politically diverse. The Gupta period is often associated with courtly literature, mathematics, astronomy, and temple culture, but achievements involved many regions and communities. Social organization included occupational, kinship, religious, and status groups whose experiences varied.

Reading the Evidence

Indian Ocean navigation depended on seasonal monsoon winds. Merchants carried spices, textiles, ivory, metals, ceramics, and ideas. Ports became multilingual spaces, and religious communities often traveled along commercial routes.

Why It Matters

South Asia was not an isolated civilization bounded by modern borders. Its history demonstrates how land empires, port cities, pastoral zones, and ocean networks interacted.

Source Study: Ashoka’s Public Edicts

Analyze the audience, location, moral language, and political purpose of selected edicts. Distinguish what the inscriptions claim from what they prove.

Vocabulary

Mauryan EmpireA major South Asian empire founded in the late fourth century BCE.
edictAn official public order or proclamation.
monsoonA seasonal wind pattern that strongly shapes Indian Ocean travel and rainfall.
diasporaA community living outside an ancestral homeland while maintaining connections.
stupaA Buddhist commemorative structure associated with relics and devotion.
decimal place-value systemA numerical system in which a digit’s position determines its value.

Key Understandings

  • Ashoka’s edicts used public inscriptions to communicate moral and administrative expectations across a diverse empire.
  • Indian Ocean merchants used predictable monsoon winds to connect East Africa, Southwest Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia.
  • South Asian religious, mathematical, literary, and artistic traditions spread through exchange and local adaptation.
