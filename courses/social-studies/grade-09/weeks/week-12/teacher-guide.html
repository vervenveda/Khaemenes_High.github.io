Week 12: Medieval Europe

Essential Question: How were land, labor, religion, law, and political authority negotiated in medieval Europe?

Mastery: At least 80% (8/10 objective questions). Tool use alone never earns mastery; historical reasoning and source handling require evaluator judgment.

Historical Safeguards

  • Use “feudalism” cautiously as a modern model; do not imply one uniform medieval European system.
  • Do not equate peasant with serf. Rural legal status and obligations varied by place and time.
  • A manor record documents obligations and administration but does not provide a complete biography of rural life.
  • Avoid a simple “church versus state” frame. Medieval jurisdictions overlapped among rulers, nobles, bishops, monasteries, towns, households, and customary/legal institutions.
  • Guilds and corporate privileges could protect members while also restricting outsiders or competition.
  • Teach Magna Carta in its 1215 baronial-political context before tracing later reinterpretations. It did not create universal democracy or modern voting rights.
  • Atlas Evidence Analysis organizes evidence, claims, timelines, provenance, and uncertainty; it does not decide historical truth for the student.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

ARSHIF: Use approved medieval history, world-classics, manuscript, legal, church/institutional, and travel resources. Students record provenance and distinguish contemporary evidence from later interpretation.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Require at least one manorial/local administrative source, one ecclesiastical/legal source, and one charter or political document. Tag claim type, origin, audience, purpose, limitation, corroboration, and missing voices.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Use the verified Atlas_Evidence_Analysis.html tool as a case workspace. Track places, institutions, claims, evidence status, timeline events, provenance, alternative interpretations, and explicit uncertainty. A route line or place association does not prove jurisdiction or control.

PROSE Editorial Suite: Recommended honors report: 700–1000 words answering how power was negotiated among rulers, nobles, religious institutions, towns, and communities, and how one institution/document later acquired a changed political meaning.

Assignment Guidance

1 · Feudalism Model Audit: Students compare varied relationships and identify where the label clarifies or conceals differences. Accept “useful,” “misleading,” or “both” when evidence supports the judgment.

2 · Manor and Household Evidence Lab: Full credit requires separating recorded obligations from assumptions in the hypothetical household model.

3 · Jurisdiction & Corporate Power Dossier: Compare ecclesiastical, royal/noble, town, guild, or university claims. Ask who granted privileges, who benefited, who was excluded, and how enforcement can be tested.

4 · Magna Carta Reception Study: Require two columns: original 1215 context/meaning and documented later use. Every claimed continuity should have a source.

Constructed Response Rubric

5 points: defensible claim about negotiated authority; three accurate pieces of evidence; at least two source types/institutional settings; explanation rather than listing; clear distinction between original historical role and later interpretation/legacy.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed reasoning concept, reopen the relevant source or Atlas case, explain why the original answer failed, correct it with evidence, and retake.
