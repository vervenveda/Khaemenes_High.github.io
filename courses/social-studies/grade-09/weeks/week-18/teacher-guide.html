Week 18: Semester Review and Midterm

Essential Question: How can students demonstrate durable understanding rather than short-term memorization?

Mastery: 80% minimum. A score below mastery triggers targeted relearning, evidence-based correction, and reassessment—not answer memorization.

Five-Day Pacing

DayFocusEvidence of Learning
MondayRetrieval DiagnosticUnaided chronology, geography, theme, and evidence reconstruction plus a personal mastery map.
TuesdayTransfer LabReasoning on unfamiliar texts, maps, artifacts, images, and data with explicit provenance and limitations.

ThursdayArgument LaboratoryTimed mini-DBQ outline with sourced groups, counterevidence, and qualification.
FridayMidterm & Error ForensicsCumulative assessment, error classification, corrected reasoning, targeted practice, and reassessment plan where needed.

Error-Forensics Categories

  • Content knowledge
  • Chronology / geography
  • Source / provenance reading
  • Map / quantitative reasoning
  • Causal / comparative reasoning
  • Language / vocabulary
  • Attention / process

For each miss require: original reasoning → error category → corrected reasoning → supporting evidence → prevention/transfer strategy.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

ARSHIF Retrieval Lab: Students retrieve first, then use approved sources only to repair diagnosed gaps and practice unfamiliar evidence.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Build a cross-unit matrix linking claim, week/theme, source category, provenance, evidence, reasoning skill, limitation, corroboration, and error-forensics notes.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Reconstruct chronology and geography across Weeks 1–17. Every proposed connection must be labeled observed, corroborated, plausible/inferred, or uncertain.

PROSE: Use for the cumulative DBQ/synthesis and post-assessment reflection explaining how the student's reasoning changed after correction.

Midterm Design Standard

The actual midterm should sample Weeks 1–17 through unfamiliar stimulus-based questions, chronology/geography reasoning, evidence evaluation, comparison/causation, short constructed responses, and a cumulative DBQ or outline. Avoid a vocabulary-dominant exam.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require examples from at least three course units or skill domains, accurate content, transfer to unfamiliar evidence, one diagnosed error category, and explanation of how targeted correction improves future reasoning.
