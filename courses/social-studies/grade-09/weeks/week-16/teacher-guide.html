Week 16: Mongol Expansion and the Black Death

Essential Question: How can the same network support conquest, commerce, communication, and disease?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Evaluate causal reasoning, chronology, regional comparison, disease-evidence handling, source corroboration, and uncertainty—not vocabulary recall or tool completion.

Historical & Evidence Safeguards

  • Reject stereotypes and inevitability: Mongol expansion requires interacting explanations involving mobility, organization, intelligence, diplomacy, logistics, incorporated specialists, siege capability, and opponent conditions.
  • Separate conquest from later governance. Mongol-ruled khanates governed regions differently through local institutions, elites, tax systems, religious policies, and administrative practices.
  • Use “Pax Mongolica” only as a qualified term for particular periods/corridors of relative security and exchange; do not let it erase conquest, coercion, conflict, chronology, or regional variation.
  • A mapped military or commercial corridor does not prove a plague transmission pathway. Require chronology plus appropriate biological, archaeological, ecological, documentary, or other corroboration.
  • “Trade spread plague” is incomplete unless students identify mechanisms, actors, vectors/reservoirs, chronology, and alternative pathways.
  • Exact mortality estimates remain uncertain because surviving records are incomplete, uneven, and regionally variable.
  • Analyze scapegoating and persecution as contingent human responses shaped by fear, prejudice, institutions, rumor, and power—not as evidence about disease causation.
  • Do not assume demographic decline automatically produced identical wage, rent, or social outcomes; institutions and power mediated effects.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

ARSHIF: Locate approved chronicles from different regions, administrative or diplomatic records, archaeology, demographic evidence, plague research summaries, mobility/trade evidence, and social-response evidence. Preserve date, place, creator, genre, and provenance.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Build two linked dossiers: Mongol power/governance and plague transmission/effects. For every major claim record evidence type, provenance, mechanism, value, limitation, confidence, and corroboration needed.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Use verified Protools/Atlas_Evidence_Analysis.html. Organize campaigns, khanates, exchange corridors, documented plague occurrences, dates, claims, evidence status, provenance, and alternative interpretations. Require distinct labels for confirmed occurrence, plausible connection, and uncertain/inferred connection.

PROSE: 700–1000 word honors synthesis answering: How can the same network support conquest, commerce, communication, and disease? Require at least three source categories, one traced mechanism, regional/chronological qualification, and explicit uncertainty.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require a defensible multicausal claim; at least three specific pieces of evidence from at least three source categories; one traced mechanism; distinction between connectivity and causation; and one meaningful chronological, regional, demographic, or transmission uncertainty.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed reasoning concept, reopen the relevant evidence/Atlas case, explain why the original answer failed, correct it with evidence, and retake. Vocabulary copying alone is insufficient.
