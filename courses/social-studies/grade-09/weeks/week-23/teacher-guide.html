Week 23: Atlantic Slavery, Colonialism, and Resistance

Essential Question: How did racial slavery become embedded in Atlantic economies, and how did enslaved people resist?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Grade systems reasoning, quantitative literacy, source provenance, legal/institutional analysis, resistance evidence, ethical explanation, and historical uncertainty—not vocabulary recall.

Historical Safeguards

  • Do not treat enslaved people primarily as numbers, commodities, or passive victims. Pair quantitative evidence with testimony, petitions, archaeology, cultural evidence, and resistance records.
  • Distinguish slavery in different historical settings from Atlantic racialized hereditary chattel slavery; avoid implying that all systems were identical.
  • Do not present “Africa” as one political actor. Identify particular states, merchants, communities, regions, and changing relationships to Atlantic demand.
  • Do not equate a law with uniform practice. Racialized slave codes reveal official rules and power claims; implementation and lived experience require corroboration.
  • Resistance includes survival, family formation, cultural continuity, work slowdowns, negotiation, escape, maroon communities, petitions, courts, revolt, testimony, and abolitionist organizing.
  • Quantitative databases are reconstructions from incomplete records. Teach estimates, missing voyages, denominators, mortality calculations, and uncertainty explicitly.
  • Do not grade moral explanation by ideological agreement. Grade historical evidence, reasoning, power analysis, consequences, perspective-taking, and ethical justification.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

Reading Lab: Pair an accessible secondary overview with Slave Voyages data, Equiano or another first-person account, and at least one resistance/legal/community source.

ARSHIF: Build a bounded multi-perspective source set including voyage records, colonial laws, advertisements, petitions, narratives, maroon evidence, court records, abolition documents, archaeology, and material culture where appropriate.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Record claim, source type, creator/institution, date/place, value, limitation, quantitative status, missing voices, corroboration, and whether the evidence primarily reveals system, experience, or resistance.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Map embarkation/disembarkation regions, major routes, plantation zones, selected maroon communities, and abolition chronology. Separate documented points from estimates/inference.

PROSE: 850–1150 words answering how racial slavery became embedded in Atlantic economies and how enslaved people resisted. Require one quantitative source, one first-person or community-centered source, one legal/institutional source, one resistance example, and one evidentiary limitation.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: classify the missed reasoning, reopen the relevant quantitative/source/legal/resistance evidence, explain why the original answer failed, correct it with evidence, and transfer the repaired reasoning to a new example before reassessment.
