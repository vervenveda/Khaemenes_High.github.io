Teacher Guide · Week 2

Geography and Human Systems

Return to Course

Essential Question

How does location shape opportunities, risks, movement, and human choices?

Mastery: five daily lessons + three principal assignments submitted + at least 80% on the ten-question objective quiz. Sub-80 attempts require review/corrections before retake. Constructed response is teacher-scored.

Teacher Intent

Week 2 establishes geographic reasoning without geographic determinism. Students learn that location, scale, projection, physical systems, migration networks, policy, technology, and power interact. Maps and datasets are treated as selective representations that must be interpreted for purpose, scale, time frame, categories, and limits.

Assignment Guidance

1 · Projection Comparison · 20 pts

Students compare at least three projection types and identify what each preserves, distorts, and is well-suited to show. Full-credit work connects projection choice to purpose rather than labeling one map “accurate” and another “wrong.”

2 · Atlas & Migration Data Investigation · 20 pts

Require a labeled map/data product, correct use of totals/rates/percentage change where applicable, one observed pattern, one plausible explanation, one competing explanation, and one evidence limitation.

3 · Evidence-Based Seminar/Argument · 30 pts

Prompt: Should schools use one standard world map, or should students routinely compare several projections? Students need at least three pieces of evidence, a serious counterclaim, and a conclusion tied to educational purpose and geographic literacy.

Constructed Response · 5 pts

Using at least two specific examples from Week 2, explain how location can shape opportunities, risks, movement, and human choices without determining them completely. Include one limitation of a map, dataset, or geographic explanation.

Content Safeguard

Avoid geographic determinism. Do not teach that climate, terrain, or location mechanically causes a society’s political, economic, or cultural outcomes. Require students to identify interacting human institutions, technologies, networks, choices, inequalities, and power.
