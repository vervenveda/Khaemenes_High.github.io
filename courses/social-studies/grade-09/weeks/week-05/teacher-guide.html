Week 5: Early Africa, South Asia, East Asia, and the Americas

Essential Question: How did early societies develop complex urban and political systems in different environments?

Mastery: Students must earn at least 80% on the 10-question objective assessment. Constructed responses and assignments require evaluator judgment.

Historical and Comparative Safeguards

  • Do not use Mesopotamia, Egypt, or any other society as a universal developmental template.
  • Do not treat the absence of deciphered writing as evidence of weak institutions or low complexity.
  • Similarity does not by itself prove diffusion, direct contact, common religion, or identical government.
  • Monumental architecture can support claims about organized labor and priorities, but cannot alone prove monarchy, consent, or a specific political structure.
  • Distinguish observation from inference. Require students to label uncertainty where the surviving evidence is incomplete.
  • Use “civilization” analytically and comparatively, never as a ranking of peoples.

Assignment Guidance

Assignment 1 — Archaeological Source Lab: Analyze an Indus city plan, Shang evidence such as a bronze or oracle-bone example, Kushite/Nubian material or monumental evidence, and an Olmec sculpture or site feature. Require students to identify what is directly observed, what is inferred, and what additional evidence would strengthen the inference.

Assignment 2 — Atlas and Data Investigation: Compare sites, river/resource settings, and exchange evidence. Provenanced nonlocal materials can support long-distance exchange; similarity alone cannot. Check labels, scale/legend where relevant, and the observation/explanation distinction.

Assignment 3 — Comparative Argument: Students answer whether complex societies require centralized political authority. Require evidence from at least three regional cases plus a serious qualification or counterexample. Score reasoning rather than the conclusion selected.

Constructed Response

Students use evidence from at least three regional cases to explain different pathways to complex urban or political systems and identify one evidentiary limitation or uncertainty.

5-point rubric: 1 comparative claim; 2 accurate regional evidence; 1 reasoning/comparison; 1 limitation or qualification. Human evaluator required.

80% Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed concept, revisit the corresponding lesson/evidence case, explain why the original response failed, and retake. Do not treat worksheet completion as mastery.
