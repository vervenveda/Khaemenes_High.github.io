Week 22: Oceanic Exploration and the Columbian Exchange

Essential Question: How did oceanic expansion create new connections while producing conquest, demographic collapse, forced labor, and ecological change?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Grade multicausal reasoning, source provenance, Indigenous agency, demographic/ecological qualification, institutional analysis, ethical reasoning, and map/data limits—not vocabulary recall.

Historical Safeguards

  • Do not reduce oceanic expansion to heroic individual explorers. Require maritime knowledge, crews, pilots, ports, state finance, commercial competition, ship technology, and prior Afro-Eurasian connections.
  • Do not describe conquest as an automatic European victory. Require Indigenous alliances, rivalries, translation, local politics, disease, military factors, contingency, resistance, and adaptation.
  • Eyewitness status does not make Columbus or other expedition narratives neutral. Source audience, purpose, language, political goals, and corroboration.
  • The Florentine Codex preserves crucial Nahua testimony and images but was compiled after the conquest through a colonial-era collaborative process; timing, mediation, language, and compilation context matter.
  • The Columbian Exchange includes plants, animals, pathogens, people, commodities, and knowledge. Movement does not automatically establish adoption, scale, or effect.
  • Demographic collapse should be explained through interacting mechanisms including pathogen exposure, warfare, forced labor, displacement, hunger, ecological disruption, and social breakdown, with regional variation and uncertain estimates.
  • Official encomienda law describes normative claims; lived labor conditions, coercion, resistance, and implementation require additional evidence.
  • Ethical analysis should distinguish intention, action, institutional structure, coercion, available choices, power, and consequence. Historical explanation does not erase harm.

Reading · Research · Evidence · Production Studio

Reading Lab: Use OpenStax Crossing the Atlantic for maritime systems and the Columbian Exchange; OpenStax European Colonization in the Americas for encomienda, settlement, labor, and Indigenous responses; and teacher-selected primary-source excerpts. The Library of Congress Florentine Codex Book XII is a high-value Indigenous/Nahua evidence source whose compilation context must be explicitly taught.

ARSHIF: Assemble approved voyage, encounter, alliance/resistance, biological-transfer, demographic, labor, legal, Indigenous, and colonial evidence. Preserve creator/community, date, place, language/translation, audience, genre, and provenance.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Build the chain: voyage → encounter → alliance/resistance → transfer → colonial institution → consequence. For each claim record evidence type, provenance, mechanism, limitation, confidence, and corroboration.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Map ports, routes, Indigenous states/communities, alliance zones, biological transfers, documented population evidence, and colonial institutions over time. Do not treat a route or transfer arrow as proof of volume, adoption, direct causation, or uniform impact.

PROSE: 850–1150 word synthesis: How did oceanic expansion create new global connections while producing conquest, demographic collapse, forced labor, and ecological change? Require Indigenous agency, at least three source categories, one traced mechanism, one institutional mechanism, and one evidentiary/demographic limitation.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require at least three specific pieces of evidence from at least three source categories; one traced voyage/encounter/transfer mechanism; Indigenous agency; one conquest, demographic, labor, or ecological consequence; and one source, map, or demographic limitation.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: classify the missed reasoning problem, reopen the relevant source or Atlas case, explain why the original reasoning failed, correct it using evidence, and demonstrate transfer on a new example before reassessment.
