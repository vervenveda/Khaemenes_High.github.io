Week 10: Byzantium and the Islamic World

Canonical student assessment: student-packet-v2.html · Canonical quiz: quiz.json

Essential Question: How did cities, states, religious/legal institutions, and multilingual scholarly networks preserve, debate, and create knowledge across regions?

Mastery: At least 80% (8/10 objective questions). Tool use alone never earns mastery; assignments and constructed response require evaluator judgment.

Historical Safeguards

  • Explain that “Byzantine Empire” is a later historical label for the eastern Roman Empire; its inhabitants generally understood themselves as Romans.
  • Do not collapse Islam, the Islamic world, and changing Muslim-ruled states into one timeless “Islamic Empire.”
  • Present sharia as a broad interpretive moral and legal tradition with historically varied jurisprudence and institutions, not one uniform modern-style statute book.
  • Distinguish legal ideals, scholarly interpretation, state policy, and lived practice. A legal text alone cannot establish universal experience.
  • Do not describe multilingual translation as passive copying. Track selection, terminology, commentary, criticism, synthesis, testing, and later use.
  • Avoid both “one civilization saved all knowledge” and false-equality narratives claiming every society contributed equally to every field.
  • Maps of knowledge routes show possible relationships, not automatic proof of direct contact, direction, frequency, volume, or causation.

Research · Evidence · Production Studio

ARSHIF: Use approved historical, philosophy, manuscript, faith/history, and world-classics corridors for contextual research. Require provenance and historical context; ARSHIF location does not itself validate a claim.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Require at least a Byzantine legal/institutional source, an Islamic legal or scholarly source, and one independent corroborating source. Students record origin, audience, purpose, claim relationship, useful evidence, limitation, and citation.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Trace one work, idea, technique, institution, or scholarly problem through at least three geographic, linguistic, or institutional contexts. Every uncertain or inferred connection must be labeled.

PROSE Editorial Suite: Recommended honors product: 700–1000 words answering “Does translation merely preserve knowledge, or can translation itself become intellectual creation?” Require thesis, cited evidence, preservation mechanism, transformation/innovation mechanism, qualification or uncertainty, revision, and conclusion.

Constructed Response Rubric

5 points: defensible claim; accurate evidence across more than one context; at least two source types; explanation of preservation and transformation/innovation; meaningful evidentiary limitation or uncertainty.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed concept, reopen the relevant evidence or network case, explain why the original answer failed, correct the reasoning in writing, and retake. Completion alone is not mastery.
