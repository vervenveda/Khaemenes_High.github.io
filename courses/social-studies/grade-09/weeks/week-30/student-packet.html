Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

After World War I, many countries faced debt, unstable borders, political violence, and contested democratic institutions. The 1929 financial crash deepened into a global depression as banks failed, demand fell, trade contracted, and unemployment soared.

People, Power, and Systems

Fascist movements promised unity, order, national revival, and destruction of political enemies. In Germany, Nazis used elections, elite bargains, emergency powers, propaganda, terror, and racial law to create a dictatorship. In the Soviet Union, Stalin imposed forced collectivization, rapid industrialization, purges, censorship, and mass repression.

Reading the Evidence

Propaganda works through repetition, emotion, symbols, selective truth, and control of alternatives. Students analyze it without reproducing dehumanizing messages uncritically. Economic data and private testimony reveal experiences that official posters conceal.

Why It Matters

Democracies responded differently through public works, welfare expansion, coalition politics, isolation, rearmament, and accommodation. Institutional choices mattered even under severe economic pressure.

Source Study: Propaganda and Economic Evidence

Compare political posters with unemployment data, legal changes, and personal testimony. Evaluate what each source reveals and conceals.

Vocabulary

Great DepressionA severe global economic contraction beginning in 1929.
fascismAn ultranationalist authoritarian ideology rejecting liberal democracy and political pluralism.
NazismThe German fascist movement centered on dictatorship, racial hierarchy, antisemitism, and expansion.
StalinismThe centralized, coercive political and economic order associated with Joseph Stalin’s rule.
appeasementA policy of making concessions to avoid conflict.
propagandaCommunication designed to shape attitudes or behavior, often selectively or emotionally.

Key Understandings

  • The Great Depression spread through interconnected finance, trade, production, and policy failures.
  • Authoritarian movements used crisis, propaganda, violence, nationalism, and institutional weakness to dismantle opposition.
  • Fascism, Nazism, Stalinism, militarism, and democratic responses must be distinguished rather than treated as identical.
