Assignment Guidance

1. Source Lab — Propaganda and Economic Evidence

Teacher note: Accept multiple interpretations when claims are supported. Do not award full evidence credit for summary without analysis.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong response will identify the source’s origin and audience, use at least two specific details, and explain how the source contributes to the question “Why did some societies respond to crisis with democratic reform while others turned toward authoritarian rule?” while naming one limitation.

2. Atlas and Data Investigation

Teacher note: Check labels, legend, scale or units, and whether the student distinguishes observation from explanation.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong product uses accurate labels and units, identifies a meaningful pattern, proposes an evidence-based explanation, and notes what the visualization cannot prove.

3. Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

Teacher note: Score the quality of reasoning rather than the political or moral position selected.

Sample-answer guidance: Answers may reasonably take different positions. Full-credit work directly answers “Can democratic institutions survive severe crisis without sacrificing rights?,” uses accurate evidence from at least two source types, and addresses a serious counterargument.

Constructed Response

Using at least three specific pieces of evidence, answer: Why did some societies respond to crisis with democratic reform while others turned toward authoritarian rule?

Exemplar: A full-credit response states a defensible answer to the essential question, uses three accurate details from the week, explains how each detail supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
