Week 1: How We Know the Past

Essential Question: How can we build a trustworthy account of the past when evidence is incomplete or disputed?

Mastery: All five daily lessons complete + all three principal assignments submitted + at least 80% on the ten-question objective quiz. Sub-80 attempts require review/corrections before retake. The constructed response is teacher-scored and belongs in the academic record.

Teacher Intent

This opening week teaches the method students will reuse throughout the course: distinguish evidence from inference, evaluate origin/audience/purpose/context, corroborate independent records, use spatial or quantitative evidence carefully, state uncertainty, and make claims no stronger than the evidence allows. The simulated town-meeting source set is a methods laboratory, not evidence about a real historical event.

Standards

Assignment Guidance

1 · Source Lab — Conflicting Eyewitness Accounts

20 points. Require OPVL, corroboration table, and a 150–250 word qualified claim. Accept multiple conclusions only when the evidence and reasoning support them.

Look for: origin and audience identified; at least two specific details; distinction between what the source shows and what the student infers; one limitation; one additional source that would help resolve uncertainty.

2 · Evidence Map & Reliability Matrix

20 points. Students map observer location and create a usefulness matrix. The map should be treated as evidence about position/visibility, not as proof of honesty or motive.

Look for: accurate labels, clear legend or key, observed pattern, plausible explanation, and explicit statement of what the visualization cannot prove.

3 · Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

30 points. Prompt: Is an openly biased source necessarily less useful than a source that presents itself as neutral?

Look for: defensible thesis, three specific pieces of evidence from at least two source types, serious counterclaim, qualification, and conclusion proportional to the evidence. Score reasoning, not political or moral agreement.

Safeguards & Mentor Use

Archaemenes may question, explain, compare, or guide source analysis, but the mentor does not award quiz credit or replace evaluator judgment. Students may use verification/research tools to test claims only when they document what they checked and where the evidence came from. AI-generated citations or invented evidence receive no credit.
