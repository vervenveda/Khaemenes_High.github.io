Week 25 · Atlantic Revolutions

Essential Question: Why did revolutions proclaim universal principles while producing unequal outcomes?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Grade multicausal explanation, ideals-versus-implementation analysis, comparative revolution, Haitian agency, continuity/change, source reasoning, and ethical evaluation.

Historical Safeguards

  • Do not reduce Atlantic revolutions to Enlightenment ideas alone; include fiscal crisis, imperial conflict, social hierarchy, organization, war, and local political traditions.
  • Declarations show claims and legitimacy arguments; constitutions, voting rules, property law, labor systems, and petitions show implementation and exclusion.
  • Center the Haitian Revolution as a major Atlantic revolution. Enslaved people were political actors who transformed the meaning of emancipation and citizenship.
  • Do not treat Latin American independence as one uniform movement or assume independence automatically produced social equality.
  • When judging revolutions, require explicit criteria. Do not reward a predetermined moral or political verdict.
  • Maps of revolutionary control or borders do not prove popular support, individual belief, or equal enforcement.

Reading Lab

Use the synchronized Reading Lab to compare Atlantic revolutions, selected declarations, excluded voices, and the 1805 Haitian Constitution. Require source provenance and distinguish revolutionary principle from implementation.

Research Studio

ARSHIF: Build a balanced source set including declarations, constitutions, petitions, military/political records, excluded voices, emancipation evidence, and post-revolution settlement records.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Track claim → actor → audience → universal principle → legal/political implementation → excluded group → contradiction/continuity → corroboration.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Map revolutionary centers, military campaigns, Atlantic connections, shifting control, and independence boundaries. Label time explicitly and preserve uncertainty.

PROSE: Explain why universal revolutionary principles produced unequal outcomes using at least three revolutions, one Haitian-centered comparison, and evidence of both change and continuity.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require a defensible thesis; at least three specific pieces of evidence from three source categories; comparison across at least two revolutions including Haiti; one example of expanded rights/sovereignty; one example of exclusion or continuity; and a clear causal explanation.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the reasoning error, return to the relevant source set, revise the claim, and transfer the corrected reasoning to a different revolution before reassessment.
