Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Atlantic revolutions grew from fiscal crisis, imperial conflict, Enlightenment arguments, local grievances, social inequality, and political organization. The American Revolution challenged British rule while preserving slavery in much of the new republic. The French Revolution attacked privilege and monarchy but passed through violence, war, and changing regimes.

People, Power, and Systems

In Saint-Domingue, enslaved people transformed a colonial uprising into the Haitian Revolution, defeated major empires, abolished slavery, and created an independent state. Across Latin America, independence movements involved creole elites, Indigenous communities, free and enslaved people, regional rivalries, and international war.

Reading the Evidence

Declarations reveal ideals, but constitutions, voting laws, property rules, and labor systems show implementation. Petitions by women, free people of color, enslaved people, and workers demonstrate how excluded groups interpreted revolutionary promises.

Why It Matters

Revolutions expand political possibility while creating new conflicts over who belongs to the people. Their legacies are best judged through both principle and lived outcome.

Source Study: Declarations and Excluded Voices

Compare revolutionary declarations with petitions or speeches from groups denied equal rights. Trace how the same language supports competing claims.

Vocabulary

revolutionA rapid and fundamental transformation in political or social order.
declarationA formal public statement of principles, grievances, or independence.
republicanismA political tradition emphasizing civic participation, public virtue, and government without hereditary monarchy.
nationalismPolitical identification with a nation and the claim that it should govern itself.
emancipationLegal release from slavery or another condition of unfreedom.
counterrevolutionA movement seeking to reverse a revolution.

Key Understandings

  • The American, French, Haitian, and Latin American revolutions influenced one another but arose from distinct social structures.
  • Rights declarations became tools for groups excluded by original revolutionary settlements.
  • The Haitian Revolution was the most successful large-scale revolt of enslaved people and transformed Atlantic politics.
