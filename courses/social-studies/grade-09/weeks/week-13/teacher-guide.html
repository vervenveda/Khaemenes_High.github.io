Week 13: East Asia, 600–1450

Essential Question: How did states and societies use learning, institutions, technology, markets, diplomacy, and adaptation to build authority—and who gained or lost access?

Mastery: 80% minimum. Grade historical reasoning, evidence handling, and qualification—not vocabulary recall or mere tool use.

Core Safeguards

  • Do not equate examinations with perfect meritocracy; preparation, wealth, gender, family networks, and access mattered.
  • Printing increased textual production and circulation but did not automatically create universal literacy.
  • Avoid technological determinism: printing, navigation, gunpowder, iron production, paper money, canals, and shipping operated within institutions, markets, labor systems, and political choices.
  • Do not describe Korea or Japan as simply “copying China.” Require evidence of selection, adaptation, rejection, and local innovation.
  • Do not reduce tributary relations to a single fixed system; ritual hierarchy, diplomacy, security, and commerce varied by partner and period.
  • A mapped route, shared institution, or similar artifact does not by itself prove direct transmission, control, or causation.

Research Studio

ARSHIF: Locate approved materials on Tang/Song administration, examinations, printing, commerce, Korea, Japan, diplomacy, technology, court culture, and warrior institutions. Preserve date, place, creator, genre, audience, and archive context.

Evidence & Citation Studio: Build a dossier from at least three source types or perspectives. Require claim, provenance, context, useful evidence, limitation, corroboration, and access/power implications.

Atlas Evidence Analysis: Use Protools/Atlas_Evidence_Analysis.html. Students may organize capitals, waterways, routes, diplomatic nodes, claims, chronology, evidence status, provenance, and alternative interpretations. Atlas organizes evidence and uncertainty; it does not decide historical truth.

PROSE: 700–1000 word honors report: How did East Asian societies adapt institutions and technologies to local political and economic goals, and why is “copying” an inadequate explanation?

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Require a defensible claim; at least three specific pieces of evidence; at least two East Asian settings; explanation of adaptation rather than simple diffusion; and one limitation, unequal-access issue, or alternative interpretation.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed reasoning concept, reopen the relevant source/Atlas dossier, explain why the original answer failed, correct it with evidence, and retake. Do not substitute vocabulary copying for correction.
