Week 8: Rome and Han China

Essential Question: How did large empires build durable institutions, and why did those institutions eventually weaken or change?

Mastery: At least 80% (8/10 objective questions). Constructed response and assignments require evaluator judgment.

Historical Safeguards

  • Do not treat Rome and Han China as identical simply because both governed large empires.
  • Do not project the fully developed examination system of later imperial China backward onto the Han without qualification.
  • Do not present the Silk Roads as one literal road or imply routine direct Rome-to-Han-capital travel; emphasize connected routes and intermediaries.
  • Treat “Romanization” as uneven spread, negotiation, and local adaptation rather than automatic cultural replacement.
  • Do not equate Roman citizenship categories with Han political or social membership.
  • Avoid monocausal “collapse” narratives. Separate long-term pressures, triggers, feedback loops, political fragmentation, and continuity.
  • Remember that western Roman imperial government fragmented while the eastern Roman Empire continued for centuries.

Assignment Guidance

1 · Law, Office, and Frontier Life: Compare Roman legal or administrative evidence with Han administrative evidence and local/frontier evidence. Require origin, audience, purpose, institutional function, useful evidence, limitation, and corroboration. A capital decree alone does not prove local implementation.

2 · Atlas and Frontier Investigation: Map Roman roads/sea lanes and Han roads, river systems, frontier defenses, and exchange corridors. Require a comparison of one Roman and one Han communication or frontier system plus a statement of what the visualization cannot prove.

3 · Durable Rule Argument: Evaluate whether law/status, officials, military infrastructure, or local elite cooperation mattered most. Require evidence from both empires and a serious counterclaim. Score historical reasoning rather than the selected factor.

Constructed Response Rubric

Prompt: Using evidence from Rome and Han China, explain how large empires built durable institutions and why those institutions later weakened, fragmented, or changed. Identify at least one important continuity after political fragmentation.

5 points: 1 defensible comparative claim; 2 accurate evidence across both empires; 1 use of at least two source types or explicit causal reasoning; 1 meaningful continuity after fragmentation.

Correction Protocol

Below 8/10: identify the missed concept, revisit the relevant evidence, explain why the original answer failed, and retake. Do not award mastery for completion alone.
