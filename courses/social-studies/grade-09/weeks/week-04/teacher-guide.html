Week 4: Rivers, Cities, Law, and Government

Essential Question: Why did early cities require new systems of law, labor, recordkeeping, and authority?

Mastery: Students must earn at least 80% on the 10-question objective quiz. Constructed responses and assignments require evaluator judgment; completion is not a substitute for academic quality.

Historical safeguards

  • Do not teach that rivers mechanically “caused civilization.” Geography creates opportunities and constraints; institutions and human choices vary.
  • Do not present the collection associated with Hammurabi as a modern statute book or proof that every case was enforced exactly as written.
  • Distinguish written law from the broader principle of rule of law.
  • Teach state capacity and inequality together: administration can coordinate public works and reserves while also concentrating resources and power.
  • Use “civilization” as the course-defined analytical term, not as a ranking of peoples as civilized/uncivilized.

Assignment Guidance

Assignment 1 — Source Lab: Compare selected paraphrased Mesopotamian legal cases with an Egyptian labor or tax record. Require origin/purpose/value/limitation, two specific details, and a corroboration question. Do not award full evidence credit for summary alone.

Assignment 2 — Atlas and Data Investigation: Check accurate river/city labels and the grain-allocation model. Students must identify assumptions and explain how the same surplus system can support collective capacity and unequal control.

Assignment 3 — Evidence-Based Argument: Accept defensible conclusions that written law made society more just, more predictable, both, or neither when evidence and reasoning support the position. Score reasoning, not viewpoint.

Constructed Response

Students explain why early cities developed new systems of law, labor, recordkeeping, and authority using at least two specific Week 4 details, one coordination benefit, and one inequality/concentrated-power consequence.

5-point rubric: 1 claim; 2 accurate evidence; 1 reasoning connection; 1 qualification/complexity. Human evaluator required.

80% Correction Protocol

Below 8/10 objective questions: identify missed concept(s), revisit the corresponding daily lesson, write a correction explaining why the original answer failed, then retake. Do not unlock progression through checkbox completion alone.
