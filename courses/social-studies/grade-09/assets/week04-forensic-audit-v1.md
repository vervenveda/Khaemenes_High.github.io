## Historical-integrity findings
- River geography is presented as opportunity/constraint rather than deterministic cause.
- Mesopotamian and Egyptian developments are compared without asserting identical political outcomes.
- Hammurabi-associated legal material is treated as evidence of legal ideals/categories, not proof of uniform enforcement or everyday experience.
- Written law is distinguished from the broader modern principle of rule of law.
- Administrative capacity and inequality are presented as potentially coexisting outcomes.
- Bureaucracy, taxation/tribute, labor, public works, and recordkeeping are treated as institutional responses to coordination problems rather than inevitable stages of civilization.
- The term “civilization” is explicitly treated analytically rather than as a ranking of peoples.
- The student argument permits multiple defensible conclusions when supported by evidence.
