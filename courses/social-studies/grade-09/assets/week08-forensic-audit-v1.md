## Scope
Rome and Han China, c. 200 BCE–500 CE.

## Historical-integrity findings
- Rome is presented as political transformation with institutional continuity rather than a sudden switch from republic to empire.
- Han administration is not retrofitted with the fully developed examination system of much later imperial China.
- Silk Roads are correctly treated as interconnected networks with multiple routes and intermediaries, not a single Rome-to-Han road.
- Romanization is treated as uneven spread, negotiation, and local adaptation rather than cultural replacement.
- Roman citizenship and Han political/social membership are kept analytically distinct.
- Imperial infrastructure is treated as enabling movement and administration without guaranteeing uniform control.
- “Collapse” is rejected as a sufficient monocausal explanation; interacting pressures and post-fragmentation continuities are required.
- Western Roman political fragmentation is distinguished from the continued eastern Roman Empire.
