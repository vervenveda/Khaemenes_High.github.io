# Week 10 Forensic Audit v1

## Status

## Scope
Byzantium and the Islamic World; c. 500–1258 CE.

## Historical-integrity findings
- The eastern Roman/Byzantine polity is presented as continuity plus change, not a wholly new state unrelated to Rome.
- “Byzantine” is treated as a later historical label and the Roman self-understanding of inhabitants is preserved.
- Islam, the Islamic world, and changing Muslim-ruled political formations are kept distinct; no timeless “Islamic Empire” is implied.
- Sharia is presented as a broad interpretive moral/legal tradition with varied historical jurisprudence, not one uniform statute book.
- Normative/legal texts are separated from evidence of lived practice and implementation.
- Translation is treated as selective preservation plus possible transformation through terminology, commentary, criticism, synthesis, testing, and later use.
- The curriculum avoids both a single-civilization “saved all knowledge” narrative and an artificial claim that every society contributed equally to every field.
- Knowledge-network maps are explicitly limited: they do not alone prove direction, frequency, volume, direct contact, or causation.
