Return to Course

Reading Protocol

For every assigned reading record: author/institution, date or historical period, genre, intended audience, central claim, two pieces of evidence, one limitation, one corroboration question, and one connection to the week's essential question. A linked source is evidence to evaluate—not an authority to copy.

Access: The core spine prioritizes free/open resources. Teachers should assign excerpts rather than entire difficult primary texts when appropriate for Grade 9.

Week 22 · Oceanic Expansion, Encounter & Columbian Exchange

Reading focus: Move beyond explorer biography. Trace the systems that enabled voyages, preserve Indigenous agency during conquest, distinguish movement from consequence in the Columbian Exchange, and compare colonial law with implementation.

ReadingTypeCurriculum synchronization
OpenStax · 5.2 Crossing the AtlanticCore secondaryMonday/Wednesday: maritime knowledge, state/commercial motives, Atlantic crossings, Treaty of Tordesillas, and Columbian Exchange. Test broad causal claims against source evidence.
OpenStax · 6.1 European Colonization in the AmericasCore secondaryThursday: encomienda, colonial settlement, labor systems, geography/economics, and varied Indigenous responses. Separate institutional description from lived practice.
Library of Congress · Florentine Codex, Book XII: The Conquest of MexicoPrimary/visual Indigenous-source labTuesday: Nahua accounts and images of conquest collected after the events. Analyze Indigenous testimony together with Sahagún's colonial compilation context, language, mediation, and chronology.
Library of Congress · Florentine Codex image sequenceVisual/material extensionAnalyze selected images as evidence: observation first, then interpretation, provenance, and corroboration.
Teacher safeguard: The Library of Congress identifies Book XII as preserving accounts from Indigenous elders who had lived through the conquest, gathered decades later within Sahagún's Franciscan project. Treat that provenance as part of the lesson rather than presenting the Codex as an unmediated transcript.
