Assignment Guidance

1. Source Lab — Laws, Testimony, and Bureaucratic Records

Teacher note: Accept multiple interpretations when claims are supported. Do not award full evidence credit for summary without analysis.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong response will identify the source’s origin and audience, use at least two specific details, and explain how the source contributes to the question “How did dictatorship, war, bureaucracy, racism, and individual choices make mass persecution and genocide possible?” while naming one limitation.

2. Atlas and Data Investigation

Teacher note: Check labels, legend, scale or units, and whether the student distinguishes observation from explanation.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong product uses accurate labels and units, identifies a meaningful pattern, proposes an evidence-based explanation, and notes what the visualization cannot prove.

3. Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

Teacher note: Score the quality of reasoning rather than the political or moral position selected.

Sample-answer guidance: Answers may reasonably take different positions. Full-credit work directly answers “How should schools balance the need to understand perpetrators’ systems with the duty to preserve victims’ humanity?,” uses accurate evidence from at least two source types, and addresses a serious counterargument.

Constructed Response

Using at least two specific pieces of evidence, answer: How did dictatorship, war, bureaucracy, racism, and individual choices make mass persecution and genocide possible?

Exemplar: A full-credit response states a defensible answer to the essential question, uses two accurate details from the week, explains how each detail supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
