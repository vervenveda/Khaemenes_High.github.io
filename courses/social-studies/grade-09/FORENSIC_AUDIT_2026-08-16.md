# Grade 9 Global Studies Honors — Forensic Curriculum Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-16  
**Branch:** `hardening/archaemenes-highschool`  
**Status:** ACTIVE REPAIR — not yet A+++ certified

## Executive finding

The Grade 9 Global Studies Honors package is structurally large and ambitious, but its current generated student portal does not yet meet the Academy's forensic A+++ standard.

The structural counts are real — 36 weeks, 180 Monday–Friday lesson slots, 108 principal assignments, weekly quizzes, a midterm, a final, and a civic capstone — but the forensic inspection found that many of those slots are generated from a small set of repeated substantive templates.

A structural count therefore must not be treated as proof of curricular uniqueness.

## Defects confirmed

### 1. Mastery target conflict

The current generated portal sets:

- `passingTarget: 70`
- `honorsTarget: 85`

The Academy-wide formal progression requirement is **80% mastery**.

The hardened formal Grade 09 course will therefore use 80% as the advancement threshold. An optional honors distinction may remain above that level, but it cannot reduce the Academy mastery gate.

### 2. Repeated weekly lesson bodies

Across the year, the same five substantive lesson shells recur with topic names substituted:

1. Monday — `Frame the Inquiry`
2. Tuesday — `Read Like a Historian`
3. Wednesday — `Map, Measure, and Compare`
4. Thursday — `Discuss, Debate, and Write`
5. Friday — `Synthesize and Demonstrate`

A predictable weekly rhythm is useful. Repeating nearly identical warmups, lesson bodies, assignment wording, and evidence descriptions for unrelated historical periods is not sufficient for A+++ approval.

### 3. Repeated assignment bodies

Each week repeats the same three principal assignments:

- `Source Lab`
- `Atlas & Data Investigation`
- `Evidence-Based Argument`

The topic title is substituted, but the learner task remains nearly identical.

The repair will preserve source analysis, map/data work, and argument as recurring disciplinary practices while varying the actual source set, evidence structure, data problem, historical method, and product.

### 4. Shared five-question quiz banks

Weeks point to small shared banks such as:

- inquiry
- geography
- civics
- economics
- belief
- conflict
- empires
- ideas
- history

Many weeks therefore receive exactly the same five objective questions.

This means a learner can encounter the same quiz repeatedly in different historical contexts.

### 5. Midterm and final reuse weekly-bank items

The cumulative exams reuse objective questions directly from the same shared weekly banks.

This creates exact repetition between weekly practice and high-stakes assessment and materially reduces assessment validity.

### 6. Correct-answer position bias

A large proportion of the current bank uses answer index `0` as the correct response.

Even when stems differ, this creates a test-taking pattern that can be learned without content mastery.

The forensic repair requires distributed answer positions and no systematic positional cue.

### 7. Independent local learner identity

The current portal still creates `First Student`, Demo Student, Add/Delete Student records under a shared browser key.

That conflicts with the Academy continuity architecture.

The hardened entry will use the active formal Grade 09 learner from the Academy Family Registry and store Global Studies records by Academy learner ID. Grade 08 advanced preview remains exploratory and cannot create formal Grade 09 mastery.

## A+++ repair standard

The repaired course must retain the existing chronological and global breadth while giving each week a distinct inquiry experience.

Examples of the required differentiation include:

- Agricultural Revolution — archaeology evidence matrix, domestication/settlement map, health/inequality trade-off analysis;
- Persia and the Greek World — imperial administration comparison, primary-source sourcing, citizenship/participation analysis;
- Rome and Han China — bureaucracy/frontier comparison and institutional endurance model;
- Indian Ocean — monsoon-route map, merchant evidence, exchange-network causal model;
- Mongol expansion and Black Death — network-flow map, mortality evidence, source corroboration, systems causation;
- Renaissance/printing — image/text source comparison, print diffusion evidence, communication-network analysis;
- Atlantic slavery — voyage data, testimony/source ethics, coerced-labor system map, resistance analysis;
- Industrial Revolution — production/urbanization data, labor evidence, competing measures of improvement;
- Imperialism — map changes, economic evidence, resistance perspectives, competing causal explanations;
- World Wars / Holocaust / genocide studies — careful chronology, institutional evidence, perpetrator/bystander/resistance analysis, survivor-source protocols;
- Cold War — proxy-conflict mapping, competing source perspectives, systems model;
- decolonization — self-determination sources, independence timelines, comparative state-building;
- globalization — trade/development/migration datasets with explicit limitations;
- comparative government/media literacy — lateral reading, institutional comparison, source triangulation;
- civic capstone — nonpartisan evidence file, stakeholder map, options matrix, public product, reflection.

## Formal 80% progression target

The hardened course will enforce:

- Week 1 available to the eligible Grade 09 learner;
- Monday → Friday instructional sequence;
- weekly mastery check opens only after the required weekly learning/evidence is reached;
- next week opens only after the current mastery check reaches **80% or higher**;
- midterm opens after the first-semester mastery gates;
- Semester II opens only after the midterm reaches **80% or higher**;
- final opens only after the full-year mastery sequence and required midterm gate;
- friendly reminders explain locked states;
- reassessment remains available;
- preview access does not create formal mastery.

For an honors course, substantial assignments should be completed before the weekly mastery check is treated as the week's formal mastery evidence.

## Resource federation targets

Existing Verve N Veda resources appropriate to this course include:

- ARSHIF — historical/cultural background and archival research;
- The Verifier — source comparison and media literacy;
- World Wire / Global News Digest — contemporary connection work;
- Evidence & Citation Studio — research provenance and citation;
- PLERA Search — research discovery;
- Ancients Trivia — low-stakes ancient-world retrieval practice;
- Civic Compass Trivia — low-stakes civics retrieval practice;
- High School Voter Portal — nonpartisan civic participation study;
- Finance Hall / Budget Planner — economic reasoning and applied financial contexts.

These should be assigned with a specific learning purpose rather than displayed as decorative links.

## Suggested ecosystem gaps

High-value reusable additions would include:

- **Source Sleuth** — sourcing, corroboration, lateral reading, reliability, and citation;
- **Timeline Atlas** — chronology + map + migration/trade/empire layers;
- **Civic Decision Lab** — nonpartisan stakeholder, constitutional, evidence, and trade-off simulation;
- **Data Detective** — graph/data interpretation and misleading-display analysis across Social Studies, Science, and Math.

## Current verdict

**Substantial foundation; forensic repair required.**

The chronological/global scope is worth preserving. The principal A+++ blockers are substantive lesson repetition, assignment repetition, shared quiz reuse, repeated exam items, answer-position bias, the old 70% progression target, and the independent local student identity.
