Week 3: Early Humanity and the Agricultural Revolution

Essential Question: Did agriculture improve human life, or did it exchange some freedoms for new forms of security and inequality?

Mastery: 80% or higher on the 10-question objective quiz. Students below mastery complete corrections before retaking. The constructed response is teacher-scored and remains part of the academic record.

Standards

  • D1.5.9-12 — Determine useful source types for historical questions.
  • D2.His.1.9-12 — Evaluate historical developments in context.
  • D3.1.9-12 — Gather and evaluate evidence using origin, context, and corroborative value.
  • D2.Geo.4.9-12 — Analyze interactions between human and physical systems.
  • D4.1.9-12 — Construct evidence-based arguments acknowledging complexity.
  • RH.9-10.1 / RH.9-10.7 / WHST.9-10.1 — Cite evidence, integrate quantitative/geographic evidence, and write discipline-specific arguments.

Historical Safeguards

  • Do not present Paleolithic lifeways as primitive or incomplete versions of farming societies.
  • Do not teach agriculture as a single invention spreading from one universal center; the course evidence supports multiple independent centers and long mixed subsistence transitions.
  • Avoid geographic determinism. Environment shaped possibilities and constraints but did not force identical social outcomes.
  • Do not infer a monarchy, class system, gender hierarchy, or specific institution from one artifact pattern alone. Require corroboration.

Assignment Guidance

1. Archaeological Evidence from an Early Village

Full-credit work distinguishes observation from inference, uses at least two material clues, identifies context/value/limitation, and names one corroborating source or evidence type.

2. Atlas and Data Investigation

Students map multiple domestication centers and compare mobility, diet, labor, population, disease, storage, land claims, and inequality. Reward qualified interpretation rather than a universal before/after story.

3. Evidence-Based Argument

Accept “improvement,” “tradeoff,” or “uneven transformation” positions when supported. Score evidence, reasoning, accuracy, complexity, and communication—not the conclusion selected.

Constructed Response Rubric · 5 points

Prompt: Using at least two specific pieces of Week 3 evidence, answer the essential question. Identify one benefit, one cost, and one limitation or uncertainty.

  • Evidence — 2: two accurate, specific Week 3 details.
  • Reasoning — 2: explains how evidence supports a defensible claim and addresses benefit/cost.
  • Accuracy & Complexity — 1: avoids universal claims and identifies uncertainty, variation, or an evidence limit.
