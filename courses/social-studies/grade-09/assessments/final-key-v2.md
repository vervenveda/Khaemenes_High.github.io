## Teacher Key & Scoring Guide

### Purpose
This final is intentionally different from a vocabulary-recall exam. It measures the disciplinary skills developed across the 36-week course: evidence evaluation, corroboration, contextualization, geography/data reasoning, causation, comparison, continuity/change, source criticism, argumentation, and civic transfer.

### Objective scoring
40 questions × 1 point = **40 points**.

## Part II — Source Analysis

### Question 41 — 10 points
A full-credit response should:
- distinguish official announcement, local administrative report, merchant petition, and archaeological evidence;
- explain what each source directly establishes and what it does not establish;
- recognize that the official report may document administrative claims or observations but does not by itself prove universal compliance;
- use corroboration across at least three source types;
- explain the disagreement rather than choosing a source solely because it is official or local;
- identify a limitation such as incomplete geographic coverage, selective survival, author purpose, or uncertainty in archaeological interpretation.

**Rubric:** Origin/context 2 · Evidence 3 · Corroboration 3 · Limitation/qualification 2.

### Question 42 — 10 points
A full-credit response should:
- identify that correlation between trade expansion and population change does not establish causation;
- explain at least two plausible mechanisms, such as employment, food supply, market access, migration, infrastructure, taxation, or political security;
- recognize that the declining node is an important counterexample to the claim of universal prosperity;
- offer an alternative explanation such as warfare, environmental change, disease, political disruption, route displacement, or local resource constraints;
- qualify the historian's claim rather than simply accepting or rejecting it.

**Rubric:** Evidence interpretation 3 · Causal mechanism 3 · Alternative explanation 2 · Qualification 2.

## Part III — Comparative Historical Reasoning

### Question 43 — 20 points
Accept many defensible comparisons. Appropriate examples include:
- Rome and Han administration;
- different imperial approaches to membership;
- medieval European political authority compared with another regional system;
- Indian Ocean and Silk Roads networks;
- different revolutionary experiences;
- industrialization in different regions;
- democratic and authoritarian responses to crisis.

Full-credit work must compare rather than write two separate mini-essays. The student should explain why a similar problem produced different outcomes and include a genuine qualification or counterexample.

**Rubric:**
- Thesis: 4
- Specific evidence from both cases: 6
- Meaningful comparison: 4
- Causal/contextual reasoning: 4
- Qualification/counterexample: 2

## Part IV — Cumulative DBQ / Civic Transfer

### Question 44 — 20 points
Students may use any four or more defensible course periods/regions. Strong responses may connect examples such as:
- early archaeological source limitations;
- law and official rhetoric versus implementation;
- Roman/Han state capacity;
- Indian Ocean or Silk Roads networks;
- political legitimacy and revolutionary rights claims;
- industrialization and competing accounts of social change;
- imperial modernization rhetoric versus local experience;
- propaganda and interwar crisis;
- modern quantitative/media/AI source evaluation.

The historical examples must do analytical work. A list of historical facts does not satisfy the prompt.

The student should explicitly distinguish analogy from proof: a historical example can illuminate a mechanism or warning without proving that a modern situation will produce the same outcome.

**Rubric:**
- Thesis: 4
- Evidence: 5
- Historical reasoning/mechanisms: 5
- Counterexample/qualification: 3
- Civic transfer: 2
- Limitation/uncertainty: 1

## Mastery Gate

**80% is the minimum mastery threshold.** A student scoring below 80% completes targeted correction and a new reassessment. Do not reuse the exact questions as the mastery reassessment.

### Error coding
Use one or more codes:

- K = foundational knowledge
- E = evidence analysis
- G = geography/data
- C = causation
- CP = comparison
- CC = continuity/change
- S = source evaluation
- A = argumentation
- T = civic transfer

### Important assessment principle
A student who can memorize terminology but cannot interpret unfamiliar evidence, explain causation, compare societies, qualify claims, or construct an evidence-based argument has **not demonstrated full course mastery**.

The final should therefore be scored for both accuracy and disciplinary reasoning.
