## Historical-integrity checks
- Paleolithic mobility is presented as an adaptive lifeway, not a deficiency or failed stage.
- Agricultural transition is presented as gradual and regionally varied rather than a single worldwide event.
- Multiple centers of domestication are explicitly represented.
- Mixed subsistence (cultivation, herding, hunting, gathering, fishing) is preserved.
- Archaeological observations are separated from inferences.
- Material evidence is not allowed to prove a specific political institution, class system, gender hierarchy, or other broad social claim without corroboration.
- Benefits and costs of agriculture are presented together: storage, population and specialization alongside labor demands, disease exposure, harvest dependence, land conflict, and inequality.
- Geographic determinism is explicitly rejected.
- Student arguments are scored for evidence and reasoning, not for choosing a preferred moral conclusion.
