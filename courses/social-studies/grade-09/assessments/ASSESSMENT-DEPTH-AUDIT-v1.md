# Grade 9 Social Studies Assessment-Depth Audit v1

Date: 2026-08-22

## Purpose
Verify that assessments measure the depth of the curriculum rather than merely recognizing broad vocabulary or generic historical principles.

## Standard
A strong weekly assessment must sample the actual week's content and disciplinary skills, require application/reasoning, distinguish evidence from inference, preserve the 80% mastery gate, and provide a meaningful correction/retake path.

## Major course-level finding: Final Examination

The current final examination is **NOT yet at the depth of the curriculum**.

Its first major section is dominated by definition/term-recognition multiple choice (e.g., primary source, absolute location, Paleolithic, civilization, urban planning, monotheism, satrapy, Mauryan Empire, Byzantine Empire, Silk Roads, feudalism, civil-service examination, natural rights, industrialization, capitalism, imperialism, militarism, Great Depression, etc.).

Vocabulary recognition is useful as a component, but it cannot serve as the principal measure of a year-long Global Studies Honors curriculum built around sourcing, corroboration, causation, comparison, systems analysis, maps/data, networks, primary sources, competing interpretations, and uncertainty.

The existing Semester 1 midterm is substantially stronger: it uses unfamiliar stimuli, source sets, implementation questions, comparative reasoning, network reasoning, and a cumulative DBQ. Therefore the midterm is closer to the target assessment philosophy than the current final.

## Required final-exam standard
The final should be rebuilt around a balanced blueprint rather than simply expanding vocabulary questions:

- 20–25% foundational knowledge/terminology
- 35–40% stimulus-based historical reasoning
- 15–20% map/data/network interpretation
- 20–25% constructed comparative/cause-and-effect reasoning
- At least one cumulative DBQ or evidence dossier
- Multiple source types
- Explicit source provenance and limitations
- Cross-regional comparison
- Continuity/change and causation
- At least one counterclaim or contradictory evidence task
- 80% mastery gate with stable question IDs and correction/retake protocol

## Important invariant
A student should not be able to pass the final by memorizing the glossary while failing to demonstrate the actual historical reasoning taught throughout the course.
