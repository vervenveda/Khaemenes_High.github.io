Capitalism, Socialism, Labor, and Reform

Essential Question: What should markets, governments, employers, workers, and communities each control?

Throughline

Economic systems → incentives and institutions → worker experience → reform strategies → distributional effects → model → historical corroboration → qualified argument.

Five-Day Pacing

  1. Monday: Build the historical frame; distinguish capitalism, laissez-faire, socialism, Marxism, and labor reform without treating labels as single fixed systems.
  2. Tuesday: Source Economic Ideas and Worker Demands; compare origin, purpose, mechanism, proposed solution, value, and limitation.
  3. Wednesday: Complete the Economic Reasoning Lab. Run controlled supply/demand tests; record predictions, results, distributional effects, and model limitations.
  4. Thursday: Construct and revise an evidence-based institutional argument; require a serious counterclaim.
  5. Friday: Complete Assessment v2.0 and reasoning corrections. Mastery threshold: 80%.

Economic Lab Protocol

Define institution → state claim → change one variable → record result → identify distributional effects → identify limitation → corroborate with historical evidence.

Emphasize that the Supply & Demand Simulator models a simplified relationship. It does not prove a particular nineteenth-century historical outcome. Students must compare model behavior with historical sources and data.

Constructed Response Rubric

  • 5: Defensible institutional argument; three accurate historical details; explains distributional effects; addresses counterclaim; identifies limitation.
  • 4: Defensible claim; three relevant details; counterclaim or limitation mostly developed.
  • 3: Claim with two relevant details and some reasoning.
  • 2: General claim with limited evidence.
  • 1: Minimal or unsupported response.
  • 0: No relevant response.

Correction Workflow

Students below 80% identify the reasoning error, write corrected reasoning with evidence/rule, and explain how the corrected strategy transfers to a similar question before retrying.

Instructional Guardrail

Score historical reasoning, sourcing, evidence, tradeoff analysis, and clarity—not political agreement. Students may reach different conclusions when their claims are historically supported and intellectually qualified.
