Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

After World War II, the United States and Soviet Union emerged with different political-economic systems and deep mutual suspicion. Disputes over Eastern Europe, Germany, reconstruction, and atomic weapons hardened into competing alliances and security structures.

People, Power, and Systems

Nuclear weapons made direct superpower war potentially catastrophic. Deterrence depended on credibility, communication, and assumptions about rational behavior. Arms races coexisted with diplomacy, treaties, espionage, science, culture, and space competition.

Reading the Evidence

Proxy wars were never merely pieces on a superpower chessboard. Local movements pursued independence, revolution, state-building, ethnic power, land reform, or regime survival. Outside intervention altered those conflicts but did not create every grievance.

Why It Matters

The Cold War ended through economic strain, reform, popular movements, nationalism within the Soviet bloc, leadership choices, and changing international conditions. Its institutions and memories continue to shape global politics.

Source Study: Competing Speeches and a Crisis File

Compare U.S., Soviet, and nonaligned speeches, then analyze a crisis timeline. Identify security claims, ideology, and missing local perspectives.

Vocabulary

Cold WarA prolonged global rivalry led by the United States and Soviet Union without direct full-scale war between them.
containmentA strategy intended to limit the expansion of Soviet influence.
nuclear deterrenceThe attempt to prevent attack through the threat of devastating retaliation.
proxy warA conflict in which outside powers support opposing sides rather than fight one another directly.
nonalignmentA policy of avoiding formal alignment with major Cold War blocs.
détenteA period or policy of reduced tension between rival states.

Key Understandings

  • The Cold War combined ideology, security fears, nuclear weapons, alliances, economics, propaganda, and decolonization.
  • Conflicts in Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan, Africa, Latin America, and the Middle East had local causes as well as superpower involvement.
  • Nonaligned states pursued their own interests and challenged a simple two-bloc interpretation.
