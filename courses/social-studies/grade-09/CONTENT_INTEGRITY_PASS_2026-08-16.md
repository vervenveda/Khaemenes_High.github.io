## What was reviewed

The 36 week-specific objective banks, independent midterm, and independent final were inspected for high-confidence factual/key defects, misleading historical compression, anachronism, deterministic-history framing, false single-cause explanations, weak source-evaluation habits, and language that could erase local agency.

The bank is intentionally written around historical reasoning rather than trivia alone. Most items already use defensible language such as `best`, `most directly`, `often`, `can`, and `should`, and repeatedly require sourcing, corroboration, contextualization, geographic reasoning, comparison, causation, uncertainty, and evidence limits.

## Repairs made in this pass

A small post-bank correction layer now replaces five items before answer-position balancing occurs.

### Week 13 — imperial Chinese examinations

Earlier wording risked implying one unchanged examination regime across all imperial Chinese history. The revised item says **later imperial China** and explains that examination systems expanded across dynasties while recruitment practices still varied by period.

### Week 20 — Peace of Westphalia

Earlier wording was broadly defensible but could reinforce the oversimplified idea that Westphalia single-handedly created modern state sovereignty. The revised item identifies the 1648 settlements with ending the Thirty Years' War and revising territorial, constitutional, and confessional arrangements while explicitly rejecting the `single birth certificate of sovereignty` shortcut.

### Week 30 — Great Depression

The previous prompt was circular: it asked when the Depression began using `after a severe global economic crisis` as the answer. The replacement assesses multicausal historical reasoning and distinguishes the 1929 crash from the broader banking, demand, trade, policy, and international financial stresses that deepened the Depression.

### Week 31 — Holocaust terminology

The revised item uses more precise language: the Holocaust specifically refers to Nazi Germany's systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million European Jews, carried out with collaborators, while also recognizing that Nazi persecution and mass murder targeted other groups under distinct policies and programs.

### Week 32 — proxy-war framing

The earlier definition centered great-power sponsorship but risked making local participants sound like passive instruments. The revised item preserves the concept of outside sponsorship while explicitly retaining local causes, goals, and agency.

## No broad rewrite was justified

The remaining weekly bank is generally cautious about:

- treating chronology as causation;
- geographic determinism;
- uniform `feudalism` claims;
- one-source narratives;
- one-cause explanations of imperial collapse, epidemic spread, revolution, industrialization, imperialism, war, authoritarianism, and decolonization;
- reducing colonized, enslaved, Indigenous, or local actors to passive recipients of outside action;
- treating maps, law codes, propaganda, travel accounts, oral traditions, testimony, or statistics as self-interpreting evidence;
- claiming that trade/globalization benefits all groups equally;
- treating economic systems as pure textbook categories;
- confusing correlation with causation;
- using GDP per capita as a complete measure of development.

That is a strength and should be preserved during later editing.
