About Government, Law & Economics Honors

Government, Law & Economics Honors integrates constitutional government, legal reasoning, civil liberties, elections, public policy, and economic analysis into one evidence-based junior course. Students examine how institutions distribute authority, how rights are protected and contested, how markets and public policy interact, and how responsible citizens evaluate competing claims, tradeoffs, and uncertainty.

Course Features

Constitution, Law & Institutions Founding principles, federalism, Congress, the presidency, agencies, courts, judicial review, legal reasoning, due process, and constitutional structure develop civic and legal literacy.
Rights, Elections & Public Life Civil liberties, equal protection, voting, parties, public opinion, media, interest groups, comparative government, and civic participation connect institutions to democratic life.
Microeconomics & Macroeconomics Scarcity, markets, firms, labor, competition, taxes, public goods, GDP, inflation, unemployment, money, banking, monetary policy, fiscal policy, and trade build economic reasoning.
Policy Analysis & Civic Capstone Students evaluate sources, data, authority, tradeoffs, distribution, implementation risk, and counterarguments before producing evidence-based public recommendations and a final civic-economic capstone.
