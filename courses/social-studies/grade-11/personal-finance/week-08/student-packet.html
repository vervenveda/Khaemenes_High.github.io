Loans, Interest, and Major Purchases

Essential question: How should a borrower compare loans for vehicles, education, housing, or other major purchases?

Key understandings

  • A longer term may lower the monthly payment while increasing total interest and time in debt.
  • Secured loans may have lower rates because collateral reduces lender loss, but the borrower risks repossession or foreclosure.
  • Loan applications require truthful information and should be evaluated through total cost, affordability, and alternatives.

Vocabulary

principalThe amount borrowed or remaining loan balance before interest and fees.
interestThe price paid for using borrowed money.
collateralProperty pledged to secure a loan.
amortizationRepayment of principal and interest through scheduled payments over time.
loan termThe length of time allowed for repayment.
down paymentMoney paid upfront, reducing the amount financed.

Weekly Reading

The Payment Is Not the Price

Dealers and lenders can make a purchase appear affordable by extending the term. Total financed cost includes price, taxes, fees, interest, add-ons, insurance requirements, and possible negative equity.

Secured and Unsecured Credit

Collateral can reduce lender risk and interest but exposes property to repossession. Unsecured loans rely more heavily on creditworthiness and may cost more.

Amortization

Early payments on many loans contain more interest because the balance is higher. Extra principal can reduce future interest when the contract permits it without penalty.

Application and Underwriting

Lenders review income, debt, credit, collateral, down payment, and documentation. Borrowers should never falsify information and should compare preapproval, rate, term, fees, and total cost before signing.
