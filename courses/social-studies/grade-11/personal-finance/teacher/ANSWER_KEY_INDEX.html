Week 1 · Financial Values, Goals, and Decision Biases
#AnswerRationale
1DFinancial goal means a specific, measurable, time-bound outcome involving money or resources.
2CTradeoff means a sacrifice of one benefit in order to obtain another.
3DPresent bias means the tendency to overweight immediate rewards relative to future consequences.
4DLoss aversion means the tendency to experience losses as more significant than equivalent gains.
5AAnchoring means overreliance on an initial number or piece of information.
6AConfirmation bias means preference for information that supports an existing belief.
7BFinancial decisions combine values, constraints, information, uncertainty, and behavior.
8BGoals become actionable when they include amount, deadline, priority, and progress measure.
9DBias can be reduced through comparison rules, waiting periods, automation, and independent verification.
10BA student delays a high-pressure decision, compares total cost and alternatives, and documents the value and goal being served.

Constructed response: When is a financially imperfect choice still reasonable because it protects an important personal value?

5 points: clear claim, accurate disciplinary reasoning, two specific pieces of evidence, meaningful qualification or counterargument, and complete explanation.

Week 4 · Budgeting, Cash Flow, and Emergency Funds
#AnswerRationale
1CBudget means a plan for allocating expected income among spending, saving, giving, debt, and goals.
2CCash flow means the timing and amount of money entering and leaving an account.
3DFixed expense means a recurring cost that is relatively stable during the planning period.
4CVariable expense means a cost that changes with use, price, or choice.
5CEmergency fund means accessible savings reserved for unexpected necessary expenses or income loss.
6DSinking fund means money accumulated gradually for a known future expense.
7AA workable budget uses actual take-home income and realistic irregular expenses.
8CCash-flow timing can cause overdraft even when monthly income exceeds monthly expense.
9DEmergency funds and sinking funds prevent routine shocks from becoming high-cost debt.
10AA student uses net income, includes irregular costs, and tests whether account timing remains positive throughout the month.

Week 5 · Taxes and Public Services
#AnswerRationale

2CPayroll tax means a tax on wages used for programs such as Social Security and Medicare.
3CSales tax means a tax imposed on specified purchases.
4AProperty tax means a tax based on assessed property value, commonly supporting local services.
5CTax deduction means an amount that reduces taxable income when allowed.
6CTax credit means an amount that directly reduces tax liability when allowed.
7AFederal income tax uses brackets applied to portions of taxable income, not one rate applied to all income.
8BPayroll, sales, property, and local taxes affect households differently.
9DTax forms and rules change, so students should use current official instructions for real filing.
10CA student applies each tax bracket only to income within that bracket and distinguishes withholding, liability, and refund.

Week 7 · Credit Cards, APR, and Consumer Borrowing
#AnswerRationale
1CAnnual percentage rate means a standardized annual rate expressing the cost of credit under disclosure rules.
2AGrace period means a period in which eligible purchases may avoid interest when statement terms are satisfied.
3CMinimum payment means the smallest payment required by the due date, often resulting in long repayment and high interest.
4DRevolving credit means credit that can be borrowed, repaid, and borrowed again up to a limit.
5BLate fee means a charge for failing to make the required payment by the deadline.
6CPromotional rate means a temporary interest rate that changes under stated terms.
7BMonthly payment alone does not reveal total borrowing cost.
8APaying only the minimum can extend debt for years and greatly increase interest.
9DPromotional rates, balance transfers, rewards, fees, and penalty terms must be evaluated together.
10BA student calculates total repayment under the actual APR and fees and does not select credit based only on the monthly minimum or rewards.

Week 13 · Saving, Compound Growth, and Inflation
#AnswerRationale
1DSimple interest means interest calculated only on principal.
2DCompound interest means interest calculated on principal plus previously credited interest.
3BFuture value means the amount a current sum is expected to grow to under stated assumptions.
4CPresent value means the current equivalent of a future sum discounted at a stated rate.
5BNominal return means return measured before adjusting for inflation.
6DReal return means return after adjusting for inflation.
7ACompounding rewards time, rate, and consistency but does not guarantee returns when rates vary or principal is at risk.
8DInflation reduces purchasing power, so nominal growth can coexist with negative real growth.
9BFees compound negatively and can substantially reduce long-term outcomes.
10AA student distinguishes nominal from real return and includes fees, taxes, rate assumptions, and compounding frequency.
