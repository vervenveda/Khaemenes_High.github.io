Credit Reports, Scores, and Identity Protection

Essential question: How can a consumer build accurate credit history while protecting identity and correcting errors?

Key understandings

  • Credit reports and scores are related but distinct; several scoring models may produce different numbers.
  • Payment history, utilization, account age, credit mix, and applications can influence scores.
  • Consumers should obtain reports through authorized channels and dispute inaccurate information promptly.

Vocabulary

credit reportA record of credit accounts, payment history, balances, inquiries, and certain public information.
credit scoreA numerical estimate of credit risk based on a scoring model and report data.
hard inquiryA credit check associated with an application that may affect scoring.
credit utilizationRevolving debt balance relative to available revolving credit.
fraud alertA notice asking creditors to take additional identity-verification steps.
credit freezeA restriction limiting access to a credit file for new-account decisions.

Weekly Reading

What a Report Contains

Credit bureaus collect information from creditors and other sources. Reports show account status, payment history, balances, inquiries, and identifying information. They do not contain every financial fact.

How Scores Work

Scoring models estimate risk from report data. A score is not a moral grade and may differ by model and lender. Paying on time and keeping revolving balances low are generally more important than chasing minor score changes.

Errors and Disputes

A consumer should document the error, contact the bureau and information provider, preserve records, and follow official procedures. Corrections can take time, so reports should be reviewed before major applications.

Identity Protection

Phishing, account takeover, synthetic identity, data breaches, and document theft can damage credit. Strong authentication, freezes, alerts, statement review, and prompt reporting reduce harm.
