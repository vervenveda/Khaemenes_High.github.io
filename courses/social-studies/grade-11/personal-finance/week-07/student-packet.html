Credit Cards, APR, and Consumer Borrowing

Essential question: How can a consumer compare credit offers by total cost rather than monthly payment or promotional language?

Key understandings

  • Monthly payment alone does not reveal total borrowing cost.
  • Paying only the minimum can extend debt for years and greatly increase interest.
  • Promotional rates, balance transfers, rewards, fees, and penalty terms must be evaluated together.

Vocabulary

annual percentage rateA standardized annual rate expressing the cost of credit under disclosure rules.
grace periodA period in which eligible purchases may avoid interest when statement terms are satisfied.
minimum paymentThe smallest payment required by the due date, often resulting in long repayment and high interest.
revolving creditCredit that can be borrowed, repaid, and borrowed again up to a limit.
late feeA charge for failing to make the required payment by the deadline.
promotional rateA temporary interest rate that changes under stated terms.

Weekly Reading

Credit as a Tool

Credit can smooth timing, enable major purchases, and provide consumer protections. It also commits future income. Responsible use begins by asking whether the purchase would still be made at the total financed cost.

APR and Interest

APR supports comparison but may not include every fee in the same way for every product. Credit-card interest is often calculated using an average daily balance or similar method described in the agreement.

Minimum-Payment Trap

A low required payment reduces immediate pressure but preserves principal and interest. Statements often include warnings comparing minimum payment with a faster repayment amount.

Rewards and Promotions

Rewards are valuable only when they exceed fees, lost discounts, and interest. A zero-percent offer has an expiration date and may charge transfer fees. Missing a payment can trigger consequences under the agreement.
