Insurance and Risk Management

Essential question: Which risks should a person retain, reduce, transfer through insurance, or avoid?

Key understandings

  • Insurance is best suited to low-frequency or uncertain losses that would be financially severe.
  • A lower premium may reflect a higher deductible, lower limit, narrower coverage, or more exclusions.
  • Policy comparison requires matching scenarios, limits, deductibles, exclusions, provider networks, and financial stability.

Vocabulary

risk managementIdentification, evaluation, and treatment of uncertain losses.
premiumThe price paid for insurance coverage.
deductibleThe amount the insured pays before specified coverage begins.
coverage limitThe maximum amount an insurer will pay under a provision.
exclusionA condition or event not covered by a policy.
liability coverageProtection against specified claims that the insured caused injury or damage to another.

Weekly Reading

Four Responses to Risk

A person can avoid a risk, reduce its probability or severity, retain it through savings, or transfer part of it through insurance. The right choice depends on likelihood, severity, ability to absorb loss, and available coverage.

Policy Structure

Declarations summarize insured property or persons, limits, deductibles, and premium. Coverage sections state protection; exclusions remove specified events; conditions define duties. Endorsements add or change terms.

Types of Insurance

Auto liability protects others and may be legally required. Health, disability, renters, homeowners, flood, life, travel, and long-term care cover different risks. One policy does not automatically substitute for another.

Claims and Underinsurance

After a loss, prompt notice, documentation, mitigation, and policy review matter. Inflation, rebuilding cost, deductibles, exclusions, and liability exposure can leave households underinsured.
