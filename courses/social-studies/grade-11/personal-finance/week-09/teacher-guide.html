Teacher Guide

Week 9 · Debt Management, Collections, and Bankruptcy

Essential question: What steps should a consumer take when debt becomes unaffordable or a collector makes a disputed claim?

Instructional caution: Distinguish factual definitions, legal holdings, economic relationships, value judgments, and policy preferences. Do not grade students for political viewpoint; grade evidence, accuracy, reasoning, citation, and disciplinary method.

Mastery evidence

  • Ignoring debt usually reduces options; early contact and documentation are important.
  • Consumers should verify a collector, the debt, the amount, ownership, and legal deadlines before paying or disclosing information.
  • Bankruptcy can provide relief but has legal, credit, cost, asset, and eligibility consequences requiring qualified advice.

Source / model emphasis

Prioritize a fictional household’s debts and necessities, draft creditor communication, and identify legitimate assistance and warning signs.

Create a comparison matrix for self-negotiation, counseling, consolidation, settlement, and bankruptcy.

Daily Teaching Notes

Monday · Frame the Inquiry

Instructional aim: Define the Week 9 problem, establish the relevant institutions or economic relationships, and construct an initial answer to the essential question.

Evidence of learning: Identify one distinction that prevents a common oversimplification of this topic.

Support: Provide a vocabulary preview, chunked text, text-to-speech, and claim/evidence sentence frames.

Extension: Identify a competing theoretical interpretation and describe what evidence could distinguish it.

Tuesday · Source, Case, or Contract Laboratory

Instructional aim: Evaluate a primary record, legal case, policy document, contract, or institutional source for authority, evidence, context, and limitation.

Evidence of learning: State one claim the focal record supports and one claim it cannot establish alone.

Support: Define specialized language, provide a structured annotation guide, and allow oral rehearsal before writing.

Extension: Locate a source from a different institutional or social position and explain how it confirms, complicates, or contradicts the first.

Wednesday · Model, Measure, and Test

Instructional aim: Interpret a quantitative model, institutional process, financial calculation, or policy tradeoff without confusing description and causation.

Evidence of learning: Explain which conclusion remains valid after the changed assumption and which conclusion does not.

Support: Provide labeled examples, formulas or process steps, and a partially completed model.

Extension: Perform a sensitivity analysis or compare an alternative policy/institutional design.

Thursday · Argument, Deliberation, and Policy

Instructional aim: Construct and defend a precise claim using disciplinary evidence, a counterclaim, and a proportionate conclusion.

Evidence of learning: Explain why the counterclaim deserves a response rather than dismissal.

Support: Use claim-evidence-reasoning frames and permit an audio or oral draft before the final written product.

Extension: Rewrite the recommendation for an audience with a different role, jurisdiction, time horizon, or risk tolerance.

Friday · Assess, Correct, and Transfer

Instructional aim: Demonstrate weekly mastery, correct misconceptions, and transfer the central concept to a new case.

Evidence of learning: Apply the week’s reasoning to this mastery action: A student verifies the debt and collector, protects essential expenses, communicates in writing, and seeks qualified help before choosing settlement or bankruptcy.

Support: Allow extended time, reduced-distraction setting, read-aloud where appropriate, and repeated attempts after documented correction.

Extension: Write one high-quality examination question and an answer key explaining why each distractor is wrong.

Assignment Scoring

Each principal assignment is worth 30 points: 8 accuracy, 8 evidence or calculation, 6 reasoning, 4 counterargument/limitation, and 4 clarity/citation. Use the course rubrics for expanded descriptors.
