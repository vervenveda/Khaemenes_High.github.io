Debt Management, Collections, and Bankruptcy

Essential question: What steps should a consumer take when debt becomes unaffordable or a collector makes a disputed claim?

Key understandings

  • Ignoring debt usually reduces options; early contact and documentation are important.
  • Consumers should verify a collector, the debt, the amount, ownership, and legal deadlines before paying or disclosing information.
  • Bankruptcy can provide relief but has legal, credit, cost, asset, and eligibility consequences requiring qualified advice.

Vocabulary

delinquencyFailure to make a required payment on time.
defaultFailure to meet contractual repayment terms, often after specified delinquency.
debt collectionEfforts to obtain payment of a debt, subject to applicable law.
credit counselingEducation and assistance with budgeting, repayment, and creditor negotiation.
debt settlementNegotiation to satisfy a debt for less than the claimed balance, with risks and tax or credit consequences.
bankruptcyA federal legal process providing structured treatment or discharge of eligible debts.

Weekly Reading

Act Early

When a payment problem appears, a borrower should list debts, protect necessities, review contracts, contact creditors, and document communications. Hardship programs or modified terms may be available before default.

Validate Collection Claims

Scams and record errors occur. Consumers should obtain written validation, preserve notices, avoid revealing sensitive data to unverified callers, and understand that making a payment can affect legal rights in some situations.

Counseling and Settlement

Reputable nonprofit counseling may help create a debt-management plan. Settlement companies can charge fees and may advise consumers to stop paying, increasing collection, tax, and lawsuit risk.

Bankruptcy

Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 serve different situations. Not every debt is dischargeable, and property treatment varies. This course explains concepts but does not provide individualized legal advice.
