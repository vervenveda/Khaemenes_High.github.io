Fiscal Policy, Deficits, and Public Debt

Essential question: When should government borrow to stabilize or invest, and when do deficits create unacceptable fiscal, inflation, or intergenerational risk?

Key understandings

  • Deficits are annual flows; debt is an accumulated stock.
  • Fiscal effects depend on economic slack, timing, composition, financing, expectations, monetary response, and implementation.
  • Debt sustainability concerns the relationship among interest rates, growth, revenue, spending commitments, and political capacity.

Vocabulary

fiscal policyGovernment decisions about spending, taxation, and borrowing that influence the economy.
automatic stabilizerA tax or spending feature that changes automatically with economic conditions, such as unemployment benefits.
multiplierThe total change in output associated with an initial change in spending or taxation under specified conditions.
crowding outReduced private investment or activity associated with government borrowing or resource use.
national debtThe accumulated outstanding federal borrowing resulting from past deficits and other financing operations.
debt serviceInterest and principal payments associated with public debt.

Weekly Reading

Discretionary and Automatic Policy

Legislatures can enact tax changes, transfers, investment, or purchases. Automatic stabilizers support income and reduce revenue during downturns without new legislation. Their speed can be valuable, but targeting and long-term design still matter.

Multipliers and Capacity

A spending increase can generate additional rounds of income and demand, especially when resources are idle. Effects may be smaller when imports rise, supply is constrained, interest rates increase, or households save the change.

Debt and Deficits

A deficit measures a period; debt accumulates borrowing. Borrowing can finance emergencies and investments whose benefits extend into the future. Persistent structural imbalance can raise interest costs, reduce flexibility, or contribute to inflation under some conditions.

Evaluating Sustainability

No single debt number determines crisis. Currency, maturity, investor confidence, central-bank policy, growth, interest rates, institutions, demographics, and tax capacity matter. Distribution also matters because debt payments are income to bondholders and liabilities of government.
