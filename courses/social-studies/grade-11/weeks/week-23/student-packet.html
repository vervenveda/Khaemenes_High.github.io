Firms, Costs, Entrepreneurship, and Competition

Essential question: How do firms decide what to produce, how much to produce, and whether to enter or leave a market?

Key understandings

  • Firms compare expected revenue with opportunity costs, including the owner’s time and capital.
  • Marginal analysis guides output decisions, while average costs and market conditions affect entry and exit.
  • Entrepreneurship includes innovation and risk but occurs within legal, financial, and social institutions.

Vocabulary

fixed costA cost that does not vary with current output in the relevant period.
variable costA cost that changes with output.
marginal costThe additional cost of producing one more unit.
revenueIncome received from selling goods or services.
profitRevenue minus explicit and implicit costs.
entrepreneurA person who organizes resources, bears uncertainty, and develops products, services, or processes.

Weekly Reading

Forms of Organization

Sole proprietorships, partnerships, corporations, cooperatives, nonprofits, and limited-liability entities distribute control, liability, taxation, capital access, and continuity differently. The best form depends on purpose and circumstances.

Costs and Output

Fixed costs do not change with current output, while variable costs do. Marginal cost asks what one additional unit costs. A firm expands output while expected additional revenue exceeds additional cost, subject to capacity and market conditions.

Profit and Opportunity Cost

Accounting profit subtracts explicit expenses. Economic profit also considers implicit opportunity costs, such as the return an owner could earn elsewhere. Positive economic profit can attract entry; persistent losses encourage exit.

Entrepreneurship and Institutions

Innovation may involve a new product, production method, organization, or market. Financing, bankruptcy law, contracts, infrastructure, education, competition, and intellectual property affect who can experiment and recover from failure.
