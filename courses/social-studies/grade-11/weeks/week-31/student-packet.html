The Federal Reserve and Monetary Policy

Essential question: How does monetary policy influence inflation, employment, credit, and financial conditions without directly controlling every price or loan?

Key understandings

  • The Federal Reserve has statutory goals involving maximum employment, stable prices, and moderate long-term interest rates.
  • Policy affects borrowing costs, asset values, expectations, exchange rates, and credit conditions with uncertain lags.
  • Central-bank independence supports credibility, while transparency and statutory oversight support democratic accountability.

Vocabulary

central bankA public institution managing monetary policy and supporting payment and financial stability functions.
federal funds rateThe interest rate on overnight reserve lending that the Federal Reserve targets within a range.
open market operationA central-bank purchase or sale of securities affecting reserves and short-term rates.
discount rateThe rate charged on eligible borrowing from Federal Reserve lending facilities.
reserve requirementA rule concerning reserves relative to deposits; requirements have changed over time and may be set at zero.
transmission mechanismThe channels through which monetary policy affects financial conditions, spending, employment, and prices.

Weekly Reading

Federal Reserve Structure

The Federal Reserve System includes the Board of Governors, regional Reserve Banks, and the Federal Open Market Committee. Its functions include monetary policy, supervision, payment systems, financial stability, and services to government and banks.

Policy Tools

The Fed influences short-term rates through administered rates, open-market operations, and its balance sheet. Lending facilities provide liquidity in specified circumstances. The traditional reserve-requirement description should be updated when current policy differs.

Transmission

Lower rates can encourage borrowing, investment, housing, asset demand, and spending; higher rates can restrain demand and inflation. Effects depend on expectations, bank health, borrower access, global conditions, and confidence.

Tradeoffs and Lags

Policy cannot precisely control inflation or employment in real time. Data are revised, causes are uncertain, and effects arrive with lags. A policy may stabilize the aggregate economy while imposing different costs on borrowers, savers, workers, and asset holders.
