Money, Banking, and the Financial System

Essential question: Why does a modern economy need trusted money and financial institutions, and what failures can turn finance into a public crisis?

Key understandings

  • Money reduces transaction costs but depends on confidence, legal institutions, and payment infrastructure.
  • Banks transform maturities and allocate credit, creating benefits and vulnerability to liquidity crises.
  • Financial regulation seeks stability, consumer protection, competition, and market integrity while preserving useful risk-taking.

Vocabulary

moneyAn asset widely accepted as a medium of exchange, unit of account, and store of value.
liquidityThe ease with which an asset can be used for payment or converted to spendable funds without major loss.
financial intermediaryAn institution connecting savers, borrowers, investors, and payment users.
deposit insuranceA public guarantee protecting eligible deposits up to legal limits.
bank runRapid withdrawals driven by fear that an institution cannot meet obligations.
systemic riskThe danger that failure or distress spreads through interconnected financial institutions and markets.

Weekly Reading

Functions of Money

Barter requires a double coincidence of wants. Money allows people to quote prices, settle obligations, store purchasing power, and coordinate complex exchange. Currency is one form; deposits and electronic balances perform much of modern payment activity.

Intermediation

Banks and other intermediaries evaluate borrowers, pool savings, process payments, and transform short-term deposits into longer-term loans. This supports investment but creates maturity mismatch: depositors may demand funds before loans are repaid.

Credit and Balance Sheets

A bank loan creates a borrower obligation and a deposit asset. Capital absorbs losses; liquidity meets withdrawals. Insolvency means assets are insufficient relative to obligations, while illiquidity means funds are not available at the required time.

Stability and Protection

Deposit insurance, capital requirements, supervision, lender-of-last-resort facilities, resolution procedures, disclosure, and consumer law address different risks. Poorly designed guarantees can also encourage excessive risk, creating moral hazard.
