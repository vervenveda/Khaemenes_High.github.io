Measuring an Economy: GDP, Productivity, and Living Standards

Essential question: What can economic indicators reveal about well-being, and what important conditions remain invisible in a single number?

Key understandings

  • GDP measures production, not directly happiness, fairness, sustainability, health, or unpaid work.
  • Real values are needed to compare output across time when prices change.
  • Average growth can occur alongside unequal gains, regional divergence, environmental loss, or reduced security.

Vocabulary

gross domestic productThe market value of final goods and services produced within a country during a period.
real GDPOutput measured with prices adjusted to remove the effect of inflation.
nominal GDPOutput measured at current prices.
GDP per capitaGDP divided by population, often used as a rough average-output measure.
productivityOutput per unit of labor or other input.
distributionHow income, wealth, consumption, or opportunity is divided across people and groups.

Weekly Reading

What GDP Counts

GDP includes consumption, investment, government purchases, and net exports of final production within a country. Intermediate goods are excluded to avoid double counting. Imports are subtracted because they appear in other spending categories but were produced elsewhere.

Nominal and Real Values

Nominal GDP can rise because output increases, prices increase, or both. Real GDP uses a price index to estimate changes in actual production. Per-capita measures adjust for population but remain averages.

Productivity and Growth

Long-run living standards depend heavily on productivity. Capital, education, health, infrastructure, technology, institutions, research, and management can raise output per worker, though benefits depend on compensation and distribution.

Beyond GDP

Unpaid caregiving, household production, leisure, inequality, environmental depletion, safety, health, and political freedom are not fully captured. Multiple indicators should be combined rather than replacing one imperfect measure with another single index.
