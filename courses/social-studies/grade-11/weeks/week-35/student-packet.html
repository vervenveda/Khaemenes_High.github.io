Public Policy Laboratory

Essential question: How can students design a lawful, evidence-based policy without hiding uncertainty, tradeoffs, distribution, or implementation risk?

Key understandings

  • Problem definition determines which causes, people, evidence, and solutions receive attention.
  • A policy recommendation must identify legal authority, jurisdiction, financing, administration, metrics, and correction mechanisms.
  • Distribution, rights, uncertainty, and alternatives must be reported rather than buried in an average net-benefit estimate.

Vocabulary

policy instrumentA tax, subsidy, rule, service, information requirement, public investment, legal right, or other mechanism used to pursue a goal.
cost-benefit analysisA structured comparison of expected benefits and costs, including limits and distribution.
cost-effectivenessComparison of ways to achieve a defined outcome at the lowest cost or greatest effect.
stakeholderA person, group, institution, or community affected by or able to influence a decision.
implementationThe administrative process of turning an adopted policy into operations and outcomes.
evaluation designA plan for measuring whether a policy caused intended results and what else occurred.

Weekly Reading

Define Before Solving

A broad concern such as housing affordability or water pollution contains multiple problems. Students specify population, location, time, mechanism, severity, and evidence. A poorly defined problem invites symbolic or misdirected policy.

Compare Instruments

The same goal may be pursued through information, incentives, standards, public services, rights, infrastructure, or institutional reform. Each tool has different administrative demands, distributional effects, evasion risks, and time horizons.

Law and Implementation

A proposal must identify which government can act, what authority exists, who administers, how decisions are reviewed, how privacy and rights are protected, and how people access the program. Implementation failure is a substantive policy failure.

Evaluation and Revision

Baseline data, outcome measures, comparison groups, phased implementation, audits, public reporting, and sunset review can improve learning. Correlation after adoption does not automatically prove causation. A strong policy includes a mechanism for correction.

Daily Lessons

Monday · Frame the Inquiry

Objective: Define the Week 35 problem, establish the relevant institutions or economic relationships, and construct an initial answer to the essential question.

Warm-up: Write an initial answer to: How can students design a lawful, evidence-based policy without hiding uncertainty, tradeoffs, distribution, or implementation risk? Mark one fact, one assumption, and one question that must be investigated.

Instruction: A broad concern such as housing affordability or water pollution contains multiple problems. Students specify population, location, time, mechanism, severity, and evidence. A poorly defined problem invites symbolic or misdirected policy.

Guided practice: Create a concept map connecting the week’s vocabulary to the central problem: Public Policy Laboratory.

Independent practice: Complete the inquiry launch: initial claim, three supporting questions, needed evidence, and one possible counterclaim.

Check: Identify one distinction that prevents a common oversimplification of this topic.

Student notes and response




Tuesday · Source, Case, or Contract Laboratory

Objective: Evaluate a primary record, legal case, policy document, contract, or institutional source for authority, evidence, context, and limitation.

Warm-up: Preview “Full Policy Memorandum.” Predict the creator’s purpose and identify information needed to verify the source.

Instruction: The same goal may be pursued through information, incentives, standards, public services, rights, infrastructure, or institutional reform. Each tool has different administrative demands, distributional effects, evasion risks, and time horizons.

Guided practice: Produce an executive summary, problem definition, jurisdiction, evidence, alternatives, economic and rights analysis, financing, implementation, evaluation, counterargument, and recommendation.

Independent practice: Complete Assignment 1. Corroborate the focal record with an independent authoritative source and note unresolved questions.

Check: State one claim the focal record supports and one claim it cannot establish alone.

Student notes and response




Wednesday · Model, Measure, and Test

Objective: Interpret a quantitative model, institutional process, financial calculation, or policy tradeoff without confusing description and causation.

Warm-up: List two variables, one constraint, one likely incentive, and one source of uncertainty in the week’s model or decision.

Instruction: A proposal must identify which government can act, what authority exists, who administers, how decisions are reviewed, how privacy and rights are protected, and how people access the program. Implementation failure is a substantive policy failure.

Guided practice: Build a decision matrix scoring at least four alternatives on effectiveness, cost, equity, rights, feasibility, speed, and reversibility.

Independent practice: Complete Assignment 2 and test the result under at least one changed assumption or alternative scenario.

Check: Explain which conclusion remains valid after the changed assumption and which conclusion does not.

Student notes and response




Thursday · Argument, Deliberation, and Policy

Objective: Construct and defend a precise claim using disciplinary evidence, a counterclaim, and a proportionate conclusion.

Warm-up: Write the strongest argument for and against the seminar question: When is it responsible to act under uncertainty, and what safeguards should accompany a policy adopted before evidence is complete?

Instruction: Baseline data, outcome measures, comparison groups, phased implementation, audits, public reporting, and sunset review can improve learning. Correlation after adoption does not automatically prove causation. A strong policy includes a mechanism for correction.

Guided practice: Sort evidence by relevance, reliability, and whether it supports, complicates, or challenges the developing claim.

Independent practice: Draft Assignment 3 with citations, counterclaim, rebuttal or concession, and an explicit statement of uncertainty or tradeoff.

Check: Explain why the counterclaim deserves a response rather than dismissal.

Student notes and response




Friday · Assess, Correct, and Transfer

Objective: Demonstrate weekly mastery, correct misconceptions, and transfer the central concept to a new case.

Warm-up: Retrieve six vocabulary terms and three core ideas without notes, then verify and correct the record.

Instruction: Review the essential question, key understandings, source findings, model results, and seminar conclusions. Distinguish settled definitions from contestable policy judgments.

Guided practice: Complete a collaborative retrieval review using new examples rather than repeating quiz wording.

Independent practice: Take the weekly quiz and complete the constructed response. Use the correction form for any missed objective item or unsupported claim.

Check: Apply the week’s reasoning to this mastery action: A student states the decision rule, cites evidence, identifies jurisdiction and implementation, and explains uncertainty and distribution.
