Supply, Demand, and Market Equilibrium

Essential question: How do decentralized choices create market prices, and when does a price fail to communicate the full social cost or value?

Key understandings

  • A movement along a curve is caused by the good’s own price; a curve shift is caused by another determinant.
  • Equilibrium is a model of coordinated exchange, not a moral judgment that an outcome is fair or socially optimal.
  • Price ceilings, price floors, taxes, subsidies, and shocks can create predictable but context-dependent changes.

Vocabulary

demandThe quantities consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a stated period.
supplyThe quantities producers are willing and able to sell at different prices during a stated period.
equilibriumA price and quantity at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
substituteA good that can replace another good in consumption or production.
complementA good commonly used with another good.
shortageA condition in which quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.

Weekly Reading

Demand and Supply Schedules

Demand reflects willingness and ability to purchase; supply reflects willingness and ability to produce. Curves summarize many individual choices under specified assumptions.

Movement Versus Shift

A change in the product’s own price causes movement along a curve. Income, tastes, expectations, number of participants, input costs, technology, policy, and related goods can shift a curve. Confusing these changes produces incorrect analysis.

Equilibrium and Adjustment

A shortage tends to create upward pressure on price; a surplus tends to create downward pressure. Adjustment may be slow or blocked when information is poor, contracts are fixed, capacity is limited, or regulation constrains price.

Model Limits

Market price may omit pollution, unpaid care, public value, market power, or unequal ability to pay. The model remains useful when its assumptions and omissions are stated explicitly.
