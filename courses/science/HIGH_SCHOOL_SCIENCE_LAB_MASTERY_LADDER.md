# High School Science — Laboratory & Inquiry Mastery Ladder

## Purpose
Practical science must become more independent each year. A learner should not merely complete more labs; the quality of experimental reasoning should advance.

## Level 1 — Grade 9: Controlled evidence
Learners should be able to:
- follow safety and stop-work boundaries;
- identify variables/comparison structure;
- measure with units and repeated trials;
- create transparent tables/graphs;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- state uncertainty/limitations;
- write an evidence-based conclusion;
- propose one justified revision or next test.

## Level 2 — Grade 10: Biological evidence design
Learners additionally should be able to:
- choose appropriate sampling or biological comparison methods;
- use controls and replicates;
- protect sensitive/personal/ecological data;
- separate correlation from causation;
- evaluate biological mechanism evidence;
- compare competing explanations;
- defend source quality and ethical boundaries.

## Level 3 — Grade 11: Quantitative laboratory evidence
Learners additionally should be able to:
- calibrate or audit a measurement model;
- use dimensional analysis and uncertainty-aware calculations;
- distinguish raw data, processed data, model output and claim;
- use blanks/standards/controls where appropriate;
- diagnose random vs systematic error;
- compare competing chemical models with quantitative evidence;
- justify safe/simulated alternatives when equipment or supervision is unavailable.

## Level 4 — Grade 12: Independent model discrimination & engineering validation
Learners additionally should be able to:
- pre-register a discriminating prediction;
- select a model and justify assumptions;
- analyze residual patterns and parameter sensitivity;
- design a test capable of separating competing models;
- quantify repeatability/uncertainty;
- iterate a prototype based on evidence;
- perform trade-off/constraint analysis;
- defend a final scientific or engineering recommendation, including limitations.

## Department evidence rule
Each course must preserve artifacts showing the appropriate level at least once per quarter or major unit block. A simulation or supplied dataset may satisfy the evidence requirement when physical work would be unsafe, inaccessible, or require specialized supervision, provided the learner still performs authentic design/analysis/evaluation reasoning.
