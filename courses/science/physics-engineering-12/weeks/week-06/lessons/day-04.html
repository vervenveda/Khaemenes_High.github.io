Reading & explanation

Predict before changing anything

The Force Builder is a digital-only model of a block on a flat support. Choose mass, opposing horizontal applied forces, two optional vertical applied forces, initial velocity and elapsed time. It calculates net forces, normal support, acceleration and idealized motion if constant forces are maintained. It does not connect to a scale or real moving object.

One variable at a time

Keep horizontal net force +8 N in two trials while changing mass from 2 kg to 4 kg. The acceleration falls from +4 m/s² to +2 m/s². To isolate an opposing force, keep mass and rightward force fixed while changing the leftward force. Explain the force sum, not just the final screen number.

Pressure is not part of the experiment

The diagram's upward normal force represents ideal flat-surface support, not a hidden hydraulic device. Changing the optional upward pull changes normal while vertical acceleration remains zero in the model. Do not attempt to simulate real lift-off by entering forces outside the provided limits. All investigation paths work with no apparatus or hazardous motion.

Distinguish effects that the model cannot predict

This model prescribes separate leftward and rightward external forces rather than calculating friction from a coefficient. The force on a real sliding surface may change direction and depends on contact. The optional 2-second displacement extrapolation is valid only while the specified forces stay constant and no event changes the motion.

Only synthetic data and virtual forces are used. Do not push or pull heavy objects, improvise hazardous ramps, or operate machinery for these activities.

Vocabulary

controlled variable
A setting held constant during a comparison.
independent variable
One deliberately changed condition.
dependent variable
The measured or calculated response to that change.
initial velocity
Velocity at the beginning of the model's chosen time interval.
model limitation
An assumption or omission limiting what can be concluded.

Worked example

Trial A: m=2 kg, ΣFx=+8 N gives ax=+4 m/s². Trial B: m=4 kg, same force gives ax=+2 m/s². The acceleration halves when mass doubles.

Controlled-force investigation extension

Design matched trials so one relationship is tested at a time. To test a∝F at fixed mass, vary net force while holding mass constant. To test a∝1/m at fixed net force, vary mass while preserving the same signed force sum. Plot the resulting pairs and state whether the pattern supports the second-law model within the simulator’s assumptions.

Preserve both raw settings and calculated outputs. A screenshot of a final acceleration is weaker evidence than a table containing mass, each applied force, net force, initial velocity, duration, and acceleration. This allows another reviewer to recompute ΣF/m and verify the model independently.

State the model limits explicitly. The simulator prescribes constant external forces and fixed contact; it does not derive realistic friction from surface properties or model changing contact forces. Do not generalize the result to a real system without checking those omitted interactions.

Evidence revision / CER

Use at least two matched trials to defend one second-law relationship. Revise the claim if more than one independent variable changed or if a simulated output is treated as a measured physical quantity.

Spiral retrieval

Recall Week 05 model-selection discipline: compare predictions with evidence, preserve assumptions, and identify what new data could falsify the chosen model.

Differentiation pathways

Foundation: use a labeled representation and unit scaffold. Core pathway: complete the quantitative model and evidence explanation. Extended: test a changed assumption, boundary condition, or competing model.

Teacher guidance

Check that the learner can select the model, preserve units, distinguish measurement from inference, and explain uncertainty. Require correction when the numerical answer conflicts with the representation or evidence.

Standards connection

HS-PS2-1 — How do interaction forces produce changes in motion?

Misconception check

Action-reaction force pairs act on different objects. They therefore do not cancel on a single object's free-body diagram.

Redraw or revise the model if your original reasoning used the misconception, then explain which evidence or Newtonian principle corrects it.
