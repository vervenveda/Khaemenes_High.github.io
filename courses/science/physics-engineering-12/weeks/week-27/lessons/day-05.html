How do speed, pressure, and continuity interact in flowing fluids?

Explicit physics instruction

Fluid dynamics synthesis connects continuity, energy, viscosity, and model limitations.

A strong explanation distinguishes mass/volume conservation from the additional assumptions needed for a Bernoulli model.

Faster flow does not universally mean lower pressure in every real situation; the simple Bernoulli relation has assumptions.

Core relation: A₁v₁=A₂v₂; ideal Bernoulli reasoning within stated assumptions.

Vocabulary and fluid-dynamics synthesis

Vocabulary: continuity; Bernoulli model; viscosity; turbulence; energy loss.

Build a fluid-flow decision chain: use continuity first to test volume conservation, then use Bernoulli only if its assumptions are credible. Add viscosity/turbulence as model extensions when measured energy terms show losses.

Keep conservation statements separate. Continuity constrains volume flow for incompressible steady flow; Bernoulli constrains ideal mechanical energy along a streamline. They are related but not interchangeable.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a synthesis CER using one continuity calculation, one pressure-speed comparison, and one model limitation. Revise any solution that jumps directly from narrow area to lower pressure without checking assumptions.

Spiral retrieval

Without notes, reconstruct area → speed → energy terms → losses/model limits.

Integrated flow challenge

A horizontal pipe narrows from 6 cm² to 3 cm² while upstream speed is 1.5 m/s. Continuity predicts 3.0 m/s downstream. Use the ideal Bernoulli model to predict a pressure decrease, then compare with a hypothetical measured decrease that is smaller than ideal.

Explain the sequence of reasoning: first verify volume-flow consistency, then compare mechanical-energy terms, then interpret any residual as evidence for losses or broken assumptions. This order prevents one model from being used to rescue another.

State one real-world factor—viscosity, roughness, turbulence, pump work, elevation, or leakage—that would require extending the simple model.

Engineering transfer check

Compare an ideal nozzle with a real rough pipe section. State which predictions continuity should still approximately constrain and which Bernoulli energy terms require an explicit loss correction.

Transfer scenario

Compare a smooth nozzle, a rough pipe, and a pipe with a pump. Continuity may apply to all three under steady incompressible conditions, but the Bernoulli energy ledger differs because roughness dissipates energy and the pump adds energy. State which term or correction changes in each case.

Final synthesis check

Explain why continuity can remain approximately valid in a real viscous flow even when the simple Bernoulli energy equation requires a loss term.
