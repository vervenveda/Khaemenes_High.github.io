Flow Rate & Continuity

How do speed, pressure, and continuity interact in flowing fluids?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

For steady incompressible flow, volume flow rate is conserved: A₁v₁=A₂v₂. Narrower cross-sectional area therefore requires greater average speed.

Pressure, speed, elevation, viscosity, and energy losses can all matter. Bernoulli-style reasoning is useful only when its assumptions are stated.

Real fluids are viscous; friction and turbulence can dissipate mechanical energy and weaken ideal predictions.

Core relation: A₁v₁=A₂v₂; ideal Bernoulli reasoning within stated assumptions.

Worked example

A flow moves through 4 cm² at 2 m/s and narrows to 2 cm². Continuity predicts v₂=(4×2)/2=4 m/s.

Guided question

A tube narrows from 6 cm² to 3 cm² while speed starts at 1.5 m/s. Find the ideal downstream speed.

Independent work

  1. Solve three continuity/flow calculations.
  2. State Bernoulli assumptions for one case.
  3. Identify one viscosity/turbulence limitation.

Misconception check

Faster flow does not universally mean lower pressure in every real situation; the simple Bernoulli relation has assumptions.

Formative check

If cross-sectional area halves for steady incompressible flow, what happens to average speed?

Reveal response

It doubles.
