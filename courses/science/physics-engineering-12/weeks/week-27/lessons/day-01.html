Flow Rate & Continuity

How do speed, pressure, and continuity interact in flowing fluids?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

For steady incompressible flow, volume flow rate is conserved: A₁v₁=A₂v₂. Narrower cross-sectional area therefore requires greater average speed.

Pressure, speed, elevation, viscosity, and energy losses can all matter. Bernoulli-style reasoning is useful only when its assumptions are stated.

Real fluids are viscous; friction and turbulence can dissipate mechanical energy and weaken ideal predictions.

Core relation: A₁v₁=A₂v₂; ideal Bernoulli reasoning within stated assumptions.

Worked example

A flow moves through 4 cm² at 2 m/s and narrows to 2 cm². Continuity predicts v₂=(4×2)/2=4 m/s.

Guided question

A tube narrows from 6 cm² to 3 cm² while speed starts at 1.5 m/s. Find the ideal downstream speed.

Misconception check

Faster flow does not universally mean lower pressure in every real situation; the simple Bernoulli relation has assumptions.

Formative check

If cross-sectional area halves for steady incompressible flow, what happens to average speed?

Reveal response

It doubles.

Vocabulary and continuity-depth extension

Vocabulary: volume flow rate; continuity; incompressible flow; cross-sectional area; average speed.

For steady incompressible flow, Q=Av and A₁v₁=A₂v₂. This is a conservation statement: the same volume per second passes each cross-section when no fluid accumulates or leaks. If area halves, average speed doubles.

Example: A₁=6 cm², v₁=1.5 m/s, A₂=3 cm² gives v₂=3.0 m/s. Before interpreting the result physically, state the assumptions: steady flow, negligible compressibility, one connected stream, and no leakage.

Quantitative continuity transfer

A pipe carries water through A₁=8.0 cm² at v₁=1.2 m/s, so the volume-flow product is 9.6 cm²·m/s in the chosen mixed-unit comparison. If the pipe narrows to 3.2 cm², continuity predicts v₂=3.0 m/s. Recompute A₂v₂ to verify the same flow-rate product.

Now introduce uncertainty. If the diameter used to calculate area is uncertain, the area uncertainty can be amplified because area depends on the square of radius. A disagreement between A₁v₁ and A₂v₂ may therefore reflect geometry calibration rather than a true continuity failure.

State whether the evidence supports a steady incompressible model before using any pressure-speed reasoning.

Continuity transfer check

Convert a circular tube diameter into area before applying continuity. Because area depends on diameter squared, halving diameter reduces area by a factor of four and ideally raises average speed by a factor of four for the same incompressible flow rate. Use this ratio check before trusting arithmetic.

Multi-step continuity example

A circular tube narrows from diameter 4.0 cm to 2.0 cm. Because area scales with diameter squared, the downstream area is one-fourth the upstream area. If upstream speed is 1.0 m/s, continuity predicts 4.0 m/s downstream. This ratio method avoids calculating π explicitly and provides a strong reasonableness check.

Now suppose measured downstream speed is only 3.6 m/s. Compare that 10% difference with the expected speed uncertainty before concluding that fluid is being lost.
