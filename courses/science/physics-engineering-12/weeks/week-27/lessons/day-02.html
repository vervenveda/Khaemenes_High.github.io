Pressure–Speed Energy Reasoning

How do speed, pressure, and continuity interact in flowing fluids?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Bernoulli reasoning tracks mechanical energy per fluid volume along a streamline under ideal conditions.

When elevation is unchanged, pressure and kinetic terms can trade off; however pumps, friction, and turbulence add or remove energy.

Use the energy statement only after checking assumptions.

Core relation: A₁v₁=A₂v₂; ideal Bernoulli reasoning within stated assumptions.

Worked example

If speed increases through a constriction at the same elevation in a low-loss ideal model, static pressure can decrease as energy shifts toward kinetic form.

Guided question

What assumptions must hold before using a simple Bernoulli pressure–speed comparison?

Independent work

  1. Solve three continuity/flow calculations.
  2. State Bernoulli assumptions for one case.
  3. Identify one viscosity/turbulence limitation.

Misconception check

Faster flow does not universally mean lower pressure in every real situation; the simple Bernoulli relation has assumptions.

Formative check

If cross-sectional area halves for steady incompressible flow, what happens to average speed?

Reveal response

It doubles.
