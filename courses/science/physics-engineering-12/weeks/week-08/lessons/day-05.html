Grade 12 · Week 08 · Friday · Day 05

Explain the Forces of a Curve

Guiding question: How can one evidence-backed claim connect Newton’s laws, turning and friction?

Objective: Write a quantitative scientific explanation and correct misconceptions about circular and frictional motion.

45–60 minutes · No apparatus · Standalone offline lesson

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Learn the science

Make the physics statement specific

A correct claim is that a moving object following a uniform circular trajectory requires a net force directed toward the circle's center; its magnitude is mv²/r. This is a resultant of actual interactions, which may be friction, tension or gravity depending on the physical case. It is incorrect to say that a constant-speed circular object has no acceleration.

Cite two different calculations

Choose m=2 kg, v=4 m/s and r=8 m: a_c=2 m/s² inward, F_net=4 N inward. At doubled speed v=8 m/s with the same radius, F_net rises to 16 N. Then use a friction example: for m=5 kg, μ_s=0.4 and g=9.8, the static limit is 19.6 N, but a 12 N applied push is opposed by only 12 N when the block remains at rest.

Address alternative explanations

A model of a tethered puck and a model of a car on a level road can both display circular motion, yet different forces provide their inward net force. A student might mistakenly add F_c separately to the tension arrow. Another might treat f_s,max as an always-on force rather than a bound. Correct both errors explicitly by naming force agents and comparing numeric predictions.

Report uncertainty and human assessment boundaries

These labs use idealized mathematical data. They cannot certify a real friction coefficient or predict safe speeds. Finish with the assumptions, one plausible deviation and an improvement to your method. Friday randomly presents 10 of 20 public open-book questions; its feedback and browser-local score are formative, not confidential midterm/final results.

Safe investigation: Use the synthetic data and digital models. No swinging masses, actual vehicle testing, sharp ramps, risky traction experiments or improvised apparatus. These calculators are not engineering safety tools.

Vocabulary

scientific claim
A precise, testable statement limited by conditions.
quantitative evidence
Numbers, units and identified data sources supporting the claim.
reasoning
Physics principles connecting evidence to a claim.
counterclaim
An alternative or misconception evaluated with evidence.
limitation
An assumption or source of uncertainty restricting conclusions.

Worked example

A 2 kg cart on an ideal circle (v=4 m/s, r=8 m) needs 4 N inward. At v=8 m/s it needs 16 N. Separately, 5 kg at μ_s=0.4 has maximum static friction 19.6 N on a flat support, but only 12 N friction is needed to balance a 12 N push.

Calculate independently and write the units and directions.
