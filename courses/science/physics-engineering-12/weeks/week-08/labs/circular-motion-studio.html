Circular Motion Studio

Rotate the position on an ideal circular path and compare the tangential velocity arrow with the inward acceleration vector.

Safety and scientific scope

This is an idealized digital model, not a safe driving-speed calculator or apparatus plan. Do not swing weighted objects, conduct vehicle testing, operate hazardous moving equipment or run risky traction experiments. All sample results come from equations, not measurements.

For uniform circular motion, inward acceleration magnitude a_c=v²/r and net radial force requirement F_c=mv²/r. The arrows point tangent and inward, respectively. 'Centripetal force' is a description of the net inward force supplied by real agents; do not add it as an extra arrow on a free-body diagram already accounting for the actual inward forces.
