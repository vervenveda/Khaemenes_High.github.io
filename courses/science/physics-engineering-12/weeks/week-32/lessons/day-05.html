EM Waves & Information Synthesis

Integrate wave physics, optics, and information-transfer models.

Safety boundary

Use simulations, printed optics, and eye-safe light only. Do not direct lasers or intense light at people.

Explicit physics instruction

Electromagnetic communication combines a physical carrier with encoding, transmission, interaction with matter, and decoding.

A strong model identifies which quantity carries information, which physics controls propagation, and which metric evaluates fidelity.

Engineering choices trade among power, bandwidth, range, resolution, robustness, and safety.

Vocabulary: carrier; encoding; propagation; fidelity; tradeoff.

Worked example

A radio system can use frequency/wavelength physics to choose a carrier while a digital code determines how information is represented on that carrier.

Guided question

EM-information synthesis extension

Build a communication chain: choose a carrier, encode information, transmit through a medium or free space, allow interaction/loss/noise, receive, decode, and evaluate fidelity. Carrier physics and information encoding are distinct layers.

Engineering tradeoffs can involve carrier frequency, bandwidth, power, attenuation, range, resolution, redundancy, and safety. A higher-frequency carrier may support different bandwidth or propagation behavior, but system performance depends on far more than frequency alone.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a synthesis CER using one c=fλ calculation, one optics interaction, and one digital-error metric. Revise any explanation that treats propagation quality and encoding quality as the same thing.

Spiral retrieval

Without notes, reconstruct carrier → propagation/interaction → noise/loss → decoding → fidelity.

Integrated communication challenge

Choose a carrier frequency, compute wavelength, describe one propagation or optics interaction, then specify a digital encoding and fidelity metric. This forces the physical-wave layer and information layer to remain distinct but connected.

State one tradeoff among bandwidth, range, power, robustness, and safety, and identify which measurement would best test whether the design meets its goal.

Design-integration transfer check

Build one end-to-end example: choose a carrier, calculate wavelength, describe one propagation loss, apply an encoding, and compute a fidelity metric. Then identify whether a proposed improvement acts on the physical channel or the information layer.

End-to-end validation check

For the communication design, identify one measurement at the carrier/propagation layer and one at the decoding/fidelity layer. A robust validation should test both rather than assuming good signal strength guarantees correct information recovery.

Final design check

Identify one proposed improvement that changes the carrier physics and one that changes only the encoding/decoding strategy. Explain how you would test each independently.
