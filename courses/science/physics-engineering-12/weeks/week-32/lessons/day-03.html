Digital Signals, Noise & Information

Explain why digital encoding can preserve information under noisy transmission conditions.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, printed optics, and eye-safe light only. Do not direct lasers or intense light at people.

Explicit physics instruction

Analog signals vary continuously, while digital systems encode information in discrete states or symbols.

Noise can distort both, but digital receivers can often regenerate states when distortion remains within decision thresholds.

Error rates, bandwidth, sampling, and redundancy affect communication quality; digital is not magically noise-free.

Vocabulary: digital signal; analog signal; noise; threshold; error rate.

Worked example

If a receiver interprets voltages above 2.5 V as 1 and below 2.5 V as 0, moderate noise may change measured voltage without changing decoded bit state.

Guided question

Why can a digital signal survive some distortion that would visibly alter an analog waveform?

Independent work

  1. Classify sample signals as analog or digital.
  2. Calculate an error rate from supplied bit counts.

Misconception check

Digital transmission does not eliminate noise; it can make correct state recovery more robust.

Formative check

Reveal response

Information-depth extension

Digital encoding maps information into discrete states, while analog signals vary continuously. Noise affects both. Digital systems can recover correct states when distortion remains within decision margins, but high noise can still produce bit errors.

Bit error rate is errors divided by transmitted bits. Redundancy can reduce effective error probability but consumes bandwidth or throughput. Sampling rate and bandwidth also constrain what information can be represented faithfully.

Evidence revision / CER

Use one noisy threshold example to defend why a digital receiver can recover a symbol correctly despite waveform distortion. Revise any claim that digital transmission eliminates noise.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 13 engineering tradeoffs with redundancy, bandwidth, and robustness.

Signal-metric transfer

If 25 errors occur in 10,000 transmitted bits, the bit error rate is 0.25%. Adding redundancy can lower effective error probability but reduces net payload efficiency. A design must balance fidelity with bandwidth and throughput.

Compare two thresholds for the same noisy digital signal and explain how changing the decision boundary can trade one class of errors against another. Preserve raw received values before calculating the final error metric.

Error-rate transfer check

Compare 10 errors in 1000 bits with 100 errors in 10,000 bits. Both are 1% error rate even though the raw error counts differ by a factor of ten. This shows why normalized metrics are needed when message lengths differ.

Sampling and bandwidth check

Explain why a digital system needs an adequate sampling rate to represent a changing signal faithfully. If sampling is too sparse, fast changes can be misrepresented even when individual transmitted bits are decoded without error.

Final information check

Compare a system with lower bit-error rate but higher latency against one with slightly higher error rate but much lower latency. State which is better only after naming the application requirement.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Digital signals remain usable after transmission conditions that distort analog signals.
