Angular Position, Velocity & Acceleration

How can angular quantities describe rotational motion?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Angular position describes orientation; angular velocity ω=Δθ/Δt and angular acceleration α=Δω/Δt describe rotational change.

Points on a rigid body share angular displacement, angular velocity, and angular acceleration, but their linear distance, speed, and tangential acceleration depend on radius.

The bridge between angular and linear motion is s=rθ and v=rω when θ is measured in radians.

Core relations: s=rθ; v=rω; a_t=rα.

Worked example

A wheel turns through 3.0 rad in 0.50 s, so average angular velocity is 6.0 rad/s.

Guided question

A disk rotates 4.0 rad in 2.0 s. Find average angular velocity.

Misconception check

Same angular speed does not mean same linear speed for points at different radii.

Formative check

What quantity must be known, in addition to ω, to determine a point’s linear speed?

Reveal response

Its distance r from the rotation axis.

Vocabulary and angular-model extension

Vocabulary: angular position; angular velocity; angular acceleration; radian; tangential speed.

A radian is defined geometrically by θ=s/r, so one radian subtends an arc equal in length to the radius. The relations s=rθ and v=rω require θ in radians because the unitless angular ratio is built into the derivation. Converting degrees to radians before substitution is therefore a model requirement, not a formatting preference.

Different points on one rigid body share the same θ, ω, and α at an instant, but linear quantities scale with radius. At ω=6 rad/s, a point at r=0.20 m moves at 1.2 m/s while a point at r=0.50 m moves at 3.0 m/s.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend the claim that same angular speed does not imply same linear speed. Use two radii and the same ω as evidence, and revise any explanation that treats radians and degrees interchangeably in s=rθ.

Spiral retrieval and safety

Recall Week 08 circular motion and compare tangential velocity with inward centripetal acceleration. Use supplied data or simulation only; no high-speed spinning apparatus is required.

Worked transfer practice

A disk starts at θ=1.0 rad and reaches θ=7.0 rad in 2.0 s with constant angular velocity. The angular displacement is 6.0 rad and average ω=3.0 rad/s. A point at r=0.40 m then moves at v=rω=1.2 m/s and traces arc length s=rΔθ=2.4 m. A second point at r=0.80 m shares the same angular motion but moves at 2.4 m/s and traces 4.8 m.

Now add angular acceleration. If ω changes from 2 to 8 rad/s in 3 s, α=2 rad/s². At r=0.50 m, tangential acceleration is 1.0 m/s². This separates three linked but distinct quantities: angular acceleration of the rigid body, tangential acceleration of the chosen point, and any centripetal acceleration associated with its instantaneous speed.
