Energy in Oscillation

What causes repeated motion around equilibrium?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Energy shifts between potential-like storage and kinetic energy while total mechanical energy remains constant in the ideal undamped model.

At turning points, kinetic energy is minimum and stored potential energy maximum; at equilibrium, the reverse occurs.

Damping transfers mechanical energy into other forms, reducing amplitude over time.

Core relation: f=1/T; for ideal SHM, restoring force ∝ −displacement.

Worked example

At an ideal oscillator’s maximum displacement, v=0 at that instant, so kinetic energy is minimum while stored energy is maximum.

Vocabulary and energy extension

Vocabulary: kinetic energy; elastic potential energy; turning point; damping; mechanical energy.

For an ideal spring oscillator, total mechanical energy is E=½kA². At displacement x, elastic potential energy is ½kx² and kinetic energy is the remainder. At x=±A, K=0; at x=0, elastic potential energy is minimum and kinetic energy is maximum.

Damping transfers mechanical energy into thermal or other forms, so amplitude decreases over time. The oscillator may remain periodic-like while no longer conserving mechanical energy exactly.

Energy partition practice

For k=20 N/m and amplitude A=0.10 m, total ideal mechanical energy is E=½kA²=0.10 J. At x=0.06 m, elastic potential energy is ½(20)(0.06²)=0.036 J, leaving K=0.064 J.

If the oscillator mass is 0.50 kg, speed magnitude at that position is found from K=½mv², giving v≈0.51 m/s. At equilibrium, all 0.10 J is kinetic in the ideal spring model, so the speed is larger.

In a damped trial, if peak amplitude decreases from 0.10 m to 0.08 m, mechanical energy falls in proportion to A² for the same k. The missing mechanical energy has transferred into other forms rather than vanished.
