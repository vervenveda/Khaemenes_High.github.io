Vocabulary and synthesis extension

Vocabulary: oscillation model; period; phase; energy exchange; model scope.

Build a representation chain linking restoring force, x–t graph, period/frequency, velocity/acceleration phase, and energy exchange. Each representation answers a different question and should remain consistent with the same oscillator model.

State the limits: ideal SHM assumes a linear restoring relation and negligible damping. Pendulum SHM additionally assumes small angles. Large amplitude or nonlinear behavior can change period and invalidate simple proportionality.

Integrated oscillation challenge

An oscillator has T=0.80 s, amplitude 0.12 m, and ideal spring constant k=25 N/m. Frequency is 1.25 Hz and total mechanical energy is ½kA²=0.18 J. At a turning point, kinetic energy is zero. At equilibrium, the ideal kinetic energy is 0.18 J.

If measured amplitude decays to 0.10 m while period remains approximately 0.80 s, the evidence supports damping that reduces energy without strongly changing the timing model. If period also shifts substantially, the simple ideal model may be failing more broadly.

Use timing, phase, and energy together; no single representation is enough to characterize the oscillator fully.

Model-selection transfer check

Given an oscillator whose amplitude decays but period remains nearly constant, choose a damped-SHM model. Given another whose period changes strongly with amplitude, explain why the ideal small-amplitude model is no longer adequate.

State which observed quantity—timing, phase, force-displacement relation, or energy decay—provides the strongest evidence for each revision.
