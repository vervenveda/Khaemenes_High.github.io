SHM Graphs & Phase

What causes repeated motion around equilibrium?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Position, velocity, and acceleration vary in phase-shifted ways during ideal oscillation.

At maximum displacement, speed is momentarily zero; at equilibrium, speed magnitude is often greatest.

Graph reading should distinguish amplitude, period, phase, and slope rather than treating the curve as a path through space.

Core relation: f=1/T; for ideal SHM, restoring force ∝ −displacement.

Worked example

A position-time graph repeats every 2.0 s, so T=2.0 s and f=0.50 Hz.

Vocabulary and phase extension

Vocabulary: phase; displacement; velocity; acceleration; turning point.

For ideal SHM, position, velocity, and acceleration are phase-shifted. At maximum positive displacement, velocity is zero while acceleration points strongly negative toward equilibrium. At equilibrium, displacement is zero while speed magnitude is maximum and acceleration is zero in the ideal spring model.

On an x–t graph, slope is velocity. The sign of acceleration follows the curvature and the restoring relation a∝-x. A graph is a time record, not the physical path traveled through space.

Phase and graph transfer practice

For x(t)=A cos(ωt), velocity is shifted by one-quarter cycle and acceleration is opposite displacement. At t=0 with x=A, velocity is zero and acceleration points toward equilibrium. One quarter-cycle later, x=0 and speed magnitude is maximum. Half a cycle later, x=-A and velocity is again zero.

Use this phase map to check a graph. If a plotted velocity peak occurs at the same time as a displacement peak, the representations are inconsistent with ideal SHM. A strong analysis checks relative timing rather than memorizing isolated graph shapes.

On a measured position-time graph, estimate period from repeated peaks and compare it with the period inferred from velocity-zero crossings. Agreement between independent features strengthens the model.

Phase-transfer check

Sketch one full cycle and mark four quarter-cycle states: +A, 0, −A, 0. At each state, label the signs of velocity and acceleration. Explain why velocity and acceleration are not generally in phase with position.

Use the slope of the position graph to verify the velocity sign at each interval rather than memorizing the sequence.

Phase-quality note

Use one quarter-cycle interval to explain why velocity can be positive while acceleration is negative. State how the signs change as the oscillator passes equilibrium and approaches the opposite turning point.
