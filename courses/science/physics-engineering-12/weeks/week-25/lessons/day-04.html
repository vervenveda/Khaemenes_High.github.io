Investigation / Data Lab

Use a tone/sound simulation at safe volume or supplied resonance dataset. Compare response amplitude versus driving frequency.

Evidence record: raw frequency/response data, graph, peak estimate, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Resonance does not mean energy is created; it means energy transfer from the driver is especially effective.

Formative check

What happens to response near a natural frequency?

Reveal response

Amplitude can increase because energy transfer from the driver becomes especially effective.

Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: resonance peak; frequency sweep; response amplitude; uncertainty; bandwidth.

Preserve raw driving-frequency and response-amplitude data before identifying the resonance peak. If the curve is broad, report a peak range rather than an unjustifiably exact natural frequency.

Repeat the sweep and compare whether the peak location shifts or whether only amplitude varies. A systematic shift may indicate changing system conditions or calibration; random vertical scatter may reflect measurement noise.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER identifying the likely resonance region and describing how uncertainty affects the claim. Revise the method if frequency steps are too coarse to resolve the peak.

Safety boundary

Use a simulation or low-volume dataset only. Hearing safety overrides the desire for stronger acoustic response.

Repeated-sweep and retrieval extension

Compare three resonance sweeps rather than relying on one. If the peak appears at 7.8, 8.0, and 8.2 Hz, report a resonance region near 8.0 Hz and describe the spread. If peak amplitude changes but location stays stable, distinguish amplitude noise from frequency uncertainty.

Spiral retrieval: connect Week 23 damping, Week 24 standing-wave boundaries, and today's resonance curve. State how each contributes to interpreting a measured peak.

Preserve the raw sweep data and step size so another reviewer can reproduce the graph and test whether interpolation between points is justified.

Peak-quality check

Plot response amplitude with uncertainty bars if available. A peak that rises only slightly above neighboring points may not support a strong resonance claim when uncertainties overlap. A clearly resolved peak across repeated sweeps is stronger evidence.

State whether the uncertainty is dominated by driving-frequency resolution, amplitude measurement, or trial-to-trial variability.
