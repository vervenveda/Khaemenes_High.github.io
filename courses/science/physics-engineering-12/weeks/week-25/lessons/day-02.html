Resonance & Natural Frequency

Why do systems respond strongly at particular frequencies?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A driven oscillator responds most strongly near a natural frequency when damping is limited.

Resonance curves show response amplitude versus driving frequency; damping broadens and lowers the peak.

Engineering must sometimes exploit resonance and sometimes suppress it.

Core relation: v=fλ; resonance near natural frequency.

Worked example

A system with natural frequency 5 Hz driven at 5.1 Hz may respond more strongly than when driven at 2 Hz, assuming comparable force and damping.

Guided question

How does increased damping change a resonance curve?

Independent work

  1. Solve three sound/resonance questions.
  2. Interpret one standing-wave or resonance graph.
  3. State one damping/boundary assumption.

Misconception check

Resonance does not mean energy is created; it means energy transfer from the driver is especially effective.

Formative check

What happens to response near a natural frequency?

Reveal response

Amplitude can increase because energy transfer from the driver becomes especially effective.
