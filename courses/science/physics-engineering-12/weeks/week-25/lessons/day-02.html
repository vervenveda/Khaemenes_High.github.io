Resonance & Natural Frequency

Why do systems respond strongly at particular frequencies?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A driven oscillator responds most strongly near a natural frequency when damping is limited.

Resonance curves show response amplitude versus driving frequency; damping broadens and lowers the peak.

Engineering must sometimes exploit resonance and sometimes suppress it.

Core relation: v=fλ; resonance near natural frequency.

Worked example

A system with natural frequency 5 Hz driven at 5.1 Hz may respond more strongly than when driven at 2 Hz, assuming comparable force and damping.

Guided question

How does increased damping change a resonance curve?

Misconception check

Resonance does not mean energy is created; it means energy transfer from the driver is especially effective.

Reveal response

Vocabulary and resonance-depth extension

Vocabulary: natural frequency; driving frequency; resonance; damping; resonance curve.

A driven system exchanges energy most efficiently near a natural frequency when damping is limited. The resonance peak is not infinitely sharp in real systems; damping lowers the peak amplitude and broadens the frequency range of response.

Compare a drive at 5.0 Hz with one at 2.0 Hz for a system whose natural frequency is near 5.1 Hz. The near-resonant drive can produce much larger steady-state amplitude even with similar forcing. That increase comes from cumulative energy transfer, not energy creation.

Evidence revision / CER

Use a resonance curve to defend how damping changes peak height and width. Revise any explanation that says resonance is simply “matching frequencies” without considering damping and steady-state response.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 23 driven oscillation and damping to acoustic resonance.

Resonance-curve transfer

Suppose response amplitudes at driving frequencies 4, 5, 6, and 7 Hz are 1.2, 3.8, 2.9, and 1.5 arbitrary units. The peak lies near 5 Hz, but a coarse frequency step prevents an exact natural-frequency claim. A second sweep with 0.2 Hz spacing around the peak would improve resolution.

Now increase damping. The expected curve becomes lower and broader. If the peak frequency shifts slightly as well, state whether that shift is larger than frequency-step uncertainty before treating it as physically meaningful.

Write a short evidence statement distinguishing a true resonance trend from a single noisy high point.

Resonance-resolution check

If the apparent resonance lies between sampled frequencies, interpolate cautiously and report a range rather than a false exact value. Compare a coarse 1 Hz sweep with a finer 0.1 Hz sweep near the peak and explain which supports a stronger natural-frequency estimate.

State one damping-related reason why the peak frequency and the undamped natural frequency may not be identical in a real system.

Resonance evidence note

Compare the full width of two resonance peaks at half their maximum response. A broader peak indicates stronger damping in the simplified model. Use that comparison to support the damping claim rather than relying only on the single highest point.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Objects can resonate when driven near natural frequencies.

Investigation / model check
