Interference, Beats & Standing Waves

Why do systems respond strongly at particular frequencies?

Explicit physics instruction

Superposition can produce constructive/destructive interference. Two nearby frequencies can create beats with a beat rate equal to the frequency difference.

Standing waves arise when reflections and superposition create persistent nodes and antinodes.

Boundary conditions determine the allowed standing-wave modes.

Core relation: v=fλ; resonance near natural frequency.

Vocabulary and interference-depth extension

Vocabulary: beat frequency; constructive interference; destructive interference; standing wave; node.

For two nearby pure tones, the simple beat frequency is |f₂−f₁|. Tones at 440 and 444 Hz produce 4 beats per second. The beat envelope reflects alternating constructive and destructive interference as the relative phase changes.

Standing waves require suitable reflections and boundary conditions. Persistent nodes are different from momentary cancellation between traveling pulses. Allowed resonant modes depend on geometry and whether boundaries behave like fixed, free, open, or closed ends.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend the difference between transient destructive interference and a standing-wave node. Revise any claim that a zero-displacement point automatically indicates a node.

Beat and standing-wave transfer

Tones at 500 and 506 Hz produce a 6 Hz beat frequency in the simple two-tone model. If measured beats occur at 5.5±0.5 Hz, the result is consistent within uncertainty. A much larger discrepancy suggests that the source frequencies or counting method need review.

For a tube or string, allowed standing-wave modes depend on boundary conditions. A fixed-fixed string has nodes at both ends; an open-closed air column follows a different mode sequence. Do not transfer one boundary formula to another system without checking the physical ends.

Use this contrast to explain why frequency spacing can be evidence about boundary conditions rather than merely a numerical pattern.
