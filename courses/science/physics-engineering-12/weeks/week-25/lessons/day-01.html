Sound Waves & Frequency

Why do systems respond strongly at particular frequencies?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium. Frequency is perceived primarily as pitch, while amplitude/intensity relates to loudness under simplified conditions.

Resonance occurs when a driving frequency is near a natural frequency, allowing efficient energy transfer and larger oscillation amplitude.

Interference and standing waves explain nodes, antinodes, beats, and many musical/acoustic patterns.

Core relation: v=fλ; resonance near natural frequency.

Worked example

If sound travels 340 m/s at 680 Hz, wavelength is λ=340/680=0.50 m. Driving a system near its natural frequency can increase amplitude even when the driving force is modest.

Guided question

A 170 Hz sound travels at 340 m/s. Find wavelength.

Independent work

  1. Solve three sound/resonance questions.
  2. Interpret one standing-wave or resonance graph.
  3. State one damping/boundary assumption.

Misconception check

Resonance does not mean energy is created; it means energy transfer from the driver is especially effective.

Formative check

What happens to response near a natural frequency?

Reveal response

Amplitude can increase because energy transfer from the driver becomes especially effective.
