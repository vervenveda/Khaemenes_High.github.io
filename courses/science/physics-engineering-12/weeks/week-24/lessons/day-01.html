Wave Variables & Energy Transfer

How do disturbances carry energy without transporting matter over long distances?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A mechanical wave is a traveling disturbance through a medium. Individual medium particles usually oscillate locally while energy propagates through the system.

Wave speed, frequency, and wavelength are related by v=fλ. Frequency is set by the source; wavelength adjusts to the wave speed of the medium.

Superposition means overlapping disturbances add algebraically. Reflection, transmission, interference, and standing-wave patterns follow from this principle.

Core relation: v=fλ.

Worked example

A wave with f=5 Hz and λ=2 m travels at v=10 m/s. If the same medium keeps v fixed and f doubles, λ must halve.

Guided question

Which wave quantities describe repetition, spacing, and propagation rate?

Independent work

Misconception check

Matter in the medium does not generally travel with the wave over long distances; the disturbance and energy propagate.

Formative check

If speed is constant and frequency doubles, what happens to wavelength?

Reveal response

It halves.
