Wave Variables & Energy Transfer

How do disturbances carry energy without transporting matter over long distances?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A mechanical wave is a traveling disturbance through a medium. Individual medium particles usually oscillate locally while energy propagates through the system.

Wave speed, frequency, and wavelength are related by v=fλ. Frequency is set by the source; wavelength adjusts to the wave speed of the medium.

Superposition means overlapping disturbances add algebraically. Reflection, transmission, interference, and standing-wave patterns follow from this principle.

Core relation: v=fλ.

Worked example

A wave with f=5 Hz and λ=2 m travels at v=10 m/s. If the same medium keeps v fixed and f doubles, λ must halve.

Vocabulary and wave-model extension

Vocabulary: mechanical wave; wavelength; frequency; wave speed; medium.

A mechanical wave transfers disturbance and energy through a medium while medium particles oscillate locally. Frequency is set by the source, while wave speed depends primarily on medium properties; wavelength adjusts through v=fλ.

A 5 Hz wave with λ=2 m travels at 10 m/s. If source frequency doubles in the same medium and wave speed remains 10 m/s, wavelength becomes 1 m. The medium particles do not move 10 m each second with the wave.

Wave-variable transfer practice

A source oscillates at 4 Hz in a medium where waves travel at 12 m/s. Wavelength is λ=v/f=3 m. If the source changes to 6 Hz while the medium remains unchanged, λ becomes 2 m while v remains 12 m/s.

Now keep the source at 4 Hz but move into a medium where wave speed is 8 m/s. The frequency remains 4 Hz because the source controls it, while wavelength changes to 2 m. This contrast separates source-controlled frequency from medium-controlled propagation speed.

Medium particles oscillate around local equilibrium positions. The traveling phase pattern and energy move through the medium even though individual particles do not migrate with the wave over long distances.

Propagation-transfer check

Compare a transverse rope wave with a longitudinal compression wave. In both, the disturbance and energy propagate, but particle motion is oriented differently relative to propagation. Use this distinction to explain why “the medium moves with the wave” is an oversimplification.

State one medium property that could change wave speed without changing source frequency.

Final transfer note

Explain how the particle motion differs for transverse and longitudinal waves while the energy-propagation idea remains the same.
