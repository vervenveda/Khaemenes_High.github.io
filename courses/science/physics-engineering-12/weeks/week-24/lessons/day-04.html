Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: wave residual; wavelength measurement; timing uncertainty; boundary condition; repeated trial.

Preserve raw wavelength, frequency, and timing evidence before calculating speed. Define residual r_v=v_observed−fλ and compare it with uncertainty. Repeated residuals that scatter around zero support the model; systematic bias may indicate calibration or changing medium conditions.

Wavelength can be difficult to measure when nodes, reflections, or finite boundaries distort the pattern. State how the measurement was defined and whether the wave was traveling or standing.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the data support v=fλ within uncertainty. Revise the method if reflections or boundary effects contaminate the measurement.

Wave residual and boundary practice

Suppose f=4.0±0.1 Hz and λ=0.75±0.02 m, predicting v≈3.00 m/s. A measured propagation speed of 3.05 m/s is plausibly consistent with uncertainty, while 3.60 m/s suggests calibration error, changing medium conditions, or a misidentified wavelength.

Repeat measurements at several frequencies in the same medium. If calculated speed remains approximately constant, the data support the model. If speed drifts systematically with frequency, investigate dispersion or measurement bias rather than forcing the constant-speed assumption.

Reflections can distort apparent wavelength near boundaries. Measure in a region where the traveling pattern is clear or explicitly model the standing-wave condition.
