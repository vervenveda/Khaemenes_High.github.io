Wave Speed Calculations

How do disturbances carry energy without transporting matter over long distances?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Use v=fλ. Units must be consistent: Hz is s⁻¹, wavelength in metres gives speed in m/s.

A wave with f=5 Hz and λ=2 m travels at v=10 m/s. If the same medium keeps v fixed and f doubles, λ must halve.

Changing source frequency does not automatically change wave speed in the same medium.

Core relation: v=fλ.

Worked example

A wave with f=5 Hz and λ=2 m travels at v=10 m/s. If the same medium keeps v fixed and f doubles, λ must halve.

Guided question

A wave travels 12 m/s at 3 Hz. Find wavelength.

Independent work

  1. Solve three wave-speed or wavelength questions.
  2. Sketch one interference case.
  3. State one medium/source assumption.

Misconception check

Matter in the medium does not generally travel with the wave over long distances; the disturbance and energy propagate.

Formative check

If speed is constant and frequency doubles, what happens to wavelength?

Reveal response

It halves.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit units, medium assumptions, and superposition reasoning.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Calculate with v=fλ.

Core: Interpret interference and standing-wave diagrams.

Extended: Evaluate residuals and boundary-condition effects.

Vocabulary and quantitative extension

Vocabulary: wave speed; frequency; wavelength; source; medium.

Use v=fλ with consistent units. If v=12 m/s and f=3 Hz, λ=4 m. If the same medium keeps v fixed while frequency rises to 6 Hz, wavelength falls to 2 m.

Changing source frequency does not automatically change wave speed in a fixed linear medium. Changing the medium can change speed even when source frequency stays fixed, which then changes wavelength.

Evidence revision / CER

Compare two cases—same medium/different source frequency and different medium/same source frequency—and defend which variable controls which outcome. Revise any solution that assumes v always changes with f.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 23 f=1/T with wave frequency and period.

Quantitative transfer practice

If a wave travels 18 m/s with wavelength 0.75 m, frequency is f=v/λ=24 Hz. The period is then T=1/f≈0.0417 s. This connects wave kinematics directly to the oscillation timing developed in Week 23.

Check units: (m/s)/m gives s⁻¹=Hz. If wavelength is supplied in centimeters, convert it to meters before substitution. Unit consistency is a useful diagnostic for preventing factor-of-100 errors.

Use a second case with constant frequency but different wave speed to predict wavelength change. Then state which physical feature—source or medium—was changed.
