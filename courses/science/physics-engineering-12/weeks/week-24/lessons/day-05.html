Vocabulary and wave synthesis extension

Vocabulary: propagation; source frequency; medium speed; wavelength; interference.

Build a wave-model map: source oscillation sets f; medium properties set v; wavelength follows λ=v/f; superposition governs overlap; boundaries control reflection and standing-wave patterns. Distinguish these roles before selecting an equation or diagram.

Energy transfer and matter transport are not the same. A wave can carry energy across a medium while individual particles remain near equilibrium and oscillate locally.

Integrated wave challenge

A 5 Hz source drives waves in a medium at 10 m/s, so λ=2 m. The wave reflects from a fixed boundary and interferes with the incoming wave. Under suitable conditions, the superposition can create nodes and antinodes separated by fractions of the wavelength.

Now increase source frequency to 10 Hz while medium speed remains 10 m/s. Wavelength becomes 1 m, so standing-wave spacing changes even though propagation speed does not. This connects source timing, medium properties, and interference geometry.

State which quantities belong to the source, which belong to the medium, and which emerge from the combined boundary/interference pattern.

Integrated transfer check

Take a 6 Hz source in a medium with speed 12 m/s. The wavelength is 2 m. If reflections create a standing wave, node spacing is related to half-wavelength under the ideal pattern. Predict the spacing, then explain how changing source frequency changes the geometry while the medium speed remains fixed.

Use this to connect source timing, propagation, and interference in one model chain.
