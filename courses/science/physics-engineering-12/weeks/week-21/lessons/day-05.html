Angular Momentum Synthesis

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Angular momentum reasoning links mass distribution, rotational speed, and external torque.

Use both the conservation model and an explicit system boundary; do not invoke conservation automatically.

Conserved angular momentum does not mean angular speed is constant; I can change.

Core relation: L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt.

Worked example

A rotating system has I=4 kg·m² and ω=2 rad/s, so L=8 kg·m²/s. If I decreases to 2 kg·m² with negligible external torque, ω rises to 4 rad/s.

Guided question

Explain why a skater-style model speeds up when mass moves inward without claiming angular momentum was created.

Misconception check

Formative check

If I is halved with L conserved, what happens to ω?

Reveal response

It doubles.
