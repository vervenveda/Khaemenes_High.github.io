Angular Momentum & Moment of Inertia

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Angular momentum for a rigid body can be modeled as L=Iω. Moment of inertia I depends on how mass is distributed relative to the axis.

When net external torque is negligible, total angular momentum is conserved. If I decreases, ω must increase so the product remains constant.

The analogy with linear momentum is useful but not identical: torque changes angular momentum just as external force/impulse changes linear momentum.

Core relation: L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt.

Worked example

A rotating system has I=4 kg·m² and ω=2 rad/s, so L=8 kg·m²/s. If I decreases to 2 kg·m² with negligible external torque, ω rises to 4 rad/s.

Guided question

Why does moving mass farther from the axis generally increase moment of inertia?

Independent work

  1. Solve three angular-momentum or torque-time problems.
  2. Declare the system and external-torque assumption.
  3. Record one unit/assumption check.

Misconception check

Conserved angular momentum does not mean angular speed is constant; I can change.

Formative check

If I is halved with L conserved, what happens to ω?

Reveal response

It doubles.
