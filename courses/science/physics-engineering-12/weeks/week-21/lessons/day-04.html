Angular-Momentum Investigation

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Explicit physics instruction

This investigation tests angular-momentum conservation using supplied or simulated data.

Use a simulation/video dataset that changes mass distribution without unsafe spinning. Compare I, ω, and L before/after.

Preserve raw angular-speed and geometry/mass-distribution evidence before calculating L.

Core relation: L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt.

Worked example

If L_before=7.8 and L_after=8.1 kg·m²/s, compare the 0.3 residual with measurement uncertainty before judging conservation.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use a simulation/video dataset that changes mass distribution without unsafe spinning. Compare I, ω, and L before/after.

Preserve raw data, calculated I/ω/L values, residuals, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Conserved angular momentum does not mean angular speed is constant; I can change.

Formative check

If I is halved with L conserved, what happens to ω?

Reveal response

It doubles.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Compute L=Iω.

Core: Solve conservation and torque-time changes.

Extended: Evaluate conservation residuals with uncertainty.

Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: angular-momentum residual; mass distribution; angular speed; uncertainty; repeated trial.

Preserve the raw geometry and angular-speed inputs used to determine I and L. If I is calculated from mass and radius, uncertainty in radius can matter strongly because many inertia expressions include r².

Define residual r_L=L_after−L_before. Compare that residual with uncertainty and any expected external angular impulse. Repeated residuals that scatter around zero support approximate conservation; a consistent sign can indicate bearing friction, external torque, or calibration bias.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the simulated data support angular-momentum conservation. Cite at least two trials, residuals, and one model limitation.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 05 residual patterns and Week 20 torque uncertainty to this conservation test.

Inertia-sensitivity practice

Suppose a two-mass system uses I=2mr². With m=1.0 kg and r=0.40±0.01 m, I≈0.32 kg·m². Because radius is squared, a small radius uncertainty has an amplified effect on I compared with an equally small fractional mass uncertainty.

If ω_before=5.0 rad/s and ω_after=8.0 rad/s, calculate L before and after with their uncertainties and compare the residual. Then repeat after adjusting the radius calibration by 0.01 m to see whether the apparent conservation error is sensitive to geometry.

Repeated-trial transfer check

Use at least three before/after trials and compare the sign and magnitude of r_L. Residuals of +0.1, −0.2, and +0.1 kg·m²/s suggest random variation around zero; residuals of −0.6, −0.7, and −0.5 suggest systematic external torque or calibration bias.

State which additional measurement—bearing torque, radius calibration, or angular-speed timing—would best test the competing explanations.

Transfer note

Compare one dataset where residuals scatter around zero with another showing a consistent negative bias. Identify which pattern better supports random uncertainty and which suggests an unmodeled external torque.

Method-quality note

Preserve the original radius and timing measurements so a second reviewer can recalculate I and ω independently. A final L value without its geometric and timing inputs is not fully auditable evidence.
