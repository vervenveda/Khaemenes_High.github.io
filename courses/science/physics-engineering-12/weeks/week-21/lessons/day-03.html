External Torque & Angular Impulse

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

External torque changes angular momentum over time. The rotational impulse relation parallels linear impulse.

A larger torque or longer torque interval produces a larger ΔL.

Internal torques can redistribute angular momentum within a system without changing its total.

Core relation: L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt.

Worked example

A net external torque of 3 N·m acting for 2 s changes angular momentum by 6 kg·m²/s.

Guided question

Vocabulary and angular-impulse extension

Vocabulary: angular impulse; torque-time graph; signed area; external torque; change in angular momentum.

The rotational impulse theorem is ΔL=∫τ_ext dt. For constant torque, this becomes ΔL=τ_extΔt. Signed area under a torque–time graph therefore plays the same role that force–time area plays for linear impulse.

A +3 N·m torque acting for 2 s produces +6 N·m·s, equivalent to 6 kg·m²/s of angular-momentum change. If a −1 N·m torque acts for 3 s afterward, it changes L by −3 kg·m²/s. Add signed angular impulses algebraically.

Evidence revision / CER

Compare two torque-time pulses with equal area but different peaks and durations. Defend which quantities remain the same and which differ. Revise any claim that peak torque alone determines ΔL.

Torque-time transfer practice

A torque-time graph holds +4 N·m for 1.5 s, then −2 N·m for 1.0 s. The signed angular impulse is +6−2=+4 N·m·s, so angular momentum increases by 4 kg·m²/s. If the initial L is 5, the final L is 9 kg·m²/s.

A triangular pulse peaking at 8 N·m over 1.0 s has area 4 N·m·s and therefore produces the same ΔL as the two-step profile. Equal angular impulse does not imply equal peak torque or equal mechanical stress.
