External Torque & Angular Impulse

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

External torque changes angular momentum over time. The rotational impulse relation parallels linear impulse.

A larger torque or longer torque interval produces a larger ΔL.

Internal torques can redistribute angular momentum within a system without changing its total.

Core relation: L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt.

Worked example

A net external torque of 3 N·m acting for 2 s changes angular momentum by 6 kg·m²/s.

Guided question

How is angular impulse related to torque-time area?

Independent work

  1. Solve three angular-momentum or torque-time problems.
  2. Declare the system and external-torque assumption.
  3. Record one unit/assumption check.

Misconception check

Conserved angular momentum does not mean angular speed is constant; I can change.

Formative check

If I is halved with L conserved, what happens to ω?

Reveal response

It doubles.
