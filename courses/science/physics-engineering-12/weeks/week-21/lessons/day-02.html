Conservation Calculations

Why can rotational speed change when mass distribution changes?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Use L=Iω and ΔL=τ_extΔt. Conservation problems compare I₁ω₁ with I₂ω₂ when external torque is negligible.

A rotating system has I=4 kg·m² and ω=2 rad/s, so L=8 kg·m²/s. If I decreases to 2 kg·m² with negligible external torque, ω rises to 4 rad/s.

Check whether the assumed system actually excludes significant external torque.

Misconception check

Conserved angular momentum does not mean angular speed is constant; I can change.

Formative check

If I is halved with L conserved, what happens to ω?

Reveal response

Vocabulary and conservation-transfer extension

Vocabulary: initial angular momentum; final angular momentum; conservation; rotational inertia; angular speed.

Build conservation solutions in a fixed order: define the rotating system, justify negligible external torque, calculate L_before=I₁ω₁, set it equal to L_after=I₂ω₂, solve, then independently verify the product.

For example, if I changes from 6 to 3 kg·m² while ω starts at 2 rad/s, L=12 kg·m²/s. The final ω must be 4 rad/s. If instead I increases to 12 kg·m², ω falls to 1 rad/s. The inverse trend comes from keeping the product fixed.

Uncertainty and evidence revision / CER

If I or ω are measured, compare the before/after L residual with uncertainty. Write a CER deciding whether conservation is supported rather than demanding exact numerical equality.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 15 system-momentum residuals to angular-momentum residuals.

Conservation transfer practice

A system begins with I₁=5.0 kg·m² and ω₁=3.0 rad/s, so L₁=15 kg·m²/s. If I₂=2.5 kg·m² and external torque is negligible, ω₂=6.0 rad/s. Verify independently that I₂ω₂ also equals 15.

Now include a measured residual. If the calculated final L is 14.6±0.6 kg·m²/s, the difference from 15 may be consistent with uncertainty. If it is 12.5±0.6, the discrepancy is too large to ignore and suggests external torque, bad inertia modeling, or measurement error.

External-torque boundary check

Consider a rotating system with L=10 kg·m²/s. If a small opposing external torque of −0.5 N·m acts for 2 s, the angular impulse is −1 kg·m²/s, so the expected final L is 9 rather than 10. A conservation calculation that ignores this torque would build in a 10% error.

Compare that predicted change with the measurement uncertainty. If the uncertainty is ±1.2 kg·m²/s, the data may not distinguish the external-torque effect clearly; if it is ±0.2, the effect should be visible.

Transfer note

Explain how the conclusion would change if a measurable external torque acted during the inertia change. State whether conservation would still be justified and what quantity should be added to the model.
