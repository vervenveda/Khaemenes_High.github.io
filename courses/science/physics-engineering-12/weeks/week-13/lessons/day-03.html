Balance Constraints and Performance

Learning objective: Evaluate designs simultaneously against useful-energy, thermal-transfer, time and input-budget constraints.

45–60 minutes · Digital investigation · Browser-local evidence

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Scientific reading

Define the design challenge precisely

Our fictional device must deliver at least 300 J of useful output in no more than 8 s, while receiving no more than 600 J input and transferring no more than 140 J by the thermal pathway. A proposal passes ONLY when all four requirements are satisfied. The four values are invented for student reasoning, not product safety limits.

A design that passes

Design A receives 500 J over 5 s, with 60% useful and 25% thermal transfer; other energy is 15%. It supplies 300 J useful, 125 J thermal and 75 J other, so it meets every numerical constraint. Input power is 100 W and useful output power is 60 W.

An attractive proposal can still fail

Design B uses 500 J over 5 s at 70% useful and 30% thermal, leaving 0% for other pathways. It offers 350 J useful, but transfers 150 J thermally and fails the invented thermal cap. Design C uses 650 J with 60% useful and 20% thermal over 5 s, but exceeds the 600 J input budget despite producing enough useful output.

A Pareto comparison

When multiple designs satisfy all requirements, compare useful output, input demands, thermal pathway and completion time. A trade-off is a choice between competing objectives. We have not modeled material cost, life cycle, accessibility, reliability, carbon emissions or actual operating temperature, so a numerical winner is not necessarily best in the real world.

All data are fictional mathematical models. Do not open mains electrical appliances, build hazardous devices or treat this as engineering safety advice.

Vocabulary

constraint
Quantitative condition a design must meet.
performance criterion
Quantity used to evaluate success.
trade-off
Competing benefit or cost between different designs.
thermal cap
Maximum allowed modeled thermal transfer in a design brief.
feasible design
Proposal satisfying all specified criteria simultaneously.

Worked example

A: 500 J, 60% useful, 25% thermal, 5 s → useful 300 J, thermal 125 J, passes. B: 500 J, 70% useful, 30% thermal, 5 s → thermal 150 J, fails 140 J cap.
