Buoyancy & Archimedes’ Principle

Why can a heavy ship float while a small dense object sinks?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Buoyant force arises from pressure differences over an immersed object.

The fluid pushes more strongly on deeper surfaces than shallower ones, producing a net upward force.

For a fully immersed object, displaced-fluid volume equals immersed object volume.

Core relations: ρ=m/V; F_b=weight of displaced fluid.

Worked example

A 2.0 kg object displaces 2.5 kg of water. The displaced-fluid weight is about 24.5 N using g=9.8 m/s², so buoyant force is about 24.5 N.

Guided question

Why does an immersed object experience a larger pressure force on its bottom surface?

Independent work

  1. Solve three density/buoyancy problems.
  2. Draw one fluid force diagram.
  3. State one assumption or uncertainty.

Misconception check

A heavier object can float if its average density is low enough and it displaces sufficient fluid.

Formative check

What sets buoyant-force magnitude in Archimedes’ model?

Reveal response

The weight of the displaced fluid.
