Density & Fluid Pressure

Why can a heavy ship float while a small dense object sinks?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Density is mass per volume, ρ=m/V. Floating depends on average density and displaced fluid, not simply on total mass.

Fluid pressure increases with depth because deeper points support more fluid above. Pressure acts in all directions within a static fluid.

Archimedes’ principle states that buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid. Floating equilibrium occurs when buoyant force balances weight.

Core relations: ρ=m/V; F_b=weight of displaced fluid.

Worked example

A 600 g sample occupying 300 cm³ has density 2.0 g/cm³.

Guided question

Calculate density for 500 g occupying 250 cm³.

Independent work

  1. Solve three density/buoyancy problems.
  2. Draw one fluid force diagram.

Misconception check

A heavier object can float if its average density is low enough and it displaces sufficient fluid.

Formative check

What sets buoyant-force magnitude in Archimedes’ model?

Reveal response

The weight of the displaced fluid.
