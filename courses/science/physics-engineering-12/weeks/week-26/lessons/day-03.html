Floating, Sinking & Stability

Why can a heavy ship float while a small dense object sinks?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Floating equilibrium requires upward buoyant force to balance downward weight.

Average density includes hollow spaces; this is why a large ship can have lower average density than water.

Stability depends on shape, center of mass, and how buoyant force shifts with tilt.

Core relations: ρ=m/V; F_b=weight of displaced fluid.

Vocabulary and floating-stability extension

Vocabulary: average density; floating equilibrium; center of mass; center of buoyancy; stability.

For floating equilibrium, buoyant force equals weight. Average density determines what fraction of an object's volume must be submerged in a uniform fluid. A hollow structure can have an average density below water even when its material density is much larger.

Stability also depends on geometry. Tilting changes the shape and location of the displaced-fluid volume, shifting the center of buoyancy. Whether the resulting torque restores or increases the tilt depends on the relation among center of mass, buoyant-force line, and hull shape.

Floating fraction and stability transfer

For a floating object in a uniform fluid, the submerged fraction is approximately ρ_object/ρ_fluid when the simple average-density model applies. An object averaging 750 kg/m³ in water therefore needs about 75% of its volume submerged at equilibrium.

Now tilt the object conceptually. The submerged shape changes, shifting the center of buoyancy. Whether that shift creates a restoring or overturning torque determines stability. Thus two objects with the same average density can float at the same overall fraction yet have very different stability.

Use this to separate “will it float?” from “will it float stably?”

Stability-model transfer check

Compare two floating shapes with the same average density but different center-of-mass heights. Both may displace the same fraction of volume, yet the lower-center-of-mass design can have a stronger restoring tendency after a small tilt.
