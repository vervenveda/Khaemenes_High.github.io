Floating, Sinking & Stability

Why can a heavy ship float while a small dense object sinks?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Floating equilibrium requires upward buoyant force to balance downward weight.

Average density includes hollow spaces; this is why a large ship can have lower average density than water.

Stability depends on shape, center of mass, and how buoyant force shifts with tilt.

Core relations: ρ=m/V; F_b=weight of displaced fluid.

Worked example

A floating object displaces exactly enough fluid that displaced-fluid weight equals object weight.

Guided question

Why can hollowing a metal shape make it float?

Independent work

  1. Solve three density/buoyancy problems.
  2. Draw one fluid force diagram.
  3. State one assumption or uncertainty.

Misconception check

A heavier object can float if its average density is low enough and it displaces sufficient fluid.

Formative check

What sets buoyant-force magnitude in Archimedes’ model?

Reveal response

The weight of the displaced fluid.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit density units, displacement reasoning, and force balance.
