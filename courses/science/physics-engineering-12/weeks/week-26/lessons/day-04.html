Buoyancy Investigation

Why can a heavy ship float while a small dense object sinks?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

This investigation tests density and displacement predictions.

Use water displacement with small safe objects or a simulation. Compare mass, displaced volume, density, and floating behavior.

Preserve raw mass/volume evidence before calculating density or buoyant force.

Core relations: ρ=m/V; F_b=weight of displaced fluid.

Worked example

If measured mass is 150 g and displaced volume is 200 cm³, average density is 0.75 g/cm³, consistent with floating in water.

Guided question

Which raw measurements test the density/floating prediction?

Investigation / Data Lab

Use water displacement with small safe objects or a simulation. Compare mass, displaced volume, density, and floating behavior.

Preserve raw mass/volume data, calculated density, displacement, uncertainty, and revision.

Misconception check

A heavier object can float if its average density is low enough and it displaces sufficient fluid.

Formative check

What sets buoyant-force magnitude in Archimedes’ model?

Reveal response

The weight of the displaced fluid.
