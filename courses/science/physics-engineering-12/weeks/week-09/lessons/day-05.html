Evidence: Why Can Orbiting Objects Fall Forever?

Objective: Develop a quantitative CER comparing falling and orbiting, with valid model limitations.

45–60 minutes · Complete offline lessons · Algebra and scientific reasoning

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Reading: Develop the model

State the claim

Near a massive spherical body, the same gravitational interaction accelerates both a dropped object and an ideal circularly orbiting object. For the circular orbit, sufficient tangential velocity and continuous inward gravity bend the path without surface contact. Orbit is not the absence of gravity; it is a type of free fall.

Use two independent calculations

An inverse-square ratio supports gravitational field weakening with distance: g at doubled radial distance is one quarter the original. A circular orbit speed and period calculation supports the claim that gravity supplies the centripetal acceleration. For a 400 km Earth-model orbit, g≈8.69 m/s² and period≈92.4 min. State constants and rounded units.

Respond to likely objections

Misconception one: astronauts float because gravity vanishes. Explain shared free fall and lack of ordinary support forces. Misconception two: orbital distance is measured only from the surface; explain r=R+h. Misconception three: outward 'centrifugal force' is an additional physical force in the Earth-centered inertial-frame diagram; draw only actual gravitational force as the radial net force.

Write limitations and plan an improved model

Perfect circles, Earth as a sphere, an isolated two-body system, no atmospheric drag and constant parameter μ are idealizations. A stronger model would consider elliptical orbits, perturbations or actual tracking observations. Five terms in context

claim–evidence–reasoning
Scientific format connecting a conclusion with quantitative evidence and principles.
inference
Conclusion supported but not directly observed in this simulation.
assumption
A condition accepted for model calculations.
counterclaim
Alternative account to be compared against evidence.
limitation
Known restriction on the model's validity.

Worked example

At h=400 km above Earth, r=6.771×10⁶ m. Using μ≈3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s² gives g≈8.69 m/s², v≈7.67 km/s and T≈92.4 min. Substantial inward gravity and tangential motion can coexist with apparent weightlessness.
