Gravitation & Orbital Motion

Five-day instructional plan with calculations, misconceptions, differentiations, accessible resources and teacher-reviewed evidence.

Purpose, sequence and safety

Essential question: How can the same gravitational interaction explain falling and orbiting? Connect Week 08 centripetal motion to the inverse-square law and orbital free fall. Lessons offer quantitative modeling related to HS-PS2-4 and HS-ESS1-4, without claiming these five sessions alone establish full standards mastery.

Prerequisites: mass and gravity forces, vector directions, Newton's three laws, radial force F=mv²/r, unit conversions between km and m, exponents and square roots. Students who need help receive the worked examples and printed CSV tables; advanced students derive circular speed and orbital period algebraically.

Safe model boundary: No launchers, rockets, unsafe drop experiments or moving apparatus. Both laboratories are offline computed models, not actual spacecraft tracking or mission/safety planning. Earth approximated by radius 6371 km and μ=3.986004418×10¹⁴ m³/s²; Moon radius 1737.4 km and μ=4.9048695×10¹² m³/s². Gravitational constant G≈6.67430×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg².

Monday — Why Astronauts Float

8-minute launch: Ask whether low Earth orbit has gravity. Students draw a curved satellite path and an inward arrow at one point.

17-minute explanation: Velocity is tangent, gravitational force and acceleration radial. Astronauts and the spacecraft share free fall and experience little support force: apparent weightlessness is not absent gravity.

15-minute check: At 400 km above Earth, r=6,771 km and g≈8.69 m/s². Compare with g≈9.82 m/s² in our Earth surface model.

5-minute exit: Explain shared free fall and two omitted realistic effects.

Tuesday — Inverse Square

Phenomenon: Double center-to-center distance while masses stay the same. Force becomes one quarter, NOT one half.

Models: F=Gm₁m₂/r², g=μ/r², and r=R+h. Altitude alone cannot be used as the r in these formulas. Field g has m/s² units, force F is in N.

Sample worked answer: g(400 km)/g(0)=(6371/6771)²≈0.885. g(800 km)/g(400 km)=(6771/7171)²≈0.891.

Practice: Analyze calculated field/force ratios in the Gravity Field Explorer and downloadable CSV.

Wednesday — Circular Orbital Speed & Period

Derivation: μm/r²=mv²/r for a circular orbit with gravitational force as its radial resultant. Satellite mass cancels, giving v=√(μ/r). Divide circumference by speed: T=2πr/v=2π√(r³/μ).

Earth model worked answer: h=400 km, r=6,771,000 m, v≈7.67 km/s and T≈92.4 min. Larger orbital radius gives lower circular speed but longer period.

Misconception probe: An ellipse is not a circular orbit; its instantaneous speed need not equal √(μ/r) at every point.

Wednesday lesson →

Thursday — Controlled Investigations

Gravity study: Three altitude trials for Earth with identical test mass; record g, F and center radius; compute one field ratio.

Orbit study: Three altitude trials for Earth; record circular speed and period; explain their opposite altitude trends.

Comparative enrichment: Repeat for the Moon, naming that its radius and μ both differ from Earth's, so this is not a one-variable experiment across planets.

Model quality: Ensure JSON notes include a testable question, named controlled variable, rounded units and one absent physical effect. Both labs provide accessible numeric tables and offline CSV alternatives.

Thursday lesson →

Friday — Claim–Evidence–Reasoning

Require a diagram with orbital tangent velocity and inward gravitational force; one correct inverse-square ratio, one orbital calculation, a limitation, a counterclaim corrected and one independent follow-up question.

Example: a satellite at 400 km has substantial gravity, ≈8.69 m/s² in the simplified model, and orbits with T≈92.4 min in the same model. A passenger floating in the spacecraft is in shared free fall, not outside Earth's gravity.

Randomized ten-question Friday exercise is open-book formative practice from a twenty-item bank. Corrective retries aim at 80%; public source-code questions and client-side scores are not proctored midterms, finals or authoritative grades.

20-point teacher-reviewed science CER rubric

Scientific claim · 4
Explains orbital free fall without asserting zero gravity, with defined model assumptions.
Quantitative evidence · 4
At least two correctly computed examples with SI units and credible precision.
Physical reasoning and diagrams · 4
Gravity acts inward while velocity is tangent; inverse-square dependence and circular condition explained.
Source/provenance · 4
Identifies calculated model results separately from actual measurements.
Limitations & correction · 4
Corrects a misconception and explains at least one omitted real-world effect.

Teacher-reviewed goal: 16/20 (80%). Document corrections. Formal examinations require a separate educator-controlled process.
