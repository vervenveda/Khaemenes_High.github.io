Use simulations or supervised low-voltage coil/magnet activities only. Do not use mains electricity, high-current coils, or improvised high-voltage equipment.

Investigation-depth extension

Preserve raw position-time or flux-time traces alongside induced-voltage data. Compare trials with similar total flux change but different durations. If the faster trial produces a larger peak emf, that supports the rate model.

Inspect both sign and magnitude. Reversing motion direction should reverse the sign pattern under the same geometry. Repeated traces that drift systematically may indicate calibration or inconsistent motion rather than physics failure.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the data support Faraday-style rate dependence and Lenz direction. Include uncertainty and one revision.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 05 residual analysis with induction-trace comparison.

Trace-analysis transfer

Compare two induced-voltage traces from equal total flux changes over different durations. The faster change should produce a taller, narrower pulse in the ideal rate model. Integrate or compare signed areas cautiously if the waveform shape changes.

If reversing magnet motion flips the voltage sign while preserving similar magnitude, that is strong evidence for the Lenz-law direction rule. Repeated mismatch in one direction suggests setup asymmetry or calibration.

Rate-versus-area check

Two voltage pulses can have different peak values yet correspond to the same total flux change if their time widths differ. Compare peak emf, pulse duration, and signed area before deciding whether the induction evidence is consistent.

Trace-quality check

Compare repeated induction traces at nominally the same speed. If pulse timing and peak magnitude vary randomly, treat that as measurement scatter; if every trace is shifted in one direction, investigate calibration or geometry before revising Faraday/Lenz reasoning.

Final induction-method check

Use the same coil geometry for two passes and compare both peak emf and flux-change duration. A fair Faraday comparison requires the changing-flux interval to be documented, not just the voltage peak.
