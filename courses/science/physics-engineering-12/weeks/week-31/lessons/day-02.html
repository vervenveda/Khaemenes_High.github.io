Electromagnetic Induction

Explain how changing magnetic flux can produce an induced emf.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations or supervised low-voltage coil/magnet activities only. Do not use mains electricity, high-current coils, or improvised high-voltage equipment.

Explicit physics instruction

Faraday's law links induced emf to the rate of change of magnetic flux through a circuit.

A larger flux change or a shorter time interval increases induced emf magnitude.

Lenz's law gives direction: the induced effect opposes the change in flux that produced it.

Vocabulary: magnetic flux; induced emf; Faraday's law; Lenz's law.

Worked example

If the same flux change occurs in half the time, induced emf magnitude doubles in the ideal proportional model.

Guided question

Compare induced emf when a magnet moves slowly versus quickly through the same coil.

Independent work

  1. Rank four flux-change scenarios by expected emf.
  2. Interpret one flux-versus-time graph.
  3. Use Lenz's-law reasoning for one direction case.

Misconception check

Induction depends on changing flux, not merely the presence of a magnetic field.

Formative check

What happens to ideal induced emf if magnetic flux stays constant?

Reveal response

It is zero.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit direction rules, rate reasoning, energy source, and model scope.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use qualitative flux-change comparisons.

Core: Interpret graphs and direction.

Extended: Connect flux derivative ideas with measured voltage traces.
