Magnetism & Induction Synthesis

Integrate fields, force, induction, and energy-conversion models.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations or supervised low-voltage coil/magnet activities only. Do not use mains electricity, high-current coils, or improvised high-voltage equipment.

Explicit physics instruction

Magnetic phenomena use two complementary ideas: magnetic fields exert forces on moving charges/currents, and changing magnetic flux can induce electric effects.

Generators and motors connect these models through energy transfer.

A strong explanation identifies which effect is acting, the direction rule, and the energy source.

Vocabulary: field; force; induction; energy conversion; model choice.

Worked example

A motor question begins with current in a magnetic field; a generator question begins with changing flux. Both require energy accounting.

Guided question

Given an unfamiliar electromechanical device, which model would you test first and why?

Independent work

  1. Solve one magnetic-force reasoning problem.
  2. Interpret one induction graph.
  3. Draw one generator/motor energy flow.
  4. Record one correction.

Spiral retrieval bridge

  1. From Week 30: connect voltage with energy per charge.
  2. From Week 29: distinguish field from force.
  3. From Week 21: connect external torque with angular-momentum change.

Misconception check

Magnetic force and electromagnetic induction are related but not interchangeable explanations.

Formative check

What distinguishes a motor model from a generator model?

Reveal response

Motors use magnetic force on current to produce motion; generators use changing flux to produce emf.
