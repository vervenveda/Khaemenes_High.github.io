Generators, Motors & Energy Conversion

Connect induction and magnetic force to energy conversion in generators and motors.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations or supervised low-voltage coil/magnet activities only. Do not use mains electricity, high-current coils, or improvised high-voltage equipment.

Explicit physics instruction

A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy through changing magnetic flux.

A motor converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation through magnetic forces on current-carrying conductors.

Neither device creates energy; efficiency is limited by heating, friction, magnetic losses, and other mechanisms.

Vocabulary: generator; motor; energy conversion; efficiency; back emf.

Worked example

A hand-cranked generator can light an LED because mechanical work changes magnetic flux and produces electrical energy transfer.

Guided question

Why does harder electrical loading make a generator harder to turn?

Independent work

  1. Trace energy through a generator and motor.
  2. Identify input/output/loss pathways.
  3. Compare ideal and real efficiency.

Misconception check

A generator does not create electrical energy from nothing; mechanical work supplies it.

Formative check

What is the energy input to a hand-cranked generator?

Reveal response

Mechanical work.
