Teacher / Parent Educator · Week 03

Acceleration & Velocity–Time Graphs

Five-day structured lesson plan, answer guidance, safe investigations and human-reviewed evidence.

Teaching purpose and academic alignment

Essential question: How can velocity change even when speed does not? Students progress from vector acceleration concepts to one-dimensional velocity–time slope and signed area as displacement. They distinguish distance from displacement during reversals and connect to constant-acceleration models. These skills support the broader HS-PS2-1 Newton's second-law course sequence and SEP graph/quantitative practices, but do not alone demonstrate mastery of the complete force-focused performance expectation.

Prerequisites: Week 02 frame, position slope, signs, units, and averaging. All graphs here use a one-dimensional straight-line mathematical model. A turning body at constant speed is a conceptual two-dimensional example and cannot be simulated fully by this one-dimensional tool.

Safety: Use the supplied idealized CSV and graph simulator; no real projectile launchers, ramps, bicycles or fast-moving objects are required.

Vocabulary reference

Velocity
Vector rate of change of position; one-dimensional value has a sign and units m/s.
Speed
Nonnegative magnitude of velocity, measured in m/s.
Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity; mean a=Δv/Δt, unit m/s².
Velocity–time graph
Plot of signed velocity vertically against time horizontally.
Signed area
Positive area above and negative area below the time axis; total is displacement.
Turning point
For nonzero constant acceleration, the instant when signed velocity crosses zero and reverses.
Constant acceleration
An acceleration that remains unchanged through a chosen interval.
Instantaneous velocity
Velocity at a particular time, represented by the slope of the position-time curve.
Uncertainty
Limits of confidence and precision in actual observed values.

Five daily teaching notes

  1. Monday: Contrast vectors pointing around a circle with one-dimensional signed velocity. Negative acceleration need not mean decreasing speed.
  2. Tuesday: Draw v(t) from +3 m/s at t=1 to −1 m/s at t=3: slope −2 m/s². Cross zero and explain reversal.
  3. Wednesday: Compare signed area versus absolute area for v₀=4 m/s, a=−2 m/s², T=4 s: signed displacement 0 m, distance 8 m.
  4. Thursday: In the simulator, record runs with a=0, a=+1, and a=−2 (all else matched); check slopes and area numerically. Note that changing acceleration affects position curvature.
  5. Friday: Require at least two quantitative pieces of evidence with units, one signed-area computation, one limitation, and an appropriately qualified model claim.

Dataset answer and worked evaluation

Supplied CSV uses x₀=0 m, v₀=4 m/s, a=−2 m/s², T=4 s. Positions at t=0,1,2,3,4 are 0,3,4,3,0 m. Velocities are +4,+2,0,−2,−4 m/s. Mean acceleration (−4−4)/4=−2 m/s². Velocity graph crosses zero at 2 s; signed area Δx=(4+−4)/2×4=0 m; two triangular area magnitudes each 4 m give total distance 8 m. Be explicit that these are ideal calculations, not real motion sensor measurements.

Misconception: when v is negative and a is negative, speed can increase. The acceleration sign alone cannot establish whether the object is speeding up.

Student differentiation

Foundation: teach a signed number line, provide labeled graph axes, calculate a slope with a model answer, and use the printed CSV. Core: independently evaluate graph slopes or areas, conduct three matched simulation runs, write evidence and corrections. Extension: critique frame assumptions or zero-crossing timing and discuss measurement uncertainty in real sensor data. Each student may use a keyboard-accessible table instead of interpreting a graph image alone.

Students have access to this public teaching material and its practice explanations. Nothing on this page is a private, authenticated teacher answer key for supervised exams.

20-point claim–evidence–reasoning rubric

Claim — 4 points
Answers essential question using the correct frame and signed variables.
Calculations — 4
Two relevant worked numerical quantities and correct SI units.
Graph reading — 4
Correct slope, area or direction interpretation with axes.
Scientific reasoning — 4
Uses appropriate equation and explicit assumptions.
Limitations — 4
Reports synthetic/ideal assumptions and one useful next test.

Review target: 16/20 (80%). Revisions document progress; a generated quiz percentage is only formative practice.

Misconception checklist

  • Changing frames changes coordinates but not the event.
  • Position–time graph slope is velocity, not acceleration.
  • Velocity–time graph slope is acceleration; signed area is displacement.
  • Distance is nonnegative even if displacement is negative or zero.
  • Negative acceleration does not automatically mean decreasing speed.
  • Simulated curves are not measurements from real sensors.
