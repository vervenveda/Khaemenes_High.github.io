Defend Your Graph Model

How do slope and area reveal different dimensions of motion?

Objective: Explain average acceleration and displacement quantitatively with graph evidence and model limitations.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice is not a supervised examination

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Read and reason

Use the correct graph operation

A defensible statement can say: 'For the ideal one-dimensional constant-acceleration model, the slope of v versus t reports acceleration, while its signed area reports displacement.' Both halves have a precise mathematical meaning. Do not write that all areas are distances or that a negative acceleration always slows an object. Direction matters.

Build two numerical pieces of evidence

Choose v₀=4 m/s, a=−2 m/s² and T=4 s. The end velocity is −4 m/s. The signed area is zero, so displacement is zero. But positive and negative triangular regions each have magnitude 4 m, giving total traveled distance 8 m. Record the axes, units and turning time. Compare the analytic result with a model graph showing the same values.

Explain a model's assumptions

The simulator assumes acceleration remains constant, speeds are low and a single straight-line coordinate is sufficient. No real object is being measured, and rounding the display can create slight visual differences. Real motion data have noise and uncertain timing. A curve fit should not be chosen solely because it is aesthetically smooth; models must be justified by physics and observations.

Correct misconceptions before the next unit

The Friday public quiz draws ten questions from the Week 03 bank, randomizing the set and answer order each attempt. Feedback supports corrections and an 80% practice target. A local browser grade is not an official closed-book exam. The teacher should separately review evidence quality and use an appropriately controlled process for high-stakes assessments.

Safety and scope: Investigate with supplied synthetic datasets and browser models. Do not launch hard objects, operate motor vehicles, set up hazardous ramps, or place anyone near moving machinery. The simulator calculates ideal kinematics; it does not measure a real device.

Worked example

If v falls from +4 m/s to −4 m/s over 4 s, slope a=−2 m/s². Signed area/displacement=0 m, whereas traveled distance=8 m. The line crosses v=0 at 2 s.

Check signs and units. Show each calculation rather than memorizing a number.
