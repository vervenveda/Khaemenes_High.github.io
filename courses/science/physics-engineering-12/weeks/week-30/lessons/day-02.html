Series & Parallel Circuits

Predict current and voltage patterns in series and parallel networks.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use only low-voltage classroom batteries/components under supervision or an approved circuit simulation. Never use mains voltage.

Explicit physics instruction

In a series path, the same current passes through each component and voltage drops add to the source voltage.

In parallel branches, the same potential difference appears across each branch while branch currents add to total current.

Equivalent resistance depends on arrangement; adding a series resistor increases total resistance, while adding a parallel branch can reduce equivalent resistance.

Core relation/model: series: R_eq=ΣR; parallel: 1/R_eq=Σ1/R

Worked example

Two 3 Ω resistors in series give 6 Ω. The same pair in parallel gives 1.5 Ω.

Guided question

Compare equivalent resistance for 4 Ω and 12 Ω in series versus parallel.

Independent work

  1. Compute equivalent resistance for three networks.
  2. Predict current/voltage patterns.
  3. Explain one result with a junction/loop argument.

Misconception check

Parallel branches do not 'split voltage' the way series components do; branch voltage is shared across the same two nodes.

Formative check

Which arrangement gives lower equivalent resistance for the same two positive resistors?

Reveal response

Parallel.

Vocabulary and network-conservation extension

Vocabulary: node; branch; series; parallel; equivalent resistance.

Series current equality follows from charge conservation along one path. Parallel branch voltages are equal because each branch connects across the same two nodes. At a junction, incoming and outgoing currents must balance in steady state.

Equivalent resistance is a model for the whole network seen by the source; it does not mean the physical resistors disappear.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend one series and one parallel prediction using node/branch reasoning rather than memorized slogans. Revise any claim that parallel branches “split voltage.”

Spiral retrieval

Connect conservation of charge at a node with earlier system-conservation reasoning.

Network transfer and uncertainty

Two resistors, 4 Ω and 12 Ω, give 16 Ω in series and 3 Ω in parallel. With a 12 V source, the series current is 0.75 A, while total current for the parallel network is 4.0 A. Topology dramatically changes source demand.

If resistor values have tolerance, equivalent resistance also has uncertainty. A design near a current limit should use a range rather than one nominal value.

At a junction, branch currents must sum to the source current in steady state; this is charge conservation expressed as a circuit constraint.

Kirchhoff-style consistency check

For a parallel network, calculate branch currents from the shared voltage and branch resistances, then verify that their sum equals the source current. For a series loop, verify that component voltage drops add to the source voltage.

These are conservation checks, not extra formulas to memorize.
