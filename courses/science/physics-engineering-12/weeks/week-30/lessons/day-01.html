Current, Voltage & Resistance

Relate current, potential difference, and resistance in a simple circuit.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use only low-voltage classroom batteries/components under supervision or an approved circuit simulation. Never use mains voltage.

Explicit physics instruction

Current is rate of charge flow. Voltage is energy transferred per unit charge between two points. Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes current under a given model.

For an ohmic component, V=IR. The relation must use compatible units: volts, amperes, and ohms.

A circuit requires a closed conducting path and an energy source; current is not 'used up' as it passes through a component.

Core relation/model: V=IR

Worked example

A 12 V source across a 4 Ω resistor gives I=12/4=3 A.

Guided question

A 9 V source drives 1.5 A through an ohmic resistor. Find R.

Independent work

  1. Solve three V=IR problems.
  2. Draw a simple source-resistor circuit.
  3. State what each variable means physically.

Misconception check

Current is not consumed by a resistor; energy is transferred while charge continues around the closed path.

Formative check

What happens to current if V stays fixed and R doubles?

Reveal response

It halves in the simple ohmic model.

Vocabulary and Ohmic-model extension

Vocabulary: current; voltage; resistance; ohmic component; closed circuit.

Current is charge flow rate I=ΔQ/Δt. Voltage is energy transferred per unit charge. Ohm's law V=IR is an empirical model for components whose resistance is approximately constant over the tested range; it is not a definition that all components must obey.

Temperature changes can make resistance change, so a resistor that appears ohmic over one range may deviate at higher power.

Ohmic transfer and uncertainty

Take V–I pairs (2 V, 0.40 A), (4 V, 0.80 A), and (6 V, 1.20 A). Each gives R≈5 Ω, supporting a constant-resistance model. If a fourth point at 8 V gives only 1.3 A, resistance has increased to about 6.2 Ω, suggesting heating or non-ohmic behavior.

Measured voltage and current uncertainty propagate into R=V/I. Near low current, fractional current uncertainty can dominate the resistance estimate.

Current-charge transfer check

If 6 C of charge passes a point in 3 s, current is 2 A. If the same current continues for 10 s, 20 C passes. Use I=ΔQ/Δt to connect microscopic charge transport with the circuit-level current variable.

State why current can remain the same through a series resistor even though energy is transferred there.
