Circuit Investigation

Test Ohm's law and series/parallel predictions with safe data.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use only low-voltage classroom batteries/components under supervision or an approved circuit simulation. Never use mains voltage.

Explicit physics instruction

A circuit investigation should preserve measured or simulated V and I pairs before fitting a model.

For an ohmic resistor, V versus I should be approximately linear with slope R.

Series/parallel predictions can be tested by comparing total current and branch behavior.

Core relation/model: slope of V vs I ≈ R

Worked example

If V/I values remain near 5 Ω across several trials, the data support an ohmic model over that range.

Guided question

What graph best tests whether resistance is approximately constant?

Independent work

  1. Preserve raw V/I data.
  2. Graph V versus I.
  3. Estimate resistance from slope.
  4. Compare one series/parallel prediction and record a residual.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use a circuit simulation or supervised low-voltage setup to compare voltage, current, resistance, series/parallel behavior, and power.

Preserve raw data, calculations, graph/model comparison, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

A single V/I point cannot establish linear ohmic behavior across a range.

Formative check

What does the slope of a V-versus-I graph represent for an ohmic resistor?

Reveal response

Resistance.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit units, assumptions, model selection, and whether the evidence supports the conclusion.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied simulated data.

Core: Fit/interpret V-I data and network behavior.

Extended: Analyze uncertainty or nonlinearity.

Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: V–I graph; slope; resistance; residual; nonlinearity.

Preserve raw V and I pairs before fitting a line. For an ohmic resistor, V versus I should be approximately linear through the relevant range, with slope R. Inspect residuals rather than relying only on a single best-fit slope.

If residuals curve systematically, resistance may be changing with temperature or the component may be non-ohmic. Repeat trials or compare current ranges before assigning a constant R.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the data support constant resistance. Include uncertainty and one revision.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 05 residual patterns with circuit-model testing.

V–I residual practice

Fit a straight line to several V–I points and inspect residuals. Residuals that scatter around zero support approximate ohmic behavior; residuals that curve upward with current suggest resistance increasing with temperature.

Preserve the tested current range. A component can be approximately ohmic over a low-power interval and non-ohmic at higher current, so model scope belongs in the conclusion.

Model-range check

Use separate low-current and high-current subsets of the V–I data. If the low-current region is linear but the high-current region curves, report an ohmic approximation only over the validated range.

State which additional temperature or repeated-trial measurement would test the heating explanation.

Final model-range check

State the validated current range for the fitted resistance and explain why extrapolating beyond that range can fail if temperature changes.

Uncertainty-fit transfer

If V and I measurements each carry uncertainty, show how that affects confidence in slope-derived resistance. A small apparent curvature should not be called non-ohmic unless it exceeds the measurement scatter and persists across repeated trials.

Final note

Repeat the fit.
