Space-system synthesis depth

An Earth-observation mission is a coupled physics and engineering system. Orbital mechanics determines where and when the platform can observe; electromagnetic interactions determine what signal reaches the sensor; calibration and data processing determine how measurements are converted into usable evidence; engineering constraints determine power, bandwidth, mass, cost, and mission lifetime.

A high-resolution sensor can generate more data and may require narrower coverage, more storage, more transmission capacity, or different orbit choices. A frequent-revisit mission may accept coarser spatial detail to observe rapid change. These tradeoffs should be stated quantitatively whenever the available evidence supports it.

Integrated evidence chain

Construct an end-to-end chain: mission goal → orbit choice → sensor bands → measured signal → calibration/processing → derived quantity → scientific inference → uncertainty → decision. Mark each step as direct measurement, calculation, model assumption, or interpretation so the evidence cannot silently change category.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend one mission architecture using an orbit model, an energy or spectral calculation, a dataset interpretation, and a constraint/criterion argument. Then introduce one changed priority and revise the recommendation if the evidence warrants it.

Cross-course transfer

Connect the mission to at least two earlier physics domains: gravity and circular motion, EM waves, circuits/power, thermal energy, or engineering optimization. Explain which model controls each subsystem and where model limits could create mission risk.

Final uncertainty check

Name the measurement or assumption that contributes most to uncertainty in the final scientific inference. Propose one additional observation, calibration step, or independent dataset that would most effectively reduce that uncertainty.

Investigation / model check

Satellite/remote-sensing dataset analysis; Solanar extension. Preserve observations or supplied data, compare them with the model, and record one limitation or source of uncertainty.
