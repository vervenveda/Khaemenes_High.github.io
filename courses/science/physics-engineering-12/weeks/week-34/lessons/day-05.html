Space Systems Synthesis

Integrate orbital physics, radiation, energy balance, and remote-sensing evidence.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Space systems combine mechanics, waves, sensing, and engineering constraints.

A strong explanation separates what the satellite measures from what scientists infer from those measurements.

System design involves tradeoffs among orbit, resolution, revisit time, bandwidth, power, and mission goals.

Vocabulary: space system; measurement; inference; mission tradeoff; evidence.

Worked example

A weather mission may prioritize frequent revisit and broad coverage, while a mapping mission may prioritize finer spatial resolution.

Guided question

Design a mission tradeoff for one Earth-observation goal and justify the orbit/sensor priorities.

Independent work

  1. Submit one orbit vector model.
  2. Submit one energy-balance calculation.
  3. Interpret one spectral dataset.
  4. Write a mission tradeoff defense.

Spiral retrieval bridge

  1. From Week 33: distinguish criteria from constraints.

Misconception check

More resolution is not always universally better; it can trade against coverage, revisit, cost, or bandwidth.

Formative check

What distinguishes measurement from inference in remote sensing?

Reveal response

Measurement is the sensor-observed signal; inference is the interpretation about the target.
