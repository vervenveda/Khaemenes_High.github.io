Spectral Signatures & Inference

Use multi-band electromagnetic measurements to infer surface or atmospheric properties.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Different materials reflect, absorb, and emit different fractions across wavelength bands, creating spectral signatures.

Inference requires calibration and comparison with references; one color or band rarely identifies a target uniquely.

Spatial, spectral, and temporal resolution tradeoffs influence what questions a remote-sensing system can answer.

Vocabulary: spectral signature; calibration; band; spatial resolution; inference.

Worked example

A surface that strongly reflects near-infrared but absorbs red light can be distinguished from another surface using a ratio or multi-band pattern.

Spectral-inference depth

A sensor records electromagnetic signal within defined wavelength bands. A spectral signature is therefore a pattern of measured response across bands, not a material label printed by the instrument. Interpretation requires calibration, reference data, viewing geometry, and attention to atmospheric or illumination effects.

Suppose a reference surface has red reflectance 0.20 and near-infrared reflectance 0.60. A simple NIR/red ratio is 3.0. A target with values 0.30 and 0.45 gives a ratio of 1.5. The difference can support classification, but overlap between materials, shadows, moisture, sensor noise, and mixed pixels can prevent a unique conclusion.

Resolution tradeoffs

Spatial resolution describes ground detail, spectral resolution describes wavelength discrimination, temporal resolution describes revisit frequency, and radiometric resolution describes sensitivity to intensity differences. Improving one dimension can increase data volume, cost, noise sensitivity, or reduce another mission capability.

Evidence revision / CER

Make a bounded claim about the most likely match for a synthetic target. Cite at least two bands or a derived ratio, compare the target with references, and state one alternative explanation. Revise the claim if calibration uncertainty or reference overlap makes the classification ambiguous.

Model and source quality

Prefer calibrated measurements and documented reference spectra over unlabeled color images. Record the band definitions, units or normalized scale, processing steps, and uncertainty so the inference remains traceable.

Spiral retrieval

Retrieve wavelength/frequency from Week 32 and measurement uncertainty from earlier labs. Explain why electromagnetic physics tells us what was measured while evidence reasoning determines how confidently we can infer what produced the signal.

Classification validation check

Test the inferred material against an independent band, ratio, or reference signature. If the second test disagrees, classify the result as unresolved rather than forcing a match, and explain which additional measurement would best discriminate between the competing interpretations.
