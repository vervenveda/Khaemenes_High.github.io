Spectral Signatures & Inference

Use multi-band electromagnetic measurements to infer surface or atmospheric properties.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Different materials reflect, absorb, and emit different fractions across wavelength bands, creating spectral signatures.

Inference requires calibration and comparison with references; one color or band rarely identifies a target uniquely.

Spatial, spectral, and temporal resolution tradeoffs influence what questions a remote-sensing system can answer.

Vocabulary: spectral signature; calibration; band; spatial resolution; inference.

Worked example

A surface that strongly reflects near-infrared but absorbs red light can be distinguished from another surface using a ratio or multi-band pattern.

Guided question

Why is a multi-band pattern stronger evidence than one brightness value?

Independent work

  1. Compare three synthetic spectral signatures.
  2. Identify one likely match to a reference.
  3. State one ambiguity or confounder.

Misconception check

A bright pixel is not automatically one material; brightness depends on illumination, geometry, sensor response, and wavelength.

Formative check

What makes a remote-sensing inference traceable?

Reveal response

Calibrated measurements, stated bands, reference comparisons, and uncertainty.
