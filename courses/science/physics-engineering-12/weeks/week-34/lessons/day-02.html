Radiation, Albedo & Energy Balance

Model incoming, reflected, absorbed, and emitted energy in a planetary system.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Albedo is the reflected fraction of incoming radiation. Absorbed fraction is approximately 1−albedo in a simple model.

Earth's long-term energy balance compares incoming solar energy with reflected and emitted energy.

A temporary imbalance changes stored energy, but local weather and climate systems add transport, feedbacks, and spatial complexity.

Vocabulary: albedo; absorbed flux; emitted radiation; energy balance; feedback.

Worked example

If incoming flux is 1000 W/m² and albedo is 0.30, the simple absorbed flux is 700 W/m².

Guided question

Calculate absorbed flux for 800 W/m² incoming and albedo 0.25.

Energy-balance depth

An energy-balance model begins with a clearly chosen system boundary. Incoming solar radiation can be reflected or absorbed; absorbed energy can increase stored energy temporarily and is ultimately transferred and emitted. Flux is power per unit area, so every term in a simple balance should carry units such as W/m².

If 800 W/m² arrives and albedo is 0.25, the simple absorbed flux is 800(1−0.25)=600 W/m². That arithmetic is only the first layer. A global or top-of-system model averages over geometry, clouds, surface type, atmosphere, day/night cycles, and energy transport, so the result should not be mistaken for a local surface temperature prediction.

Feedback and model limits

A feedback occurs when a system change alters a process that then influences the original change. Distinguish a feedback from an external forcing and distinguish short-term variability from a persistent energy imbalance. State which variables are measured directly and which are model-derived.

Evidence revision / CER

Use a flux calculation and an energy-flow diagram to support a claim about whether the simplified system is gaining, losing, or balancing energy. Revise the claim when an omitted pathway or uncertainty is large enough to alter the conclusion.

Uncertainty check

If albedo is reported as 0.30 ± 0.02, calculate the corresponding range of absorbed fraction. Explain why uncertainty in a dimensionless fraction propagates into the absorbed-flux estimate rather than disappearing during multiplication.

Spiral retrieval

Retrieve conservation of energy from the mechanics/thermal sequence and radiation from the EM-wave sequence. Explain how both ideas are needed to interpret planetary energy balance.

Balance-model transfer check

Apply the same energy ledger to a second albedo value or incoming flux. Explain whether the direction of change is consistent with the model and identify which omitted process would matter most before extending the result to a real climate system.
