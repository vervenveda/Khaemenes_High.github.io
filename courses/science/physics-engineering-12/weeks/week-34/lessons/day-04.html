Dataset-investigation depth

Begin with provenance: identify whether the supplied dataset is synthetic, classroom-curated, or derived from a documented source. Preserve the raw values before filtering, normalization, ratios, or graphing. Every transformation should be reproducible from the retained measurements.

For example, if a near-infrared/red ratio changes from 1.80 to 1.20, the absolute change is −0.60 and the relative change from the first value is about −33%. The arithmetic describes the signal change; it does not by itself identify the physical cause. Illumination, clouds, calibration drift, surface change, moisture, or mixed pixels may compete as explanations.

Graph and anomaly reasoning

Choose axes and scales that preserve the relevant pattern, label units or dimensionless ratios, and avoid connecting sparse observations in a way that implies unmeasured behavior. Compare any anomaly with expected measurement uncertainty and with nearby or repeated observations before treating it as evidence of a real system change.

Evidence revision / CER

Write one claim about the dataset, cite the raw and processed evidence, and explain why the pattern exceeds or does not exceed the uncertainty. Include one alternative explanation and revise the claim if additional evidence weakens the original interpretation.

Data ethics and model limits

Do not hide inconvenient points without a documented exclusion rule. Distinguish missing data from zero, correlation from causation, and processed visualization from the measurements used to create it.

Spiral retrieval

Apply residual, uncertainty, and graph-selection habits from earlier Physics 12 investigations. Explain how those same habits protect remote-sensing conclusions from overinterpretation.

Anomaly validation check

Recalculate the anomaly with one alternate baseline or neighboring observation. If the conclusion changes substantially, report baseline sensitivity and avoid a strong causal claim. Strong remote-sensing evidence should remain interpretable under reasonable analysis choices.
