Orbits & Remote Observation

Explain how orbital motion enables repeated Earth observation and measurement from a distance.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A satellite in orbit is continuously falling under gravity while moving tangentially fast enough to miss Earth.

Remote sensing measures electromagnetic radiation reflected or emitted by a target; it does not require direct physical contact.

Orbit choice affects revisit time, coverage, resolution, and communication geometry.

Vocabulary: orbit; remote sensing; revisit time; resolution; electromagnetic signal.

Worked example

A satellite that revisits the same region frequently may trade broad coverage for temporal detail, depending on its orbit.

Guided question

Why is a satellite in orbit still accelerating even if its speed magnitude is nearly constant?

Independent work

  1. Sketch velocity and acceleration vectors for circular orbit.
  2. Compare two orbit choices qualitatively.
  3. Name two remote-sensing measurements that rely on EM radiation.

Misconception check

Orbiting does not mean gravity is absent; gravity supplies centripetal acceleration.

Formative check

What direction is orbital acceleration in a circular model?

Reveal response

Toward the center of Earth.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to separate direct measurement from inference and audit model assumptions.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use vector diagrams.

Core: Connect orbit geometry with sensing tradeoffs.

Extended: Compare circular-orbit assumptions with real orbital limits.
