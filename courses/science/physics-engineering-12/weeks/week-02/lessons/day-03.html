What a Position Graph Actually Says

How do slopes encode velocity rather than altitude or path?

Objective: Determine the signed slope of a position–time graph and identify rest, constant velocity and reversals.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice is not a supervised examination

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Read and reason

Read the axes before interpreting

A position–time graph puts time t in seconds on the horizontal axis and signed position x in meters on the vertical. If the vertical axis is labeled displacement, it may show x−x₀ instead. Slope equals vertical change divided by horizontal change, (x₂−x₁)/(t₂−t₁); its units are m/s, making it an average velocity over the chosen interval.

The graph can rise and still mean leftward motion

A positive slope corresponds to motion toward increasing position; a negative slope corresponds to decreasing position. This depends on the defined positive direction, not on which way an object appears to move on a screen. If a graph is level while time advances, the position is constant and the object's velocity is zero over that segment. A steep slope is a larger speed magnitude for a straight segment.

Curvature and instantaneous velocity

For a curved position graph, average velocity across two times is the secant slope connecting the two plotted points. Instantaneous velocity at a time is the slope of the tangent at that time in the ideal continuous model. Curvature indicates changing velocity, although the exact value must be established by quantitative calculations. A graph turning upward does not automatically mean a vehicle is physically climbing a hill.

Evaluate the model against data

A line through two points gives a mean slope; extra points can expose curvature or measurement error. If measurements are uncertain, a plausible slope range may be better than a single overly precise value. Record graph units and coordinates used for every calculation. When choosing a model, distinguish an assumption of uniform velocity from empirical evidence that velocity is uniform.

Worked example

At t=1 s, x=4 m; at t=5 s, x=−8 m. Average velocity = (−8−4)/(5−1)=−12/4=−3 m/s.
