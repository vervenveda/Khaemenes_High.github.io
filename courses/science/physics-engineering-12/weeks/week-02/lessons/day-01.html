Two Observers, Two Descriptions

Why can a passenger rest in a train but move relative to the ground?

Objective: Define a reference frame and describe position relative to a specified origin.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice is not a supervised examination

Read and reason

The puzzle of the moving passenger

A student seated on a smoothly moving train remains at the same seat coordinate when measured by someone inside the train. A person standing beside the track measures the student moving with the train. The descriptions do not contradict each other. They answer different questions about motion relative to different reference frames. A position without a named origin and axis is incomplete information.

Select a coordinate system

One-dimensional motion is described using a signed coordinate x and a time t. Choose an origin, a positive direction, and units such as meters and seconds. A position of x = −4 m means four meters on the negative side of the origin, not a negative distance traveled. Changing the origin shifts coordinate values; changing the direction declared positive changes signs. Neither operation moves the actual object.

Inertial reference frames

For the activities in this unit, reference frames move at constant velocity relative to each other. If a train moves at +3 m/s relative to the ground, and a passenger walks at +1 m/s relative to the train, the passenger moves at +4 m/s relative to the ground in this idealized low-speed scenario. In a frame moving with the train, the same passenger is measured at +1 m/s. This uses the Galilean transformation, appropriate when speeds are far below the speed of light.

Build evidence, not just a story

The exact position coordinates depend on the origin, direction and reference frame. Record which frame you use. In a model x(t) = x₀ + vt, a frame moving at constant speed u relative to the original frame can use x′(t) = x(t) − ut when the origins coincide at time zero. Graph the same event in both frames; compare the shapes, velocities and accelerations. Do not use changing coordinate values as a claim that the physics has become inconsistent.

Safety and scope: Investigate with supplied synthetic datasets and browser models. Do not launch hard objects, operate motor vehicles, set up hazardous ramps, or place anyone near moving machinery. The simulator calculates ideal kinematics; it does not measure a real device.

Worked example

A cart is at x₀=2 m and moves +3 m/s for 4 s. Ground position = 2 + 3×4 = 14 m. A frame moving +2 m/s has x′=14−2×4=6 m at that instant. Both frames describe one event.

Check signs and units. Show each calculation rather than memorizing a number.
