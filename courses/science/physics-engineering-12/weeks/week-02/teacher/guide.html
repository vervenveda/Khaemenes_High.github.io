Teacher / Parent Educator · Week 02

Position, Displacement & Velocity

Five-day structured lesson plan, answer guidance, safe investigations and human-reviewed evidence.

Teaching purpose and academic alignment

Essential question: How does a reference frame change the description of motion? The 5-day sequence builds one-dimensional position, displacement, distance, average speed, average velocity, constant speed-frame transformations and position-time slope interpretation. It supports quantitative modeling and data analysis practices within the broader HS-PS2-1 science program; it does not claim to fully satisfy that force-focused performance expectation alone.

Before beginning: confirm students can use signed number lines, calculate rise/run, keep the unit m or m/s, and distinguish a coordinate from traveled path length. Day 01 introduces frames moving at constant velocity, avoiding an unsupported claim about accelerating or relativistic frames.

Safety: All core labs are offline algebra and fictional datasets. No running, cycling on traffic routes, ramps, moving vehicles, projectiles, or equipment is needed. A teacher can optionally supervise a stationary tabletop soft toy/marker observation, but the supplied-data path is fully sufficient.

Vocabulary reference

Reference frame
The origin, axes and observer used to assign positions and velocities.
Position
A signed location coordinate x relative to a chosen origin.
Displacement
The signed change xfinal−xinitial, measured in m.
Distance
The nonnegative length traveled along a path, measured in m.
Speed
Rate of travel as a nonnegative scalar; average speed = distance/time.
Velocity
The rate of signed displacement; average velocity = Δx/Δt, m/s.
Slope
Vertical change divided by horizontal change between two points.
Origin
The x=0 location selected for a coordinate system.
Moving frame
A reference frame that translates at velocity u relative to another frame.
Control variable
Parameter held fixed during a one-variable comparison.

Five daily teaching notes

  1. Monday: Different observer coordinates need not conflict. Give the same object two frames; ask students to define the chosen origin and positive axis.
  2. Tuesday: Walk 5 m east and 3 m west on paper. Distance 8 m, displacement +2 m. Emphasize a return trip has zero average velocity but positive average speed.
  3. Wednesday: Given (t,x)=(1 s,4 m) and (5 s,−8 m), slope = −3 m/s. Contrast a straight line with a curve through the same endpoints.
  4. Thursday: Model x₀=0, v₀=3, a=0, T=4: with u=0, Δx=12; with u=1, Δx′=8. Keep x₀,v₀,a,T fixed, export runs and compare slopes.
  5. Friday: Check claim, two numerical pieces of evidence, coordinate-frame labels, physical reasoning, one limitation and correction notes. Public quiz is formative only.

Dataset answer and worked evaluation

From the provided CSV: ground x(t) begins 2 m and ends 14 m over 4 s, so signed Δx=12 m and mean velocity +3 m/s. Observer frame with u=+1 m/s begins at 2 m and ends at 10 m, so Δx′=8 m and frame mean velocity +2 m/s. The frame's speed shift follows v′=v−u=3−1=2 m/s, consistent with the graph slopes. Acceleration is zero in both constant-speed frames.

Misconception: different position coordinates do not imply different events. The frames have different coordinate assignments, not competing physical timelines. Never call frame velocity a speed magnitude without checking the sign and reference direction.

Student differentiation

Foundation: teach a signed number line, provide labeled graph axes, calculate a slope with a model answer, and use the printed CSV. Core: independently evaluate graph slopes or areas, conduct three matched simulation runs, write evidence and corrections. Extension: critique frame assumptions or zero-crossing timing and discuss measurement uncertainty in real sensor data. Each student may use a keyboard-accessible table instead of interpreting a graph image alone.

Students have access to this public teaching material and its practice explanations. Nothing on this page is a private, authenticated teacher answer key for supervised exams.

20-point claim–evidence–reasoning rubric

Claim — 4 points
Answers essential question using the correct frame and signed variables.
Calculations — 4
Two relevant worked numerical quantities and correct SI units.
Graph reading — 4
Correct slope, area or direction interpretation with axes.
Scientific reasoning — 4
Uses appropriate equation and explicit assumptions.
Limitations — 4
Reports synthetic/ideal assumptions and one useful next test.

Review target: 16/20 (80%). Revisions document progress; a generated quiz percentage is only formative practice.

Misconception checklist

  • Changing frames changes coordinates but not the event.
  • Position–time graph slope is velocity, not acceleration.
  • Velocity–time graph slope is acceleration; signed area is displacement.
  • Distance is nonnegative even if displacement is negative or zero.
  • Negative acceleration does not automatically mean decreasing speed.
  • Simulated curves are not measurements from real sensors.
