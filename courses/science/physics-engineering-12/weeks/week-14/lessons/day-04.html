Physics & Engineering 12 · Week 14 · Thursday

Model a Controlled Impact Without Impacting Anything

Learning objective: Develop numerical impact comparisons and identify limits of collision-safety claims.

45–60 minutes · Digital-only investigations · Student work stays locally

Complete science reading

Virtual rather than physical collision tests

Real high-force impacts are hazardous and unnecessary for this course. We study a prescribed momentum change for a hypothetical object at low classroom numbers, then compare its mean force across different times. Students must not collide carts, drop weights onto padding, test protective gear on people or use these formulas as injury risk predictions.

A controlled matched trial

A 2 kg object goes from +3 m/s to −1 m/s, yielding Δp=2(−1−3)=−8 N·s. If this prescribed change happens in 0.20 s, the mean force is −40 N; at 0.80 s, mean force is −10 N. Mass, initial speed, final speed and total impulse are identical: only duration changes.

Add a force-profile comparison

For −8 N·s over 0.80 s, the equivalent rectangular pulse has force −10 N, while a symmetric triangular pulse has mean −10 N and peak −20 N. The toy force profile is independent of any actual collision material. State the waveform choice when discussing peak rather than assuming the mean equals maximum.

Scientific integrity and engineering criteria

A real engineering assessment would also require actual contact-force measurements, multiple axes, pulse duration definition, body deformation and relevant safety standards. In this virtual lesson we can prove impulse equality for chosen conditions, but cannot validate an actual padding system. Document model assumptions and a safer method of collecting future measured evidence.

Virtual experiments and synthetic data only. No physical collisions, person-to-person impacts, loaded carts or actual safety testing are required or recommended.

Vocabulary and definitions

controlled trial
Comparison where variables are specified and only one is intentionally changed.
duration
Positive interval during which the impulse is delivered.
force profile
Assumed or measured shape of force over time.
model assumption
Simplification such as one-dimensional constant mass.
peak-load limitation
Why average force cannot by itself certify maximum impact force.

Worked example
