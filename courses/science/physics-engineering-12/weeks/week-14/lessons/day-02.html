Impulse Changes Momentum

Learning objective: Use the impulse–momentum theorem and separate net impulse from work.

45–60 minutes · Digital-only investigations · Student work stays locally

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Complete science reading

Impulse is a change in momentum

For one body, net impulse J_net=Δp=m(v_final−v_initial) when mass remains constant. Its direction matters. A 1.5 kg cart slowing from +4 m/s to 0 has Δp=1.5(0−4)=−6 N·s. This negative sign indicates impulse to the left in a right-positive coordinate system.

A force sustained over time

When net force is constant during a duration Δt, J_net=F_netΔt. If force varies, impulse is the signed area under the net force-versus-time curve: J_net=∫F_net dt. The mean net force over the duration is J_net/Δt. Do not substitute one moment's force for average force without justification.

A longer interaction can reduce average force

Suppose a chosen body must undergo Δp=−12 N·s. Spread across 0.20 s, its mean net force is −60 N. Spread across 0.60 s, its mean is −20 N. Same signed impulse, one-third the average-force magnitude in the longer case. A softer contact is not intrinsically proven safer by this numerical comparison; real force distributions and injuries depend on more factors.

Force and work are not interchangeable

Impulse uses force times time and changes momentum; work uses force along displacement and changes kinetic energy when summed across forces. A force perpendicular to velocity can change direction and momentum while doing no instantaneous work. The digital studies model prescribed one-dimensional impulse rather than realistic collisions.

Virtual experiments and synthetic data only. No physical collisions, person-to-person impacts, loaded carts or actual safety testing are required or recommended.

Vocabulary and definitions

impulse
Integral of net force over time, equal to Δp.
net force
Vector sum of forces acting on the chosen body.
change in momentum
Difference p_final−p_initial.
mean force
Impulse divided by the selected positive time interval.
newton-second
Impulse unit N·s, equivalent to kg·m/s.

Worked example

A 1.5 kg cart at +4 m/s stops. Δp=−6 N·s. Over 0.2 s, mean net force is −30 N; over 0.6 s, −10 N.

Show the chosen body, coordinate sign convention, equation, units and interval.
