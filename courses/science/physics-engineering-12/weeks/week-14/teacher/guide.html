Momentum & Impulse

Daily lesson planning, accurate momentum and impulse calculations, force–time graph interpretation, safe investigations and a 20-point portfolio rubric.

Essential question, prerequisites and standard links

Why does interaction time matter during a collision? Students compare signed momentum changes, impulses, interaction times, average net force and peak values for explicitly specified force profiles. This week addresses quantitative modeling related to HS-PS2-2 and engineering reasoning connected to HS-PS2-3, but it does not alone demonstrate full mastery of those performance expectations.

Prerequisites: Weeks 06–08 vectors, net force, acceleration, positive/negative velocities and Newton's laws; Week 10 energy helps students distinguish work from impulse. Students may use the signed-unit worksheet and datasets rather than SVG.

Required activities are digital only. Never ask learners to perform physical collisions, drop weights, test helmets, strike people or use the mathematical force models as real-world impact-safety predictions. All data are hypothetical calculated examples, not measurements. Do not claim that a particular padding choice is certified safe.

Monday — Momentum and direction

Phenomenon (8 minutes): A model 2 kg cart travels at +3 m/s then −3 m/s. Is the momentum unchanged because speed is identical?

Reading (15 minutes): p=mv. Direction is specified by a signed axis. Units kg·m/s. Compare with K=½mv².

Worked answer (10 minutes): p_i=+6; p_f=−6 kg·m/s; Δp=−12 N·s. In both states K=9 J. Motion direction matters for momentum.

Investigation (12 minutes): signed value table and a sketch of one selected body with a labeled coordinate arrow. Exit: explain the difference between momentum and kinetic energy.

Tuesday — Impulse–momentum theorem

Starter: Identify the net force and duration required for a specified momentum change.

Equations: J_net=Δp=m(v_f−v_i)=F_meanΔt. For a 1.5 kg cart, +4 to 0 m/s gives −6 N·s. Over 0.20 s F_mean=−30 N; over 0.60 s F_mean=−10 N.

Misconception: N·s is the unit of impulse, N is force. Their numbers may coincide in special choices of time but the quantities are not interchangeable.

Exit: Write a complete sign-and-unit calculation for a matched pair of durations.

Tuesday lesson →

Wednesday — Time graph and peak

8-minute launch: Two mathematical pulses share area J=−12 N·s over 0.40 s. One is rectangular, the other a symmetric triangle.

Answer: Mean F=−30 N. Rectangular force is −30 N throughout; triangle peak −60 N, with area ½×0.40×(−60)=−12 N·s. Both change the body's momentum equally, but the triangle's peak magnitude is double.

Probe: If no pulse shape is known, impulse and duration determine mean but not maximum. A measured real collision may have a very different force curve.

Wednesday lesson →

Thursday — Compare controlled models

Research question: How can longer interaction duration affect average force without changing total impulse?

Worked: m=2 kg, v_i=+3, v_f=−1 → J=−8 N·s. In 0.2 s, F_mean=−40 N; in 0.8 s, F_mean=−10 N. Separately, at 0.8 s the rectangular profile peak=−10 N but a triangular profile peak=−20 N.

Student work: Three Momentum Lab and three Pulse Studio trials; one controlled duration pair, one profile pair, formulas, signs and two limitations. Both journals can be exported as JSON, or the synthetic CSV can be used offline.

Thursday lesson →

Friday — Evidence and corrections

Claim: Under fixed Δp, increasing interaction duration lowers the magnitude of mean net force, with J=F_meanΔt. Peak force depends on the modeled time profile, not only the area.

Evidence: −8 N·s/0.2 s=−40 N and −8 N·s/0.8 s=−10 N; the 0.8-second triangle would peak at −20 N for the same −8 N·s.

Assessment: Friday draws ten randomized open-book questions from a published 20-question practice bank. Corrections target 80%. Official midterm/final exams and transcript signing remain separately supervised.

20-point teacher-reviewed CER rubric

Scientific claim · 4
Correctly specifies a body, signed coordinate system, net impulse and interaction time.
Numerical evidence · 4
At least two accurate, clearly signed calculations with N, N·s and kg·m/s units.
Graph interpretation · 4
Uses signed area for impulse and distinguishes average force from peak for specified profiles.
Reasoning and controlled variables · 4
Explains impulse equality and duration comparison with sound physical reasoning.
Limitations, provenance and revisions · 4
Explicitly identifies synthetic data, missing safety evidence and corrects a misconception.

The teacher-reviewed target is 16/20. The separate open-book digital quiz is not an official assessment or credential.

Support and extension

Foundation: provide a number line, sign convention, formula card, rectangle area practice and datasets. Core: compute opposite-signed final velocities and triangular areas. Extension: compare two force profiles with equal area and write what sensors would be required to validate their real peak forces; Week 15 will extend this reasoning to an isolated two-body momentum conservation system.
