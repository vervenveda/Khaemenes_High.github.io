Rolling Without Slipping

How is energy partitioned in rolling systems?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Use K_total=½Mv²+½Iω² and v=rω. Rolling without slipping constrains v and ω so they cannot be chosen independently.

For a rolling body with M=2 kg, v=3 m/s, I=0.50 kg·m², and ω=6 rad/s, K_trans=9 J and K_rot=9 J, so total KE=18 J.

If slipping occurs, v=rω no longer applies exactly.

Core relations: K_total=½Mv²+½Iω² and v=rω.

Worked example

At r=0.50 m and v=2.0 m/s, rolling without slipping requires ω=v/r=4.0 rad/s.

Vocabulary and rolling-constraint extension

Vocabulary: rolling without slipping; contact point; translational speed; angular speed; kinematic constraint.

For pure rolling on a stationary surface, v_cm=rω links translation and rotation. The relation is a no-slip constraint, not a universal identity for every wheel. If the contact point slides relative to the surface, v and rω need not match.

At r=0.30 m and v=3.0 m/s, pure rolling requires ω=10 rad/s. If sensors report ω=8 rad/s at the same v and r, the object is slipping or one measurement/model assumption is wrong.

Evidence revision / CER

Use one consistent and one inconsistent v–rω case to defend whether rolling without slipping is supported. Revise any solution that imposes v=rω automatically.

Spiral retrieval and safety

No-slip diagnostic practice

A wheel of radius 0.25 m moves with center-of-mass speed 2.0 m/s. Pure rolling requires ω=8 rad/s. If measured ω=10 rad/s, then rω=2.5 m/s, so the contact condition is inconsistent with no-slip rolling.

Do not “fix” the mismatch by forcing v=rω. Treat it as evidence: either slipping occurs, radius is wrong, or one of the velocity measurements is biased. State which additional observation would distinguish those possibilities.

Contact-point model check

For pure rolling on a stationary surface, the instantaneous contact point is at rest relative to the surface in the ideal model. If slipping occurs, the contact point has nonzero relative motion and v=rω no longer holds exactly.

Use this physical interpretation—not only the equation—to explain what a mismatch between v and rω means and why friction conditions matter.

Transfer note

Describe the relative motion of the contact point for pure rolling versus slipping. Use that physical picture to justify why v=rω is a constraint rather than a definition.
