Energy Partition & Shape

How is energy partitioned in rolling systems?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Objects with the same mass and radius can have different moments of inertia because mass distribution differs.

A larger I allocates more of the same total energy to rotation, which can reduce translational speed after descending the same height.

This explains why shape can affect rolling motion without violating energy conservation.

Core relations: K_total=½Mv²+½Iω² and v=rω.

Worked example

Two objects have equal M and r but different I. The one with larger I generally has more rotational KE at the same ω and may roll down a ramp more slowly under equal energy input.

Guided question

Why can a ring and solid disk of equal mass/radius reach the bottom with different speeds?

Vocabulary and shape-energy extension

Vocabulary: energy partition; shape factor; rotational inertia; rolling speed; gravitational energy.

For rolling without slipping from height h with negligible losses, Mgh=½Mv²+½Iω². Substituting ω=v/r gives Mgh=½Mv²+½I(v²/r²). A larger I relative to Mr² places more of the available energy into rotation and leaves less for center-of-mass speed.

This is why a ring and solid disk of equal mass and radius can reach different speeds after descending the same height. The difference is not a violation of conservation; it is a different partition of the same initial gravitational energy.

Shape-comparison derivation

For a solid disk, I=½Mr². Substituting into the rolling-energy equation gives Mgh=½Mv²+¼Mv²=¾Mv², so v²=4gh/3. For a thin ring, I=Mr², giving Mgh=½Mv²+½Mv²=Mv², so v²=gh.

The disk therefore reaches a greater center-of-mass speed than the ring from the same height in the ideal no-slip model. Both conserve total energy; they partition it differently because their moments of inertia differ.

Dimensionless inertia comparison

Write I=kMr², where k captures shape. Substituting into rolling energy gives Mgh=½Mv²(1+k), so v²=2gh/(1+k). A smaller k produces a larger final speed in the ideal model.

Use k=1 for a ring and k=1/2 for a solid disk to recover the earlier result, then explain why the mass cancels when both shapes share the same h and radius-based inertia form.

Transfer note

Use v²=2gh/(1+k) to compare two additional shape factors and predict which rolls faster from the same height. State the assumptions behind the ranking.
