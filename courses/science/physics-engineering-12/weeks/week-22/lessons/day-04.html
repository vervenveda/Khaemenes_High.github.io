How is energy partitioned in rolling systems?

This investigation compares modeled or measured rolling behavior across shapes.

Use a ramp/rolling simulation or safe lightweight objects. Compare arrival times or modeled energy partitions for shapes with different moments of inertia.

Preserve raw time/height data and calculated energy terms before interpreting differences.

Misconception check

Rolling does not mean all kinetic energy is translational; rotation stores part of the total.

Formative check

What extra energy term must be included for a rolling object?

Reveal response

Rotational kinetic energy ½Iω².

Teacher guidance

Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: rolling-energy residual; arrival time; no-slip condition; uncertainty; model revision.

Preserve raw height, radius, mass, time, and angular/linear-speed evidence before calculating energy. Check whether v≈rω before applying the pure-rolling energy model.

Define an energy residual r_E=K_trans+K_rot−Mgh for the chosen start/end states. Compare the residual with uncertainty and expected losses. A consistent negative residual may indicate rolling resistance, slipping, deformation, or measurement bias rather than failure of energy conservation itself.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER comparing two shapes. Use energy partition, arrival behavior, residuals, and one model limitation. Revise the interpretation if the no-slip condition is not supported.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 11 energy residuals and Week 19 radius/angular-speed measurements.

Energy-residual practice

Suppose Mgh=12.0 J, K_trans=7.0 J, and K_rot=4.6 J. The residual is −0.4 J relative to the initial potential energy. If combined measurement uncertainty is ±0.6 J, the discrepancy may be consistent with the model. If the residual is −3.0 J under the same uncertainty, losses or an invalid no-slip assumption need attention.

Compare residuals for two shapes. A systematic larger loss for one shape may point to rolling resistance, deformation, or slipping rather than moment-of-inertia theory itself.

No-slip validation check

Before interpreting an energy residual as dissipation, test whether v≈rω. If the no-slip condition fails strongly, the pure-rolling energy partition itself is invalid. A negative energy residual could otherwise be incorrectly blamed on frictional loss when the real issue is using the wrong model.

State the order of checks: validate rolling constraint, calculate energy terms, then interpret residuals against uncertainty.
