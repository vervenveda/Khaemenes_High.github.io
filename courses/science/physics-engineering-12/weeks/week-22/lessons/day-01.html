Rotational Kinetic Energy

How is energy partitioned in rolling systems?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

A rolling object can have translational kinetic energy ½Mv² and rotational kinetic energy ½Iω² at the same time.

For rolling without slipping, v=rω links translation and rotation. The moment of inertia determines how much energy is stored in rotation for a given mass and speed.

Energy conservation can compare gravitational potential energy with translational and rotational kinetic energy when losses are negligible.

Core relations: K_total=½Mv²+½Iω² and v=rω.

Worked example

For a rolling body with M=2 kg, v=3 m/s, I=0.50 kg·m², and ω=6 rad/s, K_trans=9 J and K_rot=9 J, so total KE=18 J.

Guided question

Calculate rotational KE for I=0.8 kg·m² and ω=5 rad/s.

Misconception check

Rolling does not mean all kinetic energy is translational; rotation stores part of the total.

Reveal response

Vocabulary and rotational-energy extension

Vocabulary: rotational kinetic energy; translational kinetic energy; moment of inertia; angular speed; total kinetic energy.

Rotational kinetic energy K_rot=½Iω² depends on mass distribution through I and on angular speed squared. A rolling body can simultaneously translate its center of mass and rotate about that center, so total kinetic energy is K=½Mv²+½Iω².

For the example M=2 kg, v=3 m/s, I=0.50 kg·m², ω=6 rad/s, translational and rotational terms are each 9 J. A different I at the same v and ω changes the rotational energy while leaving the translational term unchanged.

Rotational-energy transfer practice

For I=0.80 kg·m² and ω=5 rad/s, K_rot=½(0.80)(25)=10 J. If angular speed doubles to 10 rad/s at unchanged I, rotational kinetic energy becomes 40 J—four times larger because of the square.

Now compare two bodies with the same ω but I values 0.40 and 0.80 kg·m². The larger-inertia body stores twice the rotational kinetic energy. This mirrors translational K=½Mv² while emphasizing that rotational mass distribution enters through I.
