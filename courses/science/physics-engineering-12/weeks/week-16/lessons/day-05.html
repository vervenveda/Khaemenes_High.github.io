Collision Synthesis & Safety Design

Defend a collision model and apply impulse reasoning to a design choice.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Collision analysis combines system momentum, kinetic-energy accounting, and force-time evidence. Each representation answers a different question.

Engineering safety often aims to reduce peak force by increasing interaction time or distance while managing the same momentum change.

A defensible design claim should state what is conserved, what changes form, and which criterion is optimized.

Vocabulary: collision model; impulse; kinetic energy; design criterion; tradeoff.

Worked example with reasoning

A crumple zone increases stopping distance/time. The vehicle’s momentum still changes to near zero, but the longer impulse interval can reduce average and peak force on occupants.

Guided question

Write a CER comparing a rigid-stop design with a padded-stop design for the same initial momentum.

Misconception check

Safety design does not 'absorb momentum'; momentum change is delivered by impulse while energy is redistributed and dissipated.

Formative understanding check

What design variable can lower average force for a fixed Δp?

Reveal response

Increasing the time over which the momentum change occurs.
