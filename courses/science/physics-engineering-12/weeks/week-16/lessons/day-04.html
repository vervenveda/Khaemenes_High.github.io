Explicit physics instruction

This investigation tests both system momentum and kinetic energy across several collision types.

Preserve raw mass and velocity measurements, then compute signed momentum and kinetic energy separately.

Use residuals to judge conservation relative to measurement uncertainty instead of demanding perfect numerical equality.

Vocabulary: collision dataset; momentum residual; energy residual; uncertainty; model classification.

Worked example with reasoning

A trial has p_before=1.50 and p_after=1.47 kg·m/s, while KE drops from 0.90 to 0.55 J. Momentum is approximately conserved within a small residual, while kinetic energy clearly decreases.

Collision-evidence investigation extension

Analyze each trial with three layers: momentum residual, kinetic-energy residual, and model/source uncertainty. Momentum residual tests approximate isolation; kinetic-energy residual distinguishes elastic from inelastic behavior once momentum consistency is established.

Preserve raw mass and velocity values, then calculate both totals independently. If momentum appears conserved but kinetic energy drops far beyond uncertainty, classify the event as inelastic. If both residuals are small relative to uncertainty, an elastic model may be supported, but do not claim exact elasticity from rounded synthetic values.

Look for systematic patterns across trials. A consistent momentum deficit can indicate external impulse or measurement bias, while a collision-type label that changes with tiny numerical noise may be uncertainty-limited.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER classifying at least one trial and naming the evidence threshold used. Revise the classification if uncertainty overlaps the decision boundary.

Dual-residual decision example

Trial A has p_before=1.20 and p_after=1.18 kg·m/s, with momentum uncertainty ±0.05 kg·m/s; KE changes from 0.80 to 0.79 J with uncertainty ±0.03 J. Both residuals are within uncertainty, so an elastic model is plausible. Trial B has a similarly small momentum residual but KE falls from 0.80 to 0.50 J, far beyond energy uncertainty, supporting an inelastic classification.

Trial C has a large momentum residual and a large KE change. Do not classify its elasticity until the system boundary or external-impulse problem is resolved, because the assumed isolated-system model itself may be invalid.
