Collision Simulation Investigation

Use supplied or simulated collision data to compare momentum and kinetic-energy models.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

This investigation tests both system momentum and kinetic energy across several collision types.

Preserve raw mass and velocity measurements, then compute signed momentum and kinetic energy separately.

Use residuals to judge conservation relative to measurement uncertainty instead of demanding perfect numerical equality.

Vocabulary: collision dataset; momentum residual; energy residual; uncertainty; model classification.

Worked example with reasoning

A trial has p_before=1.50 and p_after=1.47 kg·m/s, while KE drops from 0.90 to 0.55 J. Momentum is approximately conserved within a small residual, while kinetic energy clearly decreases.

Guided question

Which residual is more useful for deciding whether an event is elastic: momentum residual or kinetic-energy residual?

Independent work

  1. Analyze at least three supplied trials.
  2. Classify each collision.
  3. Calculate both residuals.
  4. State uncertainty and one revision.

Investigation / Data Lab

Preserve raw collision data, momentum/energy calculations, residuals, classification, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Small momentum residual does not make a collision elastic; elasticity depends on kinetic-energy behavior too.

Formative understanding check

What evidence supports an elastic classification?

Reveal response

Both momentum and kinetic energy remain constant within uncertainty.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to compare momentum, energy, and impulse representations for consistency.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied tables and formulas.

Core: Classify multiple trials from calculated evidence.

Extended: Propagate simple velocity uncertainty into both momentum and KE residuals.
