Elastic vs Inelastic Collision Models

Distinguish collision types by momentum and kinetic-energy behavior.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Momentum can be conserved across a collision even when kinetic energy changes form. Collision type is therefore not determined by momentum conservation alone.

In an ideal elastic collision, both total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved. In an inelastic collision, momentum may be conserved while kinetic energy decreases; in a perfectly inelastic collision, objects stick together.

Real collisions can include deformation, sound, and thermal transfer, so model labels describe limiting behavior.

Vocabulary: elastic; inelastic; perfectly inelastic; kinetic energy; deformation.

Worked example with reasoning

Two carts stick after collision. System momentum can remain conserved while some kinetic energy becomes internal energy. That makes the event perfectly inelastic in the ideal model.

Guided question

What evidence would distinguish an elastic rebound from an inelastic rebound?

Independent work

  1. Classify four collision scenarios.
  2. For each, state whether momentum and kinetic energy are conserved in the ideal model.
  3. Explain why sticking is a stronger condition than simply losing kinetic energy.

Misconception check

Momentum conservation does not imply kinetic-energy conservation.

Formative understanding check

What additional conserved quantity defines an ideal elastic collision?

Reveal response

Total kinetic energy, in addition to momentum.
