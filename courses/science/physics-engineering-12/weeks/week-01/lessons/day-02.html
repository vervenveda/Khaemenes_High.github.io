Vector Components & Resultants

Resolve vectors into perpendicular components and recombine them into a resultant.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Explicit physics instruction

A vector can be represented by perpendicular components. In two dimensions, horizontal and vertical components can be analyzed independently and recombined.

For right-angle components, resultant magnitude follows the Pythagorean theorem. Direction can be represented by an angle relative to a chosen axis.

Signs matter. A component west or downward may be negative depending on the coordinate convention; changing the convention changes signs but not the physical vector.

Vocabulary: component; resultant; coordinate system; magnitude; direction.

Worked example with reasoning

A displacement 3 m east and 4 m north has magnitude √(3²+4²)=5 m. Its direction is northeast; a more advanced description can use an angle from east.

Guided question

A walker travels 6 m east and 8 m north. Find the resultant magnitude and explain why 14 m is distance traveled but not displacement magnitude.

Independent work

  1. Resolve three supplied vectors into components.
  2. Recombine two perpendicular components.
  3. Draw one vector addition head-to-tail and one component diagram.

Misconception check

Vector magnitudes do not usually add arithmetically unless the vectors lie on the same line and direction.

Formative understanding check

Why is 6 m east plus 8 m north not a 14 m displacement?

Reveal response

Because the directions are perpendicular; the resultant magnitude is 10 m.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use grid-paper component diagrams.

Core: Solve component/resultant problems and state coordinate conventions.

Extended: Compare graphical and algebraic vector addition and quantify disagreement.
