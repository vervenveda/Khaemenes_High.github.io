Vector Components & Resultants

Resolve vectors into perpendicular components and recombine them into a resultant.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Explicit physics instruction

A vector can be represented by perpendicular components. In two dimensions, horizontal and vertical components can be analyzed independently and recombined.

For right-angle components, resultant magnitude follows the Pythagorean theorem. Direction can be represented by an angle relative to a chosen axis.

Signs matter. A component west or downward may be negative depending on the coordinate convention; changing the convention changes signs but not the physical vector.

Vocabulary: component; resultant; coordinate system; magnitude; direction.

Worked example with reasoning

A displacement 3 m east and 4 m north has magnitude √(3²+4²)=5 m. Its direction is northeast; a more advanced description can use an angle from east.

Guided question

A walker travels 6 m east and 8 m north. Find the resultant magnitude and explain why 14 m is distance traveled but not displacement magnitude.

Independent work

  1. Resolve three supplied vectors into components.
  2. Recombine two perpendicular components.
  3. Draw one vector addition head-to-tail and one component diagram.

Misconception check

Vector magnitudes do not usually add arithmetically unless the vectors lie on the same line and direction.

Formative understanding check

Why is 6 m east plus 8 m north not a 14 m displacement?

Reveal response

Because the directions are perpendicular; the resultant magnitude is 10 m.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit units, assumptions, coordinate choices, and evidence limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use grid-paper component diagrams.

Core: Solve component/resultant problems and state coordinate conventions.

Extended: Compare graphical and algebraic vector addition and quantify disagreement.

Vector-coordinate audit

Resolve vectors only after declaring axes. If east is +x and north is +y, a westward component is negative x and a downward component is negative y. The sign belongs to the coordinate model, while the vector itself is the physical quantity. Rotating the coordinate axes can change component values without changing the vector.

For non-right-angle vectors, use trigonometric components such as Vx=V cosθ and Vy=V sinθ when θ is measured from the +x axis. Recombine with magnitude √(Vx²+Vy²), then determine direction with an inverse tangent and a quadrant check. A calculator angle without a quadrant audit can place the vector in the wrong physical direction.

Compare graphical and algebraic solutions. A scale drawing provides useful visual evidence, but ruler thickness and drawing scale limit precision. Algebraic component methods usually provide higher numerical precision, yet still depend on accurate input magnitudes and angles. Explain disagreements by tracing uncertainty back to the representation used.

Retrieval and vector-evidence transfer

Retrieval: reconstruct the 3–4–5 resultant without notes, then explain why a head-to-tail drawing and a component calculation should represent the same vector even if their numerical precision differs.

Evidence revision: if a graphical resultant and an algebraic resultant disagree, inspect scale, angle reading, coordinate signs, rounding, and quadrant choice before deciding one method is wrong. Write a CER defending the preferred result with magnitude, direction, method, and uncertainty. Extend the reasoning to a force or velocity vector so the method transfers beyond displacement.

Component-transfer check

Use the same component method for displacement, velocity, and force. The arithmetic structure can remain identical while the physical meaning and units change. A correct vector solution therefore requires both mathematical consistency and a statement of what the vector represents in the system.
