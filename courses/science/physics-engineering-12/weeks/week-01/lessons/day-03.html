Slope, Graphs & Experimental Uncertainty

Interpret slope and spread as quantitative evidence while distinguishing random variation from systematic bias.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Explicit physics instruction

Graphs compress evidence into patterns. Slope represents a rate of change and must carry units derived from the axes.

Repeated measurements reveal spread and support estimates of random uncertainty. A tight cluster can be precise while still shifted from the true value by systematic bias.

Residuals and uncertainty should remain visible. Deleting inconvenient measurements without documented justification weakens reproducibility.

Vocabulary: slope; residual; random uncertainty; systematic bias; precision.

Worked example with reasoning

A position graph changes from 2 m at 1 s to 14 m at 5 s. Slope=(14−2)/(5−1)=3 m/s. The slope unit comes from metres divided by seconds.

Guided question

Four measurements are 9.8, 9.9, 9.8, and 9.9 while a trusted value is 10.4. What do the data suggest about precision and accuracy?

Independent work

  1. Calculate slopes from three two-point datasets.
  2. Identify one random and one systematic uncertainty source.
  3. Explain why a precise dataset can still be inaccurate.

Misconception check

A straight-looking graph does not remove uncertainty or prove causation.

Formative understanding check

What does the unit of slope come from?

Reveal response

The vertical-axis unit divided by the horizontal-axis unit.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit units, assumptions, coordinate choices, and evidence limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a slope triangle and uncertainty vocabulary.

Core: Calculate/interpret slopes and distinguish random from systematic effects.

Extended: Use residual patterns to compare two candidate models.
