Capstone: Defend an Efficient Design

Objective: Synthesize power, useful output, efficiency and energy conservation using quantitative evidence.

45–60 minutes · Offline learning · Local evidence

Scientific reading

Build a defensible claim

Claim: a design can provide the same useful work faster by transferring the energy in less time, while efficiency is a separate fraction of its input that becomes useful. A sound claim defines a time interval, intended useful output, input account and assumptions about storage. Avoid treating watt and joule as interchangeable units.

Compare the evidence

Example one: 360 J of useful energy in 6 s versus 3 s corresponds to 60 W versus 120 W, with equal useful amounts. Example two: 400 J input at 75% gives 300 J useful, 100 J other and 60 W useful over 5 seconds. Combine a rate-of-transfer comparison with a correct energy balance.

Evaluate common counterclaims

Counterclaim: a machine with twice the power always uses twice as much energy. For the same transfer, the time may simply be half. Counterclaim: all unused output is destroyed. Energy is accounted into other forms, and the surrounding system matters. Counterclaim: efficiency always equals useful power in watts. Efficiency is dimensionless and expressed as a fraction or percent.

Qualify with scientific limits

Real systems may store energy and change behavior while starting or stopping, so a simplified steady-output model cannot establish efficiency from unverified manufacturer's claims or predict real apparatus safety. The Friday ten-item randomized quiz is public open-book formative practice; a teacher separately evaluates the capstone CER and any formal supervised exam.

All numeric experiments are simulated. Do not lift unsafe loads, operate electrical devices, perform strenuous stair tests, or use these outputs to certify real equipment.

Key vocabulary

claim–evidence–reasoning
Scientific explanation backed by quantitative observations or models.
engineering justification
Argument demonstrating how a design satisfies stated constraints.
energy budget
Complete account of input, useful output and other energy transfers.
counterclaim
Alternative explanation checked against evidence.
model limitation
Assumption limiting extrapolation to real physical systems.

Worked physics example

Machine A: 360 J/6 s=60 W; B: 360 J/3 s=120 W. Candidate design: 400 J input, η=75%, t=5 s gives 300 J useful and 100 J other, with useful power 60 W.

Show the system, time interval, conversion, numerical substitutions and units.
