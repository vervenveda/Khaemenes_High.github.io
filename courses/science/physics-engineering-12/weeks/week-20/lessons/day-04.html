Vocabulary and investigation-depth extension

Vocabulary: torque residual; lever arm; measurement uncertainty; repeated trial; balance point.

Preserve raw force and distance values, then calculate signed torques independently. Define torque residual r_τ=Στ. A small nonzero residual may be consistent with force-resolution or distance-measurement uncertainty.

Estimate which measurement dominates uncertainty. Because τ=rF, relative uncertainty in either r or F contributes to torque uncertainty. Repeat the balance with different force-distance combinations that predict the same torque and examine whether residuals remain randomly distributed or show a systematic bias.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the lever data support rotational equilibrium within uncertainty. Revise the setup if residuals show a consistent sign.

Torque-residual practice

Suppose one trial gives +2.00 N·m and −1.94 N·m, leaving +0.06 N·m residual. If force and distance uncertainties imply about ±0.08 N·m uncertainty in the net result, the trial is consistent with equilibrium. A second trial with +0.30 N·m residual under the same uncertainty deserves a setup or calibration check.

Repeat at several force-distance combinations that should balance. Residuals scattered around zero suggest random measurement variation; residuals consistently positive suggest a systematic lever-arm or force calibration bias.

Preserve the original distance and force readings so the torque calculation can be independently reproduced. A final “balanced” label without raw values is weak evidence.

Repeated-trial check

Use at least three nominally balanced trials and compare the sign of each torque residual. A persistent sign suggests systematic bias; mixed signs of similar size suggest random uncertainty.

Method-quality note

Repeat the balance using a second pivot choice and confirm that the physical equilibrium conclusion is unchanged even though individual torque terms differ. This provides an independent check on sign conventions and lever-arm geometry.
