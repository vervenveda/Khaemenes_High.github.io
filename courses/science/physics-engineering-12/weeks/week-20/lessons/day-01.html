Torque & Lever Arms

Why does where a force is applied matter?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Torque measures a force’s rotational effect about a chosen pivot. For a perpendicular force, τ=rF; more generally the perpendicular lever arm matters.

Rotational equilibrium requires the vector sum of torques to be zero. A system can have nonzero forces yet zero net torque if clockwise and counterclockwise effects balance.

The pivot choice changes individual torque values but not whether a rigid body in static equilibrium satisfies the full force/torque conditions.

Core relation: τ=rF⊥ and Στ=0 in rotational equilibrium.

Worked example

A 20 N perpendicular force applied 0.50 m from a hinge produces 10 N·m of torque. The same force at 0.25 m produces only 5 N·m.

Guided question

Compare the torque of 10 N applied 0.20 m from a pivot with 5 N applied 0.40 m away.

Independent work

  1. Solve three torque or balance cases.
  2. Draw a pivot/lever-arm diagram.
  3. State one assumption or uncertainty.

Misconception check

A larger force does not always produce larger torque; lever arm and force direction matter.

Formative check

What two geometric features determine torque besides force magnitude?

Reveal response

Distance from the pivot and the force’s perpendicular component/direction.
