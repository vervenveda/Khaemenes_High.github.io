Torque & Lever Arms

Why does where a force is applied matter?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Torque measures a force’s rotational effect about a chosen pivot. For a perpendicular force, τ=rF; more generally the perpendicular lever arm matters.

Rotational equilibrium requires the vector sum of torques to be zero. A system can have nonzero forces yet zero net torque if clockwise and counterclockwise effects balance.

The pivot choice changes individual torque values but not whether a rigid body in static equilibrium satisfies the full force/torque conditions.

Core relation: τ=rF⊥ and Στ=0 in rotational equilibrium.

Worked example

A 20 N perpendicular force applied 0.50 m from a hinge produces 10 N·m of torque. The same force at 0.25 m produces only 5 N·m.

Guided question

Compare the torque of 10 N applied 0.20 m from a pivot with 5 N applied 0.40 m away.

Independent work

  1. Solve three torque or balance cases.
  2. Draw a pivot/lever-arm diagram.
  3. State one assumption or uncertainty.

Misconception check

A larger force does not always produce larger torque; lever arm and force direction matter.

Formative check

What two geometric features determine torque besides force magnitude?

Reveal response

Distance from the pivot and the force’s perpendicular component/direction.

Vocabulary and torque-geometry extension

Vocabulary: torque; pivot; lever arm; perpendicular component; rotational equilibrium.

General torque magnitude is τ=rF sinφ, where φ is the angle between the position vector from pivot to application point and the force. Equivalently, τ=F r_perp. This shows why force magnitude alone does not determine rotational effect.

Choose a sign convention, such as counterclockwise positive. The same force can create positive, negative, or zero torque depending on geometry. A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero moment arm and zero torque even if the force is large.

Evidence revision / CER

Compare two equal forces applied at different distances or angles and defend which produces larger torque. Revise any solution that uses rF without checking perpendicular geometry.

Spiral retrieval and safety

Recall Week 06 vector components. Use simulation or a light ruler model only; do not load furniture, heavy beams, or unstable supports.

Torque-angle practice

A 20 N force applied 0.50 m from a pivot at 90° gives τ=10 N·m. At 30°, the torque is 0.50×20×sin30°=5 N·m. At 0°, torque is zero because the line of action passes through the pivot direction. The same force magnitude therefore produces very different rotational effects solely from geometry.

Use a signed convention: if the 90° force tends to rotate counterclockwise, call its torque positive. An equal 10 N·m clockwise torque would be negative. Only the algebraic sum determines rotational equilibrium.

Check units: torque uses N·m, which has the same base dimensions as joules but represents rotational tendency rather than energy. Do not relabel torque values as joules.

Line-of-action check

Draw one force whose line of action passes through the pivot and one equal force whose line misses the pivot. Explain why only the second produces nonzero torque.

Phenomenon / Prediction

A door opens more easily when pushed farther from the hinge.
