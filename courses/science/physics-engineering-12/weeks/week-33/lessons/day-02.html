Decision Matrices & Tradeoffs

Use weighted evidence to compare competing designs while making assumptions visible.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit engineering instruction

A decision matrix combines criterion scores with weights. The weights encode priorities, so they must be justified rather than treated as neutral facts.

Weighted score = Σ(score×weight). The result supports comparison but does not replace engineering judgment.

Sensitivity analysis asks whether the preferred design changes when reasonable weights or scores change.

Vocabulary: decision matrix; weight; score; tradeoff; sensitivity.

Worked example

Scores 8, 6, 9 with weights 0.4, 0.3, 0.3 give 7.7.

Guided question

How would increasing the weight on safety change a decision matrix?

Independent work

  1. Build a three-criterion matrix.
  2. Calculate weighted scores.
  3. Change one weight and test whether the winner changes.

Misconception check

A high weighted score does not erase a violated hard constraint.

Formative check

What should happen to a design that violates a non-negotiable constraint?

Reveal response

It should be rejected or revised even if its weighted score is high.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge criteria, weights, assumptions, and whether the evidence justifies the revision.

Teacher guidance

Decision-matrix depth

A weighted matrix is a transparent model of priorities, not an objective truth machine. Before scoring alternatives, normalize the weights so their sum is 1.00 or 100%, define what each score means, and screen every design against hard constraints. Only feasible designs should enter the weighted comparison.

Suppose Design A scores 9, 5, 7 and Design B scores 7, 8, 6 for safety, cost, and efficiency. With weights 0.50, 0.20, 0.30, A earns 7.6 and B earns 6.9. If cost is raised while safety is lowered within a defensible range, the ranking may change. That change is not a flaw; it reveals sensitivity to priorities.

Sensitivity and uncertainty

Vary one weight or uncertain score across a reasonable interval while keeping the total weight normalized. Record the point at which the preferred design changes. A robust decision remains preferred across plausible assumptions; a fragile decision changes under small adjustments and should be reported as such.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend the selected design with a claim, the weighted calculation, constraint checks, and a sensitivity result. Revise the claim if the winner changes under a small defensible adjustment or if a score rests on weak evidence.

Spiral retrieval

Use one earlier physics result—power, efficiency, force, momentum, resistance, wave fidelity, or another measurable quantity—as a criterion. State the unit, direction of preference, and how measurement uncertainty should affect the score.

Decision robustness check

Test the matrix one more way: replace one subjective score with a measured physics quantity or normalize all alternatives against a common reference. If the ranking depends mainly on an arbitrary scoring choice, report that limitation and identify the measurement that would make the decision more defensible.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Two designs solve the same problem but optimize different constraints.

Investigation / model check

Design challenge using paper/cardboard/simulation; no hazardous construction. Preserve observations or supplied data, compare them with the model, and record one limitation or source of uncertainty.
