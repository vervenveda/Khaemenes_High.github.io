Prototype/Simulation Investigation

Test a safe design or simulation against criteria and model predictions.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit engineering instruction

Use paper/cardboard, simulation, or another teacher-approved low-risk prototype.

Preserve raw test data before scoring the design. Multiple trials are stronger than one dramatic result.

Compare measured performance with the prediction and with each criterion, then record one revision.

Vocabulary: prototype; trial; performance metric; residual; iteration.

Worked example

A design predicts 50 units but repeated trials average 46. Residual is −4 and relative error is 8%.

Guided question

Which evidence should trigger a design revision: one outlier, repeated criterion failure, or both? Explain.

Independent work

  1. Preserve raw trial data.
  2. Calculate criterion metrics.
  3. Compare prediction with measurement.
  4. Revise one design feature and justify the change.

Design Investigation

Use safe materials or simulation only. Preserve raw trial data, model prediction, criterion scores, uncertainty, and revision.

Misconception check

Iteration should respond to evidence, not merely aesthetic preference.

Formative check

What makes a design revision evidence-based?

Reveal response

It targets a measured shortfall or model mismatch tied to criteria.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge criteria, weights, assumptions, and whether the evidence justifies the revision.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied simulation data if needed.

Core: Complete prediction-test-revision loop.

Extended: Analyze repeatability and uncertainty.

Prototype investigation depth

A prototype test should change one planned feature at a time when possible and preserve raw measurements before averages, scores, or graphs are calculated. Use at least three trials for a repeatability check when the activity permits. Record instrument resolution, simulation settings, or measurement rules so another student could reproduce the test.

For a prediction of 50 units and trials of 45, 47, and 46, the mean is 46. The residual relative to the prediction is −4 units, and the relative error is 8%. The spread among trials is small compared with the model mismatch, so the evidence points more strongly toward a systematic design/model issue than toward random trial variation.

Iteration logic

Revise only after naming the measured shortfall. If the design fails a stiffness criterion but easily meets mass and cost limits, change the feature most directly connected to stiffness and predict the expected effect before retesting. Avoid changing several variables at once unless the investigation is explicitly designed to study interactions.

Evidence revision / CER

Submit a claim about whether the prototype meets its criterion, cite raw and processed evidence, and explain the role of uncertainty. After the revision, compare old and new performance using the same metric and decide whether improvement is meaningful rather than merely numerically different.

Safety and retrieval

Use simulations, paper, cardboard, low-voltage components, or other teacher-approved low-risk materials only. Retrieve one uncertainty or residual method from an earlier physics investigation and apply it to the prototype evidence.

Repeatability check

Report both the average performance and the trial-to-trial spread. A revision that improves the mean while greatly worsening repeatability may not be a genuine engineering improvement. Decide which metric matters more for the stated use and justify that choice with the design criteria.
