Criteria, Constraints & Problem Definition

Translate a real problem into measurable criteria, hard constraints, and a testable design goal.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit engineering instruction

Engineering begins by defining what success means. Criteria are measures used to compare solutions; constraints are limits that acceptable solutions must satisfy.

A vague goal such as 'make it better' should become measurable targets such as mass, cost, efficiency, safety, or error rate.

The strongest design brief separates must-have constraints from preferences and identifies what evidence will test each claim.

Vocabulary: criterion; constraint; specification; requirement; metric.

Worked example

A bridge model may require mass under 100 g as a constraint while maximizing supported load as a criterion.

Guided question

Rewrite a vague design goal into one constraint and two measurable criteria.

Independent work

  1. Create a design brief for a supplied problem.
  2. Separate constraints from criteria.
  3. Assign units or scoring rules to each criterion.

Misconception check

A criterion is not automatically a constraint; preferences and hard limits play different roles.

Formative check

What makes a criterion measurable?

Reveal response

It has an observable or calculable metric with a defined scale or unit.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge criteria, weights, assumptions, and whether the evidence justifies the revision.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a criteria/constraints organizer.

Core: Write measurable specifications.

Extended: Identify hidden stakeholder assumptions.

Engineering definition depth

Begin with the physical system rather than with a favorite solution. Identify the input, output, users, environment, and the measurable failure mode. A criterion should answer “how will performance be compared?” while a constraint should answer “what must not be violated?” Keep units attached to every quantitative requirement so that later testing can trace each claim back to evidence.

Example: a portable sensor housing might require mass ≤ 120 g, survive a 1.0 m drop, and keep internal temperature rise below 8 °C during operation. Mass and temperature rise are measurable performance specifications; the drop requirement may function as a pass/fail constraint. If the design misses a hard constraint, a high score elsewhere does not rescue it.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a claim identifying the most important design criterion. Support it with evidence from the problem context, then explain why that evidence justifies the metric and threshold. Revise the claim if the metric cannot be observed, calculated, or tested reproducibly.

Uncertainty and stakeholder check

Separate measured uncertainty from stakeholder preference. A ±2 g measurement uncertainty changes confidence in a mass result; a preference for lower cost changes the design priority. Both matter, but they are different kinds of information and should not be blended into one number without explanation.

Spiral retrieval

Retrieve one mechanics, energy, circuits, or waves quantity from earlier in Physics 12 that could become a criterion in an engineering brief. Name its SI unit, how it would be measured, and one assumption that could limit the usefulness of that metric.
