Criteria, Constraints & Problem Definition

Translate a real problem into measurable criteria, hard constraints, and a testable design goal.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit engineering instruction

Engineering begins by defining what success means. Criteria are measures used to compare solutions; constraints are limits that acceptable solutions must satisfy.

A vague goal such as 'make it better' should become measurable targets such as mass, cost, efficiency, safety, or error rate.

The strongest design brief separates must-have constraints from preferences and identifies what evidence will test each claim.

Vocabulary: criterion; constraint; specification; requirement; metric.

Worked example

A bridge model may require mass under 100 g as a constraint while maximizing supported load as a criterion.

Guided question

Rewrite a vague design goal into one constraint and two measurable criteria.

Independent work

  1. Create a design brief for a supplied problem.
  2. Separate constraints from criteria.
  3. Assign units or scoring rules to each criterion.

Misconception check

A criterion is not automatically a constraint; preferences and hard limits play different roles.

Formative check

What makes a criterion measurable?

Reveal response

It has an observable or calculable metric with a defined scale or unit.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge criteria, weights, assumptions, and whether the evidence justifies the revision.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a criteria/constraints organizer.

Core: Write measurable specifications.

Extended: Identify hidden stakeholder assumptions.
