Teacher / Parent Educator Guide · Week 11

Potential Energy & Conservation

Full five-day teaching plan, worked answers, energy-budget explanations, accommodations and a separate human-reviewed scoring rubric.

Course position and academic aim

Students now connect Week 10's work–energy theorem with energy storage and transformation. Essential question: How does system configuration store energy? This develops energy representations related to HS-PS3-2 while emphasizing systems, mathematical models and conservation; this week alone does not establish full mastery of the entire performance expectation.

Prerequisites: ½mv², net work, positive mass, Newtonian speed and the joule. A basic familiarity with square roots, squared quantities and units is helpful. Students may use a formula card and the printable model-generated datasets.

Safety: Every required experiment uses digital calculations or the downloadable CSVs. Do not release weighted objects, improvise loaded springs, pull back launch devices, create hazardous ramps or use lab values for real-device safety. Models do not simulate impact forces, spring failure, real friction coefficients or material temperature.

Monday — Gravitational configurations

8-minute launch: Is a book supported above a floor storing energy? Define the Earth–object system and the chosen floor reference.

15-minute explanation: ΔU_g=mgΔh; U_g=mgh relative to the specified zero, only when g approximately constant. Changing the zero does not change ΔU_g.

15-minute worked example: 2 kg at 5 m with g=9.8 gives 98 J. At 3 m, U=58.8 J; the difference is −39.2 J.

Exit and reflection: Distinguish zero potential relative to a datum from zero gravity.

Tuesday — Falling-energy bookkeeping

Model: A body released from rest at H=5 m reaches h=3 m. Initially U0=98 J for m=2 kg. Released energy=39.2 J. With f=0, K=39.2 J and v≈6.26 m/s. With prescribed transfer f=25%, other accounted energy=9.8 J, K=29.4 J, U=58.8 J, speed≈5.42 m/s. Sum 58.8+29.4+9.8=98 J.

Misconception: The selected f fraction is not physically derived from drag. The model does not predict collision after reaching h=0.

Activity: Compare identical masses/heights at different f values; explain changing kinetic energy and constant total.

Tuesday lesson →

Wednesday — Elastic potential energy

Model: Hooke-linear spring U_s=½kx². For k=200 N/m, x0=20 cm=0.20 m: U0=4 J. At x=10 cm=0.10 m, U=1 J and released energy=3 J. For f=0, K=3 J and m=1.5 kg implies speed magnitude=√(2×3/1.5)=2 m/s.

Misconception: Doubling compression does not merely double energy; it quadruples energy at fixed stiffness. The formula assumes deformation within the linear Hooke-law regime.

Activity: Match stiffness and compression comparisons, including careful centimeters-to-meters conversion.

Wednesday lesson →

Thursday — Compare transformations

Model comparison: For the gravitational example with f=25%, U=58.8 J, K=29.4 J and E_other=9.8 J give total 98 J. For the spring example with f=25%, U=1 J, K=2.25 J, and other energy=0.75 J give total 4 J.

Scientific distinction: Mechanical energy is not conserved in this ledger when f>0; total accounted energy is conserved. The fraction is a user-chosen modeling parameter, not a material's measured loss coefficient.

Student evidence: At least three gravity and three spring trials, two matched controls, both JSON exports, one algebraic proof and one limitation.

Thursday lesson →

Friday — Evidence case and correction

Students produce a claim–evidence–reasoning portfolio with a gravity and spring energy ledger, correct joule units, energy diagrams, and a counterclaim addressed. Teach that U belongs to an interacting system, mechanical energy can be transferred out of modeled mechanical forms, and total energy stays accounted for.

Ten randomized open-book formative questions from a 20-question bank supply corrections and a goal of 80%. Public source-code quizzes are not confidential supervised midterms or final examinations.

Friday lesson →

20-point teacher-reviewed rubric

Claim · 4
Accurately identifies system boundary and energy transformation.
Calculations · 4
Two correct worked numerical examples with SI units, reference levels and metric conversions.
Energy diagrams · 4
Potential, kinetic and other energy accounted correctly in a complete ledger.
Reasoning · 4
Uses mgh and ½kx² appropriately, distinguishing ideal mechanical conservation and broader energy accounting.
Limitations/corrections · 4
Identifies nonphysical assumptions or missing phenomena and corrects one misconception.

Review target: 16/20 = 80%. Separate educator-approved supervised examinations remain necessary for confidential midterm/final grading.

Accessibility and differentiation

Foundation: provide precomputed bars and hand-drawn energy tiles, a metric conversion reference and the CSV data instead of interactive SVG. Core: use conservation equations to explain two controlled comparisons. Extension: derive spring-energy ratios and compare system boundaries with work by external forces.
