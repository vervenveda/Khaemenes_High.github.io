Conservation and Nonmechanical Energy Transfer

Learning objective: Compare gravitational and elastic transformations and reconcile energy bars when losses are present.

45–60 minutes · Safe offline simulations · No physical apparatus required

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Scientific reading and explanation

Define mechanical and total energy

Mechanical energy includes kinetic energy and the potential energies under study. With friction, drag or material deformation, the modeled mechanical energy may decrease, but energy does not disappear. A larger system ledger can include transferred thermal or internal energy, and total energy remains accounted for. The exact energy recipient depends on the real physical system.

Separate an ideal model from a dissipation parameter

In our digital drop model, the selected fraction f of released potential energy enters a nonmechanical account. In our digital spring model, the same parameter acts on released elastic energy. It does not purport to calculate actual drag coefficients, friction forces, damping rates, or temperatures. The ratio is user-prescribed to explore conservation, not observed.

Use energy bars with exact numbers

For m=2 kg, H=5 m, h=3 m, f=25%, the total initial gravitational energy is 98 J, the remaining gravitational energy is 58.8 J, kinetic energy is 29.4 J, and nonmechanical transferred energy is 9.8 J. Their sum remains 98 J. For k=200 N/m, x_0=20 cm, x=10 cm, f=25%, the total initial spring energy is 4 J, the remaining spring energy is 1 J, kinetic energy is 2.25 J, and nonmechanical transferred energy is 0.75 J.

Control variables and protect the evidence

Keep system settings fixed when comparing two transfer fractions. Export each lab's notebook so both the parameters and observations travel together. The labs are offline equation explorers and synthetic data, not tools for physical ramp experiments, loaded springs or real materials testing.

Synthetic data and virtual laboratories only. Do not improvise loaded springs, hazardous ramps or object drops. These tools do not certify equipment safety.

Vocabulary

energy ledger
Table of energy stored, transferred and accounted for.
mechanical energy
Kinetic plus modeled potential energies.
thermal/internal energy
Other energy forms that can increase as mechanical energy transfers.
dissipation parameter
A prescribed mathematical fraction representing transfer from mechanical forms.
controlled comparison
Runs differing by one deliberate variable.

Worked example

Drop: 58.8+29.4+9.8=98 J. Spring: 1+2.25+0.75=4 J. The examples have different energy sources, but both demonstrate that a decrease in mechanical energy need not mean energy vanished.
