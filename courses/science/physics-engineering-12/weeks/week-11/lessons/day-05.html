An Energy Conservation Evidence Case

Learning objective: Defend a scientific claim using both gravitational and elastic potential energy calculations.

45–60 minutes · Safe offline simulations · No physical apparatus required

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Scientific reading and explanation

State the conservation claim precisely

For a defined system in the mathematical models, stored gravitational or elastic potential energy can be transformed into kinetic energy. When a loss fraction is specified, some released potential becomes a different accounted energy form. Total accounted energy equals initial stored energy even though mechanical energy may decrease. The claim should distinguish perfect ideal conservation of mechanical energy from more general conservation of total energy.

Use quantitative evidence from two systems

For a 2 kg object dropping from 5 m to 3 m without losses, released gravitational energy is 39.2 J. For an ideal spring k=200 N/m compressed from 20 cm to 10 cm, released elastic energy is 3 J. Both use an energy-difference equation and yield a nonnegative kinetic-energy prediction when released from rest.

Answer the common counterclaims

Counterclaim one: potential energy belongs solely to an object independent of its interacting system. Clarify the interacting bodies and their configuration. Counterclaim two: thermal transfer means total energy is destroyed. Include the other-energy bar. Counterclaim three: doubling compression merely doubles spring energy. Use the squared deformation formula to correct it.

Qualification and assessment integrity

Gravitational U=mgh assumes approximately constant g near the surface; spring U=½kx² requires Hooke-linear response. The energy-transfer fraction is invented for controlled mathematical comparison rather than derived from actual friction. Friday's ten randomized questions are open-book practice toward an 80% correction target; teachers separately review student claims, diagrams and calculations. Nothing here replaces a supervised midterm or final.

Synthetic data and virtual laboratories only. Do not improvise loaded springs, hazardous ramps or object drops. These tools do not certify equipment safety.

Vocabulary

claim–evidence–reasoning
Scientific argument supported by correctly interpreted results.
energy transformation
Transfer between kinetic, potential and other modeled forms.
conservation equation
An energy ledger equating initial and final accounted totals.
counterclaim
Misconception or alternative account tested against evidence.
scientific limitation
Assumption restricting when a formula applies.

Worked example

Gravity: m=2 kg, Δh=2 m gives released ΔU=39.2 J. Spring: k=200 N/m, x0=0.20 m and x=0.10 m gives released energy 3 J. Identify K versus transferred thermal energy in each case, and show a complete ledger.
