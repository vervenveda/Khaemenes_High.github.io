Grade 12 · Week 11 · Tuesday

Gravitational Potential Becomes Motion

Learning objective: Use an idealized falling-object energy ledger and calculate speed from gravitational energy released.

45–60 minutes · Safe offline simulations · No physical apparatus required

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Scientific reading and explanation

Build an initial energy budget

An object released from rest at initial height H has initial gravitational potential energy U_0=mgH, relative to a floor reference. For the ideal fall from H to h without drag or losses, its kinetic energy at h is K=mg(H−h), while potential energy is U=mgh. Their sum remains mgH. This model assumes the same g at both heights.

Speed from released energy

Since K=½mv², the ideal speed magnitude at height h is v=√(2g(H−h)). Mass cancels in the ideal fall-from-rest formula; that cancellation is not evidence that real-world air drag affects all shapes identically. At H=5 m and h=3 m, released energy per kg is 19.6 J/kg, giving speed √39.2≈6.26 m/s.

Add transfers without losing energy

With air resistance, some initially stored gravitational energy is transferred to thermal/internal energy of the surroundings, air and object. In this lab we specify a fraction f of released energy as the nonmechanical transfer: E_other=fmg(H−h). The remaining kinetic energy is K=(1−f)mg(H−h). Then U+K+E_other=mgH. This prescribed fraction is a mathematical teaching model, not a real drag law.

Stop before contact

The model predicts a state just before any contact at h=0; it does not predict what happens during impact. The speed is a magnitude, not a signed vertical velocity. Dropping real objects from height is unnecessary; students should use virtual outputs and the printable calculated dataset.

Synthetic data and virtual laboratories only. Do not improvise loaded springs, hazardous ramps or object drops. These tools do not certify equipment safety.

Vocabulary

mechanical energy
Sum of kinetic and modeled potential energies.
kinetic energy
Nonnegative energy of motion K=½mv².
released gravitational energy
Decrease mg(H−h) of potential energy.
nonmechanical transfer
Energy entering internal, thermal or other forms.
ideal free fall
Motion under gravity without nonconservative losses.

Worked example

For 2 kg falling from H=5 m to h=3 m, 39.2 J of gravitational energy is released. If f=0.25, other forms gain 9.8 J and K=29.4 J; U=58.8 J, totaling 98 J. Speed magnitude=√(2×29.4/2)≈5.42 m/s.
