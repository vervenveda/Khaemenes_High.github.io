Grade 12 · Week 10 · Tuesday

Read the Angle and the Work Graph

Learning target: Use cosine and force–displacement graphs to calculate work consistently.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · No physical apparatus needed

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Concept reading

Work is a dot product

W=F⃗·d⃗=Fd cosθ. Only the force component parallel to displacement contributes to work. At θ=60°, cos60°=0.5; a 20 N force over 3 m transfers 30 J by this interaction. At θ=120°, cos120°=−0.5 and the same magnitudes yield −30 J. The sign describes the direction of energy transfer, not the sign of a force's magnitude.

The area under a force–position curve

For force parallel to the displacement that varies with position, work is the signed area under the F_parallel-versus-position graph: W=∫F_parallel dx. This lesson's digital work tool uses a constant force, so the graph is a horizontal line and the area is just F_parallel×d. For a genuinely variable force, you must integrate or estimate area in sections; blindly multiplying one point's force by distance is not generally valid.

Choose axes and separate contributions

Suppose a force to the right is +10 N while a resistive force to the left is −4 N over a +5 m horizontal displacement. The applied force does +50 J; resistance does −20 J. Net work is +30 J. This is a scalar sum of work from distinct force agents, not a new force acting on the cart.

Know when the shortcut is invalid

An angled handle may pull a rolling cart, but vertical forces can change its normal contact force and therefore its friction. Our single-force calculator does not automatically compute a friction law or a path with changing direction. It displays exactly the work of one prescribed force on a prescribed straight displacement.

All investigations use calculated synthetic data. Never construct dangerous ramps, pull heavy loads or use these classroom equations as equipment-safety guidance.

Vocabulary in context

dot product
Scalar product F⃗·d⃗=Fd cosθ.
cosine
Ratio determining the projection of force along displacement.
signed area
Positive area above an axis and negative area below it.
force–position graph
Plot of force component against location, used to find work.
net work
Algebraic sum of work done by all external forces on the object.

Worked example

F=20 N, d=3 m: W at 60° is +30 J and at 120° is −30 J. Separately, +10 N and −4 N along a +5 m displacement do +50 J and −20 J, respectively; W_net=30 J.
