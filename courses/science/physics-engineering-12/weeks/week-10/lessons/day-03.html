Grade 12 · Week 10 · Wednesday

Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem

Learning target: Connect net work to changes in kinetic energy without confusing it with the work of one force.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · No physical apparatus needed

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Concept reading

Speed stores kinetic energy

For ordinary nonrelativistic speeds, kinetic energy is K=½mv². Mass is in kilograms, speed in m/s, and energy in joules. Kinetic energy is always nonnegative because v² is nonnegative; doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy at unchanged mass. A 4 kg cart moving at 3 m/s has K=½×4×9=18 J.

The work–energy theorem uses net work

For a constant-mass particle in an inertial frame, the sum of the work done by all forces equals the change in kinetic energy: W_net=K_final−K_initial. A single applied force's work is not automatically net work; if friction opposes the motion, its negative work must also be included. If net work is zero, the speed magnitude does not change even if direction can change.

Solve forward with safe bounds

For an initial speed v₀ and known net work W_net, K_final=½mv₀²+W_net. If this expression is nonnegative, the final speed magnitude is √(2K_final/m). If it would be negative, the requested displacement/time scenario is incompatible with continuing forward under the assumed constant net force; a real body can stop or reverse before that point. The model explicitly flags such contradictions rather than taking √ of a negative energy.

Energy-transfer accounting needs a body boundary

Work is transfer; kinetic energy is a state quantity. A cart may lose kinetic energy as friction transfers energy into thermal energy of surrounding surfaces. A broader conservation explanation including thermal and gravitational energy belongs in Week 11; today's work–energy equation simply reconciles net external work with a chosen cart's K change.

All investigations use calculated synthetic data. Never construct dangerous ramps, pull heavy loads or use these classroom equations as equipment-safety guidance.

Vocabulary in context

kinetic energy
Energy of motion, K=½mv² in the nonrelativistic model.
net work
Sum of work by all forces; equals ΔK in the particle model.
work–energy theorem
W_net=K_final−K_initial.
nonnegative energy
Kinetic energy cannot be less than zero.
energy transfer
Movement of energy into or out of a specified system.

Worked example

A 4 kg cart starts at 3 m/s: K_initial=18 J. If net work adds +32 J, K_final=50 J and final speed=√(2×50/4)=5 m/s. With net work −18 J, it reaches K_final=0 and speed=0.

Show chosen system, directions, SI units and numerical substitutions.
