Energy Transfer Lab and Average Power

Learning target: Run virtual work–energy comparisons and calculate average mechanical power.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · No physical apparatus needed

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Concept reading

Design a matched energy investigation

Choose a mass, initial speed and prescribed net work. Record initial K, final K and final speed magnitude. Change only net work between runs to test whether ΔK=W_net. Next, hold net work fixed and vary mass to examine the different speeds that can result from the same energy transfer. All values are calculated examples, not measured motion trials.

Power measures the rate of transfer

Average power is P_avg=W/Δt with joules divided by seconds, giving watts (W). Positive average work gives positive signed power in this accounting, while negative work yields negative signed power. A machine transferring 120 J of mechanical energy over 4 s has an average transfer rate of 30 W. Average power is not the same as instantaneous power F⃗·v⃗ if force or speed varies.

Compute work and power separately

The Work & Power Studio calculates W=Fd cosθ for one constant force, then divides by the stated duration to give the average signed power by that force. The Energy Transfer Lab treats entered work as NET work and computes a new K; it does not infer net work from unspecified friction, gravity or other forces. Do not silently equate one-force work with net work.

Explain practical limitations

This lab deliberately avoids actual carts, weighted ramps, exercise measurements or hazardous mechanical devices. Its equations assume constant mass, Newtonian speed ranges, known work transfer and positive duration. It is not an energy efficiency certificate or a calculator for motor safety and should not be used to design real moving equipment.

All investigations use calculated synthetic data. Never construct dangerous ramps, pull heavy loads or use these classroom equations as equipment-safety guidance.

Vocabulary in context

average power
Work or energy transferred per time interval, P_avg=W/Δt.
watt
SI power unit, 1 W = 1 J/s.
trial
One repeatable setting of controlled variables and recorded results.
kinetic-energy change
Difference K_final−K_initial.
model provenance
Statement indicating results were calculated rather than measured.

Worked example

For a constant aligned 30 N force acting through 4 m, W=120 J. Over 4 s, average power from that interaction is 120/4=30 W. Separately, for a 4 kg cart at 3 m/s receiving +32 J net work, final K=50 J and speed=5 m/s.
