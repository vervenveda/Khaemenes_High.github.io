Scientific Claim: Work Changes Motion

Learning target: Synthesize force, angle, displacement, net work, kinetic energy and power with quantitative evidence.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · No physical apparatus needed

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Concept reading

Write a clear system-specific claim

Mechanical work depends on the component of force along displacement. For a moving object, the NET work of all forces equals the change in kinetic energy, while average power measures how fast energy is transferred. A valid claim specifies the moving object, sign conventions and whether a displayed quantity is single-force work or net work.

Provide at least two worked cases

Case A: 20 N at 60° over 3 m does +30 J work by that force; at 120°, it does −30 J. Case B: a 4 kg cart initially moving at 3 m/s has K_i=18 J; adding net work 32 J gives K_f=50 J and speed 5 m/s. Case C: delivering 120 J in 4 s gives average power 30 W. Each calculation answers a different physical question.

Challenge misconceptions with evidence

Misconception: every force does work. A perpendicular force can do zero work despite changing the direction of motion. Misconception: work can never be negative. Forces opposing displacement do negative work. Misconception: work by one force necessarily equals ΔK. The work–energy theorem uses the sum across all forces.

Explain model boundaries and corrective work

The prescribed-work simulator does not implement friction dynamics or changes in force over a curved path. If net work would drive kinetic energy below zero, identify that the simple continued-motion scenario is invalid and revise the assumptions or intended displacement. Finish with evidence provenance and a safer controlled comparison. Friday's public randomized quiz is an open-book formative check, not a secure midterm or final.

All investigations use calculated synthetic data. Never construct dangerous ramps, pull heavy loads or use these classroom equations as equipment-safety guidance.

Vocabulary in context

claim–evidence–reasoning
Explanation combining a precise statement, numerical support and principles.
work sign
Positive, negative or zero depending on force-displacement projection.
state quantity
Property of a body's state, like kinetic energy.
transfer rate
Energy per unit time, expressed as power.
limitation
Condition narrowing where the model is valid.

Worked example

For F=20 N, d=3 m, θ=60°, W=30 J. For m=4 kg and v₀=3 m/s, K_i=18 J. With net work +32 J, K_f=50 J; speed magnitude=5 m/s. Report both without treating the 30 J single-force work as automatically the cart's net work.
