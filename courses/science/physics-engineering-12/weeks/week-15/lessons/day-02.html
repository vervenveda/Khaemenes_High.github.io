One-Dimensional Collision Calculations

Use momentum conservation to solve for an unknown speed in a two-object interaction.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

For a closed one-dimensional system, Σp_before=Σp_after. Write every object’s signed momentum term before substituting numbers.

If objects stick together, they share one final velocity. If they separate, each final velocity remains a separate unknown or measured quantity.

A numerical solution should be checked against direction, magnitude, and the original system momentum.

Vocabulary: initial momentum; final momentum; perfectly inelastic; signed velocity; conservation equation.

Worked example with reasoning

A 2 kg cart at +4 m/s sticks to a stationary 2 kg cart: 8=(4)v_f, so v_f=+2 m/s.

Guided question

A 3 kg cart at +2 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart at −2 m/s. Find the shared final velocity.

Independent work

  1. Solve four one-dimensional collision problems.
  2. For each, audit momentum before and after.
  3. Explain one negative velocity physically.

Misconception check

Do not drop signs when adding momentum; opposite directions are not both positive.

Formative understanding check

Why can a final velocity be negative?

Reveal response

The system’s net momentum can point in the declared negative direction.

Collision-equation discipline extension

Build every one-dimensional collision solution in the same order: choose a positive direction, list masses and signed velocities, calculate total initial momentum, write the system equation, solve for the unknown, then verify the final total momentum independently. This separates physics reasoning from arithmetic and catches sign mistakes early.

For perfectly inelastic collisions, the shared final velocity follows v_f=(m1v1i+m2v2i)/(m1+m2). Its sign comes from the total initial momentum, not from whichever object is heavier or moving faster. A final velocity near zero can represent nearly canceling momenta rather than “no collision effect.”

Check reasonableness after solving. The final shared speed should be consistent with the total momentum and combined mass. If the answer points opposite the total initial momentum, a sign error is likely.

Uncertainty and evidence revision / CER

If one measured velocity is uncertain, estimate how that uncertainty changes the calculated total momentum and final velocity. Write a CER defending the solved direction and magnitude. Revise any solution that drops velocity signs or treats a negative result as unphysical.

Worked transfer and reasonableness check

A 3 kg cart moves at +2 m/s and sticks to a 1 kg cart moving at −2 m/s. Initial momentum is 3(2)+1(−2)=+4 kg·m/s. The combined mass is 4 kg, so v_f=+1 m/s. Recompute the final momentum: 4(+1)=+4 kg·m/s. The sign and total match the system ledger.

Now test a near-canceling case: 2 kg at +1.5 m/s collides with 3 kg at −1.0 m/s. Initial momentum is +3−3=0, so a perfectly inelastic final state has v_f=0. This does not mean no forces acted; it means the vector momenta canceled for the combined system. Compare the kinetic energy before and after and explain why zero final momentum does not imply zero energy transfer.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Two carts interact and total momentum remains nearly constant despite individual changes.

Investigation / model check

Virtual cart collision or supervised low-energy carts. Preserve observations or supplied data, compare them with the model, and record one limitation or source of uncertainty.
