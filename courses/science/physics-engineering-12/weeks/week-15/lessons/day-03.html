Recoil, Separation & Internal Forces

Apply conservation to systems that begin together and separate through internal forces.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Momentum conservation applies to recoil and explosion-style models as well as collisions. If initial total momentum is zero and external impulse is negligible, final momenta must sum to zero.

Internal forces occur in equal-and-opposite pairs, but the objects can have different accelerations because their masses differ.

Equal and opposite momentum does not imply equal kinetic energy or equal speed.

Vocabulary: recoil; internal force; action–reaction; momentum balance; center-of-mass motion.

Worked example with reasoning

A 1 kg cart recoils at +4 m/s from a 2 kg cart. Zero initial momentum requires the 2 kg cart to move at −2 m/s.

Guided question

Why does the lighter object often move faster in a zero-total-momentum separation?

Independent work

  1. Solve three recoil scenarios.
  2. Compare equal-and-opposite momenta with speeds.
  3. Explain how Newton’s third law and momentum conservation are consistent.

Misconception check

Equal force does not mean equal acceleration or equal speed when masses differ.

Formative understanding check

If total momentum starts at zero, what must the vector sum of final momenta be?

Reveal response

Zero, if external impulse is negligible.
