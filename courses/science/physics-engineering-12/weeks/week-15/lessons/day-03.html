Recoil, Separation & Internal Forces

Apply conservation to systems that begin together and separate through internal forces.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Momentum conservation applies to recoil and explosion-style models as well as collisions. If initial total momentum is zero and external impulse is negligible, final momenta must sum to zero.

Internal forces occur in equal-and-opposite pairs, but the objects can have different accelerations because their masses differ.

Equal and opposite momentum does not imply equal kinetic energy or equal speed.

Vocabulary: recoil; internal force; action–reaction; momentum balance; center-of-mass motion.

Worked example with reasoning

A 1 kg cart recoils at +4 m/s from a 2 kg cart. Zero initial momentum requires the 2 kg cart to move at −2 m/s.

Guided question

Why does the lighter object often move faster in a zero-total-momentum separation?

Recoil and internal-force extension

In a recoil or separation event, internal forces redistribute momentum while the system total remains constrained by external impulse. If the system starts at zero momentum and external impulse is negligible, the final momenta must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. The lighter object therefore has the larger speed magnitude because p=mv.

Do not confuse equal and opposite momentum with equal kinetic energy. For equal |p|, kinetic energy can be written K=p²/(2m), so the lower-mass object has the larger kinetic energy. This provides a useful cross-check and shows why momentum and energy describe different aspects of the same event.

Newton's third law explains the paired internal forces, while impulse over the interaction explains equal-and-opposite momentum changes. Conservation emerges from the cancellation of internal impulses within the chosen system, not from the absence of forces.
