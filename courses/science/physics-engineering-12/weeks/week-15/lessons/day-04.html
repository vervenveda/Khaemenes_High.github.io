Collision-Data Investigation

Use supplied or simulated collision data to test momentum conservation and quantify uncertainty.

Explicit physics instruction

A conservation claim should be tested by comparing total momentum before and after across repeated trials.

Calculate Δp_system=p_after−p_before and compare that residual with measurement uncertainty. A small nonzero residual may be consistent with friction or measurement limits.

Preserve raw mass/velocity data before calculated momenta so the analysis remains reproducible.

Vocabulary: raw data; momentum residual; uncertainty; repeated trial; conservation test.

Worked example with reasoning

Trial: p_before=1.20 kg·m/s and p_after=1.16 kg·m/s gives Δp=−0.04 kg·m/s. Whether that meaningfully violates conservation depends on the uncertainty scale.

Conservation-test extension

A convincing conservation test uses repeated trials and compares the system momentum residual with both measurement uncertainty and expected external impulse. Define r_p=p_after−p_before for each trial, then examine whether residuals scatter around zero or show a systematic bias.

Preserve raw masses and velocities before any derived calculations. Momentum uncertainty can be estimated from uncertainty in mass and velocity; when velocity uncertainty dominates, the sign and magnitude near zero deserve special care. A small residual is only meaningful when interpreted against that uncertainty scale.

Use pattern evidence, not a single trial. Residuals that consistently lean positive or negative may signal friction, timing bias, calibration error, or an incomplete system boundary rather than random noise.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a CER deciding whether the dataset supports approximate momentum conservation. Cite repeated residuals, uncertainty, and one plausible external-impulse source. Revise any claim that declares conservation from one nearly matching pair of numbers.

Residual-pattern transfer

Suppose four trials produce momentum residuals of +0.03, −0.02, +0.01, and −0.04 kg·m/s with an estimated uncertainty of ±0.05 kg·m/s. Those residuals scatter around zero and are individually smaller than the uncertainty scale, so the data are consistent with conservation. A second dataset of −0.09, −0.11, −0.10, and −0.12 kg·m/s shows a systematic negative pattern that likely requires an external impulse or calibration explanation.

Use the pattern, not just the average. A mean residual near zero can hide large alternating errors, while a consistent bias can reveal a missing force. State what additional measurement—such as friction impulse, timing calibration, or a wider velocity uncertainty analysis—would best test the competing explanation.
