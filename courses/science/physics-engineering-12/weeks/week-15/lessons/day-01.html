System Momentum & External Impulse

Define a momentum system and identify when total momentum can be treated as conserved.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Momentum is a vector quantity p=mv. For a chosen system, internal interaction forces can redistribute momentum among objects while the system total changes only through net external impulse.

Conservation is therefore conditional: define the system boundary first, then evaluate whether external impulse during the interaction is negligible.

Sign conventions matter in one-dimensional problems. Opposite directions must carry opposite signs.

Vocabulary: momentum; system boundary; external impulse; conservation; sign convention.

Worked example with reasoning

A 2 kg cart at +3 m/s and a 1 kg cart at −2 m/s have total momentum 2(3)+1(−2)=+4 kg·m/s.

Guided question

Two carts interact briefly on a nearly frictionless track. What evidence would justify treating their combined momentum as conserved?

Independent work

  1. Calculate total momentum for three two-object systems.
  2. Declare a positive direction before each calculation.
  3. Identify internal versus external forces in one collision scenario.

Misconception check

Momentum conservation does not mean each object keeps the same momentum; only the defined system total is constrained.

Formative understanding check

What changes a system’s total momentum?

Reveal response

Net external impulse acting on the system.

System-boundary and external-impulse extension

Momentum conservation is never a statement about an isolated algebraic formula; it is a statement about a chosen system during a stated time interval. Before writing Σp_before=Σp_after, identify the bodies inside the boundary and list any external forces that act over the interaction time. A small external force may still produce a negligible impulse if the interaction is brief, while a modest force acting for longer can measurably change the system momentum.

Use Δp_system=J_external as the governing check. Conservation is the special case in which the net external impulse is negligible relative to the momentum scale and measurement uncertainty. This framing prevents students from claiming conservation simply because two objects collide or because internal forces come in third-law pairs.

Track direction explicitly. A positive system total can emerge from several signed object momenta, and one object may reverse while the total remains unchanged. The conservation equation constrains the vector sum, not each body's individual momentum.

Uncertainty and evidence revision / CER

Write a CER defending whether conservation is appropriate for one supplied collision. Use system definition, estimated external impulse, and signed before/after momentum as evidence. Revise any claim that ignores the interaction interval or treats internal forces as external momentum losses.

Quantitative transfer practice

Consider a 1.5 kg cart at +2.0 m/s and a 2.5 kg cart at −1.0 m/s. The initial system momentum is +3.0−2.5=+0.5 kg·m/s. If a small external friction force of −0.20 N acts on the two-cart system for 0.40 s during the interaction, the external impulse is −0.08 N·s, so the expected final system momentum is about +0.42 kg·m/s rather than exactly +0.50. This example shows how conservation becomes an approximation tied to impulse scale.

Compare the size of that external impulse with the momentum uncertainty from measured velocities. If each velocity is known only to ±0.05 m/s, the measurement uncertainty may be comparable to or larger than the modeled external impulse. A defensible conclusion should therefore state whether the evidence can actually distinguish perfect conservation from a small external effect.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Two carts interact and total momentum remains nearly constant despite individual changes.

Investigation / model check
