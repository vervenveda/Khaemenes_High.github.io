Teacher / Parent Educator · Week 04

Two-Dimensional Motion & Projectiles

Five-day lesson plan, instructional references, misconceptions, accessible lab options and review rubrics.

Academic purpose and safe learning

Students build a two-dimensional kinematics model under constant downward gravity. They decompose vectors, solve vertical impact time, find horizontal range and explain independence only under stated no-drag assumptions. These lessons support the quantitative modeling and scientific inquiry practices of the broader HS-PS2-1 physics unit; they do not individually claim to fulfill all Newton's-law performance expectations.

Prerequisites: Week 02 coordinates and displacement, Week 03 velocity/acceleration, slope, graph axes and algebra; Week 04 also uses introductory sine/cosine; Week 05 uses squaring and means. Differentiation: provide a partially labeled diagram and pre-calculated table for foundation learners; use the full simulator for core; ask extended learners to critique simplifying assumptions.

No physical projectile launching, sealed devices, moving vehicles, dangerous ramps or recordings of others are necessary. Both studios are dry/no-hazard offline digital models with printable fictional datasets. The simulations are not real measurements or predictions of safe actual launch paths.

Worked reference answers

For h=19.6 m, vy0=0 and g=9.8 m/s², t=√(2h/g)=2.0 s. With vx0=5 m/s range=10 m; with vx0=10 m/s range=20 m. Launch v0=10 m/s at θ=30° gives vx0≈8.66 m/s, vy0=5 m/s. Level-ground flight time 2vy0/g≈1.0204 s, range≈8.84 m and apex height≈1.28 m. For complementary 30°/60° launches at equal initial speed and level-ground conditions, ideal ranges agree within rounding; time and apex differ. This is not true of all launch heights or real-world drag.

20-point CER review rubric

Claim · 4
Scientific answer limited by defined system and assumptions.
Numerical evidence · 4
At least two valid calculations or observations with units and data provenance.
Interpretation · 4
Accurate vector components, time, range or axis interpretation.
Reasoning · 4
Correct physical equation and logically connected explanation.
Limitations · 4
Fair counterclaim, potential uncertainties and a useful next experiment.

Teacher review target: 16/20 (80%). Allow revisions and preserve evidence of initial work separately.

Common misconceptions and extension

  • Horizontal velocity changes vertical impact time even without drag. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • Every launch has a 45° maximum range regardless of height. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • The apex is always at the impact time. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • A trajectory plotted against x represents a time graph. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • Simulation predictions certify safe real launches. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.

Public open-book practice items and browser-local evidence are not secure, supervised final examinations. Teacher-verified assessments require a separate educator-controlled process.
