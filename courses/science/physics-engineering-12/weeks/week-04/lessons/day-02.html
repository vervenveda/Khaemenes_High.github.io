Vectors, Angles and Components

Learning objective: Resolve an initial velocity vector into horizontal and vertical components with units.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice, not a supervised examination

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Read and reason

A velocity is more than one number

A velocity specified as 12 m/s is incomplete if the direction matters. In two-dimensional coordinates, the launch velocity can be represented by vx0 and vy0. The magnitude tells us the initial speed. An angle θ measured counterclockwise upward from +x determines the components: vx0=v0 cos θ and vy0=v0 sin θ. These relationships use right-triangle geometry and require that θ be measured from the horizontal.

The components share a common direction convention

Choose +x as right and +y as up. A launch at +30° above +x has positive vx0 and vy0. If θ is negative, the initial vertical component points downward. In the current learning studio, angles are restricted to 0° through 80°, speed and starting height are nonnegative, and gravity points downward. This limits the situations the tool models while keeping the x/y equations explicit.

Connect components to time

With negligible drag, vx(t)=vx0 is constant while vy(t)=vy0−gt changes. The vertical velocity becomes zero at an apex, t_apex=vy0/g, only if the apex occurs before landing and vy0 is positive. The horizontal position increases linearly x(t)=x0+vx0t, while y(t)=h+vy0t−(g/2)t² is a downward-opening quadratic. Their combined positions form a curved trajectory.

Evaluate the boundary of the model

Sin and cosine are geometric definitions for vector decomposition; the same initial speed yields different horizontal and vertical components at different angles. Do not confuse a horizontal launch at 0° from nonzero height with a launch from the ground where immediate contact with ground is a boundary condition. Air drag and spin are absent here. The equations cannot certify safe real launching distances or conditions.

Safety: Only use synthetic data and offline simulations for the core work. No real projectile launching, motor vehicles, improvised ramps, unsafe falling objects or video recording of others is needed.

Vocabulary in context

vector
A quantity with magnitude and direction.
magnitude
The nonnegative size of a vector.
horizontal component
The part of a vector aligned with the chosen x axis.
vertical component
The part of a vector aligned with the chosen y axis.
launch angle
The initial direction above the horizontal in this model.

Use every term in a short original sentence or model explanation.

Worked example

For v0=10 m/s and θ=30°, vx0=10 cos30°≈8.66 m/s; vy0=10 sin30°=5.00 m/s. At t=1 s, vy≈5.00−9.8=−4.8 m/s, but vx≈8.66 m/s remains unchanged.
