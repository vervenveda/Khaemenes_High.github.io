Trajectories and Impact Time

Learning objective: Find flight time and range analytically and verify the result against a graph/table.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice, not a supervised examination

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Read and reason

Solve the landing-height condition

To find when a projectile reaches flat ground at y=0, solve 0=h+vy0t−(1/2)gt². For nonnegative height and a suitable launch, use the nonnegative root t=(vy0+sqrt(vy0²+2gh))/g. This formula combines the upward initial velocity with gravitational acceleration. Units inside the square root are (m/s)² and the final result is seconds.

Distance depends on horizontal speed and flight time

With horizontal acceleration zero, x displacement equals vx0×t_flight. If you double vx0 while keeping vy0 and launch height fixed, impact time is unchanged and range doubles. Changing the angle at fixed speed changes both components, so the range result is not generally monotonic in angle. Starting height also matters: from a platform, a 45° launch is not automatically maximum range.

Interpreting the apex

If vy0>0, the model reaches vy(t)=0 at t_apex=vy0/g. The apex height is h+vy0²/(2g). If the launch is horizontal (vy0=0), the launch point is already the maximum altitude in this model. The point t_apex is not the impact time unless the trajectory has some special boundary; compute impact from y(t)=0 separately.

Check reasonableness before accepting a solution

The negative algebraic root normally corresponds to a time before the modeled launch and is rejected. Check whether y(t) is nonnegative before impact and whether a trial duration includes impact. A plotted trajectory represents position against position—not against time—so axes must be explicitly labeled x (m) and y (m). A ballistic classroom model is not an experimental observation or a real launch plan.

Safety: Only use synthetic data and offline simulations for the core work. No real projectile launching, motor vehicles, improvised ramps, unsafe falling objects or video recording of others is needed.

Vocabulary in context

trajectory
The sequence of positions followed by a moving object.
apex
The highest point along an upward-then-downward trajectory.
range
Horizontal distance between launch and ideal landing points.
time of flight
Elapsed time from launch to modeled impact.
quadratic equation
A polynomial equation containing a squared variable.

Use every term in a short original sentence or model explanation.

Worked example

At h=0, v0=10 m/s, θ=30°: vy0=5 m/s and vx0≈8.66 m/s. Flight time=(5+sqrt(25))/9.8≈1.0204 s; range≈8.84 m; apex time≈0.5102 s; apex height≈1.28 m.
