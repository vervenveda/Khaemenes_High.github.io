Assessment boundary

This page contains public formative support only. It does not expose protected final questions, answer keys, grading logic, or authentication details.

Explicit course synthesis

A final defense should explain which models the learner can use, which evidence supports that claim, and where uncertainty or growth areas remain.

Select representative artifacts across mechanics, waves/fields, investigation, and engineering rather than repeating similar work.

A strong reflection names corrections and model revisions as evidence of growth.

Vocabulary: portfolio defense; synthesis; evidence selection; limitation; reflection.

Worked example

A balanced defense might include a mechanics investigation, a waves or circuits analysis, a remote-sensing dataset, and the engineering capstone.

Guided question

Which four artifacts make the strongest nonredundant case for course mastery, and why?

Independent work

  1. Select four representative artifacts.
  2. Write a concise course defense.
  3. Name two persistent strengths and two next-step targets.
  4. Record one example where revision improved your model.

Misconception check

A polished portfolio is not necessarily a strong one unless the evidence is traceable and explained.

Formative check

What makes a final defense credible?

Reveal response

Specific evidence, correct reasoning, honest limits, and documented growth.

Mentor evidence conference

Archaemenes may challenge reasoning and help plan corrections but cannot reveal protected answers or grant official final mastery.

Teacher guidance

Core: Integrate evidence across domains.

Extended: Evaluate evidence quality and future transfer.
