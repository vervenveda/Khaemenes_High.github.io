Capstone Defense & Iteration

Defend the final design/model with evidence, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and a justified next iteration.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit capstone instruction

A capstone defense should answer: What problem? What model? What prediction? What evidence? What changed? What remains uncertain?

The goal is not to claim perfection; it is to show disciplined modeling and iteration.

A final recommendation should be tied directly to criteria and constraints, with remaining limitations named.

Vocabulary: defense; iteration; tradeoff; evidence; limitation.

Worked example

A design can be recommended even with an 8% model error if it meets constraints, performs consistently, and the residual is understood well enough for the decision context.

Guided question

Write a one-paragraph defense for a design that improved one criterion but worsened another.

Independent work

  2. Submit predictive model and uncertainty.
  3. Submit test evidence and revision.
  4. Write final recommendation and next iteration.

Capstone defense depth

A credible engineering defense is an evidence chain, not a sales pitch. Connect the system definition to the governing physics, quantitative prediction, uncertainty, test evidence, residuals, criteria, constraints, revisions, and final recommendation. Each conclusion should be traceable to the evidence that supports it.

A design with an 8% prediction error may still be fit for purpose if it satisfies all constraints, performs repeatably, and the residual is understood relative to the decision context. A smaller numerical error is not automatically better if the design fails a hard requirement or if the measurement is unreliable.

Tradeoff and robustness check

Change one priority or uncertain parameter within a reasonable range. If the recommendation changes, report the decision as sensitive and explain what additional evidence would reduce uncertainty. If it remains stable, explain why the recommendation is robust.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a final claim naming the recommended design or model. Support it with at least one quantitative result, one uncertainty statement, and one criterion/constraint comparison. Include one unresolved limitation and one next iteration that would most improve the evidence.

Cross-course transfer

Identify at least two physics domains represented in the capstone and explain how their models interact. Then identify one domain that was deliberately excluded because it would not improve the decision.

Portfolio readiness

Preserve the problem statement, prediction, raw data, calculations, graphs, revision history, and final defense as one traceable artifact suitable for the Week 36 portfolio audit.
