Prototype / Simulation Plan

Design a safe test that can discriminate between the model and alternatives.

Explicit capstone instruction

A useful experiment or simulation changes one relevant feature at a time when possible and records raw evidence.

Define independent, dependent, and controlled variables before testing.

Plan repeated trials, calibration, and a revision criterion so the capstone does not become a one-shot demonstration.

Vocabulary: test plan; independent variable; control; trial; calibration.

Worked example

If model A predicts 10 units and model B predicts 16 units, a test with ±5-unit uncertainty cannot discriminate well; improving measurement precision may be more valuable than adding complexity.

Test-plan depth

A useful capstone test must be capable of distinguishing among plausible outcomes. Define the independent variable, dependent variable, controls, calibration method, trial count, and analysis metric before running the test. Preserve raw values and simulation settings so the procedure can be reproduced.

If model A predicts 10 units and model B predicts 16 units but the measurement uncertainty is ±5 units, the test has weak discriminating power. Improving the measurement method may provide more information than building a more elaborate prototype.

Information-gain reasoning

Choose test conditions where the competing models or design alternatives differ most strongly while remaining safe and within the model's valid range. Avoid collecting many low-value trials when a smaller number of carefully chosen conditions would better challenge the prediction.

Revision trigger

Define a threshold in advance: for example, a residual larger than expected uncertainty, repeated failure of a criterion, or a systematic pattern across trials. Predefining the trigger reduces the temptation to reinterpret disappointing results after the fact.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend why the proposed test is sufficient to evaluate the model. Include one alternative explanation for a possible mismatch and identify what extra evidence would separate that explanation from true model failure.

Safety and retrieval

Use teacher-approved low-risk materials, low-voltage components, simulations, or supplied datasets. Retrieve one strong control-variable or calibration practice from an earlier Physics 12 investigation and incorporate it into the capstone plan.
