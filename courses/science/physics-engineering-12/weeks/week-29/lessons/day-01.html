Charge, Interaction & Coulomb Reasoning

Model electric interaction with charge sign, separation, and inverse-square proportionality.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

Electric charge comes in positive and negative forms. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract; the force acts along the line joining the charges.

Coulomb interaction strength scales with the product of charge magnitudes and inversely with the square of separation. Doubling distance reduces ideal force to one-fourth.

The model is powerful but simplified: point-charge assumptions and surrounding materials can matter.

Vocabulary: Coulomb interaction; charge sign; inverse-square; separation; point-charge model.

Worked example

If separation doubles while charge magnitudes stay fixed, force becomes one-fourth as large because F∝1/r².

Guided question

Two charges move from distance r to 3r. What fraction of the original force remains?

Independent work

  1. Rank four charge pairs by attraction/repulsion and relative force strength.
  2. Solve three inverse-square ratio problems.
  3. State one assumption behind treating objects as point charges.

Misconception check

Doubling distance does not halve an inverse-square force; it quarters it.

Formative check

What determines whether electric force is attractive or repulsive?

Reveal response

The signs of the interacting charges.

Uncertainty and Coulomb-model extension

Because Coulomb force scales as 1/r², separation uncertainty matters strongly at small distances. If r is measured with a few percent uncertainty, the force prediction can carry roughly twice that fractional uncertainty from distance alone before charge uncertainty is included.

Finite object size, charge redistribution, and dielectric materials also limit the point-charge model. State whether the source objects are small compared with their separation before treating them as ideal points.

Evidence revision / CER

Use one inverse-square ratio case to defend how force changes with distance. Revise any explanation that relies only on “closer means stronger” without the squared scaling or sign of the charges.

Spiral retrieval

Connect Week 09 gravitational inverse-square reasoning with electric interaction, noting the crucial difference that electric force may attract or repel.

Quantitative Coulomb transfer

Compare two point-charge interactions. If q₁ and q₂ stay fixed while separation changes from r to 2r, force becomes one-fourth. If one charge doubles while distance stays fixed, force doubles. If both charge magnitudes double and distance doubles, the factors cancel: charge product rises by four while r² rises by four.

Use ratio reasoning before full substitution. It reduces arithmetic and makes inverse-square structure visible. Then state whether attraction or repulsion follows from the signs.

Multi-step inverse-square check

Compare three cases from the same reference interaction: double one charge, triple separation, and reverse one charge sign. Magnitude scaling and direction must be handled separately: the first doubles force magnitude, the second reduces it to one-ninth, and the third leaves magnitude unchanged while switching attraction to repulsion or vice versa.

Use this to separate algebraic scaling from physical direction.

Final scaling note

Use one combined charge-distance ratio to verify both magnitude scaling and interaction direction before accepting the result.
