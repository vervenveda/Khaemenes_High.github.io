Charge, Interaction & Coulomb Reasoning

Model electric interaction with charge sign, separation, and inverse-square proportionality.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

Electric charge comes in positive and negative forms. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract; the force acts along the line joining the charges.

Coulomb interaction strength scales with the product of charge magnitudes and inversely with the square of separation. Doubling distance reduces ideal force to one-fourth.

The model is powerful but simplified: point-charge assumptions and surrounding materials can matter.

Vocabulary: Coulomb interaction; charge sign; inverse-square; separation; point-charge model.

Worked example

If separation doubles while charge magnitudes stay fixed, force becomes one-fourth as large because F∝1/r².

Guided question

Two charges move from distance r to 3r. What fraction of the original force remains?

Independent work

  1. Rank four charge pairs by attraction/repulsion and relative force strength.
  2. Solve three inverse-square ratio problems.
  3. State one assumption behind treating objects as point charges.

Misconception check

Doubling distance does not halve an inverse-square force; it quarters it.

Formative check

What determines whether electric force is attractive or repulsive?

Reveal response

The signs of the interacting charges.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use sign diagrams and ratio tables.

Core: Solve force-direction and inverse-square comparisons.

Extended: Analyze where finite-size or medium effects limit the point-charge model.
