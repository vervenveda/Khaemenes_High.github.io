Electrostatics Simulation Investigation

Use a safe electrostatics simulation to test inverse-square field behavior and superposition.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

This investigation uses simulation or supplied data only—no high-voltage devices.

Measure field magnitude at several distances from one modeled charge, then compare E with 1/r².

Add a second source charge and compare measured field direction with qualitative vector superposition.

Vocabulary: simulation; field map; inverse-square fit; superposition; residual.

Worked example

If E=900 N/C at r=1 unit, ideal inverse-square prediction at r=3 is 100 N/C.

Guided question

Which transformed graph would help test E∝1/r² most directly?

Independent work

  1. Preserve raw r and E data.
  2. Calculate 1/r² for each point.
  3. Graph E versus 1/r².
  4. Add a second source and compare vector prediction with the simulated field.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use simulation or supplied data only. Preserve raw values, transformed quantities, graph, residuals, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

A visually curved E-versus-r graph can still represent a precise inverse-square relationship.

Formative check

What would support the inverse-square model?

Reveal response

A near-linear relationship between E and 1/r² with small residuals.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit sign conventions, vector/scalar distinctions, units, and model assumptions.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied values if simulation access is limited.

Core: Test inverse-square scaling and field direction.

Extended: Fit a line to E vs 1/r² and discuss residuals/uncertainty.
