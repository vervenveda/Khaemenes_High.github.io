Electric Potential & Energy

Connect electric potential difference with energy change per unit charge.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

Electric potential is energy per unit charge. A potential difference ΔV corresponds to energy change ΔU=qΔV.

Potential is scalar, while electric field is vector. This distinction makes potential useful for energy accounting.

Charges can gain or lose electric potential energy depending on both charge sign and potential change.

Vocabulary: electric potential; voltage; electric potential energy; scalar; energy per charge.

Worked example

A +0.50 C charge moves through +6 V. ΔU=qΔV=0.50×6=3 J.

Guided question

A −2 C charge moves through +4 V. What is ΔU, and what does the sign mean?

Independent work

  1. Solve three ΔU=qΔV problems.
  2. Distinguish field arrows from equipotential ideas.
  3. Explain why voltage is not itself energy.

Misconception check

Voltage is energy per charge, not total energy by itself.

Formative check

What additional quantity is needed to convert voltage change into energy change?

Reveal response

Charge q.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit sign conventions, vector/scalar distinctions, units, and model assumptions.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a sign/energy table.

Core: Solve potential-energy changes with signed charge.

Extended: Connect field direction to decreasing potential for positive test-charge motion.

Potential-energy depth extension

Electric potential is scalar, so contributions from multiple sources add algebraically without vector directions. Potential difference tells how energy per charge changes; ΔU=qΔV then depends on the sign of the moving charge.

For a negative charge moving through a positive ΔV, ΔU is negative. That sign is meaningful and should not be replaced by an absolute value. Keep field direction and potential sign conventions separate.

Evidence revision / CER

Use one positive-charge and one negative-charge case through the same potential difference to defend why their energy changes have opposite signs. Revise any statement that treats voltage as total energy.

Uncertainty and retrieval

If voltage is measured, propagate that uncertainty into ΔU. Recall Week 10 work/energy sign reasoning.

Potential-transfer practice

A +2 μC charge moves through a potential increase of +300 V, giving ΔU=+6.0×10⁻⁴ J. A −2 μC charge through the same ΔV has ΔU=−6.0×10⁻⁴ J. Same voltage change, opposite energy response because q changes sign.

Use this to separate field/potential from force/energy on a particular charge. The source field can be unchanged while the test charge experiences opposite force or energy change.

Potential-superposition check

At a point influenced by multiple source charges, add scalar potential contributions algebraically. A zero total potential does not require zero electric field. Construct or describe a configuration where potentials cancel while field vectors do not.

Use that contrast to reinforce why field and potential are different models.

Final potential check

Construct a case with zero potential but nonzero field and explain why scalar cancellation does not require vector cancellation.
