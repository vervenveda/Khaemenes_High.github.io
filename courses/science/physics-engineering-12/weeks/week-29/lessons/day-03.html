Electric Potential & Energy

Connect electric potential difference with energy change per unit charge.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

Electric potential is energy per unit charge. A potential difference ΔV corresponds to energy change ΔU=qΔV.

Potential is scalar, while electric field is vector. This distinction makes potential useful for energy accounting.

Charges can gain or lose electric potential energy depending on both charge sign and potential change.

Vocabulary: electric potential; voltage; electric potential energy; scalar; energy per charge.

Worked example

A +0.50 C charge moves through +6 V. ΔU=qΔV=0.50×6=3 J.

Guided question

A −2 C charge moves through +4 V. What is ΔU, and what does the sign mean?

Independent work

  1. Solve three ΔU=qΔV problems.
  2. Distinguish field arrows from equipotential ideas.
  3. Explain why voltage is not itself energy.

Misconception check

Voltage is energy per charge, not total energy by itself.

Formative check

What additional quantity is needed to convert voltage change into energy change?

Reveal response

Charge q.
