Electric Fields as a Spatial Model

Represent how a charge creates an electric field that predicts force on a test charge.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations, supplied data, and ordinary classroom-static examples only. Do not use mains electricity or improvised high-voltage devices.

Explicit physics instruction

Electric field describes the force per unit positive test charge at a location: E=F/q_test.

Field direction is defined as the direction a positive test charge would accelerate. Around a positive source charge, field points outward; around a negative source, inward.

Field is a property of the source configuration and location, not of the test charge used to measure it.

Vocabulary: electric field; test charge; field direction; source charge; superposition.

Worked example

If a +2 μC test charge experiences 0.010 N east, field magnitude is E=0.010/(2×10⁻⁶)=5000 N/C east.

Guided question

Why does reversing the sign of the test charge reverse its force but not the field itself?

Independent work

  1. Draw field directions around isolated positive and negative charges.
  2. Calculate E from three F/q cases.
  3. Use superposition qualitatively for two-source-charge arrangements.

Misconception check

Electric field and electric force are related but are not the same quantity.

Formative check

What is the operational definition of electric field?

Reveal response

Force per unit positive test charge.
