Conduction, Convection & Radiation

How does microscopic particle motion explain temperature and heat transfer?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Conduction transfers energy through microscopic interactions within contacting materials.

Convection transports energy through bulk fluid motion, while radiation transfers energy electromagnetically and does not require matter.

More than one mechanism can act at the same time.

Core relation: q=mcΔT; energy conservation across a defined thermal system.

Worked example

A hot object cooling in air can conduct energy to touching supports, transfer by convection to moving air, and radiate infrared energy simultaneously.

Vocabulary and transfer-mechanism extension

Vocabulary: conduction; convection; radiation; temperature gradient; energy transfer.

Conduction transfers energy through microscopic interactions in matter; convection transfers energy through bulk fluid motion; radiation transfers energy electromagnetically and can occur through vacuum. Real systems often involve all three simultaneously.

A hot object cooling in air can conduct through supports, convect to moving air, and radiate infrared energy. The dominant mechanism depends on geometry, material properties, temperature difference, and surroundings.

Evidence revision / CER

Use one cooling scenario to defend which mechanisms are present and which likely dominate. Revise any explanation that assigns exactly one mechanism when evidence supports several.

Spiral retrieval and safety

Connect Week 27 convection as fluid motion with thermal transfer. Avoid hot-surface experimentation.

Mechanism-comparison transfer

Consider a warm cup cooling on a table. Conduction transfers energy into the table at the contact region, convection carries energy into moving air, and radiation transfers electromagnetic energy to the surroundings. All three can occur simultaneously.

Now place the same object in vacuum while keeping radiative surroundings similar. Convection disappears and conduction may be reduced if contact is removed, but radiation remains. This comparison is evidence that radiation does not require matter.

Explain why identifying a transfer mechanism requires physical context, not only a temperature-time graph.
