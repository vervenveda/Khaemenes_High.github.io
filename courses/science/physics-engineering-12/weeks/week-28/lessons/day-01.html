Temperature, Internal Energy & Heat

How does microscopic particle motion explain temperature and heat transfer?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Temperature is related to average microscopic kinetic energy, while internal energy depends on both microscopic energy and the amount/type of matter.

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Conduction, convection, and radiation are transfer mechanisms, not substances stored inside objects.

For temperature change without phase change, q=mcΔT connects transferred energy with mass, specific heat, and temperature change.

Core relation: q=mcΔT; energy conservation across a defined thermal system.

Worked example

Heating 100 g of water by 5°C with c=4.18 J/(g·°C) requires q=100×4.18×5=2090 J.

Guided question

Why can a bathtub and a cup at the same temperature have different internal energies?
