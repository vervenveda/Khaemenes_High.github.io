Temperature, Internal Energy & Heat

How does microscopic particle motion explain temperature and heat transfer?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Temperature is related to average microscopic kinetic energy, while internal energy depends on both microscopic energy and the amount/type of matter.

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. Conduction, convection, and radiation are transfer mechanisms, not substances stored inside objects.

For temperature change without phase change, q=mcΔT connects transferred energy with mass, specific heat, and temperature change.

Core relation: q=mcΔT; energy conservation across a defined thermal system.

Worked example

Heating 100 g of water by 5°C with c=4.18 J/(g·°C) requires q=100×4.18×5=2090 J.

Guided question

Why can a bathtub and a cup at the same temperature have different internal energies?

Vocabulary and thermal-energy extension

Vocabulary: temperature; internal energy; heat; specific heat; thermal equilibrium.

Temperature describes a thermal state related to microscopic motion, while internal energy depends on the total microscopic energy of the system and therefore on amount and material. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; it is not a substance stored inside an object.

For a temperature change without phase change, q=mcΔT. Heating 0.100 kg of water by 5°C with c≈4180 J/(kg·°C) requires about 2090 J.

Evidence revision / CER

Defend why two objects at the same temperature can have different internal energies. Revise any explanation that treats temperature as “amount of heat.”

Spiral retrieval and safety

Recall Week 11 system energy accounting. Use safe simulations or approved low-temperature datasets rather than hot-object experiments.

Thermal-state transfer calculation

Compare 0.10 kg and 1.0 kg of water at the same temperature. They share the same temperature but the larger sample has far greater thermal mass and internal energy. Raising both by 5°C requires ten times more energy for the larger sample because q=mcΔT.

For 0.50 kg of water heated by 10°C, q≈0.50×4180×10=20,900 J. If the heater supplies only 18,000 J to the water, the missing amount signals either measurement uncertainty or energy transfer to the container and surroundings.

Use this to separate temperature equality from internal-energy equality.

Heat-capacity transfer check

Two materials of equal mass receive the same energy but have different specific heats. The material with smaller c undergoes the larger temperature change. Use q=mcΔT to predict the ratio before inserting numbers.

Thermal comparison example

A 0.50 kg aluminum block with c≈900 J/(kg·°C) and 0.50 kg water sample with c≈4180 J/(kg·°C) each receive 4500 J. The aluminum warms by about 10°C while the water warms by only about 2.2°C. Same energy transfer does not imply same temperature change.

Final thermal check

Compare equal-temperature samples of different materials and masses, and explain why neither temperature nor mass alone determines total internal energy.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Objects at the same temperature can contain different thermal energy.
