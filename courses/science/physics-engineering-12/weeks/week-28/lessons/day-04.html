Vocabulary and cooling-curve investigation extension

Vocabulary: cooling curve; average cooling rate; exponential-like trend; residual; uncertainty.

Preserve raw time-temperature data before calculating interval rates. A sample cooling from 80°C to 60°C in 10 min averages −2°C/min over that interval, but later intervals may cool more slowly as the temperature difference to the environment shrinks.

Compare a constant-rate model with the data. Systematic curvature in the residuals is evidence that a constant-rate assumption is poor. Use a more realistic trend only when the evidence justifies it.

Cooling-model transfer analysis

Suppose a sample cools by 12°C in the first 5 minutes, 8°C in the next 5, and 5°C in the next 5. The average cooling rate is clearly not constant. A constant-rate model would show systematic residuals.

Plot temperature versus time and compare interval slopes. If the environment temperature is approximately fixed, the decreasing rate is qualitatively consistent with the shrinking temperature difference that drives transfer.

Preserve raw times and temperatures so alternative models can be tested later. Spiral retrieval: connect Week 23 damping curves and Week 05 residual patterns to this cooling analysis.
