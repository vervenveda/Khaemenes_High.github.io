Vocabulary and calorimetry-depth extension

Vocabulary: specific heat; heat capacity; temperature change; sign convention; calorimetry.

Use q=mcΔT with a declared system sign convention. If the object warms, ΔT is positive and the chosen object gains thermal energy; if it cools, q for that object is negative under the common convention.

Example: 200 g of water cooling by 3°C gives q=200×4.18×(-3)≈-2508 J. The surroundings receive approximately that amount only if other losses are negligible.

Calorimetry transfer practice

A 0.20 kg metal sample with c=450 J/(kg·°C) cools by 30°C. Its thermal-energy change is q=0.20×450×(-30)=-2700 J. In an ideal isolated calorimeter, another component would gain +2700 J.

Real calorimetry requires accounting for the container and environmental losses. If measured gains and losses differ by several hundred joules, compare that residual with sensor and heat-capacity uncertainty before declaring energy nonconservation.

State the system boundary explicitly: sample alone, sample plus water, or sample plus water plus calorimeter.
