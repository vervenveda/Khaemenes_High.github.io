Thermal Physics Synthesis

How does microscopic particle motion explain temperature and heat transfer?

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Thermal synthesis connects microscopic models, q=mcΔT, transfer mechanisms, and energy conservation.

Use the Friday transfer to distinguish temperature from energy and mechanism from amount.

Two objects at the same temperature need not contain the same internal energy; mass and material matter.

Core relation: q=mcΔT; energy conservation across a defined thermal system.

Worked example

Heating 100 g of water by 5°C with c=4.18 J/(g·°C) requires q=100×4.18×5=2090 J.

Vocabulary and thermal synthesis extension

Vocabulary: thermal system; internal energy; heat transfer; mechanism; model limit.

Build a thermal-model chain: temperature difference drives energy transfer; q=mcΔT relates energy amount to temperature change under stated conditions; conduction, convection, and radiation describe pathways; energy conservation tracks the broader system.

Do not infer total internal energy from temperature alone, and do not infer transfer mechanism from the value of q alone. Different questions require different representations.

Evidence revision / CER

Write a synthesis CER using one q=mcΔT calculation, one cooling-curve interpretation, and one mechanism statement. Revise any explanation that confuses temperature, heat, and internal energy.

Spiral retrieval

Without notes, reconstruct temperature → transfer → q calculation → mechanism → conservation/model limits.

Integrated thermal challenge

A 0.25 kg sample with c=900 J/(kg·°C) cools by 20°C, giving q=-4500 J for the sample. Build an energy-flow diagram showing where that energy can go through conduction, convection, and radiation.

Then compare the measured cooling curve with a constant-rate model. If the rate decreases over time, revise the model rather than treating the changing slope as experimental error automatically.

Finish by distinguishing four ideas explicitly: temperature is a state variable, internal energy is stored microscopic energy, heat is transfer due to temperature difference, and conduction/convection/radiation are transfer mechanisms.

Thermal-model transfer check

Given the same q but different masses and specific heats, predict which object shows the larger temperature change. Then state why that comparison still does not identify the transfer mechanism.

Integrated energy-account example

For a cooling metal block, calculate q from mass, specific heat, and temperature change, then map plausible destinations for that energy through conduction, convection, and radiation. State which quantities are directly measured and which pathway claims remain inferred.

Final synthesis check

Identify one thermal claim that requires a measured transfer mechanism rather than being justified by q=mcΔT alone.

Phenomenon / Prediction

Objects at the same temperature can contain different thermal energy.
