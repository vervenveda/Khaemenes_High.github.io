Teacher / Parent Educator · Week 05

Kinematics Inquiry & Model Selection

Five-day lesson plan, instructional references, misconceptions, accessible lab options and review rubrics.

Academic purpose and safe learning

Students compare linear x=b+mt with quadratic x=b+mt+ct² using supplied synthetic data. They compute signed residuals, training RMSE and a holdout prediction, then select a plausible explanation with justified limitations. These lessons support the quantitative modeling and scientific inquiry practices of the broader HS-PS2-1 physics unit; they do not individually claim to fulfill all Newton's-law performance expectations.

Prerequisites: Week 02 coordinates and displacement, Week 03 velocity/acceleration, slope, graph axes and algebra; Week 04 also uses introductory sine/cosine; Week 05 uses squaring and means. Differentiation: provide a partially labeled diagram and pre-calculated table for foundation learners; use the full simulator for core; ask extended learners to critique simplifying assumptions.

No physical projectile launching, sealed devices, moving vehicles, dangerous ramps or recordings of others are necessary. Both studios are dry/no-hazard offline digital models with printable fictional datasets. The simulations are not real measurements or predictions of safe actual launch paths.

Worked reference answers

For x(t)=1+2t (m), a=0 and x(3)=7 m. For x(t)=1+2t+0.5t² (m), modeled acceleration a=1 m/s² and x(3)=11.5 m. A signed residual is observed minus predicted position, for example xobs=5.3 m, xpred=5.0 m → +0.3 m. RMSE=√(Σ residual²/n) for the training points in meters. The studio fits the first five synthetic records and reserves the sixth as a holdout. The quadratic has more parameters and usually smaller training residuals; explain why holdout performance, mechanistic plausibility and uncertainty also count.

20-point CER review rubric

Claim · 4
Scientific answer limited by defined system and assumptions.
Numerical evidence · 4
At least two valid calculations or observations with units and data provenance.
Interpretation · 4
Accurate residual, RMSE, model coefficients or holdout interpretation.
Reasoning · 4
Correct physical equation and logically connected explanation.
Limitations · 4
Fair counterclaim, potential uncertainties and a useful next experiment.

Teacher review target: 16/20 (80%). Allow revisions and preserve evidence of initial work separately.

Common misconceptions and extension

  • A curve automatically proves constant acceleration. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • The quadratic is always the best model because it has more parameters. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • A residual is predicted minus observed rather than observed minus predicted. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • RMSE has no physical units. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.
  • One holdout point guarantees general predictive accuracy. — have students locate the invalid assumption and supply a correction.

Public open-book practice items and browser-local evidence are not secure, supervised final examinations. Teacher-verified assessments require a separate educator-controlled process.
