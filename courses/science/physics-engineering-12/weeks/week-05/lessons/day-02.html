Residuals: The Difference Matters

Learning objective: Compute signed residuals and explain why patterns diagnose model mismatch.

45–60 minutes · Offline-capable · Public practice, not a supervised examination

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Read and reason

Define residual before interpreting

For each time, compute residual r = measured position − predicted position. If a model predicts 5.0 m when the data point is 5.3 m, residual is +0.3 m. If it predicts 5.6 m instead, residual is −0.3 m. The sign matters: it tells you whether the model is underpredicting or overpredicting. Do not take an absolute value before studying the pattern.

Patterns can expose a wrong model

Suppose a cart truly follows a curved position trend. A best-fit straight line may underestimate at the ends and overestimate near the middle, producing a U-shaped residual pattern. A fitted quadratic can better capture the curvature in these specific synthetic cases. However, extra model parameters always improve or maintain in-sample least-squares error, so lower error by itself is not proof of a better underlying physical law.

Units and transparency

Residuals of position are measured in meters. Residuals from a velocity graph would have m/s units. Keep an original copy of the observations. Don't secretly change data values to improve a model fit. Plot residual versus time with a labeled zero line, and explain whether large residuals occur at particular times or are scattered without an obvious pattern.

Use the comparison responsibly

Scientists may compare the root-mean-square error (RMSE), the square root of the average squared residual. The units match the measured quantity. It measures training-data fit, not necessarily predictive truth. A fair model comparison also considers the number of parameters, mechanistic plausibility, measurement uncertainty and results on unseen data. This week's simulator includes a held-out point to discourage choosing a more flexible equation solely by its in-sample RMSE.

Safety: Only use synthetic data and offline simulations for the core work. No real projectile launching, motor vehicles, improvised ramps, unsafe falling objects or video recording of others is needed.

Vocabulary in context

residual
Observed position minus predicted position, in meters.
error
Difference between prediction/measurement and a reference, depending on context.
fit
The agreement between a model and a set of data under an explicit criterion.
scatter
The spread of recorded values around a trend.
systematic pattern
A nonrandom structure in residuals that may reveal model mismatch.

Use every term in a short original sentence or model explanation.

Worked example

Measured x=5.3 m, predicted x=5.0 m: residual +0.3 m. A second model predicts x=5.6 m: residual −0.3 m. Both have absolute residual 0.3 m at this one point; you need all trials to compare models.
