Model Choice: Force, Energy, or Momentum?

Choose among force, energy, and momentum models based on the question and available evidence.

Weekly EvidencePhysics CourseScience Hall

Explicit physics instruction

Newton’s laws are strongest when forces and acceleration are central. Energy models simplify problems where only initial/final states matter. Momentum models excel for brief interactions and collisions.

The models are compatible, not competing theories. One situation can be analyzed several ways, but one representation may be far more efficient.

A model choice must state assumptions: isolated system, negligible drag, conservative interaction, or known external impulse.

Vocabulary: model choice; force; energy; momentum; assumption.

Worked example with reasoning

A cart descends a nearly frictionless ramp: energy is efficient for speed change. The same cart colliding with another is more naturally handled with momentum over the short interaction.

Guided question

For a bouncing ball, which model would you choose to find impact speed? Which for collision impulse? Explain.

Independent work

  1. Match six scenarios to a primary model.
  2. For two scenarios, solve using a second model as a cross-check.
  3. State assumptions for each choice.

Misconception check

Using more equations does not automatically make a solution stronger; model relevance matters.

Formative understanding check

What determines a good model choice?

Reveal response

The system, target quantity, available evidence, and justified assumptions.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge model choice, stage boundaries, and residual interpretation.

Teacher guidance

Extended: Compare two valid methods and discuss efficiency and assumptions.
