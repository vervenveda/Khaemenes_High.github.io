# Chemistry & Materials Science 11 — Secure Assessment Blueprint

**Course code:** KH-SCI-CHEM11  
**Status:** Public cognitive blueprint; **no protected questions, stimuli, answer choices, answer keys, form seeds, or confidential scoring material belong in this file.**  
**Version:** 1.0 · 2026-10-24

## Purpose

This blueprint makes the Academy's assessment expectations inspectable without publishing the confidential material used for supervised secure forms. It describes **what is measured, how much evidence is required, how mastery is interpreted, and what remains protected**.

The public course repository continues to contain learner-facing instruction, practice, weekly formative checks, CER work, labs/simulations, corrections, and portfolio evidence. Those materials are valuable academic evidence, but they are not substitutes for a confidential supervised form when one is required.

## Non-negotiable assessment rules

- **80% mastery floor** on essential assessed outcomes.
- Weekly checks are **open-book formative/mastery evidence** unless a page explicitly states otherwise.
- The cumulative **midterm and final are closed-book supervised assessments** when used as secure summative evidence.
- A submitted objective attempt is immutable; improvement occurs through correction and a fresh authorized reassessment.
- Best demonstrated mastery may be retained, but the historical attempt record is not rewritten.
- Essays, CERs, lab reports, investigations, engineering designs, presentations, and portfolio defenses remain **educator-reviewed performance evidence** and are not auto-converted into authoritative grades by an objective quiz engine.
- Accommodations may change access, timing, format, assistive technology, or response mode without lowering the reasoning target.
- No browser-local public file is treated as authenticated server-side privacy or credential authority.

## Standards / domain coverage

NGSS HS-PS1-1–8 · HS-PS3-1–5 · HS-ETS1-1–4

Secure and public assessment evidence should sample across the following domains rather than overfitting to a narrow vocabulary bank:

- Measurement, matter, atomic structure, periodic patterns and evidence
- Bonding, molecular structure, intermolecular forces and material properties
- Chemical reactions, stoichiometry, limiting quantities and quantitative models
- Gases, solutions, concentration and particle-level explanation
- Thermochemistry, kinetics, energy profiles and reaction-rate evidence
- Equilibrium, acids/bases, buffers and system response
- Electrochemistry, nuclear chemistry, materials science and sustainable design
- Laboratory method, data quality, uncertainty, safety, CER and model evaluation

## Cognitive evidence design

Across a substantial form, item/task selection should represent all four evidence families below. Percentages are target ranges, not an exposed secure-form recipe.

| Evidence family | Target range | What the learner must do |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Conceptual / model reasoning | 20–35% | Select, compare, revise, or explain a scientific model or causal mechanism. |
| Quantitative / data reasoning | 25–40% | Interpret tables, graphs, measurements, rates, proportional relationships, uncertainty, or calculations in context. |
| Evidence / method evaluation | 20–35% | Evaluate controls, variables, sampling, measurement quality, source limits, competing explanations, or experimental method. |
| Transfer / design / limitation | 10–25% | Apply learning to a novel phenomenon, diagnose a model failure, evaluate a design tradeoff, or state a defensible limitation. |

A form should not become a vocabulary test merely because terminology is important. Vocabulary is assessed through its use in explanation, modeling, quantitative work, evidence evaluation, and scientific communication.

## Weekly assessment requirement

For every **ordinary instructional week** (Weeks 1–17 and 19–35), the available weekly check must include at least one item/task based on a **novel phenomenon, data display, quantitative situation, experimental method, model evaluation, or evidence-quality problem**.

Weeks **18 and 36 are cumulative assessment/portfolio windows**, so they are not required to carry a separate ordinary-week bank.

The public question bank may provide formative examples of this reasoning. Secure supervised items must be independently protected.

## Cumulative checkpoints

### Midterm · Week 18
- 15-item cumulative objective check in the published course map.
- Closed-book when administered as secure evidence.
- Samples multiple previously taught domains and more than one cognitive evidence family.
- Corrections and portfolio review follow the attempt; corrections do not silently overwrite the original attempt.

### Final · Week 36
- 30-item cumulative objective check in the published course map.
- Closed-book when administered as secure evidence.
- Cumulative across the course rather than concentrated only on late units.
- Accompanied by portfolio/capstone defense or equivalent educator-reviewed evidence.

## Performance evidence

Objective scores are only one part of the science record. The Academy should preserve representative evidence from:
- CER or evidence-based scientific writing;
- investigation/lab planning and records;
- quantitative work and graphs/tables;
- model construction or revision;
- uncertainty/error analysis;
- design decisions and limitations;
- corrections and reflection;
- portfolio/capstone defense.

Hazardous laboratory performance must never be required without qualified supervision, appropriate facilities, PPE, disposal planning, and current risk review. A safe simulation/data alternative may assess the same reasoning target where hands-on work is inappropriate.

## Secure boundary

### Safe to publish
- course standards and domains;
- approximate cognitive-demand ranges;
- assessment length stated in the course map;
- mastery threshold;
- open-book / closed-book policy;
- public rubrics and performance expectations;
- accommodation principles;
- correction/reassessment policy;
- validation summaries that reveal no protected content.

### Must remain protected
- secure question prompts and source stimuli;
- secure answer choices and correct-answer mappings;
- answer keys;
- exact secure form composition;
- randomization seeds or form-generation secrets;
- confidential reviewer notes;
- authentication/signing material;
- private learner records.

Protected questions/keys remain behind the Academy assessment authority boundary. The public learner repository may transmit only the approved public-safe response contract when a supervised assessment service is used.

## Release test

This blueprint is considered satisfied only when:
1. the ordinary-week public bank has at least one data/evidence/model/quantitative/application item for every ordinary instructional week;
2. the course map identifies Week 18 as the midterm and Week 36 as the final;
3. the public assessment page links to this blueprint;
4. no protected item bank or key has been copied into this document.
