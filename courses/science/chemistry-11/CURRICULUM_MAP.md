# Chemistry 11 — Curriculum Map

## Course
International Chemistry & Materials Science 11 · KH-SCI-CHEM11 · 36 weeks · 180 daily lesson blocks.

## Sequence
- Week 01: Chemical Safety, Measurement & Evidence
- Weeks 02–05: Atomic Models, Isotopes, Electrons/Spectra, Periodic Patterns
- Weeks 06–09: Ionic/Covalent Bonding, Molecular Geometry/Polarity, Metals/Solids
- Weeks 10–13: Intermolecular Forces, Gases, Solutions, Mixtures/Separation/Spectroscopy
- Weeks 14–17: Reaction Evidence, Mole/Stoichiometry, Limiting Reactants/Yield, Redox/Solution Stoichiometry
- Week 18: MID — Midterm Examination & Corrections
- Weeks 19–22: Kinetics
- Weeks 23–25: Thermochemistry, Hess's Law, Entropy/Direction
- Weeks 26–28: Equilibrium and Solubility
- Weeks 29–31: Acids, Bases, pH, Titration, Buffers
- Weeks 32–34: Free Energy, Electrochemistry, Nuclear/Materials/Environmental Chemistry
- Week 35: Materials Science & Sustainable Chemistry Capstone
- Week 36: FINAL — Comprehensive Examination & Portfolio Defense

## Instructional rhythm
Model → concept → data/math → investigation/design → CER/mastery.

## Depth standard
Ordinary teaching lessons should expose substantial mechanism, quantitative reasoning, evidence/source quality, uncertainty/model limits, retrieval, and transfer. Assessment weeks may be shorter when they preserve a supervised assessment boundary.

## Laboratory / inquiry
Target ≥25% qualifying inquiry/laboratory instructional time. Safe simulation/data alternatives are required when physical chemistry procedures need equipment, PPE, ventilation, disposal controls, or qualified supervision.

## Release boundary
Source-level curriculum quality is separate from browser/device, accessibility, protected assessment, transcript authority, accreditation, AP authorization, and jurisdictional credit approval.

## Canonical weekly contracts
The entries below are synchronized to `course-map.json` schema 2.1. They are the course authority for each week's quantitative, evidence, inquiry, and retrieval expectations; daily lessons may implement them in greater detail.

| Week | Topic | Quantitative contract | Evidence / inquiry contract | Retrieval / transfer contract |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Chemical Safety, Measurement & Evidence | Significant figures, density, dimensional analysis, percent error, uncertainty. | Trace measurement → units → uncertainty → claim; distinguish hazard evidence from safety rules. Safety/SDS analysis and supervised or simulated measurement design. | Retrieve unit conversion and proportional reasoning; revise a measurement claim when uncertainty changes. |
| 02 | Atomic Models & Evidence | Atomic notation, proton/neutron/electron counts, charge accounting. | Compare model predictions with scattering/evidence patterns; distinguish observation from historical model. Model-evidence card investigation. | Retrieve conservation/particle-counting rules; revise atomic models from contradictory evidence. |
| 03 | Isotopes, Mass & Nuclear Composition | Weighted-average atomic mass, isotope abundance, normalization checks. | Audit mass-spectrum assumptions, abundance normalization, and data lineage. Mass-spectrum data analysis; no radioactive materials. | Retrieve atomic notation; transfer isotope reasoning to unfamiliar abundance datasets. |
| 04 | Electrons, Spectra & Quantized Energy | c=λν, E=hν, unit conversion, graphical/tolerance reasoning. | Separate raw spectral lines from model inference; compare residuals/tolerance and model limits. Virtual emission-spectrum analysis. | Retrieve atomic-model revision; explain how spectra constrain electron-energy models. |
| 05 | Periodic Patterns & Predictive Chemistry | Trend datasets, comparative ratios, graph-based periodic-property reasoning. | Use evidence to distinguish trend from exception; justify predictions with effective nuclear attraction and shielding. Periodic-trend data investigation. | Retrieve electron structure; transfer trend reasoning to unfamiliar elements/ions. |
| 06 | Ionic Bonding & Lattices | Charge balance, formula-unit ratios, lattice/property comparisons. | Connect ionic formulas and lattice models to macroscopic properties; preserve model boundaries. Ionic model/property investigation or safe simulation. | Retrieve periodic trends and ion formation; revise formulas from charge-conservation errors. |
| 07 | Covalent Bonds & Lewis Models | Valence-electron accounting, formal charge, Lewis-structure comparison. | Compare Lewis/resonance models and state where octet/formal-charge heuristics fail. Lewis-model comparison/design task. | Retrieve ionic/covalent distinctions; transfer bonding models to unfamiliar molecules. |
| 08 | Molecular Geometry & Polarity | Electron-domain counting, VSEPR geometry, polarity/vector cancellation. | Validate shape/polarity predictions against symmetry and molecular evidence. Physical or virtual molecular-model investigation. | Retrieve Lewis structures; revise polarity claims when geometry changes. |
| 09 | Metals, Solids & Materials Structure | Density/property comparisons, weighted engineering decisions. | Use conductivity, density, structure, and defect evidence to justify material choice. Materials-property investigation/design challenge. | Retrieve bonding/structure; transfer evidence to a new material-selection problem. |
| 10 | Intermolecular Forces | Boiling/vapor-pressure trends, comparative IMF reasoning. | Separate molecular structure, IMF model, and bulk-property observation. IMF/property data investigation. | Retrieve polarity; revise claims that confuse bond strength with intermolecular force. |
| 11 | Gases & Particle Motion | Boyle/Charles/Avogadro relationships, PV=nRT, transformed graphs, unit-compatible R. | Use graph shape, controls, and residuals to test gas models; distinguish ideal from real behavior. Gas-law data/simulation investigation. | Retrieve particle-motion model; transfer dimensional analysis to gas systems. |
| 12 | Solutions & Concentration | Molarity, dilution, serial dilution, reverse preparation planning. | Audit meniscus, calibration, transfer, and precision/accuracy evidence. Solution-preparation simulation or supervised lab. | Retrieve mole/volume reasoning; correct dilution plans from conservation-of-solute errors. |
| 13 | Mixtures, Separation & Spectroscopic Evidence | Recovery, purity, mass balance, Rf, calibration curves, interpolation/extrapolation. | Separate raw signal, processed measurement, calibration model, and claim; use blanks/standards/duplicates. Separation/spectroscopy investigation. | Retrieve solution concentration; transfer analytical-evidence logic to unknown samples. |
| 14 | Reaction Evidence & Chemical Equations | Balanced equations, coefficient ratios, atom/mass conservation. | Distinguish reaction evidence from symbolic representation; use conservation as the governing check. Reaction-evidence studio with safe supplied-data alternatives. | Retrieve particle/accounting rules; revise equations by conservation rather than visual balancing alone. |
| 15 | The Mole & Stoichiometry | Moles, molar mass, mole ratios, mass↔mole and mass↔mass stoichiometry. | Audit dimensional-analysis pathways and conservation/reasonableness. Stoichiometry investigation/problem studio. | Retrieve balanced equations; transfer coefficient meaning to quantitative conversion chains. |
| 16 | Limiting Reactants & Yield | Limiting reactant, theoretical/actual yield, percent yield, boundary sensitivity. | Compare common-product pathways, diagnose >100% yield, separate random/systematic causes. Limiting-reactant/yield investigation. | Retrieve stoichiometry; revise limiting-reactant claims when near-stoichiometric uncertainty matters. |
| 17 | Redox & Solution Stoichiometry | Oxidation numbers, electron accounting, n=MV solution stoichiometry, equivalence ratios. | Separate formal oxidation-state bookkeeping from observed solution evidence; preserve charge/atom conservation. Redox/solution-stoichiometry virtual investigation. | Retrieve stoichiometry and ions; transfer electron/accounting logic to coupled redox problems. |
| 18 | Midterm Examination & Corrections | Mixed semester quantitative retrieval across Weeks 01–17. | Use readiness evidence, correction classification, and portfolio traceability without exposing protected MID content. Public practice/portfolio audit only; supervised MID authority remains separate. | Repair prerequisite chains, then verify transfer with new public practice items. |
| 19 | Reaction Rates & Rate Data | Average/instantaneous rate, tangent slopes, initial-rate ratios, empirical order inference. | Separate raw measurements, calculated rates, fits, and mechanism claims; track timing/mixing uncertainty. Rate-data investigation. | Retrieve concentration, stoichiometry, and graph reasoning; revise rate-law claims from controlled comparisons. |
| 20 | Collision Theory & Conditions | Rate ratios under temperature/concentration/surface-area changes. | Use collision frequency, energy distribution, and effective-collision fraction without overgeneralizing. Controlled rate-factor simulation/data investigation. | Retrieve kinetic-temperature and concentration models; transfer collision theory to new rate scenarios. |
| 21 | Mechanisms & Catalysts | Mechanism step summation, rate-law compatibility, energy-profile comparison. | Test mechanisms against net stoichiometry, empirical rate law, intermediates, and catalyst behavior. Competing-mechanism evidence investigation. | Retrieve rate laws; revise mechanism claims when one evidence category fails. |
| 22 | Kinetics Inquiry | Replicate rate metrics, model predictions, residuals, controlled-variable analysis. | Use falsifiable competing models, residual structure, controls, and contrary evidence. Kinetics inquiry with competing-model design. | Retrieve Weeks 19–21; transfer model-discrimination logic to unfamiliar kinetics data. |
| 23 | Thermochemistry & Calorimetry | q=mcΔT, calorimeter heat capacity, qsystem≈−qsurroundings, molar ΔH. | Trace raw mass/temperature → q → reaction heat → molar enthalpy; diagnose heat-loss bias. Calorimetry investigation or safe data alternative. | Retrieve energy conservation and units; revise sign/scaling errors with independent checks. |
| 24 | Bond Energy & Hess's Law | Bond-energy estimates, Hess reversal/scaling/cancellation, state labels, uncertainty accumulation. | Compare bond-energy, Hess, and calorimetry pathways; preserve thermodynamic states and input uncertainty. Hess-cycle/data investigation. | Retrieve enthalpy scaling; transfer state-function reasoning to alternate pathways. |
| 25 | Energy, Entropy & Direction | ΔG=ΔH−TΔS, unit conversion, crossover temperature, microstate/probability models. | Replace 'disorder' slogans with dispersal/multiplicity evidence; separate thermodynamics from kinetics. Entropy/probability simulation. | Retrieve thermochemistry; revise direction claims using ΔH, ΔS, T, and model limits. |
| 26 | Dynamic Equilibrium & K | K expressions and transformations, Q vs K, dynamic rate/concentration graphs. | Separate equal rates from equal concentrations; distinguish composition tendency from speed. Equilibrium simulation/data investigation. | Retrieve kinetics and thermodynamics; predict direction from Q/K rather than slogans. |
| 27 | Le Châtelier & System Response | Q/K response to concentration, volume/partial-pressure, inert-gas, and temperature changes. | Separate instantaneous disturbance from later reaction response; identify whether Q, K, both, or only rates change. Perturbation simulation with controls. | Retrieve Week 26; revise Le Châtelier explanations through explicit equilibrium-expression reasoning. |
| 28 | Solubility & Coupled Equilibria | Molar solubility, Ksp, Qsp, stoichiometric s relationships, post-mixing concentrations, precipitation thresholds. | Separate Ksp, solubility, Qsp, and observed precipitation; preserve temperature, ionic background, units, and detection limits. Solubility/precipitation data investigation. | Retrieve equilibrium expressions and solution concentration; transfer Q/K logic to coupled solubility systems. |
| 29 | Acids, Bases & Molecular Models | Conjugate-pair bookkeeping, qualitative strength/equilibrium comparisons, indicator-range bracketing. | Compare Arrhenius and Brønsted–Lowry scope; separate model classification from direct indicator/conductivity evidence. Indicator/conjugate-pair investigation. | Retrieve equilibrium and water behavior; revise acid/base claims based on formula appearance alone. |
| 30 | pH & Titration Reasoning | pH=−log[H3O+], inverse logs, pOH/Kw, strong-species stoichiometry, n=MV titration calculations. | Separate strength, concentration, pH, equivalence, and endpoint; audit logarithm and mole-ratio errors. Virtual titration/data investigation. | Retrieve solution stoichiometry and logs; transfer model selection across titration regions. |
| 31 | Buffers & Chemical Regulation | Henderson–Hasselbalch, half-equivalence/pKa, buffer stoichiometry, ratio vs total concentration, capacity. | Separate pH prediction from capacity prediction; use stoichiometry before equilibrium after strong-acid/base challenge. Buffer challenge simulation/data investigation. | Retrieve conjugate pairs and titration curves; design and stress-test a buffer near target pH. |
| 32 | Free Energy & Chemical Driving Forces | ΔG vs ΔG°, ΔG=ΔH−TΔS, crossover T, ΔG°=−RT lnK, conceptual ΔG=ΔG°+RT lnQ. | Cross-check free-energy direction through thermodynamic and equilibrium routes; preserve standard vs actual-state conditions. Free-energy data investigation. | Retrieve entropy/equilibrium; revise sign/unit/state errors and validate physical coupling. |
| 33 | Electrochemistry & Energy Conversion | Half-reaction balancing, E°cell=E°cathode−E°anode, ΔG°=−nFE°, voltage/current/energy/power distinctions. | Connect redox bookkeeping, measured polarity, standard potential, and real-device limitations; keep electrons out of salt bridge. Electrochemical-cell investigation or safe simulation. | Retrieve redox and free energy; transfer electron-flow/potential reasoning to unfamiliar cells. |
| 34 | Nuclear Chemistry, Materials & Environmental Chemistry | Nuclear equation conservation, exponential half-life, decay timelines, mass-energy reasoning. | Separate nuclear from chemical change; compare fission/fusion and lifecycle evidence; distinguish hazard from risk. Decay/lifecycle data investigation; no radioactive materials. | Retrieve isotopes and energy; transfer exponential and evidence-matrix reasoning to materials/environmental decisions. |
| 35 | Materials Science & Sustainable Chemistry Capstone | Functional-unit metrics, normalized decision scores, weighted matrices, sensitivity analysis, evidence ranges. | Trace source → calculation → criterion → recommendation; separate measured evidence from stakeholder weighting. Sustainable-chemistry capstone design/evidence investigation. | Retrieve models across the course; stress-test recommendations under changed constraints and uncertain inputs. |
| 36 | Comprehensive Final & Laboratory Portfolio Defense | Mixed cross-unit quantitative retrieval and model selection; no protected-final item reproduction. | Map readiness, portfolio traceability, correction-transfer evidence, and course-defense claims while preserving final-exam authority. No new wet lab; public practice, portfolio audit, and supervised-final support only. | Repair earliest prerequisite weaknesses and verify transfer with new public examples. |
