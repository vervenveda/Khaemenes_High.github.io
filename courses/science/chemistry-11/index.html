About International Chemistry & Materials Science 11

International Chemistry & Materials Science 11 is a quantitative laboratory-science course that develops chemical reasoning from atomic structure and bonding through reactions, stoichiometry, kinetics, thermochemistry, equilibrium, acids and bases, electrochemistry, nuclear chemistry, and materials science. Students connect particle-scale models with measurable evidence, use mathematical relationships to explain chemical behavior, evaluate uncertainty, and apply chemistry to responsible material and design decisions.

Course Features

Atomic Structure & Bonding Atomic models, isotopes, spectra, periodic trends, ionic and covalent bonding, molecular geometry, polarity, intermolecular forces, and structure–property relationships establish the foundations of chemical behavior.
Reactions & Quantitative Chemistry Chemical equations, the mole, stoichiometry, limiting reactants, yield, redox chemistry, solutions, gases, concentration, and dimensional analysis build rigorous quantitative fluency.
Energy, Rates & Equilibrium Kinetics, collision theory, catalysts, thermochemistry, calorimetry, equilibrium, acids and bases, electrochemistry, and nuclear processes connect energy and reaction behavior across systems.
Materials, Inquiry & Sustainable Design Laboratory evidence, model comparison, data analysis, uncertainty, materials properties, engineering criteria, sustainable design, cumulative assessment, and capstone work connect chemistry to real decisions.
