# Chemistry 11 — Assessment Framework

## Mastery
Essential outcomes use an 80% mastery target with correction/reassessment. Below-mastery work should preserve the same conceptual target and make the student's reasoning visible.

## Evidence domains
1. Conceptual/mechanistic chemistry
2. Quantitative reasoning and units
3. Investigation/data quality
4. CER, model limits, transfer, and source quality

## Weekly evidence
The five-day rhythm should culminate in evidence that includes model/representation, quantitative analysis, investigation or data interpretation, and a claim-evidence-reasoning or design-defense artifact.

## Quantitative expectations
Students must show units, dimensional reasoning, assumptions, significant-figure/precision choices where relevant, and distinguish calculated output from chemical interpretation.

## Laboratory evidence
Investigation credit requires a clear question, variables/controls where applicable, safety boundary, observations/data, uncertainty, and interpretation. Hazardous procedures are educator-supervised only; safe simulations/data alternatives are acceptable.

## Midterm and final
Week 18 MID and Week 36 FINAL are cumulative assessment/synthesis weeks. Public browser pages are formative/non-authoritative unless separately supervised and authenticated.

## Reassessment
Corrections must diagnose the original error, cite the governing chemistry principle or evidence, and demonstrate transfer to a changed context rather than memorizing an answer.

## Authority boundary
Public practice scores do not independently control transcript credit, credential issuance, or protected summative authority.
