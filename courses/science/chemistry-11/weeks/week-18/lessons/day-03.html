Lab & Evidence Portfolio Audit

Audit semester evidence for traceability, raw data, calculations, uncertainty, and revision history.

Assessment boundary

This week contains public formative review, evidence audit, and correction support only. Protected midterm questions, answer keys, grading logic, and authentication details remain outside the learner-facing repository.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Strong laboratory evidence preserves raw observations before graphs or derived quantities.

Each conclusion should be traceable through transformations, units, assumptions, and uncertainty.

Missing evidence should be repaired only from authentic records; never invent data to make a portfolio appear complete.

Vocabulary: portfolio audit; raw data; data lineage; uncertainty; revision.

Worked example

A graph with no raw table cannot be independently reproduced. A final calculation with no units or assumptions cannot be fully audited.

Guided question

Choose one lab from the semester and trace every step from raw evidence to conclusion.

Independent work

  1. Audit at least three lab/investigation records.
  2. Flag missing raw data, units, uncertainty, or reasoning.
  3. Create one repair plan based only on authentic records.

Misconception check

Portfolio cleanup must not rewrite history or fabricate observations.

Formative check

What makes evidence traceable?

Reveal response

Raw data, documented transformations, units, assumptions, and revision history.

Mentor evidence conference

Archaemenes may challenge reasoning and help plan corrections but cannot reveal protected answers or grant official mastery.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use an evidence checklist.

Core: Complete a three-record audit.

Extended: Trace uncertainty through one multistep calculation.

Week 18 portfolio-audit checkpoint

Purpose: Verify that the semester evidence record shows growth, correction, and scientific reasoning—not just task completion.

Retrieval: Review laboratory/data artifacts, CERs, calculations, model revisions, and major correction notes.

Evidence-quality task: For each artifact, identify raw evidence, processed evidence, claim, reasoning, uncertainty, and revision history.

Evidence revision: Replace weak artifacts that show only a final answer with ones that preserve the reasoning chain.

Transfer: Select one artifact that best demonstrates growth from an early misconception to a corrected model.

Boundary: Portfolio evidence supports learning review but does not independently authorize official midterm credit.

Portfolio-quality extension

Audit whether each selected artifact is trustworthy, complete, and traceable enough that another reviewer could reconstruct how the conclusion was reached.

Portfolio traceability extension

A strong portfolio artifact should preserve enough context that another reviewer can identify the question, raw evidence, processing steps, claim, correction history, and final reasoning. Screenshots or final numbers without that chain are weaker evidence.

Include at least one early-semester and one later-semester artifact so growth can be compared directly. Explain what changed in the student's reasoning, not just that a later score was higher.

Artifact-selection note

Prefer artifacts that preserve raw evidence, processing, revision, and final reasoning over polished outputs that hide how the conclusion was reached.

Portfolio-authenticity note

Keep original work and correction history together so growth is visible. A final polished artifact without its revision trail provides weaker evidence of learning.
