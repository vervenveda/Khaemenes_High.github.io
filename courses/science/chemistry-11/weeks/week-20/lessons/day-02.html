Temperature & Rate

Use particle-energy distributions to explain why temperature often changes reaction rate.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied datasets only unless a teacher provides an explicitly approved classroom-safe demonstration. Do not mix household chemicals or improvise reactive systems.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Higher temperature increases average kinetic energy and changes the fraction of particles above an activation-energy threshold.

Rate can increase strongly even when average kinetic energy rises modestly because the high-energy tail changes.

Temperature affects rate; it does not change stoichiometric coefficients.

Vocabulary: temperature; kinetic energy; activation threshold; energy distribution; rate ratio.

Worked example

A simulated reaction rate rises from 0.020 to 0.050 M/s when temperature increases under otherwise identical conditions; the rate ratio is 2.5.

Guided question

Calculate the rate ratio when rate changes from 0.012 to 0.036 M/s.

Independent work

  1. Compare three temperature-rate datasets.
  2. Calculate rate ratios.
  3. Explain the trend using the energy-distribution model.

Misconception check

Temperature does not simply 'make particles bigger' or change the balanced reaction.

Formative check

What key distribution change helps explain faster reaction at higher temperature?

Reveal response

A greater fraction of particles have energy at or above the activation threshold.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit controlled variables, particle-level reasoning, rate ratios, and model scope.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use energy-distribution sketches.

Core: Compare rate ratios and explanations.

Extended: Evaluate where the simple model needs Arrhenius-style refinement.
