Collision Theory

Explain reaction rate using collision frequency, orientation, and activation-energy requirements.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied datasets only unless a teacher provides an explicitly approved classroom-safe demonstration. Do not mix household chemicals or improvise reactive systems.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Collision theory proposes that particles must collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation for reaction to occur.

Not every collision is effective. The fraction of effective collisions depends on energy distribution and molecular geometry.

The model explains why changing conditions can alter rate without changing the balanced equation.

Vocabulary: collision theory; effective collision; orientation; activation energy; rate.

Worked example

Two particles may collide frequently but react slowly if most collisions do not exceed the activation-energy threshold.

Guided question

Why can a reaction remain slow even when collisions are frequent?

Independent work

  1. Classify collision sketches as likely effective/ineffective.
  2. Explain two reasons a collision may fail.
  3. Connect one rate observation to collision theory.

Misconception check

More collisions do not guarantee proportionally more successful reactions.

Formative check

What two broad conditions make a collision effective?

Reveal response

Sufficient energy and a suitable orientation.
