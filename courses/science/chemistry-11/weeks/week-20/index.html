Original investigation A6 · Collision theory and reaction rate

Inquiry: How can a simulated temperature increase alter the rate of successful particle collisions?

Fictional simulation: Under identical particle count and model rules, successful collisions per 1000 modeled steps: low-temperature runs 40, 42, 38; high-temperature runs 65, 68, 63.

  1. Identify temperature setting as independent variable, successful collision frequency as dependent variable, and particle number/model rules as controls.
  2. Calculate mean, range and the percent change in successful-collision frequency between conditions. Display means and replicate ranges on a labeled graph.
  3. Distinguish collision frequency, energy threshold and probability of a successful reaction; explain why not every collision produces products.
  4. Discuss simulation assumptions and test one rival explanation (for example a change in particle concentration) while maintaining the intended controls.

Worked-check: means 40.0 and 65.33 successes per 1000 steps, approximately 63.3% higher in the high-temperature model. Both sets are synthetic, not measured reaction kinetics.

Safety: No heating or mixing. Save table, calculations and CER in Thursday evidence.
