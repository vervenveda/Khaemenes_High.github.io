Particle-Distribution Investigation

Use simulation to test how accessible arrangements change with volume, mixing, or phase-like constraints.

Safety boundary

Use particle-distribution simulations and supplied data only. No new chemical mixing is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Preserve initial and final model conditions.

Count or compare accessible categories/configurations qualitatively.

Use repeated random trials where available to connect probability with multiplicity.

Vocabulary: simulation; multiplicity; probability; accessible state; entropy.

Worked example

If particles can occupy twice as many equivalent cells, the number of possible arrangements grows rapidly.

Guided question

What simulation change increases the number of accessible positional states?

Week 25 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Particle-distribution models can illustrate why some macrostates are overwhelmingly more probable than highly concentrated arrangements.

Retrieval: Connect microstate multiplicity to Day 1 entropy and Day 3 thermodynamic direction.

Quantitative transfer: Count or compare accessible arrangements in a simplified two-compartment model and relate multiplicity to probability.

Evidence-quality task: Keep the model scale explicit; a classroom token simulation illustrates statistics but is not a literal molecular trajectory.

Evidence revision: If a rare clustered state appears, do not call it impossible; interpret it as low probability.

Uncertainty: Small simulations fluctuate more strongly than macroscopic systems, where enormous particle numbers make dominant distributions overwhelming.

Distribution-investigation extension

Repeat the simulation several times, compare outcome frequencies, and explain how increasing the number of particles changes the stability of the most probable distribution.

Probability-model extension

For a small two-compartment model, enumerate possible arrangements and group them by macrostate. The middle distribution often has more microstates than extreme clustering. Repeating the model demonstrates that the most probable macrostate appears most often but not exclusively.

Then scale the reasoning conceptually: in a macroscopic sample, particle number is so large that overwhelmingly probable distributions dominate observed behavior.
