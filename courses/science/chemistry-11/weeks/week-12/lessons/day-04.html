Solution-Concentration Investigation

Use supplied preparation or simulation data to compare target and actual concentrations.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Explicit science instruction

This investigation uses supplied mass, molar-mass, and final-volume data. Students calculate expected concentration, compare with a reference or simulated measurement, and explain discrepancies.

Preserve raw quantities before calculation. Report units and significant figures consistent with the supplied measurements.

A difference between target and measured concentration may reflect volume reading, transfer loss, incomplete dissolving, calibration, or model assumptions.

Vocabulary: target concentration; measured concentration; percent difference; calibration; uncertainty.

Worked example with reasoning

A target solution is 0.500 M while a simulated measurement gives 0.485 M. Percent difference relative to target is |0.485−0.500|/0.500×100=3.0%.

Guided question

Which raw measurement would most strongly affect calculated molarity if it has the largest relative uncertainty?

Independent work

  1. Calculate target molarity from supplied preparation data.
  2. Compare with a simulated measured concentration.
  3. Calculate percent difference.
  4. Identify one likely uncertainty source and one procedural revision.

Investigation / Data Lab

Preserve raw preparation data, calculations, target/measured comparison, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Agreement with a target does not prove every procedural step was perfect; uncertainty still needs to be documented.

Formative understanding check

Why preserve both target and measured concentration?

Reveal response

Their difference is evidence about method performance and uncertainty.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to check unit conversions, model choice, and whether the calculation matches the particle story.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a calculation template with units.

Core: Complete target-versus-measured analysis and one revision.

Extended: Estimate which measurement contributes most to concentration uncertainty.
