Molarity and Quantitative Concentration

Calculate and interpret molarity as moles of solute per litre of solution.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Explicit science instruction

Molarity is defined as M=n/V, where n is moles of solute and V is total solution volume in litres.

Unit discipline matters: millilitres must be converted to litres before substitution. The denominator is solution volume, not solvent volume.

A concentration value is intensive: doubling both solute amount and final solution volume leaves molarity unchanged.

Vocabulary: molarity; mole; litre; concentration; intensive property.

Worked example with reasoning

A solution contains 0.50 mol solute in 2.00 L solution. M=0.50/2.00=0.250 mol/L.

Guided question

How many moles are present in 125 mL of a 0.40 M solution? Show the mL→L conversion.

Independent work

  1. Solve four molarity problems for M, n, or V.
  2. Perform a unit audit on each.
  3. Explain why 1 mol in 1 L and 2 mol in 2 L have the same molarity.

Misconception check

Using millilitres directly in M=n/V gives a result off by a factor of 1000.

Formative understanding check

What volume must appear in the molarity denominator?

Reveal response

The final total solution volume expressed in litres.
