Reaction Quotient & Direction Concept

Compare a current composition with equilibrium using a reaction-quotient concept.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied concentration data only; no unsupervised reactive mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A reaction quotient Q has the same algebraic form as K but uses current, not necessarily equilibrium, values.

If QK, it tends to proceed in reverse.

This predicts direction of change, not speed.

Vocabulary: reaction quotient; Q; direction; composition; equilibrium.

Worked example

If K=10 and current Q=2, the system is product-poor relative to equilibrium and tends forward.

Guided question

What direction is predicted if Q is larger than K?

Independent work

  1. Compare Q and K for four cases.
  2. Predict direction.
  3. State why no rate is implied.

Misconception check

Q versus K predicts direction, not how quickly equilibrium is reached.

Formative check

What does Q=K indicate?

Reveal response

The composition is at equilibrium under the model.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit equilibrium assumptions, graph interpretation, and whether the evidence supports the conclusion.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use inequality cases.

Core: Predict direction.

Week 26 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: The reaction quotient Q uses the equilibrium-expression form with current conditions to predict the direction of net change.

Retrieval: Reuse Day 2 K expressions and Week 25 thermodynamic direction.

Quantitative reasoning: Calculate or compare Q and K: Q<K predicts net forward change, Q>K predicts net reverse change, and Q=K indicates equilibrium.

Evidence-quality task: Treat Q versus K as a direction test, not a rate prediction.

Evidence revision: Correct any claim that a catalyst changes Q, K, or equilibrium composition; it changes how quickly equilibrium is approached.

Model limit: The direction rule assumes the same reaction definition and temperature used for K.

Q-versus-K transfer extension

Evaluate three starting mixtures with different Q values and predict the direction of net change for each before any detailed ICE-table calculation.

Worked Q-versus-K extension

If K=10 and the current mixture gives Q=2, the system is reactant-heavy relative to equilibrium and net forward reaction is predicted until Q approaches K. If Q=40, net reverse reaction is predicted. If Q=10, the system is at equilibrium under those conditions.

Do not interpret the magnitude of Q−K as a direct reaction-rate value. Direction and speed are separate questions.

Direction-with-Q extension

Use Q as a snapshot of the current composition and K as the equilibrium benchmark. The comparison predicts the direction of net change needed to approach equilibrium, but it does not describe how far composition will shift without additional calculation. Keep this distinction clear before moving into ICE-table work or detailed equilibrium solving.

Q interpretation check

After comparing Q and K, state only the net reaction direction. Do not infer the final equilibrium concentrations or reaction speed unless additional information is available.
