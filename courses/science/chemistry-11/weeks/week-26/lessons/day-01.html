Dynamic Equilibrium

Explain how forward and reverse reactions can continue while macroscopic concentrations remain stable.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied concentration data only; no unsupervised reactive mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At dynamic equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

Equal rates do not mean reactant and product concentrations are equal; they become constant at values set by the system and temperature.

Equilibrium requires a reversible process in a closed-enough system for both directions to matter.

Vocabulary: dynamic equilibrium; forward rate; reverse rate; concentration; reversible reaction.

Worked example

If forward and reverse rates both become 0.20 mol/L·s, concentrations can remain constant even though molecules continue reacting.

Guided question

Why can concentration stay constant while particles continue to react?

Independent work

  1. Interpret a rate-versus-time graph.
  2. Distinguish equal rate from equal concentration.
  3. State one system assumption.

Misconception check

Equilibrium is not a stopped reaction.

Formative check

What equality defines dynamic equilibrium?

Reveal response

Forward rate equals reverse rate.
