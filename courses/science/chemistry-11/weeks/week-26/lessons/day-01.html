Dynamic Equilibrium

Explain how forward and reverse reactions can continue while macroscopic concentrations remain stable.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied concentration data only; no unsupervised reactive mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At dynamic equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

Equal rates do not mean reactant and product concentrations are equal; they become constant at values set by the system and temperature.

Equilibrium requires a reversible process in a closed-enough system for both directions to matter.

Vocabulary: dynamic equilibrium; forward rate; reverse rate; concentration; reversible reaction.

Worked example

If forward and reverse rates both become 0.20 mol/L·s, concentrations can remain constant even though molecules continue reacting.

Guided question

Why can concentration stay constant while particles continue to react?

Misconception check

Equilibrium is not a stopped reaction.

Formative check

What equality defines dynamic equilibrium?

Reveal response

Forward rate equals reverse rate.

Dynamic-equilibrium transfer extension

Sketch forward and reverse rates approaching equality and explain why concentrations can remain constant even though both reaction directions continue.

Dynamic-rate extension

Imagine a reversible reaction beginning with mostly reactants. The forward rate starts high while the reverse rate is initially low. As products accumulate, the reverse rate rises and the forward rate may fall until the two rates become equal. At that point, concentrations remain constant even though molecular conversion continues in both directions.

Equilibrium is therefore a rate balance, not a static molecular standstill.

Equilibrium-graph extension

On a concentration-time graph, equilibrium is indicated by concentrations becoming constant, not necessarily equal. On a rate-time graph, equilibrium is indicated by forward and reverse rates becoming equal. Using both representations together helps prevent the common mistake of equating flat concentration lines with stopped molecular activity.

Rate-balance check

At equilibrium, forward and reverse rates are equal at that moment, while their common value can change if temperature or a catalyst changes.

Final equilibrium note

Constant concentrations at equilibrium are maintained by equal opposing rates, not by cessation of reaction.

Rate-composition distinction extension

Use paired rate and concentration graphs to separate two different equilibrium signatures. Concentrations become constant because the opposing rates are equal, not because both rates become zero. After a catalyst is added at constant temperature, both forward and reverse rates can increase while the equilibrium concentrations remain unchanged. This comparison makes clear that kinetics controls how fast equilibrium is reached, while the equilibrium composition is governed by thermodynamic conditions.
