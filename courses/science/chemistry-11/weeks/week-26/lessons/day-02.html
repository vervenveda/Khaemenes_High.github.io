Equilibrium Expressions & K

Write and interpret simple equilibrium-constant expressions.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use simulations and supplied concentration data only; no unsupervised reactive mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

For aA+bB⇌cC+dD, an equilibrium expression compares product and reactant concentrations raised to stoichiometric powers in the simplified model.

Pure solids and liquids are commonly omitted from concentration-based K expressions.

K magnitude indicates product- or reactant-favored composition, not reaction speed.

Vocabulary: equilibrium constant; K; expression; stoichiometric exponent; composition.

Worked example

For A⇌2B, Kc=[B]²/[A].

Guided question

Write Kc for 2A+B⇌3C and explain each exponent.

Independent work

  1. Write four K expressions.
  2. Interpret large/small K qualitatively.
  3. Identify species omitted in simple heterogeneous cases.

Misconception check

Large K does not mean the reaction is fast.

Formative check

What does K mainly describe?

Reveal response

Equilibrium composition tendency at a given temperature.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit equilibrium assumptions, graph interpretation, and whether the evidence supports the conclusion.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use expression templates.

Core: Write and interpret K.

Extended: Compare K with kinetic information.

Week 26 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: The equilibrium constant summarizes the relative activities or effective concentrations of products and reactants at equilibrium.

Retrieval: Use Day 1 dynamic equilibrium and balanced-equation stoichiometry.

Quantitative reasoning: Construct K expressions using stoichiometric exponents and omit pure solids and liquids in the simplified concentration expression.

Evidence-quality task: Interpret large K as product-favored and small K as reactant-favored without claiming complete conversion.

Evidence revision: Do not insert coefficients as multipliers; they become exponents in the equilibrium expression.

Model limit: Concentration-based K expressions approximate activity behavior and depend on temperature.

Equilibrium-expression transfer extension

Write K expressions for homogeneous and heterogeneous reactions and explain why pure solids or liquids are excluded from the simplified form.
