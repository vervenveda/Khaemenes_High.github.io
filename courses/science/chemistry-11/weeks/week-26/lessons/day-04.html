Misconception check

A flat concentration graph alone does not reveal whether a reversible microscopic process continues; rate evidence strengthens the claim.

Formative check

Constant concentrations and equal forward/reverse rates.

Week 26 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Equilibrium simulations reveal how composition responds to a disturbance while K remains fixed at constant temperature.

Retrieval: Use dynamic equilibrium and Q-versus-K direction reasoning from Days 1–3.

Quantitative transfer: Record pre-disturbance concentrations, apply a concentration or pressure change, calculate how Q changes qualitatively or quantitatively, and predict the net shift.

Evidence-quality task: Change one condition at a time and distinguish instantaneous concentration changes from subsequent reaction adjustment.

Evidence revision: Do not explain every shift with vague “stress relief”; use Q-versus-K reasoning where possible.

Uncertainty: Simulation behavior reflects model assumptions and may simplify nonideal systems.

Equilibrium-investigation extension

Compare a concentration disturbance and a catalyst addition. Explain why one changes Q and drives net reaction while the other changes rates without changing equilibrium composition.

Disturbance-analysis extension

Suppose product concentration is suddenly increased. Immediately after the disturbance, Q increases because the numerator changes before any reaction response occurs. If Q becomes greater than K, the system undergoes net reverse reaction until Q returns to K.

A catalyst behaves differently: it speeds both forward and reverse processes without changing K or causing an instantaneous composition change.

Pressure-disturbance extension

For gas-phase equilibria, a volume change alters partial pressures immediately and therefore changes Q. Predict the direction by comparing how the pressure change affects numerator and denominator terms, especially when the two sides contain different total moles of gas. Avoid using a memorized “shift to fewer moles” rule when Q reasoning is available.

Instant-versus-response check

Separate the immediate composition change caused by the disturbance from the later chemical shift that restores Q to K.

Immediate-change versus response extension

For any perturbation, separate the instant of disturbance from the later chemical response. A concentration or volume change alters current concentrations or partial pressures immediately, so Q changes before the system reacts. Only afterward do forward and reverse rates become unequal and drive composition toward the condition Q=K. A catalyst is different: it changes the rates directly but does not create an instantaneous Q shift or change K at fixed temperature.
