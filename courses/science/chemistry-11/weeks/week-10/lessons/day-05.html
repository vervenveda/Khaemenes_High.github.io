Intermolecular-Forces Synthesis

Defend a boiling or evaporation prediction using molecular structure, multiple IMF types, and data.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallPrevious

Explicit science instruction

A complete IMF explanation starts with molecular structure, identifies all relevant interactions, compares size and shape, and then predicts a measurable property.

Use matched data when available and state confidence. Strong reasoning avoids single-label shortcuts such as 'hydrogen bond means highest boiling point.'

Structure–property claims are comparative: the same interaction can matter differently depending on molecular size, number of sites, and geometry.

Vocabulary: structure–property; interaction hierarchy; matched evidence; prediction; limitation.

Worked example with reasoning

A small alcohol and an ether of similar molar mass differ in donor ability. The alcohol’s additional hydrogen-bond network can raise its boiling point relative to the ether, while both still experience dispersion.

Guided question

Write a CER predicting which of two supplied molecules should evaporate more slowly and explain at least two structural factors.

Misconception check

IMF names are tools for explaining patterns, not a guaranteed property ranking by themselves.

Formative understanding check

What makes an IMF prediction stronger than simply naming the strongest interaction?

Reveal response

It integrates all relevant interactions, size/shape, and matched evidence.
