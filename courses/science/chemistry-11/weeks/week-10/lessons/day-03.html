IMFs, Boiling, and Evaporation Data

Use matched property data to connect intermolecular attraction with boiling and evaporation trends.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Boiling requires molecules to enter the gas phase, so stronger intermolecular attractions generally require more energy to overcome under comparable conditions.

Evaporation rate depends on temperature, surface conditions, vapor pressure, and intermolecular attraction. A matched comparison controls as many of those factors as possible.

Correlation with boiling point is strongest when size and molecular structure are considered alongside IMF type.

Vocabulary: boiling point; vapor pressure; evaporation; matched comparison; confounder.

Worked example with reasoning

Two similar-size liquids have boiling points 65°C and 98°C. If the higher-boiling liquid can hydrogen-bond while the other cannot, the interaction model offers a plausible explanation, while still requiring attention to structural differences.

Guided question

Why is comparing water with a very large hydrocarbon weaker evidence for an IMF effect than comparing structurally similar molecules?

Independent work

  1. Graph a supplied boiling-point series.
  2. Annotate likely dominant interaction patterns.
  3. Identify one outlier and propose a structural explanation.
  4. State one confounding variable in an evaporation comparison.

Misconception check

Higher boiling point does not automatically prove one specific IMF without structural evidence.

Formative understanding check

What does a strong structure–property argument require besides a property difference?

Reveal response

A relevant structural comparison and reasoning that connects structure to intermolecular attraction.
