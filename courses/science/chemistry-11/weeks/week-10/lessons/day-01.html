Dispersion Forces and Polarizability

Explain why all atoms and molecules experience dispersion forces and how polarizability affects their strength.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallNext

Explicit science instruction

Electron density fluctuates continuously. Temporary uneven distributions can induce neighboring dipoles, producing London dispersion attraction even in nonpolar substances.

Dispersion generally grows as electron clouds become larger and more polarizable. Shape also matters because extended contact can increase interaction opportunities.

Dispersion is present in polar molecules too; stronger named interactions are added to it rather than replacing it.

Vocabulary: dispersion force; instantaneous dipole; induced dipole; polarizability; contact area.

Worked example with reasoning

Within a homologous series, larger molecules often boil at higher temperatures because their larger electron clouds are more polarizable and support stronger dispersion attraction.

Guided question

Why can two nonpolar molecules have very different boiling points? Give two structural factors that can matter.

Independent work

  1. Rank three nonpolar molecules by expected dispersion strength using size data.
  2. Explain how shape can affect intermolecular contact.
  3. State one reason molecular mass alone is an incomplete predictor.

Misconception check

Nonpolar does not mean 'no intermolecular attraction.'

Formative understanding check

Do noble-gas atoms experience intermolecular attraction?

Reveal response

Yes. Temporary dipoles produce dispersion forces even in monatomic species.
