Pressure, Particle Motion, and Kinetic Molecular Theory

Explain gas pressure and temperature using a particle-motion model.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Explicit science instruction

Gas pressure results from particle collisions with container walls. In the kinetic molecular model, particles are in constant random motion and intermolecular attractions are neglected for an idealized gas.

Temperature in kelvin is related to average translational kinetic energy. Raising temperature increases average particle speed, changing collision frequency and/or force depending on the constraint.

Ideal-gas models are approximations. Real gases can deviate at high pressure or low temperature when particle volume and attractions matter more.

Vocabulary: pressure; temperature; kinetic molecular theory; collision; ideal gas.

Worked example with reasoning

A sealed rigid container is warmed. Volume is fixed, so faster average particle motion leads to more frequent and more forceful wall collisions; pressure rises.

Guided question

Compare what changes microscopically when a gas is heated at constant volume versus heated at constant pressure.

Independent work

  1. Draw particle models for low- and high-temperature gas samples.
  2. Explain pressure without saying particles 'contain pressure.'
  3. Identify one condition where ideal-gas behavior becomes less accurate.

Misconception check

Temperature is not the amount of heat stored in a sample; it is related to average particle kinetic energy.

Formative understanding check

What creates gas pressure in the particle model?

Reveal response

Collisions of moving gas particles with container walls create the macroscopic pressure signal.
