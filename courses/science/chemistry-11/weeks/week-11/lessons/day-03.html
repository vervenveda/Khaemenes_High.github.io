Charles, Avogadro, and the Ideal Gas Equation

Connect volume with absolute temperature and amount of gas, then combine relationships in PV=nRT.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Explicit science instruction

At constant pressure and amount, ideal-gas volume is proportional to absolute temperature: V/T is constant. Temperature must be in kelvin.

At constant pressure and temperature, gas volume is proportional to amount n. These relationships combine with Boyle’s law in PV=nRT.

The gas constant R carries units that must match pressure and volume units. Dimensional consistency is part of the calculation, not an afterthought.

Vocabulary: Charles’s law; Avogadro’s law; ideal gas law; kelvin; gas constant.

Worked example with reasoning

At constant pressure, a gas at 300 K occupies 2.0 L. Heating to 450 K predicts V₂=2.0×450/300=3.0 L.

Guided question

Why is using 30°C directly in V₁/T₁=V₂/T₂ incorrect? Convert it and explain the physical reason for an absolute scale.

Independent work

  1. Solve two temperature–volume problems using kelvin.
  2. Solve one PV=nRT problem with supplied R and compatible units.
  3. Perform a unit audit for each calculation.

Misconception check

Celsius is useful for everyday temperature differences but cannot replace kelvin in direct gas-law proportionality.

Formative understanding check

Why must absolute temperature be used in gas-law ratios?

Reveal response

The proportional model is referenced to an absolute zero of thermal motion; Celsius has an arbitrary zero offset.
