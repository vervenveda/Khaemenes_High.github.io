The Missing Mass Mystery

Learning objective: Define the measured system and distinguish disappearing mass from matter transferred elsewhere.

45–60 minutes · Free · Offline capable · Public formative practice

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Reading and understanding

When a scale reading falls

Imagine an invented experiment in which a container and its contents start at 120.0 g, and the same container and remaining contents later read 116.4 g. It is tempting to announce that matter disappeared. That would not yet be justified. A balance reports the material still resting on it. What if some material left as gas? The 3.6 g difference might still be present in the surrounding room. The first question must be: what was included in the measurement?

Signs and their limits

Chemists seek evidence of new substances using observations such as temperature change, formation of a precipitate, or gas production. But bubbling can also occur when water boils or dissolved gas comes out of a liquid. A color change can sometimes arise from physical conditions. Observations help us form hypotheses; they seldom identify specific products by themselves. Design a controlled test instead of guessing the gas identity from bubbles.

Atom conservation

Ordinary chemical reactions rearrange atoms to make new substances. Atoms are not casually created or destroyed. A closed system therefore retains its overall matter and mass when the reaction is accounted for completely. In an open system, some matter can leave the system being weighed. It is crucial to distinguish the mass of a measured subsection from the mass of everything involved, even if it is not all on the balance.

A safe way to test

We will use a digital accounting model and fabricated numbers instead of creating gas under pressure. Never cap a gas-producing reaction: pressure can build dangerously. A useful prediction might be that the same modeled reaction shows a lower balance reading in an open setup but an unchanged overall reading within a closed accounting boundary. The computation illustrates matter transfer, not the performance of a real experiment.

Lab safety: These activities use data and symbolic chemistry only. Never seal a real gas-producing reaction vessel or mix unknown chemicals.

Vocabulary in context

reactant
A substance present at the start of a chemical reaction.
product
A substance made during a chemical reaction.
system boundary
The defined region whose matter or mass is counted.
open system
A region through whose boundary matter may cross.
closed system
A region with no matter crossing its defined boundary.

Worked example

Synthetic open case: 120.0 g initially; 116.4 g left on balance; 3.6 g gas counted outside. Total 116.4 + 3.6 = 120.0 g. State the accounting boundary for each number.
