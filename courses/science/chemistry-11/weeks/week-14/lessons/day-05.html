Grade 11 · Week 14 · Friday · Day 05

Defend the Conservation Claim

Learning objective: Use an atom ledger, mass balance, and evidence-based reasoning to defend a qualified chemical explanation.

45–60 minutes · Free · Offline capable · Public formative practice

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Reading and understanding

State exactly what you learned

A good claim addresses one scientifically meaningful question. Instead of saying only 'mass is always conserved,' state that the fictional open model's apparent reduction in measured mass is explained by a transfer of gas across the chosen weighing boundary. Make the distinction between what the balance reads and what exists everywhere in the full accounting system. Do not label simulated output as real measured gas.

Two independent forms of reasoning

The balanced equation 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O illustrates that hydrogen and oxygen atoms are conserved in a symbolic reaction model: four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms on each side. The separate synthetic balance model starts at 120.0 g and ends with 116.4 g inside the measured region and 3.6 g elsewhere. These are not numbers computed directly from the chemical equation; they are a distinct model showing matter transfer.

A limitation makes the work stronger

The model simplifies away instrument noise, other mass transfers, dissolved gas, and variations among real apparatuses. It cannot reveal what particular gas formed, whether a specific reaction actually occurred, or whether all products were captured. A better study would explicitly define what is weighed, compare controls, repeat measurements and use teacher-approved identification methods. Good reporting identifies these possibilities even when arithmetic is correct.

Practice and correct

The open-book Friday check selects ten items from a twenty-item bank and shuffles their answer orders. Correct answers receive explanations; incorrect answers point back to ideas to review. Aim for 80% in this practice, but remember public code and local browser storage do not constitute secure, supervised examinations. Formal marks require a separately authenticated educator process, and written evidence should still be reviewed by a human teacher.

Lab safety: These activities use data and symbolic chemistry only. Never seal a real gas-producing reaction vessel or mix unknown chemicals.

Vocabulary in context

claim
A concise, answerable scientific statement.
evidence
A set of relevant observations or measurements supporting a claim.
reasoning
An explanation connecting evidence with scientific principles.
counterclaim
An alternative explanation worth examining.
limitation
A reason why evidence cannot justify every possible inference.

Worked example

Claim: mass inside an open weighing boundary may decrease when gas leaves. Evidence: 120.0 g − 3.6 g = 116.4 g inside; 116.4 + 3.6 = 120.0 g total, plus a separate atom count of 4 H and 2 O on both sides of 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Limitation: these are fictional cases.
