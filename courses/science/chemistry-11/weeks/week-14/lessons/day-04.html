Where Did the Gas Go?

Learning objective: Model mass conservation in open and closed accounting systems while documenting uncertainty.

45–60 minutes · Free · Offline capable · Public formative practice

MondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFriday

Reading and understanding

What is the boundary today?

Suppose a fictional gas-producing process begins with an apparatus and contents totaling 120.0 g. You may choose an open accounting boundary, where the modeled gas portion leaves the weighed region, or a closed accounting boundary, where the gas remains inside the system. If you select 3.6 g of gas formed, the open scale reading is represented as 116.4 g inside and 3.6 g outside. The complete mass account is unchanged.

Why a closed accounting model is not a real sealed jar

Gas produced in a real reaction can generate pressure in a tightly closed rigid container. Students must never seal, cap or improvise a gas-producing setup. The digital model sidesteps chemical and pressure hazards entirely. The program does not produce actual gases or measure a reaction. Its output is arithmetic under declared assumptions, not laboratory evidence. Supplied CSV cases permit the same learning without operating the simulator.

Make a fair comparison

Keep the starting mass and hypothetical gas amount the same when comparing the two boundaries. Change only the boundary setting. Record the mass counted inside the weighed region and the gas amount counted outside; add them to verify 120.0 g in each trial. Then select another gas amount while recording what changed. Doing this in matched pairs helps explain a cause without confusing boundary choice with reaction quantity.

Think like a measurement scientist

Real balances have limited resolution; uncertainty can make tiny differences hard to distinguish. Liquid splashes, evaporation, gas remaining dissolved, instrument drift or weighing an inconsistent apparatus could also change an experimental reading. The fictional model assumes none of those complications. A scientifically careful conclusion compares model values, states assumptions and proposes an actual measurement design that would be safer and more convincing if approved by a trained educator.

Lab safety: These activities use data and symbolic chemistry only. Never seal a real gas-producing reaction vessel or mix unknown chemicals.

Vocabulary in context

mass balance
Accounting of mass within, entering and leaving a defined system.
gas escape
Transfer of gaseous matter to outside the region being measured.
measured mass
Mass registered for the material included on a balance.
controlled variable
A condition kept the same during a comparison.
uncertainty
A limit to the precision or interpretation of a measurement.

Worked example

Open model: 120.0 − 3.6 = 116.4 g inside; add 3.6 g counted outside to recover 120.0 g. Closed model with the same 3.6 g generated: 120.0 g inside; gas outside = 0.0 g.
