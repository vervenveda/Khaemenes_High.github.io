Chemical Reactions & Conservation

Teaching notes, misconceptions, reference answers, differentiated learning and evidence criteria

Learning progression

For HS-PS1-2 and HS-PS1-7, build student models and reasoning about atom conservation. These lessons support—not alone complete—the performance expectations. Prerequisites: element symbols, chemical formulas and basic mass subtraction/addition.

Suggested daily pacing: 8 minutes phenomenon/prediction; 17 minutes reading/model; 15 minutes interactive or dataset inquiry; 10 minutes student notebook and rubric; 5 minutes retrieval and feedback.

No real chemistry is required. In particular, do not seal gas-producing reactions in containers: pressure can build. No household cleaner mixtures, gas production, ingestion or dangerous chemical handling.

Monday · The Missing Mass Mystery

Target: Define the measured system and distinguish disappearing mass from matter transferred elsewhere.

Teacher prompt: What observation would help distinguish gas escape from spilling or measurement error?

Class evidence: Record your prediction for an open versus closed boundary, define which matter is counted, and propose a different possible cause of apparent mass loss.

Tuesday · Language of Chemical Equations

Target: Interpret coefficients, subscripts and physical state symbols without changing chemical identities.

Teacher prompt: Why must we never change a formula subscript to balance an equation?

Class evidence: Explain how the coefficient and subscript differ using 2H₂O and H₂O₂. Define all common state symbols and write a short explanation of why equation balancing alone is not proof of a reaction.

Wednesday · Become an Atom Accountant

Target: Balance equations in simplest whole-number form and audit every element before accepting an answer.

Teacher prompt: Why does a chemical equation need equal atom totals but not equal numbers of molecules?

Class evidence: Solve at least seven unique equations in the interactive Balance Arena. Record atom ledgers for two and explain a corrected mistake.

Thursday · Where Did the Gas Go?

Target: Model mass conservation in open and closed accounting systems while documenting uncertainty.

Teacher prompt: Which parts of the model are measured, computed or assumed?

Friday · Defend the Conservation Claim

Target: Use an atom ledger, mass balance, and evidence-based reasoning to defend a qualified chemical explanation.

Teacher prompt: What evidence would you need to identify an actual gas rather than assume its identity?

Class evidence: Write a complete CER with two numbers and units, one balanced atom ledger, a possible competing explanation and a proposed safe next investigation. Complete the ten-question check and export your notebook.

Worked responses and reference values

Fictional open mass example: 120.0 g initial, 3.6 g modeled gas escapes measured region, measured after = 116.4 g, complete accounted total 116.4 + 3.6 = 120.0 g. Closed accounting retains 120.0 g inside. This is an assumed model, NOT a real biological or chemical measurement.

Balanced equations for guided instruction: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃; 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃; CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O; CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. Coefficients conserve every element; they are not gram quantities.

CER assessment rubric (20 points)

Claim: 4
Precise answer and system boundary.
Numeric evidence: 4
Two values with units, all mass accounted.
Atom ledger: 4
Balanced formula with smallest integers and atom totals.
Reasoning: 4
Explains rearrangement, gas transfer and distinct measurements.
Limitation: 4
Identifies model assumptions and proposes a better study.

16/20 is 80%. Review constructed responses before recording educator marks.

Misconceptions and extension

  • “Mass disappeared”: establish what is on the balance.
  • “A bubble identifies the gas”: insist on product identification evidence.
  • “Changing subscripts balances”: explain it changes the substance.
  • “Molecules must match”: atom totals match, molecule counts need not.
  • “The game proves a real reaction”: separate symbolic balancing from experiment.

Foundation: prefill an atom ledger; Core: solve seven puzzles and paired mass trials; Extension: compare ±0.2 g measurement uncertainty with a hypothetical 0.1 g difference.
