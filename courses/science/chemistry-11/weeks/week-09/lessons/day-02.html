Network Solids, Molecular Solids, and Ionic Solids

Compare how different extended structures produce different material properties.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Solids can be organized as ionic lattices, metallic structures, network covalent frameworks, or collections of discrete molecules. The relevant structural unit changes the property model.

Network covalent solids contain extensive covalent connectivity, so disrupting the solid can require breaking strong bonds. Molecular solids are held together between molecules by intermolecular forces.

Classifying a material requires converging evidence because real substances may be disordered, mixed, layered, or otherwise more complex than ideal categories.

Vocabulary: network covalent; molecular solid; ionic solid; structure; bonding.

Worked example with reasoning

Diamond and graphite are both carbon-based, but their bonding networks differ. Their different structures help explain why their hardness and electrical behavior differ strongly despite identical elemental composition.

Guided question

Why is elemental composition alone insufficient to predict material properties?

Independent work

  1. Create a comparison table for ionic, metallic, network, and molecular solids.
  2. Use supplied property data to classify four anonymous materials.
  3. Explain one ambiguous case.

Misconception check

Same composition does not guarantee same properties; structure and bonding arrangement matter.

Formative understanding check

What additional information is needed beyond chemical formula to predict many solid-state properties?

Reveal response

Particle-scale structure and bonding arrangement are essential.
