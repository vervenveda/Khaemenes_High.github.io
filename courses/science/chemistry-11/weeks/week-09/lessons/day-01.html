Metallic Bonding & Delocalized Electrons

Explain how a delocalized-electron model accounts for conductivity, malleability, and metallic bonding.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallNext

Explicit science instruction

Metallic solids are modeled as positive ion cores surrounded by mobile, delocalized valence electrons. The attraction between the cores and shared electron cloud holds the solid together.

Because electrons are mobile through the structure, many metals conduct electricity in the solid state. Because bonding is not limited to one rigid directional pair, layers of atoms can shift without immediately destroying all bonding.

The model is qualitative: conductivity and strength also depend on composition, defects, temperature, and crystal structure.

Vocabulary: metallic bonding; delocalized electron; conductivity; malleability; lattice.

Worked example with reasoning

Copper conducts as a solid because mobile electrons can respond to an electric field. An ionic crystal can contain charged particles yet fail to conduct in the solid state because its ions are not free to move.

Guided question

Why does the metallic model explain both conductivity and malleability better than a model of isolated atom pairs?

Independent work

  1. Compare metallic and ionic particle models.
  2. Explain solid-state conductivity in each.
  3. Draw how a metal layer can shift while delocalized bonding persists.

Misconception check

Metals do not conduct because whole atoms flow through the wire; charge transport is associated with mobile electrons.

Formative understanding check

Why can a solid metal conduct while a solid ionic crystal often cannot?

Reveal response

The metallic model contains mobile electrons, while ions in an ionic solid are fixed in lattice positions.
