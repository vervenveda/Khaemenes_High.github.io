Common-Ion & Coupled Equilibrium

Explain how adding a common ion can shift a dissolution equilibrium.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use solubility curves, supplied datasets, and simulations only. Do not perform unsupervised precipitation chemistry or mix household chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Adding an ion already present in a dissolution equilibrium changes Qsp immediately.

The system can respond by favoring the solid phase, reducing additional dissolution in the simplified common-ion model.

This is an application of equilibrium response, not a separate law.

Vocabulary: common ion; coupled equilibrium; Qsp; shift; solubility.

Worked example

Adding X⁻ to MX(s)⇌M⁺+X⁻ raises Qsp and can favor formation of more solid MX.

Guided question

Why can adding a soluble salt containing X⁻ reduce the solubility of MX?

Independent work

  1. Predict three common-ion effects.
  2. Connect each to Qsp/Ksp.
  3. Identify one case with no common ion.

Misconception check

The common-ion effect changes equilibrium composition; it does not change Ksp at constant temperature.

Formative check

What stays constant during a common-ion perturbation at fixed temperature?

Reveal response

Ksp.
