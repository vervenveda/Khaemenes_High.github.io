Ksp Concept & Ion Products

Interpret a simplified solubility-product expression and compare ion-product reasoning qualitatively.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use solubility curves, supplied datasets, and simulations only. Do not perform unsupervised precipitation chemistry or mix household chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

For a sparingly soluble ionic solid, Ksp describes the equilibrium relationship among dissolved ion concentrations.

A current ion product Qsp can be compared with Ksp: QspKsp suggests precipitation is favored in the simplified model.

Stoichiometric exponents come from the dissolution equation.

Vocabulary: Ksp; ion product; dissolution equilibrium; precipitation; stoichiometry.

Worked example

For MX(s)⇌M⁺+X⁻, Ksp=[M⁺][X⁻]. For MX₂, the X concentration term is squared.

Guided question

Write a Ksp expression for M(OH)₂(s)⇌M²⁺+2OH⁻.

Independent work

  1. Write four Ksp expressions.
  2. Compare Qsp and Ksp qualitatively.
  3. Predict dissolve/precipitate direction.

Misconception check

A small Ksp does not mean zero solubility.

Formative check

What does Qsp>Ksp suggest?

Reveal response

The dissolved-ion product exceeds equilibrium and precipitation is favored.

Week 28 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Ksp describes the equilibrium ion activity relationship for a sparingly soluble ionic solid.

Retrieval: Reuse Week 26 equilibrium-expression rules and dissolution stoichiometry.

Quantitative reasoning: Write Ksp expressions with stoichiometric exponents and calculate ion concentrations from molar solubility when appropriate.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish Ksp from solubility; different stoichiometries can make direct ranking misleading.

Evidence revision: Do not include the pure solid in the Ksp expression.

Model limit: Concentration-based Ksp work approximates activities and can deviate in concentrated ionic solutions.

Ksp transfer extension

For AB(s)⇌A⁺+B⁻ and A₂B(s)⇌2A⁺+B²⁻, derive Ksp in terms of molar solubility s and compare how stoichiometry changes the relationship.

Worked Ksp extension

For AgCl(s)⇌Ag⁺+Cl⁻, let molar solubility be s in pure water. Then [Ag⁺]=s and [Cl⁻]=s, so Ksp=s². If Ksp=1.8×10⁻¹⁰, s≈√(1.8×10⁻¹⁰)≈1.3×10⁻⁵ M.

For CaF2(s)⇌Ca²⁺+2F⁻, [Ca²⁺]=s and [F⁻]=2s, so Ksp=s(2s)²=4s³. This is why two salts with similar Ksp values can have different molar solubilities.

Always derive the concentration relationships from dissolution stoichiometry before solving for s.

Ion-product and Ksp extension

Use the same algebraic form for Qsp as for Ksp, but substitute the current ion concentrations rather than equilibrium concentrations. If Qsp<Ksp, the solution is unsaturated with respect to that solid; if Qsp=Ksp, it is at saturation equilibrium; if Qsp>Ksp, precipitation is thermodynamically favored.

For salts with unequal stoichiometry, dilution changes ion concentrations differently from changing Ksp. Always calculate the post-mixing or post-dilution ion concentrations first.
