Safety boundary

Use solubility curves, supplied datasets, and simulations only. Do not perform unsupervised precipitation chemistry or mix household chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Solubility equilibrium combines macroscopic limits with microscopic dissolution/precipitation balance.

Ksp is a temperature-dependent equilibrium constant; Qsp describes the present dissolved-ion state.

Common ions and concentration changes shift composition by changing Qsp, while temperature can change Ksp.

Vocabulary: Ksp; Qsp; saturation; common ion; equilibrium.

Worked example

A solution can be unsaturated by a solubility curve yet still require ion-specific Qsp reasoning in a sparingly soluble salt model.

Week 28 synthesis challenge

Defend a precipitation or solubility prediction using dissolution stoichiometry, Ksp, Qsp, one common-ion effect, and one model limitation.

Precipitation-audit extension

For a mixing problem, first calculate the diluted ion concentrations after the solutions combine, then construct Qsp from those post-mixing concentrations. Comparing pre-mixing concentrations directly to Ksp can give the wrong result because total volume changes.

Only after Qsp is compared with Ksp should the precipitation prediction be made.

Solubility synthesis extension

Build a four-part audit: write the dissolution equation, construct Ksp, determine current ion concentrations, and compare Qsp with Ksp. Then state whether the system is unsaturated, saturated, or supersaturated with respect to the solid.

Finally, distinguish equilibrium prediction from observation: cloudiness or crystal formation is evidence of precipitation, while Qsp/Ksp is the model used to predict whether precipitation should occur.

Final precipitation check

Always use post-mixing concentrations before comparing Qsp with Ksp in a precipitation prediction.
