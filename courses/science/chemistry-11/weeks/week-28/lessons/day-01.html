Solubility & Saturation

Distinguish unsaturated, saturated, and supersaturated states using particle and graph models.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use solubility curves, supplied datasets, and simulations only. Do not perform unsupervised precipitation chemistry or mix household chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Solubility is the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve under specified conditions.

An unsaturated solution can dissolve more solute; a saturated solution is at the solubility limit; a supersaturated state is metastable and contains more dissolved solute than the usual equilibrium limit.

Temperature can change solubility, so the condition must be stated with the value.

Vocabulary: solubility; saturation; unsaturated; supersaturated; equilibrium.

Worked example

If a solubility curve gives 36 g solute per 100 g water at 25°C, a 20 g sample in that amount of water is unsaturated.

Guided question

A solution contains 40 g solute per 100 g water where the curve shows 35 g. What state is suggested if all 40 g are dissolved?

Independent work

  1. Classify four curve points.
  2. Read solubility at three temperatures.
  3. Explain one condition dependence.

Misconception check

Concentration and solubility are not synonyms; concentration can be below the solubility limit.

Formative check

What defines a saturated solution?

Reveal response

It is at the equilibrium solubility limit under the stated conditions.
