Solubility Data Investigation

Use solubility curves and supplied ion data to test saturation and precipitation predictions.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use solubility curves, supplied datasets, and simulations only. Do not perform unsupervised precipitation chemistry or mix household chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Preserve raw temperature, concentration, and solubility-limit data before classifying samples.

Graph or read the solubility curve first, then compare the sample state with the equilibrium limit.

For ion-product cases, preserve all concentrations before calculating Qsp.

Vocabulary: solubility curve; saturation; ion product; classification; uncertainty.

Worked example

At 30°C a curve gives 45 g/100 g water. A measured 43 g dissolved sample is near saturation but below the modeled limit.

Guided question

Which measurement uncertainty could change a near-boundary saturation classification?

Week 28 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Solubility datasets can reveal equilibrium trends but require careful distinction among mass solubility, molar solubility, and Ksp.

Retrieval: Combine saturation, Ksp, and common-ion reasoning.

Quantitative transfer: Convert between g/L and mol/L when needed, calculate ion products, and compare with Ksp under stated conditions.

Evidence-quality task: Record temperature, ionic background, and measurement basis before comparing values.

Evidence revision: If two sources disagree, inspect units and conditions before concluding one is wrong.

Uncertainty: Solubility measurements near very low concentrations can be sensitive to contamination and detection limits.

Solubility-data audit extension

Evaluate two datasets for the same salt and identify which provides the stronger basis for estimating molar solubility and precipitation risk.

Solubility-data quality extension

Convert all compared values to a common basis before ranking. A mass solubility in g/L cannot be compared directly with a Ksp value without accounting for molar mass and dissolution stoichiometry. Preserve temperature and ionic background because both can shift measured behavior.

When values approach detection limits, report whether “below detection” is being treated as zero; those are not the same claim.

Precipitation-threshold extension

For a precipitation dataset, calculate ion concentrations after mixing using total combined volume. Then compute Qsp and compare it with Ksp. This sequence makes the threshold decision auditable and prevents false precipitation predictions caused by using pre-mixing concentrations.

When Qsp is very close to Ksp, measurement uncertainty can make the classification sensitive; report that boundary condition explicitly.

Final uncertainty check

When Qsp is close to Ksp, report whether measurement uncertainty could change the precipitation classification.

Post-mixing concentration extension

For precipitation predictions, calculate ion concentrations after the solutions are combined. Use moles contributed by each solution divided by the total final volume, then build Qsp from those diluted concentrations. Comparing pre-mixing concentrations directly with Ksp can produce a false prediction. If Qsp lies close to Ksp, compare the difference with measurement uncertainty before declaring precipitation certain.
