Reading Buffer Curves

Use pH-versus-added-acid/base graphs to compare buffer performance.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use buffer simulations and supplied datasets only. Do not use biological or medical dosing contexts or unsupervised chemical mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A buffer curve shows how pH responds as acid or base is added.

A flatter region indicates stronger resistance to pH change over that range.

The point where the curve steepens signals that buffering capacity is being exhausted.

Vocabulary: buffer curve; slope; capacity; pH change; exhaustion.

Worked example

Buffer A changes 0.3 pH unit after a fixed acid addition while Buffer B changes 1.4; A shows greater resistance under those conditions.

Guided question

Which graphical feature best indicates buffering capacity over a tested range?

Independent work

  1. Compare two curves.
  2. Estimate where capacity is exceeded.
  3. Calculate ΔpH for three additions.

Misconception check

A perfectly flat line is not expected in real buffering data.

Formative check

What does a steepening curve suggest?

Reveal response

The buffer is approaching or exceeding its capacity.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit conjugate-pair logic, equilibrium response, and whether capacity claims match the data.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Read ΔpH from graphs.

Core: Compare buffer curves quantitatively.

Extended: Relate slope changes to composition limits.
