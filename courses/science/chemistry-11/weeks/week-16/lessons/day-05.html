Limiting Reactants & Yield Synthesis

Integrate stoichiometric constraints, theoretical prediction, measured yield, and evidence-based correction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper-token models, supplied calculations, and simulations only. No unsupervised chemical reactions are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Limiting-reactant analysis turns a balanced equation into a resource-allocation model: one input becomes the binding constraint.

Theoretical yield is the model prediction; actual yield is evidence; percent yield compares the two.

A strong conclusion explains both the quantitative result and why real systems may depart from the ideal stoichiometric prediction.

Vocabulary: constraint; theoretical yield; actual yield; percent yield; model limit.

Worked example

A reaction can be perfectly balanced on paper yet produce less than theoretical yield because the balanced equation does not include every real-world loss pathway.

Guided question

Write a CER explaining why one reactant limits product and why actual yield can be lower than predicted.

Independent work

  2. Write a CER using quantitative evidence.
  3. Record one corrected misconception.
  4. Identify one uncertainty source.

Spiral retrieval bridge

Misconception check

Stoichiometric balance does not imply 100% experimental yield.

Formative check

What is the conceptual difference between limiting reactant and percent yield?

Reveal response

The limiting reactant sets the maximum product; percent yield compares measured product with that maximum.
