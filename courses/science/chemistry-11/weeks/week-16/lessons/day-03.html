Actual Yield & Percent Yield

Calculate percent yield and interpret deviations from theoretical prediction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper-token models, supplied calculations, and simulations only. No unsupervised chemical reactions are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Actual yield is the measured amount of product recovered. Percent yield = actual/theoretical ×100%.

A yield below 100% can reflect incomplete reaction, side reactions, transfer loss, purification loss, or measurement uncertainty.

A value above 100% usually signals wet/impure product, measurement error, or a problem with the theoretical model—not 'extra chemistry success.'

Vocabulary: actual yield; percent yield; recovery; purity; uncertainty.

Worked example

Theoretical yield is 10.0 g and actual yield is 8.50 g, so percent yield=85.0%.

Guided question

Actual yield is 12.4 g and theoretical yield is 15.5 g. Calculate percent yield.

Independent work

  1. Calculate percent yield for four datasets.
  2. Diagnose one plausible cause for low yield.
  3. Explain one reason a measured yield might exceed 100%.

Misconception check

Percent yield above 100% is not physically interpreted as producing more pure product than stoichiometry allows.

Formative check

What denominator is used in percent-yield calculation?

Reveal response

Theoretical yield.

Week 16 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Actual yield often differs from theoretical yield because real reactions and measurements are imperfect.

Retrieval: Use Day 2 theoretical yield as the denominator in percent-yield reasoning.

Quantitative reasoning: Calculate percent yield = actual/theoretical ×100%, preserve units before forming the dimensionless ratio, and interpret values near or above 100% critically.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish low yield due to incomplete reaction from low measured yield due to transfer or recovery losses.

Evidence revision: Treat percent yield above 100% as a signal to investigate contamination, retained solvent, weighing error, or wrong theoretical yield—not as “extra product.”

Model limit: Percent yield summarizes outcome efficiency but does not by itself identify the mechanism of loss.

Yield-diagnostic transfer extension

Compare two experimental runs with different percent yields and propose at least two distinct causal explanations for each. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate among them.

Percent-yield worked extension

If theoretical yield is 12.5 g and actual recovered product is 10.8 g, percent yield = (10.8/12.5)×100% = 86.4%. A result below 100% may reflect incomplete reaction, side reactions, product remaining on equipment, filtration loss, or other recovery issues.

If an apparently dry product gives 104% yield, do not round the anomaly away. Investigate retained solvent, contamination, balance error, or an underestimated theoretical yield.

Percent-yield interpretation check

Percent yield is dimensionless because the mass or mole units cancel in the ratio. Keep the same quantity basis in numerator and denominator; do not compare actual grams with theoretical moles.
