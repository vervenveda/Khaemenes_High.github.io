Theoretical Yield

Calculate the maximum possible product from the limiting reactant.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper-token models, supplied calculations, and simulations only. No unsupervised chemical reactions are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Theoretical yield is the maximum product predicted by stoichiometry if the limiting reactant is fully converted according to the model.

The calculation begins with the limiting reactant, follows the mole ratio, then converts product moles into the requested unit.

Theoretical yield is a model prediction, not a guarantee that a real experiment reaches that amount.

Vocabulary: theoretical yield; limiting reactant; product moles; molar mass; prediction.

Worked example

If 1.00 mol O₂ is limiting in 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, theoretical yield is 2.00 mol H₂O, about 36.0 g.

Guided question

A reaction predicts 0.75 mol product with molar mass 80 g/mol. What is theoretical yield in grams?

Independent work

  1. Solve three theoretical-yield problems.
  2. Show the full conversion chain.
  3. State the limiting reactant used in each.

Misconception check

Theoretical yield must come from the limiting reactant, not from averaging predictions from both reactants.

Formative check

What does theoretical yield represent?

Reveal response

The stoichiometric maximum under the stated model.

Week 16 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Theoretical yield is the maximum product amount predicted by the balanced equation and the limiting reactant.

Retrieval: Begin with a verified limiting reactant before calculating product mass.

Quantitative reasoning: Convert limiting-reactant amount through the balanced mole ratio to product moles, then to the requested unit.

Evidence-quality task: Label theoretical yield as a calculated upper-bound model result, not an observed laboratory mass.

Evidence revision: If theoretical yield is based on the excess reactant, recalculate from the limiting reactant.

Model limit: Theoretical yield assumes complete conversion with no side reactions, product loss, or measurement error.

Theoretical-yield transfer extension

Calculate product yield from two reactants, then compute the amount of excess reactant remaining. Use that leftover amount as an independent check on the limiting-reactant conclusion.

Yield-and-excess extension

Continue the limiting-reactant example by calculating how much O2 is actually consumed. Three moles H2 require 1.5 mol O2, leaving 0.5 mol O2 excess. This leftover calculation provides an independent consistency check: the limiting reactant should be fully consumed in the ideal model while some excess remains.

Theoretical yield should be reported in the requested unit and tied explicitly to the limiting reactant, not simply the first reactant listed.

Theoretical-yield check

Compare theoretical yield with the amount predicted from the nonlimiting reactant. The limiting-reactant pathway must give the smaller product amount. If not, revisit the limiting-reactant calculation before reporting yield.

Upper-bound note

Theoretical yield is an upper bound within the stated reaction model, not a guarantee of what a laboratory procedure will recover.

Theoretical-yield validation extension

After calculating theoretical yield, verify it with a second path whenever possible. Confirm that the limiting reactant is fully consumed in the ideal model, calculate excess reactant remaining, and check that the product amount matches the balanced-equation ratio. A theoretical yield that implies negative excess or exceeds what either reactant can support signals an earlier setup error. Keep prediction and observation separate so later percent-yield analysis remains traceable.
