Theoretical Yield

Calculate the maximum possible product from the limiting reactant.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper-token models, supplied calculations, and simulations only. No unsupervised chemical reactions are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Theoretical yield is the maximum product predicted by stoichiometry if the limiting reactant is fully converted according to the model.

The calculation begins with the limiting reactant, follows the mole ratio, then converts product moles into the requested unit.

Theoretical yield is a model prediction, not a guarantee that a real experiment reaches that amount.

Vocabulary: theoretical yield; limiting reactant; product moles; molar mass; prediction.

Worked example

If 1.00 mol O₂ is limiting in 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, theoretical yield is 2.00 mol H₂O, about 36.0 g.

Guided question

A reaction predicts 0.75 mol product with molar mass 80 g/mol. What is theoretical yield in grams?

Independent work

  1. Solve three theoretical-yield problems.
  2. Show the full conversion chain.
  3. State the limiting reactant used in each.

Misconception check

Theoretical yield must come from the limiting reactant, not from averaging predictions from both reactants.

Formative check

What does theoretical yield represent?

Reveal response

The stoichiometric maximum under the stated model.
