Ions, Charge, and Electrostatic Attraction

Explain how electron transfer creates ions and why opposite charges attract in ionic compounds.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext

Explicit science instruction

Ions form when electron count differs from proton count. Losing electrons produces a positive ion; gaining electrons produces a negative ion. The nucleus does not change during ordinary ion formation.

Ionic bonding is modeled as electrostatic attraction among oppositely charged ions. A crystal contains a repeating three-dimensional arrangement rather than isolated molecule pairs.

Charge and stoichiometry are linked: a stable empirical formula must represent overall electrical neutrality.

Vocabulary: ion; cation; anion; valence electron; electrostatic attraction.

Worked example with reasoning

Magnesium commonly forms Mg²⁺ and chlorine forms Cl⁻. Two chloride ions are required for each magnesium ion, giving MgCl₂ and net charge +2 + 2(−1)=0.

Guided question

Use electron-count reasoning to explain why Na⁺ and O²⁻ combine in a 2:1 ratio.

Independent work

  1. Write ion symbols for Li, Mg, Al, O, and Cl in a simple main-group model.
  2. For three cation–anion pairs, calculate the smallest whole-number charge balance.
  3. Explain why the formula ratio describes electrical neutrality rather than a molecule count.

Misconception check

Ionic bonding is not simply one atom handing an electron to one partner and then becoming a permanently isolated pair; the solid is a many-ion electrostatic lattice.

Formative understanding check

Why is MgCl rather than MgCl₂ inconsistent with Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ charges?

Reveal response

MgCl would have net +1 charge; two Cl⁻ ions are needed to balance one Mg²⁺.
