Formula Units and Charge Audits

Construct neutral ionic formulas and verify them using explicit charge accounting.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

An ionic formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of ions needed for neutrality. Subscripts apply to the species they follow; parentheses preserve a polyatomic ion when more than one copy is required.

A reliable method is to calculate total positive and negative charge separately, then reduce the ratio if a common factor remains.

The cross-over shortcut can produce a candidate formula, but a charge audit is the scientific check that confirms it.

Vocabulary: formula unit; subscript; polyatomic ion; charge audit; empirical ratio.

Worked example with reasoning

For Al³⁺ and O²⁻, the least common charge magnitude is 6: two Al³⁺ ions contribute +6 and three O²⁻ ions contribute −6, so the formula is Al₂O₃.

Guided question

Construct the formula for Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ and show the total positive and negative charge.

Independent work

  1. Build formulas for Na⁺/S²⁻, Mg²⁺/N³⁻, Al³⁺/Cl⁻, and NH₄⁺/SO₄²⁻.
  2. Write a charge audit beside every formula.
  3. Identify one formula that can be reduced and explain why empirical formulas use the simplest ratio.

Misconception check

Subscripts are not ionic charges. Charges describe individual ions; subscripts describe the count ratio in the formula unit.

Formative understanding check

Why is Ca₃(PO₄)₂ neutral?

Reveal response

Three Ca²⁺ ions contribute +6 and two PO₄³⁻ ions contribute −6.

Week 6 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Ionic formulas encode the smallest whole-number charge-balanced ratio, not a count of discrete molecules.

Retrieval: Reuse Day 1 ion-charge reasoning and Week 5 periodic predictions before constructing a formula.

Quantitative transfer: For Ca2+ and PO4^3−, find the least common multiple of charge magnitudes, derive Ca3(PO4)2, and explicitly verify +6 + (−6)=0.

Evidence-quality task: Treat cross-over as a shortcut only; use a full charge audit as the validation step.

Evidence revision: Check whether subscripts can be reduced and whether parentheses correctly preserve polyatomic ions.

Model limit: A neutral empirical formula may not uniquely determine metal oxidation state when multiple charge states are chemically possible.

Reverse-formula transfer extension

Given an empirical formula and one known ion charge, infer the other charge. Then identify a case where several oxidation-state assignments could fit unless additional chemical evidence is supplied.

Formula-validation extension

After deriving an ionic formula, test it in both directions: calculate net charge from the proposed subscripts, then ask whether any common factor can reduce the ratio. For polyatomic ions, verify that parentheses preserve the ion as a unit. This two-way audit is more reliable than applying a shortcut without checking the chemistry.
