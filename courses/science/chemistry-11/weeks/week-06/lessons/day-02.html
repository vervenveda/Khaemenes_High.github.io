Formula Units and Charge Audits

Construct neutral ionic formulas and verify them using explicit charge accounting.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

An ionic formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of ions needed for neutrality. Subscripts apply to the species they follow; parentheses preserve a polyatomic ion when more than one copy is required.

A reliable method is to calculate total positive and negative charge separately, then reduce the ratio if a common factor remains.

The cross-over shortcut can produce a candidate formula, but a charge audit is the scientific check that confirms it.

Vocabulary: formula unit; subscript; polyatomic ion; charge audit; empirical ratio.

Worked example with reasoning

For Al³⁺ and O²⁻, the least common charge magnitude is 6: two Al³⁺ ions contribute +6 and three O²⁻ ions contribute −6, so the formula is Al₂O₃.

Guided question

Construct the formula for Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ and show the total positive and negative charge.

Independent work

  1. Build formulas for Na⁺/S²⁻, Mg²⁺/N³⁻, Al³⁺/Cl⁻, and NH₄⁺/SO₄²⁻.
  2. Write a charge audit beside every formula.
  3. Identify one formula that can be reduced and explain why empirical formulas use the simplest ratio.

Misconception check

Subscripts are not ionic charges. Charges describe individual ions; subscripts describe the count ratio in the formula unit.

Formative understanding check

Why is Ca₃(PO₄)₂ neutral?

Reveal response

Three Ca²⁺ ions contribute +6 and two PO₄³⁻ ions contribute −6.

Public self-check only.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use color-coded charge blocks.

Core: Construct and audit formulas including polyatomic ions.

Extended: Reverse-engineer likely ion charges from a neutral empirical formula and state ambiguity where multiple oxidation states are possible.
