Model Fitting & Residuals

Compare simple rate models using transformed data and residual patterns.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied datasets and approved simulations. Any physical demonstration must be teacher-supervised and explicitly classroom-safe; no improvised reactive mixtures.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A model should be judged by how well it predicts data, not by whether a curve looks familiar.

Residuals are observed minus predicted values. Random small residuals support adequacy better than patterned residuals.

Transformations can make relationships easier to compare, but the raw data must remain preserved.

Vocabulary: model fit; residual; transformation; prediction; evidence.

Worked example

Two models have similar average error, but one leaves a curved residual pattern while the other leaves random scatter; the second is usually better supported.

Guided question

Why can residual pattern matter more than a single fit score?

Independent work

  1. Fit two supplied simple models.
  2. Calculate residuals.
  3. Graph residuals.
  4. Rank the models and justify the choice.

Misconception check

A high correlation does not guarantee a correct causal or mechanistic model.

Formative check

What residual pattern suggests systematic model failure?

Reveal response

A nonrandom pattern such as curvature or consistent sign bias.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit controls, competing predictions, residuals, uncertainty, and whether the conclusion exceeds the evidence.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied prediction tables.

Core: Compare model residuals.

Extended: Discuss overfitting and alternative models.

Week 22 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: A model that appears to fit a graph can still be systematically wrong when residuals reveal structure.

Retrieval: Connect Week 19 rate curves with quantitative model comparison.

Quantitative reasoning: Calculate predicted values, residual = observed − predicted, and inspect whether residuals scatter randomly or show curvature/trend.

Evidence-quality task: Do not judge fit solely by visual closeness or a single summary statistic.

Evidence revision: Structured residuals indicate the model misses a pattern and should be revised or replaced.

Model limit: A better fit can still overfit noise if complexity increases without independent justification.

Residual-analysis extension

Compare two candidate models using residual plots and identify which one better captures the systematic pattern without unnecessary complexity.

Residual-pattern extension

Residuals should be examined against the independent variable or fitted value. Random positive and negative scatter around zero is more consistent with an adequate model; a curved pattern suggests missing nonlinearity, while a funnel-shaped spread suggests changing variance.

Do not automatically choose the model with the smallest residual sum if it adds unnecessary parameters. Prefer the simplest model that captures the systematic pattern and survives independent checks.

Residual-quality note

Interpret systematic residual structure as evidence that the model is missing a pattern, not simply as inconvenient noise.

Final residual note

Use residual structure to decide whether the mismatch is random scatter, systematic curvature, or changing variance across the dataset.
