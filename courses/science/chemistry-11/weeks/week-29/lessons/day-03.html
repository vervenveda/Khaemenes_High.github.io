Amphoteric Behavior & Water

Explain how one species can act as acid or base depending on reaction partner.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual indicators or teacher-supervised food-safe indicator only. Never mix cleaners, household acids/bases, or unknown chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

An amphoteric/amphiprotic species can donate or accept a proton depending on context.

Water can act as a base with a stronger acid and as an acid with a stronger base.

Acid/base labels describe roles in a specific reaction, not permanent identities independent of context.

Vocabulary: amphoteric; amphiprotic; role; proton donor; proton acceptor.

Worked example

H2O accepts H+ from HCl but donates H+ to NH3 in the reverse conceptual comparison.

Guided question

Why can HCO3− act as either an acid or a base?

Independent work

  1. Classify water's role in four reactions.
  2. Identify two amphiprotic species.
  3. Write one role-dependent explanation.

Misconception check

A substance can have different acid/base roles in different reactions.

Formative check

What determines acid/base role in Brønsted chemistry?

Reveal response

Whether the species donates or accepts a proton in that reaction.
