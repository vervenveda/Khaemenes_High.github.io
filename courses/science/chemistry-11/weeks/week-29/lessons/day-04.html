Virtual Indicator Investigation

Use indicator data to connect macroscopic observations with acid/base classifications.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual indicators or teacher-supervised food-safe indicator only. Never mix cleaners, household acids/bases, or unknown chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Indicators change molecular form over a pH range and therefore change color.

Indicator color is evidence of an approximate pH region, not an exact identity of the solute.

Use calibration/reference colors or a simulation rather than guessing from appearance alone.

Vocabulary: indicator; pH range; calibration; observation; inference.

Worked example

If an indicator reference changes from yellow below pH 6 to blue above pH 8, green near the transition supports an intermediate range rather than an exact pH.

Guided question

Why is one indicator color insufficient to identify a specific acid?

Independent work

  1. Preserve indicator observations.
  2. Compare with reference ranges.
  3. Classify samples broadly.
  4. State one uncertainty and revision.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use simulation or food-safe supervised indicator only. Preserve raw observations, reference comparison, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Indicator color estimates acidity/basicity range; it does not identify the chemical substance.

Formative check

What strengthens an indicator-based conclusion?

Reveal response

Reference calibration and multiple consistent observations.
