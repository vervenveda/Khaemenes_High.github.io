Conjugate Acid–Base Pairs

Identify conjugate pairs that differ by one proton.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual indicators or teacher-supervised food-safe indicator only. Never mix cleaners, household acids/bases, or unknown chemicals.

Explicit chemistry instruction

When an acid donates H+, it becomes its conjugate base.

When a base accepts H+, it becomes its conjugate acid.

Conjugate partners differ by exactly one proton and corresponding charge change.

Vocabulary: conjugate acid; conjugate base; pair; proton; charge.

Worked example

NH4+/NH3 are a conjugate acid–base pair because they differ by one H+.

Guided question

Identify the conjugate base of H2CO3 and the conjugate acid of HCO3−.

Independent work

  1. Mark conjugate pairs in four equations.
  2. Write one missing conjugate partner.
  3. Audit charge changes.

Misconception check

Conjugate acid/base does not mean two unrelated substances with opposite labels.

Formative check

How do conjugate partners differ?

Reveal response

By one proton.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit proton-transfer roles, conjugate-pair logic, and model scope.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use paired-species tables.

Core: Identify and generate conjugate pairs.

Extended: Relate conjugate strength qualitatively.
