Gibbs Free Energy as a Direction Model

Use ΔG to integrate enthalpy and entropy into a prediction of thermodynamic direction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied graphs and calculations only. No new reactive chemistry is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At constant temperature and pressure, Gibbs free energy combines enthalpy and entropy effects.

In the simplified criterion, ΔG<0 indicates thermodynamic favorability in the forward direction, ΔG>0 favors the reverse, and ΔG=0 corresponds to equilibrium.

This is a direction/equilibrium model, not a rate model.

Vocabulary: Gibbs free energy; ΔG; enthalpy; entropy; spontaneity.

Worked example

If ΔH=−40 kJ and TΔS=−10 kJ, then ΔG=−30 kJ and the forward direction is thermodynamically favored.

Guided question

If ΔH=+20 kJ and TΔS=+35 kJ, what is ΔG and what direction is favored?

Independent work

  1. Classify four ΔG signs.
  2. Calculate three ΔG values.
  3. State why ΔG does not predict speed.

Misconception check

Negative ΔG does not guarantee a fast reaction.

Formative check

What does ΔG=0 signify in this model?

Reveal response

Equilibrium under the stated conditions.

Week 32 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Gibbs free energy combines enthalpy and entropy into a criterion for thermodynamic direction at constant temperature and pressure.

Retrieval: Connect Week 25 ΔG reasoning with the equilibrium concepts developed since Week 26.

Quantitative reasoning: Interpret ΔG<0 as forward-favored, ΔG>0 as reverse-favored, and ΔG=0 as equilibrium for the stated conditions.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish thermodynamic favorability from kinetics and from actual reaction completion.

Evidence revision: Correct the statement “negative ΔG means the reaction happens instantly.”

Model limit: ΔG depends on temperature, pressure, and composition; standard-state ΔG° is not the same as ΔG under arbitrary conditions.

Direction-model transfer extension

Compare three reactions with negative, positive, and zero ΔG and state what can and cannot be inferred about direction, speed, and equilibrium.

Actual-state free-energy extension

Standard free energy ΔG° refers to standard-state conditions, while actual ΔG depends on current composition through the reaction quotient. A reaction with positive ΔG° can still proceed forward under sufficiently reactant-rich conditions, and a reaction with negative ΔG° can be driven in reverse under product-rich conditions.

This distinction connects free energy directly to the Q-versus-K framework from equilibrium.

Composition-dependence extension

Use ΔG=ΔG°+RT ln Q conceptually to connect standard thermodynamics with actual composition. When Q<K, ΔG is negative for the forward direction; when Q>K, ΔG is positive; when Q=K, ΔG=0.

This relationship shows that direction under current conditions cannot always be inferred from ΔG° alone.

Free-energy state check

State whether each value is ΔG or ΔG° before interpreting direction. Standard-state values summarize a reference condition; actual ΔG must reflect the current reaction quotient.

Use Q-versus-K as an independent direction check whenever both equilibrium and free-energy information are available.

Actual-versus-standard free-energy extension

State whether a value is ΔG or ΔG° before using it to predict direction. ΔG° describes standard-state conditions, while actual ΔG also depends on current composition through Q. A negative ΔG° does not guarantee forward reaction under every mixture, and a positive ΔG° does not forbid forward reaction if the starting composition is sufficiently reactant-rich. Cross-check direction with Q versus K whenever both are available.
