Gibbs Free Energy as a Direction Model

Use ΔG to integrate enthalpy and entropy into a prediction of thermodynamic direction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied graphs and calculations only. No new reactive chemistry is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At constant temperature and pressure, Gibbs free energy combines enthalpy and entropy effects.

In the simplified criterion, ΔG<0 indicates thermodynamic favorability in the forward direction, ΔG>0 favors the reverse, and ΔG=0 corresponds to equilibrium.

This is a direction/equilibrium model, not a rate model.

Vocabulary: Gibbs free energy; ΔG; enthalpy; entropy; spontaneity.

Worked example

If ΔH=−40 kJ and TΔS=−10 kJ, then ΔG=−30 kJ and the forward direction is thermodynamically favored.

Guided question

If ΔH=+20 kJ and TΔS=+35 kJ, what is ΔG and what direction is favored?

Independent work

  1. Classify four ΔG signs.
  2. Calculate three ΔG values.
  3. State why ΔG does not predict speed.

Misconception check

Negative ΔG does not guarantee a fast reaction.

Formative check

What does ΔG=0 signify in this model?

Reveal response

Equilibrium under the stated conditions.
