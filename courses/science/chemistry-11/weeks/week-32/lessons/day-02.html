Temperature Dependence of Free Energy

Explain how temperature can change thermodynamic direction when entropy matters.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied graphs and calculations only. No new reactive chemistry is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Because ΔG=ΔH−TΔS, the entropy term grows in magnitude with temperature.

For some sign combinations, a process can switch from unfavorable to favorable as temperature changes.

The sign and units of ΔS must be handled carefully, often converting J to kJ.

Vocabulary: temperature; ΔS; sign; crossover; units.

Worked example

If ΔH=+30 kJ and ΔS=+0.10 kJ/K, ΔG becomes zero near T=300 K.

Guided question

Why can an endothermic process become favorable at sufficiently high temperature?

Independent work

  1. Solve two crossover-temperature problems.
  2. Audit ΔS units.
  3. Classify four sign combinations qualitatively.

Misconception check

Temperature does not affect every process in the same direction; signs of ΔH and ΔS matter.

Formative check

Which term carries explicit temperature dependence?

Reveal response

TΔS.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit signs, units, thermodynamic conditions, and whether rate/direction are being kept distinct.

Teacher guidance

Week 32 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Temperature changes the balance between enthalpy and entropy contributions in ΔG=ΔH−TΔS.

Retrieval: Reuse Week 25 sign-combination reasoning and Week 32 Day 1 direction criteria.

Quantitative reasoning: Solve or estimate the temperature at which ΔG changes sign by setting ΔG=0, with compatible units.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish a calculated crossover temperature from a claim that kinetics becomes fast at that temperature.

Evidence revision: Correct calculations that combine kJ/mol enthalpy with J/mol·K entropy without conversion.

Model limit: Treating ΔH and ΔS as temperature-independent across a wide range is an approximation.

Temperature-crossover transfer extension

For each ΔH/ΔS sign combination, determine whether increasing temperature makes the process more or less favorable and identify any crossover behavior.

Worked crossover-temperature extension

If ΔH=+50 kJ/mol and ΔS=+150 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹, first convert ΔS to 0.150 kJ·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹. Setting ΔG=0 gives T=ΔH/ΔS≈333 K. Above that temperature, the entropy term outweighs the positive enthalpy and ΔG becomes negative under the simplified model.

Always interpret the crossover as thermodynamic, not kinetic; the reaction may still be slow.

Temperature-model extension

After finding a crossover temperature, test which side of that value gives negative ΔG by substituting one lower and one higher temperature. This guards against sign errors when ΔH and ΔS have the same sign.

Also state that using constant ΔH and ΔS over a broad temperature range is an approximation whose quality depends on the system.

Crossover-validation check

After computing the crossover temperature, substitute a temperature on each side of it and calculate or sign-check ΔG. This confirms which region is thermodynamically favored and catches ratio/sign mistakes.

Report whether the assumed constant ΔH and ΔS approximation is reasonable over the temperature range considered.

Final temperature note

State whether the predicted direction change is thermodynamic only or whether separate kinetic evidence supports an observable rate change on the same timescale.
