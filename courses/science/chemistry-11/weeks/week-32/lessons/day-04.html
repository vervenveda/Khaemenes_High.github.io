Explicit chemistry instruction

Preserve raw thermodynamic data before calculating ΔG.

Calculate ΔG across several temperatures and graph the result.

Compare predicted sign changes with the modeled process behavior and discuss uncertainty.

Vocabulary: thermodynamic dataset; temperature; ΔG; sign change; uncertainty.

Worked example

If ΔG crosses from positive to negative between 280 and 320 K, the crossover region is evidence of temperature-dependent favorability.

Week 32 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Free-energy datasets can test whether thermodynamic predictions agree across ΔH, ΔS, ΔG, and equilibrium evidence.

Retrieval: Integrate Days 1–3 with Weeks 24–26 thermochemistry/equilibrium reasoning.

Quantitative transfer: Calculate ΔG from ΔH and ΔS at stated temperatures or infer ΔG° from K when appropriate, preserving units and constants.

Evidence-quality task: Keep standard-state values separate from measurements made under nonstandard composition.

Evidence revision: If signs inferred from K disagree with calculated ΔG°, audit logarithm sign, units, and reaction direction.

Uncertainty: Experimental ΔH, ΔS, and K values all carry uncertainty that can matter near ΔG≈0.

Free-energy data audit extension

Compare two independent routes to the same direction prediction and identify which assumptions control agreement.

Free-energy data-quality extension

Compare a ΔG estimate from ΔH−TΔS with one inferred from equilibrium data. Agreement supports internal consistency; disagreement should trigger checks of temperature, standard-state assumptions, reaction direction, logarithm sign, and units.

Near ΔG=0, even modest experimental uncertainty can change the sign, so report whether the direction prediction is robust or borderline.

Independent-route extension

If both K and ΔH/ΔS data are available, use them as independent checks on the sign of free energy. A large K should correspond to negative ΔG° for the reaction as written. If the signs disagree, verify reaction direction before assuming the numerical data are inconsistent.

Near equilibrium, report whether uncertainty in K or thermodynamic inputs could plausibly change the sign conclusion.

Data-consistency check

Compare the sign predicted from ΔH−TΔS with the sign inferred from K through ΔG°=−RT ln K. If the two routes disagree, audit reaction direction, temperature, standard-state assumptions, and units before interpreting the chemistry.
