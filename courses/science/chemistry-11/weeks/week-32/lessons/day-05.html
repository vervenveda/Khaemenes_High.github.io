Free-Energy Synthesis

Integrate enthalpy, entropy, temperature, equilibrium, and kinetics without conflating them.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied graphs and calculations only. No new reactive chemistry is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Thermodynamic favorability depends on ΔG; equilibrium occurs where net driving force vanishes; kinetics controls rate.

Temperature can shift favorability because it scales the entropy term.

A complete explanation states conditions and keeps direction separate from speed.

Vocabulary: thermodynamics; equilibrium; kinetics; temperature; driving force.

Worked example

A reaction can have ΔG<0 yet proceed slowly if the activation barrier is large.

Week 32 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Free energy unifies enthalpy, entropy, temperature, equilibrium, and coupled-process reasoning.

Retrieval: Integrate ΔH, ΔS, ΔG, K, Q, and coupling.

Evidence-quality task: Separate standard-state thermodynamic values from actual-state direction predictions.

Evidence revision: Correct one sign error, one unit error, and one misuse of ΔG° in place of ΔG.

Transfer: Evaluate an unfamiliar process and determine which thermodynamic information is sufficient to predict direction.

Model limit: Thermodynamic favorability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or specify mechanism.

Week 32 synthesis challenge

Defend a thermodynamic prediction using ΔH, ΔS, temperature, ΔG or ΔG°, equilibrium evidence, one uncertainty, and one kinetic limitation.

Thermodynamic synthesis extension

Use a decision chain: identify whether values are standard or actual-state, calculate or infer ΔG, interpret sign for direction, compare with K/Q evidence where available, and then separately assess kinetic accessibility.

For any coupled process, verify that the favorable step is chemically linked to the unfavorable step rather than merely present in the same mixture.

Coupling-validation extension

For coupled reactions, add the chemical equations and cancel intermediates before adding free energies. The net ΔG can be negative even if one component step is unfavorable, provided the favorable step is sufficiently negative and the processes are physically linked.

State explicitly what mechanism or shared intermediate provides that linkage.

Coupling-evidence check

A favorable net ΔG is necessary for thermodynamic drive but does not prove that two reactions are actually coupled. Identify the shared intermediate, transfer step, or mechanism that physically links them, then state whether kinetic barriers could still limit the observed process.

Final coupling note

Verify both the favorable net ΔG and the physical linkage between component reactions before describing a process as thermodynamically coupled.

Thermodynamic decision audit

Use one decision chain: identify standard versus actual conditions, calculate or infer ΔG, compare with K or Q if available, state the thermodynamic direction, then separately assess kinetic accessibility. For coupled reactions, verify the chemical linkage or shared intermediate before adding free energies. A favorable summed ΔG is meaningful only when the processes are physically coupled rather than merely written on the same page.
