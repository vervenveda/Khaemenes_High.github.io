Free Energy, Equilibrium & Coupling

Connect ΔG with equilibrium and explain how favorable processes can drive unfavorable ones when coupled.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied graphs and calculations only. No new reactive chemistry is required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At equilibrium, the system has no net thermodynamic driving force under the model, so ΔG for the reaction condition is zero.

A favorable process can be coupled to an unfavorable one if the combined ΔG is negative.

Coupling requires a real shared mechanism or pathway; simply adding unrelated equations does not make processes influence each other.

Vocabulary: equilibrium; coupling; driving force; combined process; pathway.

Worked example

A +10 kJ process coupled to a −25 kJ process has combined ΔG=−15 kJ if the coupling is mechanistically valid.

Guided question

What condition must be met before two ΔG values can be meaningfully combined?

Independent work

  1. Add three coupled ΔG pairs.
  2. Identify favorable combined processes.
  3. State one mechanism requirement.

Misconception check

Unrelated favorable reactions do not automatically drive unfavorable ones.

Formative check

What matters for a coupled process overall?

Reveal response

The sum of ΔG values for a genuinely linked pathway.
