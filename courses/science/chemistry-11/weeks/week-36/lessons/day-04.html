Supervised Final & Correction Protocol

Clarify the protected final-exam boundary and prepare a rigorous post-assessment correction process.

Assessment boundary

This public module contains formative review/support only. It does not expose protected final questions, answer keys, grading logic, or authentication details.

Explicit chemistry support

The official final is closed-book and supervised. Public browser practice supports preparation but does not authenticate official mastery.

After authorized score return, corrections should identify whether an error came from concept, model choice, stoichiometry, algebra, sign, unit, graph, or evidence interpretation.

Protected questions, answer keys, grading rules, signing logic, and authentication details do not belong in the learner-facing public repository.

Vocabulary: supervised final; correction; error analysis; mastery; protected content.

Worked example

If a learner chooses equilibrium reasoning for a kinetics question, the correction should identify the model-selection error and rewrite the reasoning using rate evidence.

Guided question

Why is an error-category correction stronger than copying the correct answer?

Independent work

  1. Create an error-category checklist.
  2. After authorized score return, complete one correction per missed category.
  3. Keep protected item text out of public notes unless explicitly approved.

Week 36 supervised-final checkpoint

Purpose: Preserve a strict boundary between public preparation/correction and official supervised assessment.

Assessment boundary: Confidential prompts, answer keys, custody, authoritative grading, and official reassessment remain outside this public lesson layer.

Correction protocol: After authorized review, classify missed work by concept, model selection, representation, calculation, or evidence-reasoning error.

Evidence revision: Write a corrected explanation or worked example that addresses the underlying misconception rather than copying an answer.

Mastery: Correction evidence may document learning growth while final grade authority remains governed separately.

Integrity: Public formative content must never reveal protected final-exam material.

Correction-quality extension

For each missed objective, state what failed, what principle resolves it, and how the corrected reasoning transfers to a new problem.

Correction-transfer extension

A valid correction should include the original error type, governing principle, reconstructed reasoning, and a new transfer problem. This prevents answer-copying and demonstrates that the underlying misconception has been repaired.

Keep public correction examples generic enough that they do not reveal protected final prompts or keys.

Reassessment-boundary extension

Public correction work may document conceptual repair, but official reassessment must use separate protected items and authoritative recording. This prevents a public lesson from becoming an answer-bearing duplicate of the final.

Correction quality should be judged by transfer to a new example, not by reproducing the original solution.
