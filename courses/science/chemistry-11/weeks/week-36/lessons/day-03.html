Laboratory & Evidence Portfolio Audit

Audit the yearlong chemistry evidence record for traceability, uncertainty, and authentic revision.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Assessment boundary

This public module contains formative review/support only. It does not expose protected final questions, answer keys, grading logic, or authentication details.

Explicit chemistry support

A strong portfolio preserves raw observations, processed data, calculations, graphs, uncertainty, model revisions, and conclusions.

Each conclusion should be traceable back to original evidence and documented transformations.

Missing evidence should not be invented; repair only from authentic records.

Vocabulary: portfolio audit; data lineage; raw evidence; uncertainty; revision history.

Worked example

A final concentration value without its raw volume/mass data and calculation trail is not fully auditable.

Guided question

Choose one lab and trace raw evidence to final claim. Where is the weakest link?

Independent work

  1. Audit at least four investigation records.
  2. Flag missing raw data, units, uncertainty, or reasoning.
  3. Write one authentic repair plan.

Misconception check

Portfolio cleanup must not rewrite history or fabricate observations.

Formative check

What makes a chemistry evidence record reproducible?

Reveal response

Raw data, documented calculations, units, assumptions, and revision history.

Mentor evidence conference

Archaemenes may challenge reasoning and help plan corrections, but cannot reveal protected answers, bypass assessment gates, or grant official mastery.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use an evidence checklist.

Core: Complete a full audit.

Extended: Trace uncertainty through one multi-step calculation.

Week 36 portfolio-audit checkpoint

Purpose: Verify that the final chemistry portfolio documents reasoning growth, not just completed assignments.

Retrieval: Review labs, calculations, CERs, model revisions, capstone evidence, and correction records.

Evidence-quality task: Each selected artifact should preserve raw evidence, processing, claim, reasoning, uncertainty, and revision history.

Evidence revision: Replace polished outputs that hide the reasoning chain with traceable artifacts where possible.

Transfer: Choose one artifact showing cross-unit integration, such as thermodynamics with equilibrium or redox with free energy.

Boundary: Portfolio evidence supports course review but does not replace official final-exam authority.

Portfolio-traceability extension

Audit whether another reviewer could reconstruct how each major conclusion was reached from the preserved evidence.

Portfolio-growth extension

Select paired artifacts from early and late in the course showing the same skill at different stages. Annotate what changed in unit discipline, model selection, evidence reasoning, uncertainty, or revision quality.

Growth claims should point to visible changes in reasoning, not only higher scores.

Portfolio-authenticity extension

Keep original work, feedback, correction, and final revision linked as one evidence chain. A polished final artifact without its earlier version cannot show how reasoning changed.

Include at least one example where the student's model or claim changed because new evidence required revision.

Portfolio-evidence validation

Require each final artifact to point to its original question, raw evidence, processing steps, feedback, correction, and final claim. This keeps the portfolio auditable and prevents a polished endpoint from hiding whether the scientific reasoning actually improved.
