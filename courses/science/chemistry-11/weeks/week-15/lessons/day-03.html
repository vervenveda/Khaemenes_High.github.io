Stoichiometric Ratios from Balanced Equations

Use balanced-equation coefficients as mole ratios between reactants and products.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper models, supplied data, and approved simulations only. No unsupervised reactive mixtures are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A balanced equation preserves atom counts and provides proportional mole relationships among substances.

Coefficients become conversion factors such as 2 mol H₂ per 1 mol O₂. They do not directly describe gram ratios.

Choose the ratio that cancels the starting substance and leaves the target substance.

Vocabulary: stoichiometric ratio; coefficient; mole ratio; reactant; product.

Worked example

For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 3.0 mol O₂ corresponds to 6.0 mol H₂O if H₂ is available in excess.

Guided question

A balanced equation gives 2A + 3B → 4C. How many moles C form from 1.5 mol B with excess A?

Independent work

  1. Write three mole-ratio conversion factors from one equation.
  2. Solve three mole-to-mole problems.
  3. Explain why an unbalanced equation cannot support valid stoichiometry.

Misconception check

Coefficients are mole ratios, not subscripts and not direct mass ratios.

Formative check

What must be done before using an equation for stoichiometry?

Reveal response

Balance it.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Highlight coefficients and build ratios.

Core: Solve mole-to-mole conversions.

Extended: Compare multiple valid paths and check atom conservation.

Week 15 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Balanced coefficients encode fixed particle and mole relationships among reactants and products.

Retrieval: Connect Week 14 coefficient balancing to quantitative mole ratios rather than treating coefficients as decorative integers.

Quantitative reasoning: Use a balanced equation to construct mole-ratio conversion factors and show why subscripts are never changed during stoichiometric calculation.

Evidence-quality task: Verify the equation is balanced before using any coefficient ratio.

Evidence revision: If a predicted amount violates conservation or coefficient ratios, audit the equation and conversion factor orientation.

Model limit: Stoichiometric ratios describe the ideal reaction equation; actual experimental yield can differ because reactions may be incomplete or competing processes may occur.

Mole-ratio transfer extension

For a reaction with at least three species, derive two different valid mole-ratio pathways to the same target product and show that both agree when the equation is balanced correctly.

Coefficient-ratio extension

In 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, the coefficients support 2 mol H2 : 1 mol O2 : 2 mol H2O. From 3.0 mol O2, the ideal product is 6.0 mol H2O if H2 is abundant. The same equation also predicts 6.0 mol H2 required. Make the ratio source explicit every time.

Coefficients scale reaction events; subscripts define substance identity. Changing a coefficient changes amount, while changing a subscript creates a different chemical species.

Ratio-orientation check

Write the desired unit on top of the mole-ratio conversion factor and the starting unit on the bottom. If the starting species does not cancel, the ratio is inverted. This unit test is faster and more reliable than memorizing which coefficient goes where.

Mole-ratio provenance extension

Every stoichiometric conversion factor should be traceable to one balanced equation. Write the balanced equation beside the calculation and circle the two coefficients used in the ratio. If the equation changes, the mole ratio changes. Before calculating, predict whether the target amount should be larger or smaller than the starting amount from the coefficient ratio alone; after calculating, verify that the result agrees with that proportional expectation.
