Molar Mass & Mass–Mole Conversion

Calculate molar mass from formula and convert between grams and moles.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper models, supplied data, and approved simulations only. No unsupervised reactive mixtures are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance, usually expressed in g/mol.

For a compound, add atomic masses according to subscripts. Parentheses and coefficients must be interpreted carefully.

Mass-to-mole conversion uses n=m/M. The molar mass belongs to the chemical formula, not to the sample size.

Vocabulary: molar mass; formula mass; grams; moles; conversion factor.

Worked example

H₂O has molar mass about 18.02 g/mol. A 36.04 g sample therefore contains 2.00 mol.

Guided question

Calculate the molar mass of CO₂ and determine moles in 22.0 g.

Independent work

  1. Calculate molar masses for four formulas.
  2. Convert three masses to moles.
  3. Convert two mole amounts to grams.

Misconception check

Do not multiply molar mass by a coefficient unless the question is about that many moles of substance.

Formative check

What determines a compound's molar mass?

Reveal response

Its chemical formula and constituent atomic masses.

Week 15 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Equal mole amounts of different substances have different masses because their formula units contain different combinations of atoms.

Retrieval: Use Day 1 mole reasoning and periodic atomic masses to build molar mass.

Quantitative reasoning: Calculate molar mass from a chemical formula, then convert grams ↔ moles with dimensional analysis and visible unit cancellation.

Evidence-quality task: Keep measured sample mass separate from calculated molar mass and from amount in moles.

Evidence revision: If the converted mole amount increases when dividing a fixed mass by a larger molar mass, audit the setup because the direction is wrong.

Model limit: Reported molar masses reflect average isotopic composition and stated atomic-weight conventions.

Mass–mole transfer extension

Work one forward and one reverse problem. Before calculating, estimate whether the mole amount should be above or below one mole, then compare the exact result with that expectation.
