The Mole as a Counting Unit

Explain why chemists use the mole to connect particle counts with measurable amounts.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use paper models, supplied data, and approved simulations only. No unsupervised reactive mixtures are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A mole is a counting unit equal to 6.022×10²³ entities. The entity may be atoms, molecules, ions, formula units, or other specified particles.

The mole bridges microscopic particle counts and macroscopic measurements. Always name the entity being counted.

Scientific notation and dimensional analysis prevent scale errors when converting between particles and moles.

Vocabulary: mole; Avogadro constant; entity; particle count; dimensional analysis.

Worked example

2.00 mol of molecules contains 2.00×6.022×10²³ = 1.20×10²⁴ molecules.

Guided question

How many molecules are in 0.250 mol? Show the conversion factor.

Independent work

  1. Convert four mole amounts to particle counts.
  2. Convert two particle counts back to moles.
  3. Label the entity in every answer.

Misconception check

A mole is not a mass unit; it is a counting unit.

Formative check

What quantity connects moles and particle number?

Reveal response

Avogadro's constant, 6.022×10²³ entities/mol.
