Defend the Atomic-Mass Claim

Integrate isotope notation, spectrum evidence, and weighted averages into one coherent explanation.

Explicit science instruction

Mass number is an integer count for one nucleus. Isotopic mass is a measured quantity for one isotope. Periodic-table atomic mass is an abundance-weighted population average.

A spectrum can reveal more than one isotope population. Once relative abundance is estimated, a weighted average explains why the table value can fall between isotope masses.

Vocabulary: mass number; isotopic mass; abundance; weighted average; evidence chain.

Worked example with reasoning

A synthetic spectrum shows masses 63 and 65 at about 69% and 31%. The weighted average lies between 63 and 65 and closer to 63 because that isotope is more abundant.

Misconception check

A non-integer periodic-table mass does not describe one fractional nucleus; it summarizes a population of isotopes.

Formative understanding check

Why can two samples of the same element have slightly different weighted average masses?

Reveal worked response

The samples can contain different isotope abundances while retaining the same proton number and elemental identity.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to find any place where mass number, isotope mass, or weighted atomic mass are being used as if they were the same quantity.
