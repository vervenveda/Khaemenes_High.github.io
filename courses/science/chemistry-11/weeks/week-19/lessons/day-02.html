Rate Curves & Instantaneous Slope

Interpret concentration-time curves and distinguish average from instantaneous rate.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied kinetics datasets and approved simulations. No unsupervised reactive mixtures are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A concentration-time graph encodes reaction progress. The secant slope gives average rate over an interval; the tangent slope estimates instantaneous rate at one time.

Curved data indicate that rate changes as the reaction proceeds.

Slope units come from concentration divided by time and must be reported explicitly.

Vocabulary: rate curve; secant slope; tangent slope; instantaneous rate; derivative concept.

Worked example

A curve drops by 0.12 M over 6 s, giving average slope −0.020 M/s. A tangent at 3 s may be steeper or shallower depending on the curve.

Guided question

How would you estimate instantaneous rate from a graph without calculus notation?

Independent work

  1. Calculate two secant slopes.
  2. Estimate one tangent slope from a supplied graph.
  3. Compare early and late reaction rates.

Misconception check

A single average rate can hide large changes in rate during the interval.

Formative check

What graphical feature represents instantaneous rate?

Reveal response

The slope of the tangent line at that time.

Week 19 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Reaction-rate curves often steepen or flatten over time, so one average slope may not represent the entire process.

Retrieval: Use Day 1 average-rate reasoning before interpreting a tangent or local slope.

Quantitative reasoning: Estimate instantaneous rate from the slope of a tangent line, preserving units and sign convention.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish a smooth fitted curve from raw measurements and avoid treating interpolation as direct observation.

Evidence revision: If the estimated instantaneous rate depends strongly on where the tangent is drawn, report the method and uncertainty rather than a falsely exact value.

Model limit: Graphical slope estimates depend on data density, noise, and curve-fitting choices.

Rate-curve transfer extension

Compare average rate over a broad interval with instantaneous rate near the beginning and later in the reaction. Explain why they differ and what the difference reveals about reaction progress.

Slope-estimation extension

Use two points on a tangent line rather than two nearby raw data points when estimating an instantaneous rate from a smooth curve. For example, if the tangent passes through (20 s, 0.12 M) and (40 s, 0.20 M), slope ≈ 0.08 M/20 s = 4.0×10⁻³ M/s.

Compare that with a broad-interval average rate. If the reaction slows over time, the early tangent slope should be steeper than a late one. This provides a graphical reasonableness check on the calculation.
