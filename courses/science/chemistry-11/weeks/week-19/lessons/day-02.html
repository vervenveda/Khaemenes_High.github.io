Rate Curves & Instantaneous Slope

Interpret concentration-time curves and distinguish average from instantaneous rate.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use supplied kinetics datasets and approved simulations. No unsupervised reactive mixtures are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A concentration-time graph encodes reaction progress. The secant slope gives average rate over an interval; the tangent slope estimates instantaneous rate at one time.

Curved data indicate that rate changes as the reaction proceeds.

Slope units come from concentration divided by time and must be reported explicitly.

Vocabulary: rate curve; secant slope; tangent slope; instantaneous rate; derivative concept.

Worked example

A curve drops by 0.12 M over 6 s, giving average slope −0.020 M/s. A tangent at 3 s may be steeper or shallower depending on the curve.

Guided question

How would you estimate instantaneous rate from a graph without calculus notation?

Independent work

  1. Calculate two secant slopes.
  2. Estimate one tangent slope from a supplied graph.
  3. Compare early and late reaction rates.

Misconception check

A single average rate can hide large changes in rate during the interval.

Formative check

What graphical feature represents instantaneous rate?

Reveal response

The slope of the tangent line at that time.
