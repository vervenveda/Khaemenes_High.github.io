Hess's Law & State Functions

Combine thermochemical equations to obtain the enthalpy of a target reaction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use equation cards, simulations, and supplied energy data only. No combustion or reactive demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Enthalpy is a state function: ΔH depends on initial and final states, not the route.

When equations are reversed, the sign of ΔH reverses; when coefficients are multiplied, ΔH scales by the same factor.

Adding valid reaction steps cancels intermediates and adds their ΔH values.

Vocabulary: Hess's law; state function; pathway; intermediate; thermochemical equation.

Worked example

If A→B has +20 kJ and B→C has −50 kJ, then A→C has −30 kJ.

Guided question

What happens to ΔH when a thermochemical equation is doubled?

Independent work

  1. Combine three two-step Hess problems.
  2. Reverse/scale equations correctly.
  3. Audit cancelled species.

Misconception check

Hess's law is not averaging pathway energies; it is algebraic addition of thermochemical steps.

Formative check

Why can different pathways give the same net ΔH?

Reveal response

Enthalpy depends only on initial and final states.
