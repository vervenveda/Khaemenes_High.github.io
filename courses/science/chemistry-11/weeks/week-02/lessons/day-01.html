From Indivisible Spheres to a Nuclear Atom

Explain why atomic models are revised when new evidence cannot be explained by an older model.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied data, paper or digital models, and approved simulations unless a separately supervised laboratory protocol is provided. Do not improvise physical procedures from this page.

Explicit science instruction

Scientific models are evidence-based representations, not literal pictures. Dalton’s model explained fixed composition but not internal structure. Electrical evidence revealed electrons, so the indivisible-sphere model became insufficient.

Rutherford’s scattering results forced another revision: most projectiles passed through thin foil while a tiny fraction deflected sharply. A diffuse-positive model could not explain those rare large-angle events well.

The bounded conclusion is that positive charge and most mass are concentrated in a small nucleus; later evidence is needed for a fuller electron model.

Vocabulary: scientific model; electron; nucleus; scattering; model revision.

Worked example with reasoning

Synthetic result: 9,960 of 10,000 particles pass nearly straight, 38 deflect modestly, and 2 deflect sharply. The rare sharp events matter because they require a localized region capable of strong repulsion.

Guided question

Classify “most particles passed through,” “positive charge is concentrated,” and “two particles scattered backward” as observation or inference.

Independent work

  1. Create a table: model, evidence explained, evidence not explained.
  2. Draw a before/after model from Thomson to Rutherford and annotate the evidence that forced the revision.
  3. Explain why model revision is a strength of science.

Misconception check

A model is revised because evidence demands it, not simply because a newer idea exists.

Formative understanding check

Did Rutherford’s experiment directly photograph the nucleus?

Reveal worked response

No. The nucleus was inferred from the scattering pattern.

Public self-check only.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Identify the observation and model change with a supplied organizer.

Core: Explain how scattering challenged Thomson’s model.

Extended: Compare competing models and propose a future discriminating observation.
