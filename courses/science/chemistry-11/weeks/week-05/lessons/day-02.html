Atomic Radius & Effective Nuclear Attraction

Explain broad atomic-radius trends using shell number, shielding, and effective nuclear attraction.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Across a period, electrons are added to the same principal shell while proton number rises. The increasing effective attraction generally pulls the electron cloud closer, so atomic radius tends to decrease from left to right.

Down a group, an additional principal shell increases distance and shielding. Those effects generally outweigh the larger nuclear charge, so radius increases down the group.

Trend language should remain broad because electron-electron interactions and measurement definitions can produce deviations.

Vocabulary: atomic radius; shielding; effective nuclear charge; principal shell; trend.

Worked example with reasoning

Na and Cl are in Period 3. Cl has more protons while its valence electrons remain in the third principal shell, so stronger effective attraction generally produces a smaller radius for Cl.

Guided question

Predict which is larger, Li or K, and explain using shell number and shielding rather than a memorized arrow.

Independent work

  1. Rank Na, Mg, and Cl by expected radius.
  2. Rank Li, Na, and K by expected radius.
  3. Explain both rankings with shell and shielding reasoning.

Misconception check

Radius does not decrease across a period because electrons are “used up”; the electrostatic balance changes.

Formative understanding check

Why does radius generally increase down a group?

Reveal response

Additional occupied shells increase distance and shielding, generally producing a larger electron cloud.
