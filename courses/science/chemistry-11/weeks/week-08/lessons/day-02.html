Bond Dipoles and Molecular Polarity

Distinguish bond polarity from overall molecular polarity using geometry and vector reasoning.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

A polar bond has unequal electron sharing and can be represented by a bond-dipole vector pointing toward the more electronegative end.

Overall molecular polarity depends on the vector sum of all bond dipoles. Geometry therefore matters: polar bonds can cancel in a symmetric molecule.

A molecule can be nonpolar overall without having nonpolar bonds. Conversely, an asymmetric arrangement of polar bonds can produce a net molecular dipole.

Vocabulary: bond dipole; molecular dipole; polarity; electronegativity; vector sum.

Worked example with reasoning

CO₂ contains two polar C=O bonds, but its linear geometry places equal bond dipoles in opposite directions, so they cancel. H₂O is bent, so its O–H dipoles do not cancel and the molecule is polar.

Guided question

Explain why BF₃ and NH₃ can contain polar bonds but differ in overall molecular polarity.

Independent work

  1. Draw bond-dipole arrows for CO₂, H₂O, BF₃, and NH₃.
  2. Predict whether each molecule is polar or nonpolar overall.
  3. Write one sentence linking symmetry to dipole cancellation.

Misconception check

Bond polarity and molecular polarity are not the same property; the latter depends on both bond dipoles and molecular geometry.

Formative understanding check

Can a molecule with polar bonds be nonpolar overall?

Reveal response

Yes. If geometry is sufficiently symmetric, the bond-dipole vectors can cancel.

Public self-check only.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use arrow diagrams on supplied molecular shapes.

Core: Predict overall polarity from bond polarity plus shape.

Extended: Treat bond dipoles as vectors and explain qualitative cancellation using symmetry.
