Geometry & Polarity Synthesis

Defend a molecular-polarity prediction from Lewis structure through geometry to net dipole.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousWeek Evidence

Explicit science instruction

A complete polarity explanation should move through four linked representations: valence-electron accounting, Lewis structure, electron domains, and three-dimensional geometry.

Only after shape is established should bond-dipole directions be combined. This prevents the common error of labeling a molecule polar simply because it contains polar bonds.

Corrections belong in the evidence record. If the geometry changes after identifying a lone pair, revise the polarity claim rather than hiding the first model.

Vocabulary: representation chain; geometry; dipole; symmetry; revision.

Worked example with reasoning

For NH₃: Lewis structure gives three N–H bonds and one lone pair; four domains imply tetrahedral electron-domain geometry; atomic positions form a trigonal pyramid; the N–H dipoles do not cancel, so the molecule is polar.

Misconception check

The correct polarity claim must follow from geometry, not from electronegativity difference alone.

Formative understanding check

Reveal response

Lewis/electron-domain evidence, molecular geometry, bond-dipole directions, and whether those dipoles cancel.
