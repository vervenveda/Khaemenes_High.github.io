Wavelength, Frequency, and Photon Energy

Calculate frequency from wavelength and compare photon energies.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Electromagnetic waves satisfy c = λν. For light in vacuum, c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s. Convert nanometers to meters before substitution: 1 nm = 1×10⁻⁹ m.

Photon energy is E = hν. Higher frequency therefore means higher photon energy. Since wavelength and frequency are inversely related, shorter wavelength corresponds to higher photon energy.

Vocabulary: wavelength; frequency; speed of light; photon energy; inverse relationship.

Worked example with reasoning

For λ=500 nm, λ=5.00×10⁻⁷ m. Then ν=(3.00×10⁸)/(5.00×10⁻⁷)=6.00×10¹⁴ Hz. A 400 nm photon therefore has higher energy than a 700 nm photon.

Guided question

Calculate frequency for 600 nm light and show the wavelength conversion.

Independent work

  1. Calculate frequencies for 400, 500, and 750 nm.
  2. Rank their photon energies.
  3. Describe one unit-conversion error that would change the answer by orders of magnitude.

Misconception check

Longer wavelength does not mean higher photon energy; wavelength and frequency are inversely related.

Formative understanding check

Which has greater photon energy, 450 nm or 650 nm light?

Reveal response

450 nm light because it has higher frequency.
