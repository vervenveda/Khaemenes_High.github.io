Multi-Criteria Decision Matrix

Use weighted quantitative evidence to compare candidate materials or processes.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Capstone work must use data, simulation, paper models, or teacher-approved low-risk materials only. Do not synthesize hazardous substances or improvise chemical processes.

Explicit capstone instruction

Normalize or score each candidate on defined criteria using transparent rules.

Apply weights that reflect stated priorities, then calculate weighted totals.

A high score cannot excuse violation of a hard safety or feasibility constraint.

Vocabulary: decision matrix; weight; normalization; tradeoff; sensitivity.

Worked example

Scores 8, 6, 9 with weights 0.4, 0.3, 0.3 produce a weighted score of 7.7.

Guided question

How would doubling the weight on lifecycle impact change the interpretation of a decision matrix?

Independent work

  1. Build a decision matrix for at least three candidates.
  2. Calculate weighted totals.
  3. Reject any constraint violations.
  4. Run one sensitivity test.

Misconception check

Decision-matrix weights encode values/priorities; they are not purely objective facts.

Formative check

What should happen to a candidate that violates a non-negotiable safety constraint?

Reveal response

It should be rejected or redesigned regardless of weighted score.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to challenge criteria, chemistry models, evidence quality, tradeoffs, and uncertainty.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a simple three-criterion matrix.

Core: Calculate and defend rankings.

Extended: Perform sensitivity analysis.
