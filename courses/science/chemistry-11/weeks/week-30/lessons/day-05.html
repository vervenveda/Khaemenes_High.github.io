pH & Titration Synthesis

Integrate logarithmic pH reasoning with stoichiometric titration evidence.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual titration or teacher-supervised neutralization in a properly equipped lab only. Never titrate household cleaners or unknown solutions.

Explicit chemistry instruction

pH describes concentration on a logarithmic scale; titration stoichiometry tracks reacting amounts.

A strong solution keeps concentration, strength, endpoint, and equivalence distinct.

Use curve evidence and balanced equations together.

Vocabulary: pH; stoichiometry; equivalence; endpoint; logarithmic scale.

Worked example

A titration calculation can determine moles at equivalence while the pH curve independently shows the rapid transition region.

Guided question

Write a four-step method for solving a simple titration problem.

Independent work

  1. Solve one pH calculation.
  2. Solve one titration calculation.
  3. Interpret one curve.
  4. Record one correction.

Spiral retrieval bridge

  1. From Week 29: distinguish acid strength from concentration.
  2. From Week 17: use solution stoichiometry carefully.
  3. From Week 12: convert molarity and volume with units.

Misconception check

Acid strength, acid concentration, and pH are related but not interchangeable.

Formative check

What mathematical feature makes pH non-linear?

Reveal response

It is logarithmic.
