Titration Stoichiometry & Equivalence

Use stoichiometry to interpret an acid–base titration at equivalence.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual titration or teacher-supervised neutralization in a properly equipped lab only. Never titrate household cleaners or unknown solutions.

Explicit chemistry instruction

At equivalence, reacting acid/base amounts satisfy the balanced stoichiometric ratio.

For simple monoprotic strong acid–strong base reactions, moles acid and base are equal at equivalence.

Endpoint is an observed indicator/instrument signal; equivalence is the stoichiometric condition.

Vocabulary: titration; equivalence point; endpoint; aliquot; stoichiometry.

Worked example

25.0 mL of 0.100 M monoprotic acid contains 0.00250 mol H+. It requires 0.00250 mol OH- for 1:1 equivalence.

Guided question

How many moles are in 20.0 mL of 0.150 M acid?

Independent work

  1. Solve three equivalence stoichiometry problems.
  2. Distinguish endpoint from equivalence.
  3. Audit units and reaction ratio.

Misconception check

Endpoint and equivalence are not identical concepts, though a good indicator makes them close.

Formative check

What determines mole ratio at equivalence?

Reveal response

The balanced reaction stoichiometry.
