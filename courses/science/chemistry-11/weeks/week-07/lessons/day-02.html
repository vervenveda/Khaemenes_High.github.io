Lewis Structures and Valence-Electron Accounting

Build Lewis structures by accounting for valence electrons, connectivity, bonds, and lone pairs.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Lewis structures track valence electrons using bonds and lone-pair dots. Begin by summing valence electrons from all atoms and adjust for overall ionic charge.

A bond line represents a shared pair of electrons. After choosing a plausible skeleton, distribute electrons to satisfy common duet/octet patterns, then verify the total electron count.

Lewis models are bookkeeping tools. They are useful for connectivity and electron-pair patterns but do not show true electron-cloud shape or molecular motion.

Vocabulary: Lewis structure; valence electron; lone pair; bonding pair; octet model.

Worked example with reasoning

CO₂ has 4 + 2(6)=16 valence electrons. A structure O=C=O uses two double bonds and leaves two lone pairs on each oxygen, accounting for all 16 electrons.

Guided question

Count total valence electrons for NH₃ and sketch a Lewis structure that uses all electrons.

Independent work

  1. Count valence electrons for H₂O, CH₄, CO₂, and NH₃.
  2. Draw one defensible Lewis structure for each.
  3. Perform a final electron audit on every structure.

Misconception check

An octet is a useful main-group pattern, not an absolute rule that applies unchanged to every element and compound.

Formative understanding check

Why should you count electrons again after finishing a Lewis structure?

Reveal response

The final audit checks that the drawing uses exactly the available valence electrons and matches the overall charge.

Public self-check only.
