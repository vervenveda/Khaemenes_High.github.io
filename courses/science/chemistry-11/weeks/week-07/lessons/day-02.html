Lewis Structures and Valence-Electron Accounting

Build Lewis structures by accounting for valence electrons, connectivity, bonds, and lone pairs.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Lewis structures track valence electrons using bonds and lone-pair dots. Begin by summing valence electrons from all atoms and adjust for overall ionic charge.

A bond line represents a shared pair of electrons. After choosing a plausible skeleton, distribute electrons to satisfy common duet/octet patterns, then verify the total electron count.

Lewis models are bookkeeping tools. They are useful for connectivity and electron-pair patterns but do not show true electron-cloud shape or molecular motion.

Vocabulary: Lewis structure; valence electron; lone pair; bonding pair; octet model.

Worked example with reasoning

CO₂ has 4 + 2(6)=16 valence electrons. A structure O=C=O uses two double bonds and leaves two lone pairs on each oxygen, accounting for all 16 electrons.

Guided question

Count total valence electrons for NH₃ and sketch a Lewis structure that uses all electrons.

Independent work

  1. Count valence electrons for H₂O, CH₄, CO₂, and NH₃.
  2. Draw one defensible Lewis structure for each.
  3. Perform a final electron audit on every structure.

Misconception check

An octet is a useful main-group pattern, not an absolute rule that applies unchanged to every element and compound.

Formative understanding check

Why should you count electrons again after finishing a Lewis structure?

Reveal response

The final audit checks that the drawing uses exactly the available valence electrons and matches the overall charge.

Week 7 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: A valid Lewis structure must conserve the available valence electrons while giving a chemically plausible arrangement.

Retrieval: Reuse periodic-group valence patterns from Week 5 before drawing bonds and lone pairs.

Quantitative reasoning: Total valence electrons before drawing, subtract electrons assigned to bonds and lone pairs, and confirm that the final count matches the original total.

Evidence-quality task: Treat octet satisfaction as a useful heuristic rather than an infallible law; identify where hydrogen, odd-electron species, or expanded-valence cases require special handling.

Evidence revision: Audit structures for electron-count errors before changing atom connectivity.

Model limit: Multiple Lewis structures can satisfy bookkeeping, so additional criteria such as formal charge and experimental structure may be needed.

Lewis-accounting transfer extension

Construct two candidate structures for the same formula, perform full valence-electron audits, and explain what further evidence or model criterion would be needed to prefer one.

Electron-accounting extension

After completing a Lewis structure, perform three audits: total valence electrons conserved, formal charges sum to the overall charge, and the proposed connectivity is chemically plausible. Passing only one audit is insufficient. This separates arithmetic correctness from model plausibility and creates a traceable correction process.
