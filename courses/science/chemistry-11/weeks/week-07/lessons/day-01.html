Covalent Bonding as Shared Electron Density

Explain covalent bonding as an electrostatic model involving shared electron density between nuclei.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext

Explicit science instruction

Covalent bonds are modeled as shared electron density attracted by two nuclei. This differs from the extended ion lattice model used for ionic solids.

Sharing can be unequal when atoms differ in electronegativity, so a bond can be polar even though electrons are not fully transferred.

A structural formula is a representation of connectivity and electron allocation. It is not a photograph of a molecule.

Vocabulary: covalent bond; shared electron density; electronegativity; bond polarity; connectivity.

Worked example with reasoning

In HCl, the H–Cl bond is covalent because electron density is shared, but chlorine attracts that density more strongly. The bond is therefore polar rather than purely ionic or perfectly equal.

Guided question

Explain why H₂ and HCl are both covalent while differing in bond polarity.

Independent work

  1. Classify four simple bonds as nonpolar covalent, polar covalent, or strongly ionic-model candidates using supplied electronegativity data.
  2. Draw electron-density arrows for two polar bonds.
  3. State one limitation of treating bonding categories as rigid boxes.

Misconception check

Covalent does not mean electrons must be shared perfectly equally.

Formative understanding check

Why can a bond be covalent and polar at the same time?

Reveal response

Electron density can be shared while being distributed unequally toward the more electronegative atom.
