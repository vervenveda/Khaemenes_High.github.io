Bond Order, Resonance, and Formal Charge

Compare multiple Lewis representations using bond order, resonance, and formal-charge reasoning.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience HallArchaemenesPreviousNext

Explicit science instruction

Single, double, and triple bonds represent increasing shared electron-pair count in Lewis notation. Higher bond order often corresponds to shorter, stronger bonds within comparable bonding situations.

Some species cannot be represented well by one localized Lewis structure. Resonance uses multiple contributing structures to represent delocalized electron density.

Formal charge is a bookkeeping quantity used to compare candidate Lewis structures. It does not mean the atom literally carries that exact localized charge in the real molecule.

Vocabulary: bond order; resonance; delocalization; formal charge; contributing structure.

Worked example with reasoning

For CO₃²⁻, drawing one C=O and two C–O bonds gives several equivalent placements of the double bond. The actual bonding is better represented as delocalized across the three C–O positions than as one bond permanently different.

Guided question

Why are three equivalent resonance contributors useful for carbonate, and what would be misleading about choosing only one as literal reality?

Independent work

  1. Calculate formal charges in two candidate Lewis structures for a supplied molecule.
  2. Identify equivalent resonance contributors where appropriate.
  3. Explain how resonance changes interpretation of bond equality.

Misconception check

Resonance does not mean the molecule rapidly flips between drawings; the drawings collectively represent one delocalized electronic structure.

Formative understanding check

What is formal charge mainly used for in Lewis-model reasoning?

Reveal response

It helps compare candidate electron-accounting structures and check plausibility.
