Half-Life & Lifecycle Investigation

Use a coin/dice decay model and material-lifecycle data to compare statistical decay and environmental tradeoffs.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use coin/dice half-life models, simulations, and trusted lifecycle datasets only. Do not use radioactive sources or hazardous materials.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A coin/dice simulation can model random decay in a population without using radioactive material.

Record the number remaining after each round and compare with the ideal halving pattern.

A separate lifecycle dataset can compare material extraction, use, and end-of-life impacts.

Vocabulary: decay model; stochastic process; lifecycle; raw data; residual.

Worked example

If 100 tokens begin and 54 remain after one round, the difference from ideal 50 is expected random variation, not model failure by itself.

Guided question

Why do small simulated populations fluctuate more strongly around the ideal half-life curve?

Independent work

  1. Run or analyze a safe decay simulation.
  2. Graph remaining count.
  3. Compare with ideal exponential model.
  4. Analyze one material lifecycle dataset and state one tradeoff.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use simulation/data only. Preserve raw counts, model comparison, lifecycle data, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

A random simulation should not be expected to match the ideal half-life curve exactly in every trial.

Formative check

What improves agreement with the expected decay curve?

Reveal response

Larger populations and repeated trials.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism, quantitative evidence, tradeoffs, and uncertainty.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied simulation data.

Core: Compare stochastic and ideal models.

Extended: Quantify residuals and lifecycle tradeoffs.
