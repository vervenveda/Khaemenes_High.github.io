Mechanism Evidence Investigation

Use supplied rate and energy-profile evidence to compare competing mechanisms.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use energy-profile diagrams, supplied datasets, and approved simulations only. No catalyst/reaction demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

This investigation compares two or more proposed mechanisms against experimental evidence.

Preserve the supplied rate behavior, intermediates, and energy profiles before ranking models.

A stronger mechanism explains both overall stoichiometry and observed kinetics with fewer unsupported assumptions.

Vocabulary: mechanism evidence; rate law; intermediate; energy profile; model comparison.

Worked example

Mechanism A reproduces stoichiometry but predicts dependence on a reactant absent from the observed rate pattern; Mechanism B matches both. Evidence favors B.

Guided question

What two evidence types should a proposed mechanism explain?

Independent work

  1. Preserve the supplied evidence table.
  2. Check whether each mechanism sums to the overall reaction.
  3. Compare predicted/observed rate behavior.
  4. Rank mechanisms and state uncertainty.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use supplied mechanism, rate, and energy-profile evidence. Preserve the comparison matrix, residual conflicts, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

A mechanism that balances atoms but fails the kinetics test is not well supported.

Formative check

What makes one mechanism stronger than another?

Reveal response

Better agreement with multiple independent evidence streams.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism consistency, rate evidence, catalyst claims, and model limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use a mechanism-comparison matrix.

Core: Rank competing models.

Extended: Discuss nonuniqueness and additional tests.

Week 21 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Mechanism proposals are tested by whether multiple independent observations agree with their predictions.

Retrieval: Combine Days 1–3 mechanism, rate-law, and catalyst reasoning.

Quantitative transfer: Compare predicted rate dependence, intermediate behavior, and catalyst effect with a supplied dataset or simulation.

Evidence-quality task: Rank direct kinetic observations, inferred intermediates, and model-based claims by evidential strength.

Evidence revision: If one candidate matches the rate law but fails another observation, preserve the contradiction rather than selecting it prematurely.

Uncertainty: Agreement with available evidence supports a mechanism but does not prove uniqueness.

Mechanism-evidence audit extension

Build a comparison table for two mechanisms using net reaction, rate-law compatibility, intermediate predictions, and catalyst behavior. Identify which evidence is most discriminating.

Competing-mechanism extension

Score each mechanism against independent evidence categories rather than averaging all evidence together. A mechanism that fails net stoichiometry should be rejected immediately; one that passes stoichiometry but conflicts with the rate law remains unsupported even if its energy profile looks plausible.

If two candidates survive, identify a predicted intermediate, isotope effect, catalyst response, or concentration dependence that differs between them and would provide a discriminating test.
