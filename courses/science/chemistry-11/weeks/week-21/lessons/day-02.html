Rate-Determining Step

Explain how a slow elementary step can control observed reaction rate.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use energy-profile diagrams, supplied datasets, and approved simulations only. No catalyst/reaction demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

In a multistep mechanism, the slowest kinetically controlling step can limit overall throughput.

The rate-determining-step idea is a simplified model; real mechanisms may involve comparable steps or pre-equilibria.

A proposed rate-determining step must be consistent with experimental rate behavior.

Vocabulary: rate-determining step; slow step; mechanism; empirical rate; consistency.

Worked example

If Step 1 is much slower than Step 2, reactants can accumulate before Step 1 while Step 2 consumes intermediate rapidly.

Guided question

Why is the slowest step compared with a bottleneck?

Independent work

  1. Rank simple mechanisms by likely bottleneck.
  2. Compare a proposed slow step with supplied rate data.
  3. State one limitation of the bottleneck analogy.

Misconception check

The slow step cannot be chosen only from which step 'looks complicated'; evidence is required.

Formative check

What evidence can support a rate-determining-step claim?

Reveal response

Observed kinetics consistent with the reactants/order implied by the proposed controlling step.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism consistency, rate evidence, catalyst claims, and model limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use bottleneck diagrams.

Core: Connect mechanism to rate evidence.

Extended: Analyze cases where one-step control is oversimplified.

Week 21 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: In a multistep pathway, the slowest kinetically controlling step can strongly influence the observed rate behavior.

Retrieval: Reuse elementary-step logic from Day 1 and empirical rate-law evidence from Week 19.

Quantitative reasoning: Compare the concentration dependence predicted by a proposed slow step with the measured rate law.

Evidence-quality task: Treat “rate-determining step” as a simplified kinetic model, not a universal statement that one step is always infinitely slower than all others.

Evidence revision: If the slow-step prediction disagrees with the observed rate law, revise the mechanism or consider pre-equilibrium/complex behavior.

Model limit: Real kinetics can involve comparable step rates, reversibility, and changing control across conditions.

Rate-law compatibility extension

For a candidate mechanism, derive the simple rate expression suggested by its proposed slow step and compare it with the experimental rate law. Identify the exact mismatch if the mechanism fails.

Slow-step reasoning extension

Suppose a candidate slow elementary step is A + B → intermediate. A simple mechanism would predict rate proportional to [A][B]. If the measured rate is independent of B, that candidate slow step is inconsistent unless an earlier equilibrium or another mechanism changes the observable dependence.

Use this mismatch constructively: the rate law can eliminate mechanisms even when it does not identify a unique replacement.

Rate-law validation note

Use at least one independent trial comparison to verify that the proposed slow-step dependence matches the observed concentration response.

Final rate-law note

A mechanism that predicts the wrong concentration dependence is inconsistent with the observed kinetics even if its stoichiometry is correct.

Rate-law discrimination extension

Use the observed rate law to eliminate incompatible slow-step proposals before ranking remaining mechanisms. A candidate that predicts dependence on a species absent from the empirical rate law needs additional justified structure, such as a pre-equilibrium, before it remains viable. Record exactly which concentration dependence is predicted and which is observed. This turns the rate law into a discriminating test rather than a decorative formula attached after the mechanism is chosen.
