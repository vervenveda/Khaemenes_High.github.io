Rate-Determining Step

Explain how a slow elementary step can control observed reaction rate.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use energy-profile diagrams, supplied datasets, and approved simulations only. No catalyst/reaction demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

In a multistep mechanism, the slowest kinetically controlling step can limit overall throughput.

The rate-determining-step idea is a simplified model; real mechanisms may involve comparable steps or pre-equilibria.

A proposed rate-determining step must be consistent with experimental rate behavior.

Vocabulary: rate-determining step; slow step; mechanism; empirical rate; consistency.

Worked example

If Step 1 is much slower than Step 2, reactants can accumulate before Step 1 while Step 2 consumes intermediate rapidly.

Guided question

Why is the slowest step compared with a bottleneck?

Independent work

  1. Rank simple mechanisms by likely bottleneck.
  2. Compare a proposed slow step with supplied rate data.
  3. State one limitation of the bottleneck analogy.

Misconception check

The slow step cannot be chosen only from which step 'looks complicated'; evidence is required.

Formative check

What evidence can support a rate-determining-step claim?

Reveal response

Observed kinetics consistent with the reactants/order implied by the proposed controlling step.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism consistency, rate evidence, catalyst claims, and model limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use bottleneck diagrams.

Core: Connect mechanism to rate evidence.

Extended: Analyze cases where one-step control is oversimplified.
