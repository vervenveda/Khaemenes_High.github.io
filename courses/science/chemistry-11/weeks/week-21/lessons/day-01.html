Reaction Mechanisms & Elementary Steps

Explain why an overall reaction can proceed through multiple elementary steps.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use energy-profile diagrams, supplied datasets, and approved simulations only. No catalyst/reaction demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A reaction mechanism is a sequence of elementary steps whose sum reproduces the overall reaction.

Intermediates are formed in one step and consumed in another; they cancel from the overall equation.

The overall balanced equation does not usually reveal the detailed mechanism by itself.

Vocabulary: mechanism; elementary step; intermediate; overall reaction; pathway.

Worked example

Step 1 forms intermediate X; Step 2 consumes X. Adding the steps cancels X, leaving the overall reaction.

Guided question

Why can two mechanisms share the same overall balanced equation?

Independent work

  1. Identify intermediates in three mechanisms.
  2. Add elementary steps to recover an overall equation.
  3. Explain why mechanism evidence must come from kinetics or other observations.

Misconception check

A balanced equation is not automatically an elementary molecular event.

Formative check

What is an intermediate?

Reveal response

A species produced in one elementary step and consumed in a later step.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism consistency, rate evidence, catalyst claims, and model limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use step-cancellation tables.

Core: Analyze multistep mechanisms.

Extended: Evaluate whether proposed steps reproduce both stoichiometry and kinetic evidence.

Week 21 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Many overall reactions occur through several elementary steps rather than one simultaneous molecular event.

Retrieval: Connect Week 19 empirical rate laws and Week 20 collision theory to multistep reaction models.

Quantitative/model transfer: Sum proposed elementary steps and verify that intermediates cancel to reproduce the overall reaction.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish a plausible mechanism from a uniquely demonstrated mechanism; several pathways can fit the same overall equation.

Evidence revision: Reject any proposed mechanism that fails to sum to the observed net reaction or conflicts with the empirical rate law.

Model limit: Elementary-step molecularity is a model of individual events and should not be inferred directly from overall stoichiometric coefficients.

Mechanism-consistency extension

Given two candidate mechanisms, test each against the overall equation and identify any intermediates. State which candidate survives the first consistency check and what additional rate evidence would still be needed.

Elementary-step audit extension

For a proposed two-step mechanism, add the elementary equations algebraically. Species produced in one step and consumed in another should cancel as intermediates; catalysts may be consumed early and regenerated later. The remaining species must match the experimentally observed overall reaction exactly.

Then compare molecularity with plausibility. Unimolecular and bimolecular elementary steps are common modeling choices, while highly simultaneous many-particle collisions are generally less plausible and should demand stronger evidence.
