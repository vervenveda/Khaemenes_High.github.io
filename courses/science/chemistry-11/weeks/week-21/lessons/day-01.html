Reaction Mechanisms & Elementary Steps

Explain why an overall reaction can proceed through multiple elementary steps.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use energy-profile diagrams, supplied datasets, and approved simulations only. No catalyst/reaction demonstrations are required.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A reaction mechanism is a sequence of elementary steps whose sum reproduces the overall reaction.

Intermediates are formed in one step and consumed in another; they cancel from the overall equation.

The overall balanced equation does not usually reveal the detailed mechanism by itself.

Vocabulary: mechanism; elementary step; intermediate; overall reaction; pathway.

Worked example

Step 1 forms intermediate X; Step 2 consumes X. Adding the steps cancels X, leaving the overall reaction.

Guided question

Why can two mechanisms share the same overall balanced equation?

Independent work

  1. Identify intermediates in three mechanisms.
  2. Add elementary steps to recover an overall equation.
  3. Explain why mechanism evidence must come from kinetics or other observations.

Misconception check

A balanced equation is not automatically an elementary molecular event.

Formative check

What is an intermediate?

Reveal response

A species produced in one elementary step and consumed in a later step.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit mechanism consistency, rate evidence, catalyst claims, and model limits.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use step-cancellation tables.

Core: Analyze multistep mechanisms.

Extended: Evaluate whether proposed steps reproduce both stoichiometry and kinetic evidence.
