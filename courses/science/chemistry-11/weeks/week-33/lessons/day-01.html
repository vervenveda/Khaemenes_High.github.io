Oxidation, Reduction & Electron Flow

Identify oxidation and reduction and track electron transfer explicitly.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons.

In a redox process, electrons lost by one species are gained by another.

Oxidation numbers are bookkeeping tools that help identify which atoms are oxidized or reduced.

Vocabulary: oxidation; reduction; electron transfer; oxidation number; redox.

Worked example

Zn→Zn²⁺+2e⁻ is oxidation because zinc loses electrons.

Guided question

In Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu, which process occurs?

Independent work

  1. Assign oxidation/reduction in four half-reactions.
  2. Track electron count.
  3. Identify oxidizing and reducing agents.

Misconception check

Oxidation does not require oxygen; the modern definition is electron loss.

Formative check

What happens to electrons during reduction?

Reveal response

They are gained.
