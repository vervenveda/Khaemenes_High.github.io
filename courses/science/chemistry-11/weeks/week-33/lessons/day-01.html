Oxidation, Reduction & Electron Flow

Identify oxidation and reduction and track electron transfer explicitly.

Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons.

In a redox process, electrons lost by one species are gained by another.

Oxidation numbers are bookkeeping tools that help identify which atoms are oxidized or reduced.

Vocabulary: oxidation; reduction; electron transfer; oxidation number; redox.

Worked example

Zn→Zn²⁺+2e⁻ is oxidation because zinc loses electrons.

Guided question

In Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu, which process occurs?

Independent work

  1. Assign oxidation/reduction in four half-reactions.
  2. Track electron count.
  3. Identify oxidizing and reducing agents.

Instructional depth extension · oxidation-number evidence

Oxidation is loss of electrons and reduction is gain of electrons, but oxidation numbers provide a bookkeeping method when explicit electrons are not written. An increase in oxidation number identifies oxidation; a decrease identifies reduction.

Worked example: in Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, zinc changes from 0 to +2 and is oxidized, while copper changes from +2 to 0 and is reduced. The electron counts must balance: two electrons lost by Zn match two gained by Cu²⁺.

The oxidizing agent is the species that is reduced, and the reducing agent is the species that is oxidized. Naming the agent requires following the electron-transfer evidence, not the everyday meaning of “oxidizing.”

Misconception check

Oxidation does not require oxygen; the modern definition is electron loss.

Formative check

What happens to electrons during reduction?

Reveal response

They are gained.

Week 33 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Redox reactions transfer electrons between species, and electrochemical systems make that transfer measurable as electrical work.

Retrieval: Connect Week 17 oxidation-number bookkeeping with Week 32 free-energy direction.

Quantitative/model transfer: Identify oxidation and reduction half-reactions, track electron loss/gain, and verify equal electron counts before combining them.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish formal oxidation-state bookkeeping from the physical electron flow through an external circuit.

Evidence revision: Correct any claim that oxidation means “adding oxygen” in every context or that electrons flow through the salt bridge.

Model limit: Half-reactions are bookkeeping models that represent net electron transfer, not necessarily elementary mechanistic steps.

Electron-flow transfer extension

For a redox pair, write both half-reactions, identify electron donor/acceptor, and explain which species is oxidized and reduced using both oxidation numbers and electron accounting.
