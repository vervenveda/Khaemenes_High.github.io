Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Electrochemical cells translate chemical free-energy differences into electrical work through separated redox reactions.

A strong explanation tracks electrons, ions, half-reactions, voltage, and energy source.

Real battery performance also depends on resistance, capacity, materials, kinetics, and safety.

Vocabulary: redox; cell potential; energy conversion; capacity; internal resistance.

Worked example

A galvanic cell can have a favorable voltage yet deliver limited total energy if only a small amount of reactant is available.

Week 33 synthesis challenge

Defend an electrochemical-cell prediction using balanced half-reactions, cell notation, E°cell, ΔG° sign, one uncertainty source, and one real-device limitation.

Electrochemical systems extension

Compare ideal thermodynamic voltage with real device performance. Open-circuit voltage reflects driving force, while current under load depends on resistance and kinetics. A battery can have favorable ΔG and still deliver poor power if transport or electrode processes are slow.

Use an end-to-end audit: balance half-reactions, assign electrodes, predict electron direction, calculate E°cell, infer ΔG° sign, then identify at least one nonideal device limitation.

Cell-performance extension

Separate voltage, current, energy, and power. Cell potential describes driving force per unit charge; current describes charge flow rate; electrical energy depends on charge transferred and voltage; power depends on how quickly energy is delivered.

This distinction helps explain why two cells with similar voltage can perform very differently under load because of internal resistance, electrode area, transport limits, and reaction kinetics.
