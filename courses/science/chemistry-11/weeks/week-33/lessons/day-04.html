Electrochemistry Investigation

Use a virtual galvanic cell to connect half-reactions, voltage, and electron flow.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Preserve selected electrodes/electrolytes and measured simulated voltage before interpretation.

Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions that correspond to the observed electron direction.

Compare two cells while changing only one component where possible.

Vocabulary: virtual cell; half-reaction; voltage; controlled comparison; uncertainty.

Worked example

If Cell A gives 1.10 V and Cell B 0.50 V under comparable conditions, A provides larger electrical energy per coulomb.

Guided question

Which change should be controlled when comparing two electrode combinations?

Independent work

  1. Preserve cell setup and voltage.
  2. Write half-reactions.
  3. Trace electron/ion paths.
  4. Compare two cells and state one uncertainty.

Investigation / Data Lab

Use simulation or supervised low-risk cells only. Preserve setup, voltage, half-reactions, comparison, uncertainty, and one revision.

Week 33 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Electrochemical measurements can test whether a proposed cell model predicts the observed voltage and polarity.

Retrieval: Combine Days 1–3 redox, cell architecture, and potential reasoning.

Quantitative transfer: Record electrode identities, solution conditions, measured voltage, polarity, and expected E°cell under the stated approximation.

Evidence-quality task: Audit meter polarity, electrode cleanliness, concentration differences, salt-bridge continuity, and contact resistance.

Evidence revision: If measured voltage is lower than ideal, investigate resistance and nonstandard conditions before concluding the redox model is wrong.

Uncertainty: Small voltage differences can be sensitive to instrument resolution and concentration assumptions.

Electrochemistry-investigation extension

Compare two candidate cell setups and determine which should produce the larger voltage, then explain which measurement would best test that prediction.

Electrochemical data-quality extension

Compare measured voltage with the standard prediction only after checking whether the solutions are actually near standard concentration and temperature. A lower observed voltage can result from concentration differences, internal resistance, electrode surface condition, or slow electron-transfer kinetics rather than an incorrect redox pairing.

Reverse the meter leads once as a polarity check. The measured magnitude should be similar but the sign should reverse, which helps distinguish wiring errors from chemical behavior.

Measurement-diagnostic extension

Use repeated voltage readings to distinguish drift from random noise. If voltage slowly decreases while the circuit remains open, inspect solution mixing, electrode surface changes, or meter loading. If the sign flips unexpectedly, verify lead orientation and half-cell identity before revising the chemistry.

Compare measured polarity with the predicted spontaneous electron-flow direction as an independent consistency check.

Electrochemical evidence extension

Preserve raw voltage data, electrode identities, electrolyte conditions, temperature, and measurement polarity. A measured cell potential can differ from the standard value because concentrations are nonstandard, internal resistance exists, electrodes are contaminated, or the meter connection is reversed. Diagnose the direction of discrepancy and separate model conditions from instrument behavior before revising the redox chemistry.
