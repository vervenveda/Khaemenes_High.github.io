Cell Potential & Energy Conversion

Interpret cell potential as evidence about electrical energy per charge.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Cell potential is a voltage difference generated by the redox system.

A positive cell potential for the chosen direction is consistent with a thermodynamically favorable galvanic process under stated conditions.

Voltage is energy per charge, so total energy transfer also depends on the amount of charge moved.

Vocabulary: cell potential; voltage; energy per charge; spontaneous direction; electrode potential.

Worked example

A 1.10 V cell can transfer 1.10 J of energy per coulomb of charge in the ideal interpretation.

Guided question

Why can't voltage alone tell you the total energy delivered by a battery?

Independent work

  1. Compare supplied cell potentials.
  2. Identify favorable direction.
  3. Convert voltage conceptually to J/C.

Instructional depth extension · cell potential and thermodynamic meaning

Standard cell potential is found from reduction potentials using E°cell=E°cathode−E°anode when both table entries are written as reductions. A positive E°cell supports a spontaneous galvanic direction under standard-state assumptions.

Worked example: if E°cathode=+0.34 V and E°anode=−0.76 V, then E°cell=0.34−(−0.76)=1.10 V.

Voltage is energy per charge, so a larger cell potential indicates greater electrical energy transfer per coulomb—not automatically greater total energy capacity. Capacity also depends on how much reactant can undergo redox change.

Misconception check

Higher voltage does not by itself tell you battery capacity or total stored energy.

Formative check

What does one volt equal dimensionally?

Reveal response

One joule per coulomb.
