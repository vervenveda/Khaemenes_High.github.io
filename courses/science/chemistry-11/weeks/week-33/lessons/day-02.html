Galvanic Cells & Electrode Roles

Explain how separated redox half-reactions can drive electron flow through a circuit.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use a galvanic-cell simulation or teacher-supervised low-risk cells only. No mains electricity, concentrated electrolytes, or improvised hazardous cells.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A galvanic cell separates oxidation and reduction so electrons travel through an external circuit.

Oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode.

Ion movement through the electrolyte or salt bridge maintains charge balance.

Vocabulary: anode; cathode; galvanic cell; salt bridge; electron flow.

Worked example

In a Zn/Cu galvanic model, zinc can oxidize at the anode while copper ions reduce at the cathode.

Guided question

Why is an ion-conducting path needed in addition to the wire?

Instructional depth extension · galvanic-cell architecture

In a spontaneous galvanic cell, oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction occurs at the cathode. Electrons travel through the external circuit from anode to cathode, while ion migration through the electrolyte/salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality.

Worked example: for a Zn/Cu cell, Zn(s) → Zn²⁺+2e⁻ occurs at the anode and Cu²⁺+2e⁻ → Cu(s) occurs at the cathode. Electrons therefore move through the wire from the zinc electrode toward the copper electrode.

The salt bridge does not carry electrons through solution. It permits ionic movement that prevents charge buildup from stopping the redox process.

Misconception check

Electrons move through the external conductor; the salt bridge primarily carries ions.

Formative check

Where does oxidation occur in a galvanic cell?

Reveal response

At the anode.
