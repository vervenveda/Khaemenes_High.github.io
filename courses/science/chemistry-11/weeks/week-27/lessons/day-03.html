Temperature & K

Explain why temperature can change the equilibrium constant.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual equilibrium perturbations and supplied datasets only; no unsupervised color-change chemistry.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Temperature is special because it changes K, not just Q.

Heating favors the endothermic direction while cooling favors the exothermic direction in the simplified model.

Treating heat as a reactant/product is bookkeeping shorthand rather than a literal species model.

Vocabulary: temperature; K; endothermic; exothermic; equilibrium constant.

Worked example

For an exothermic forward reaction, heating tends to favor the reverse direction and changes K.

Guided question

Why is temperature different from adding reactant?

Independent work

  1. Predict four temperature responses.
  2. Use the sign of ΔH.
  3. Identify which perturbation changes K.

Misconception check

A catalyst does not change K or equilibrium position.

Formative check

What common perturbation changes K directly?
