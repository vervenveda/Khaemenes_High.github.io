KHAEMENES ACADEMY · CHEMISTRY 11 · WEEK 27 · DAY 03

Temperature & K

Explain why temperature can change the equilibrium constant.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual equilibrium perturbations and supplied datasets only; no unsupervised color-change chemistry.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Temperature is special because it changes K, not just Q.

Heating favors the endothermic direction while cooling favors the exothermic direction in the simplified model.

Treating heat as a reactant/product is bookkeeping shorthand rather than a literal species model.

Vocabulary: temperature; K; endothermic; exothermic; equilibrium constant.

Worked example

For an exothermic forward reaction, heating tends to favor the reverse direction and changes K.

Guided question

Why is temperature different from adding reactant?

Independent work

  1. Predict four temperature responses.
  2. Use the sign of ΔH.
  3. Identify which perturbation changes K.

Misconception check

A catalyst does not change K or equilibrium position.

Formative check

What common perturbation changes K directly?

Reveal response

Temperature.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit whether Q, K, and rate effects are being kept distinct.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use ΔH labels.

Core: Predict temperature effects.

Extended: Connect response with thermodynamic reasoning.

Week 27 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Temperature is different from concentration or pressure because changing temperature can change the equilibrium constant itself.

Retrieval: Connect Week 23–25 thermochemistry to equilibrium composition.

Quantitative/model transfer: Treat heat as a conceptual reactant for endothermic direction or product for exothermic direction when building a qualitative prediction, then state that K changes with temperature.

Evidence-quality task: Separate kinetic speeding from equilibrium-position change; heating affects both rate and thermodynamic balance.

Evidence revision: Correct the claim that catalysts and temperature act the same way on equilibrium.

Model limit: Qualitative heat-placement is a teaching model; quantitative K(T) behavior requires thermodynamic relationships.

Temperature-equilibrium transfer extension

Predict how increasing temperature changes K for an endothermic versus exothermic reaction and explain why the direction cannot be inferred from collision theory alone.
