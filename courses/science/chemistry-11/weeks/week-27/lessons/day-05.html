Le Châtelier Synthesis

Defend system-response predictions using Q/K and thermochemical reasoning.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual equilibrium perturbations and supplied datasets only; no unsupervised color-change chemistry.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Le Châtelier's principle is most reliable when grounded in equilibrium expressions and thermodynamics.

Concentration and pressure stresses primarily change Q; temperature can change K.

Catalysts alter the approach rate but not equilibrium composition.

Vocabulary: Le Châtelier; Q; K; catalyst; perturbation.

Worked example

A catalyst helps a system reach equilibrium faster from either direction but leaves K and the equilibrium composition unchanged.

Guided question

Explain why catalyst addition is not an equilibrium shift.

Independent work

  1. Predict one concentration case.
  2. Predict one pressure case.
  3. Predict one temperature case.
  4. Classify whether Q, K, or both change.

Spiral retrieval bridge

  1. From Week 26: explain Q versus K.
  2. From Week 23: distinguish heat flow from temperature.
  3. From Week 21: distinguish catalyst effects on rate from thermodynamics.

Misconception check

Not every change to a system changes equilibrium position.

Formative check

What does a catalyst change?

Reveal response

Rates/pathway, not K or equilibrium position.
