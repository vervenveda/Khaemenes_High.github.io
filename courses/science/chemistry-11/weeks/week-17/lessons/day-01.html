Oxidation Numbers & Electron Transfer

Use oxidation numbers to identify oxidation and reduction in simple reactions.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use oxidation-number analysis, supplied datasets, and approved simulations. Any solution work should remain virtual or teacher-supervised with classroom-safe materials.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Oxidation numbers are bookkeeping values that help track electron transfer. Oxidation is an increase in oxidation number; reduction is a decrease.

The species oxidized loses electron density in the bookkeeping model, while the reduced species gains it.

Oxidizing and reducing agents are named by what they cause in the other species.

Vocabulary: oxidation number; oxidation; reduction; oxidizing agent; reducing agent.

Worked example

In Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, Zn goes 0→+2 and is oxidized; Cu goes +2→0 and is reduced.

Guided question

For Mg + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂, identify what is oxidized and reduced.

Independent work

  1. Assign oxidation numbers in five simple species.
  2. Identify oxidation and reduction in three reactions.
  3. Name the oxidizing and reducing agents.

Misconception check

Oxidation does not require oxygen; it is defined by electron/oxidation-number change.

Formative check

What happens to oxidation number during oxidation?

Reveal response

It increases.
