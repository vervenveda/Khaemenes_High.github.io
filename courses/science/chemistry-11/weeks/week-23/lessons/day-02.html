Calorimetry & q=mcΔT

Calculate heat transfer from mass, specific heat, and temperature change.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual calorimetry or teacher-supervised water-based calorimetry only. No unsupervised flames, reactive chemicals, or improvised heat sources.

Explicit chemistry instruction

For a substance with approximately constant specific heat, q=mcΔT.

Mass must match the units of specific heat, and ΔT=T_final−T_initial.

In simple calorimetry, heat lost by one part is approximated as heat gained by another when container/environment losses are negligible.

Vocabulary: calorimetry; specific heat; temperature change; energy conservation.

Worked example

100 g water warms by 5.0°C. With c=4.18 J/(g·°C), q=100×4.18×5.0=2090 J.

Guided question

A 50 g water sample cools by 8°C. Calculate q for the water and interpret the sign.

Independent work

  1. Solve four q=mcΔT problems.
  2. Perform a unit/sign audit.
  3. Write one assumption behind ideal calorimetry.

Misconception check

A negative ΔT does not mean negative temperature; it means the sample cooled.

Formative check

What determines the sign of q in q=mcΔT?

Reveal response

The sign of ΔT under the chosen convention.
