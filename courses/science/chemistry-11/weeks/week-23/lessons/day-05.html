Thermochemistry Synthesis

Defend an energy-transfer claim using calorimetry, enthalpy, and model assumptions.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual calorimetry or teacher-supervised water-based calorimetry only. No unsupervised flames, reactive chemicals, or improvised heat sources.

Explicit chemistry instruction

A complete thermochemistry explanation links observed temperature change to heat transfer and then to the system’s enthalpy change.

The sign convention, system boundary, units, and ideal-calorimeter assumptions must remain explicit.

A strong claim is proportional to the data and uncertainty.

Vocabulary: energy accounting; enthalpy; calorimetry; uncertainty; model scope.

Worked example

Observed water warming supports an exothermic-system claim only after identifying the water as surroundings and accounting for sign reversal.

Week 23 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Thermochemistry links observable temperature change to conservation-based energy accounting.

Retrieval: Integrate system boundaries, q sign, calorimetry, and enthalpy scaling.

Evidence-quality task: Separate measured temperatures, calculated heat, inferred reaction heat, and normalized molar enthalpy.

Evidence revision: Correct one sign-convention error and one molar-scaling error.

Transfer: Evaluate whether two experiments with different sample amounts can still yield the same molar ΔH.

Model limit: A consistent ΔH estimate depends on clearly defined reaction conditions and an adequate energy-balance model.

Week 23 synthesis challenge

Complete a calorimetry-to-enthalpy chain from raw data through q, reaction heat, molar ΔH, uncertainty, and one experimental limitation.

Thermochemistry audit extension

Use an end-to-end check: define system, determine ΔT, calculate q for the measured surroundings, infer qreaction with sign reversal, divide by reacting moles if a molar enthalpy is needed, and state the dominant uncertainty.

Then test reasonableness. Repeating the same reaction with twice as much material should roughly double total q but leave molar ΔH similar if conditions and model assumptions are comparable.
