KHAEMENES ACADEMY · CHEMISTRY 11 · WEEK 23 · DAY 04

Calorimetry Investigation

Use virtual or supervised water calorimetry to estimate an energy change and evaluate heat-loss uncertainty.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual calorimetry or teacher-supervised water-based calorimetry only. No unsupervised flames, reactive chemicals, or improvised heat sources.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Preserve raw mass and temperature observations before calculating heat.

Use q=mcΔT for the water/surroundings model, then infer the opposite sign for the reacting system when appropriate.

Compare repeated trials and discuss container heat capacity, environmental exchange, and sensor resolution.

Vocabulary: raw data; calorimeter; heat loss; trial; uncertainty.

Worked example

If water gains +2.1 kJ, an ideal two-part model assigns about −2.1 kJ to the reaction system.

Guided question

Which uncontrolled pathway would make the measured water heat smaller than the true released heat?

Independent work

  1. Preserve raw mass/time/temperature data.
  2. Calculate q for each trial.
  3. Compare trials and calculate one residual or spread measure.
  4. Write one uncertainty and one method revision.

Investigation / Data Lab

Preserve raw calorimetry data, calculations, trial comparison, uncertainty, and one revision.

Misconception check

Calorimetry rarely captures every joule; model limits must be reported rather than hidden.

Formative check

What is the strongest reason to repeat a calorimetry trial?

Reveal response

To evaluate repeatability and uncertainty rather than trust one measurement.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit signs, units, system boundaries, and whether the evidence supports the energy claim.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use supplied data if needed.

Core: Complete full energy accounting.

Extended: Estimate heat-loss bias or container effects.
