System, Surroundings & Heat Flow

Distinguish system and surroundings and assign signs to heat transfer.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual calorimetry or teacher-supervised water-based calorimetry only. No unsupervised flames, reactive chemicals, or improvised heat sources.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Thermochemistry tracks energy transfer between a defined system and its surroundings.

When the system absorbs heat, q_system is positive; when the system releases heat, q_system is negative under the usual chemistry sign convention.

A temperature change in the surroundings can provide evidence about the system’s energy change, but only after the boundary and assumptions are stated.

Vocabulary: system; surroundings; heat; exothermic; endothermic.

Worked example

A reaction warms surrounding water. The water gains heat, so the reacting system released energy to the surroundings.

Guided question

Why can the sign of q differ for the system and surroundings?

Independent work

  1. Classify four scenarios as exothermic or endothermic.
  2. Draw an energy-flow diagram for one reaction.
  3. State the chosen system boundary.

Misconception check

Temperature and heat are not the same quantity; temperature is an intensive measure while heat is energy transferred.

Formative check

What sign does q_system have for an endothermic process?

Reveal response

Positive.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit signs, units, system boundaries, and whether the evidence supports the energy claim.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use energy-flow arrows.

Core: Explain sign convention and system boundary.

Extended: Analyze how redefining the system changes bookkeeping.

Week 23 chemistry depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Energy transfer in chemical processes is interpreted by defining a system and surroundings before assigning heat-flow direction.

Retrieval: Connect prior reaction models to a new conservation question: where does energy go when temperature changes?

Quantitative/model transfer: Use sign conventions consistently: heat absorbed by the system is positive q, while heat released by the system is negative q.

Evidence-quality task: Distinguish measured temperature change from the inferred direction of energy transfer.

Evidence revision: Correct explanations that label a process exothermic or endothermic without specifying the system.

Model limit: Temperature and heat are related but not identical; heat depends on amount, material, and process.

System-boundary transfer extension

For a dissolving, combustion, or phase-change scenario, define the system explicitly, identify the surroundings, and defend the sign of q for the system.

Heat-flow worked extension

If a reaction warms 100.0 g of solution from 22.0°C to 28.5°C, the surroundings gained energy. Under the simple calorimetry model, qsurroundings is positive and qreaction is negative. The sign follows the defined system, not whether the thermometer reading itself is “positive.”

Use the energy-conservation relation qsystem + qsurroundings ≈ 0 when the apparatus is treated as effectively isolated. If the container absorbs appreciable heat or energy escapes to the room, that simple balance becomes incomplete.

Energy-conservation check

When the chosen system loses energy, the surroundings should gain a comparable amount under the simplified isolated model. If the signs do not oppose each other, revisit the system boundary and sign convention before interpreting the chemistry.
