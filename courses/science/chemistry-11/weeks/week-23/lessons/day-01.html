System, Surroundings & Heat Flow

Distinguish system and surroundings and assign signs to heat transfer.

Weekly EvidenceChemistry CourseScience Hall

Safety boundary

Use virtual calorimetry or teacher-supervised water-based calorimetry only. No unsupervised flames, reactive chemicals, or improvised heat sources.

Explicit chemistry instruction

Thermochemistry tracks energy transfer between a defined system and its surroundings.

When the system absorbs heat, q_system is positive; when the system releases heat, q_system is negative under the usual chemistry sign convention.

A temperature change in the surroundings can provide evidence about the system’s energy change, but only after the boundary and assumptions are stated.

Vocabulary: system; surroundings; heat; exothermic; endothermic.

Worked example

A reaction warms surrounding water. The water gains heat, so the reacting system released energy to the surroundings.

Guided question

Why can the sign of q differ for the system and surroundings?

Independent work

  1. Classify four scenarios as exothermic or endothermic.
  2. Draw an energy-flow diagram for one reaction.
  3. State the chosen system boundary.

Misconception check

Temperature and heat are not the same quantity; temperature is an intensive measure while heat is energy transferred.

Formative check

What sign does q_system have for an endothermic process?

Reveal response

Positive.

Mentor evidence conference

Ask Archaemenes to audit signs, units, system boundaries, and whether the evidence supports the energy claim.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: Use energy-flow arrows.

Core: Explain sign convention and system boundary.

Extended: Analyze how redefining the system changes bookkeeping.
