Purpose and instruction

The final asks whether you can transfer biological reasoning into unfamiliar contexts. Build a course map linking chemistry of life, cells, membranes, energy, signaling, homeostasis, division, genetics, molecular information, evolution and ecology. Use arrows labeled with causal or functional relationships.

Mark each connection by evidence type: model, quantitative data, investigation, or CER. This reveals whether knowledge is integrated or merely memorized as isolated units.

Student task

  1. Create a one-page map with at least 25 concepts.
  2. Include at least eight cross-unit links.
  3. Mark five quantitative tools and five model limitations.
  4. Color-code secure/uncertain/review-needed concepts.
  5. Choose three weakest links for focused review.

Mastery check

Readiness means you can explain relationships and transfer them, not merely recognize familiar vocabulary.

Week 36 final-integration checkpoint

Cumulative phenomenon: An unfamiliar biological case can require molecular, cellular, genetic, evolutionary, and ecological reasoning at the same time. Final mastery means connecting scales rather than recalling isolated chapters.

Vocabulary in context: mechanism, system, scale, evidence chain, uncertainty, model, transfer.

Worked synthesis: Start with the observed pattern, identify the biological scale, choose the relevant mechanism, connect one quantitative relationship, and state one limitation. Then link the explanation to at least one other course domain.

Evidence-quality task: Label which parts of the integration map are direct observations, model-based inferences, or broader interpretations.

Retrieval: Retrieve one concept each from cellular biology, genetics, evolution, and ecology without notes and connect them causally.

Integration-map extension

Choose one real or simulated biological case and draw a causal chain that crosses at least three scales—for example DNA sequence → protein function → cell behavior → organism phenotype → population consequence. Mark each arrow as measured, inferred, or model-dependent, then identify the weakest link in the chain.
