Purpose and instruction

The portfolio defense should show a progression from early models to more mature evidence-based reasoning. Select artifacts across laboratory/simulation work, quantitative analysis, models, CER writing, genetics, evolution/ecology and the capstone.

A strong defense includes what changed in your thinking, where evidence forced revision, which uncertainties remain and how you learned to distinguish measurement, model and inference.

Student task

  1. Select 8–10 artifacts spanning the year.
  2. For each, state the learning target and evidence of growth.
  3. Include at least two correction artifacts.
  4. Defend one quantitative model and one investigation.
  5. Explain one ethical/safety boundary that improved your science.
  6. Write a final reflection: what can you now do that you could not do in Week 1?

Mastery check

Course completion is strongest when the record preserves both successes and revisions. A portfolio is evidence of learning history, not a polished folder that hides mistakes.
