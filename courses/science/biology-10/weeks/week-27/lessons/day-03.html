Biogeography as Historical Evidence

Use geographic distributions, barriers and island patterns to test historical explanations.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use published fossil images, maps, anatomical diagrams and fictional/public molecular datasets. Distinguish direct observations from historical inference. Represent scientific models accurately while keeping philosophical or theological claims separate from empirical claims; no student belief is graded.

Explicit science instruction

Biogeography examines where organisms occur and how distributions relate to geology, dispersal, extinction and evolutionary history. Islands often contain close relatives of nearby mainland groups plus unique radiations, while continental patterns can reflect ancient connections and barriers.

Present distribution is shaped by both history and ecology, so no single map automatically reveals cause.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional archipelago has three related lizard species, each closest genetically to mainland species 200 km west rather than to ecologically similar lizards on a distant continent. Dispersal plus island divergence is one testable historical explanation.

Guided question

What evidence would distinguish recent dispersal from ancient continental separation?

Independent work

  1. Interpret a distribution map.
  2. Propose two historical explanations.
  3. List geological and molecular evidence each predicts.
  4. Identify one ecological confounder.

Misconception check

“Species live where they are best adapted” is incomplete; historical barriers, chance dispersal and extinction also shape distributions.

Formative understanding check

Why are island endemics informative?

Worked response

Their restricted distributions and relationships can test hypotheses about colonization, isolation and diversification.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
