Molecular Evidence & Sequence Comparison

Use sequence similarity and differences as evidence while distinguishing measured distance from inferred relationship.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use published fossil images, maps, anatomical diagrams and fictional/public molecular datasets. Distinguish direct observations from historical inference. Represent scientific models accurately while keeping philosophical or theological claims separate from empirical claims; no student belief is graded.

Explicit science instruction

DNA and protein sequences provide many comparable characters. Closely related lineages often share more derived sequence features and fewer differences, but rates vary among genes and lineages. Alignment quality, selection, recombination and gene duplication can complicate inference.

A sequence distance is a measured/computed difference; a phylogenetic relationship is an inference from patterns across data and models.

Worked example with reasoning

Four fictional species have sequence differences from A: B=2, C=7, D=8 across a short region. B is most similar to A in this region, but one short sequence alone is not enough to establish the entire species tree.

Guided question

Why is “fewest differences” useful but not always sufficient for reconstructing ancestry?

Independent work

  1. Calculate pairwise differences for four sequences.
  2. Build a simple distance matrix.
  3. Propose a grouping.
  4. List two reasons another gene might disagree.

Investigation / Data Lab

Evolution evidence stations

Question: Do independent evidence streams converge on the same relationship hypothesis?

  1. Analyze four supplied stations: fossil/stratigraphic data, anatomical characters, biogeographic distribution, and a short molecular alignment.
  2. At each station, record direct observations separately from historical inference.
  3. Score whether each station supports, conflicts with, or is neutral toward two competing relationship hypotheses.
  4. Identify one uncertainty or alternative explanation per station.
  5. Write a synthesis explaining whether convergence increases confidence and which evidence could revise the model.

Evidence standard: preserve the four-station matrix and an observation-versus-inference column.

Misconception check

Sequence similarity is not a percentage of “how evolved” an organism is; all living lineages have their own histories.

Formative understanding check

What is directly observed in a sequence comparison?

Worked response

The base or amino-acid states and their similarities/differences after alignment.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
