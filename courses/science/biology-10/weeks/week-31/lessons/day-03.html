Food Webs & Indirect Effects

Predict direct and indirect ecological effects while keeping alternative pathways and uncertainty visible.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied ecological datasets, safe non-contact observation, and paper/digital food-web models. Do not disturb wildlife or reveal sensitive species locations. Energy-flow models are ecosystem approximations and should include units, area/time scales, and uncertainty.

Explicit science instruction

Food webs are networks rather than ladders. A predator can affect prey directly and producers indirectly; omnivory and alternative resources create multiple pathways. Trophic cascades are indirect effects across levels, but strength depends on context.

Worked example with reasoning

In a fictional lake, predator P eats grazer G; G consumes algae A. Reducing P may increase G and reduce A, but if G has alternative predators or switches food, the response can differ. The web supports a prediction, not certainty.

Guided question

Why does removing one species not have a single guaranteed outcome?

Independent work

  1. Build an eight-node food web.
  2. Identify three direct and three indirect paths.
  3. Predict removal effects with one alternative pathway.
  4. Label documented versus hypothesized links.

Misconception check

Food-web arrows conventionally show energy/matter transfer from resource to consumer, not who “attacks” whom.

Formative understanding check

If A is eaten by B, which direction is the energy-flow arrow?

Worked response

A → B.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.

Mentor evidence conference
