Primary Production: GPP, Respiration & NPP

Quantify how producers capture energy and how respiration changes the energy available for growth and consumers.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied ecological datasets, safe non-contact observation, and paper/digital food-web models. Do not disturb wildlife or reveal sensitive species locations. Energy-flow models are ecosystem approximations and should include units, area/time scales, and uncertainty.

Explicit science instruction

Gross primary production (GPP) is the rate at which producers capture chemical energy through photosynthesis. Producers also use energy in cellular respiration. Net primary production (NPP) is commonly modeled as GPP − producer respiration and represents energy stored in new biomass over a specified area and time.

Vocabulary: GPP; NPP; respiration; biomass; productivity; producer; energy rate.

Worked example with reasoning

A grassland has GPP 2,000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ and producer respiration 900 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹. NPP = 1,100 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹. Units matter because productivity is a rate per area and time.

Guided question

Why is NPP smaller than GPP?

Independent work

  1. Calculate NPP for four datasets.
  2. Track units.
  3. Explain what NPP can support in a food web.
  4. State one measurement limitation.

Misconception check

Plants do respire. Photosynthesis does not mean producers keep all captured energy as biomass.

Formative understanding check

GPP=1500, respiration=600. NPP?

Worked response

900 in the same energy/area/time units.
