Water-Only Capillary Action & Surface-Tension Investigation

Design and evaluate a safe water-only investigation using repeated measurements, controlled variables, and uncertainty to test a property of water.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use only clean water, paper or paper towel, a ruler, a washable dropper or spoon, and optionally a clean coin. Wipe spills promptly. Do not add cleaners, solvents, dyes, flames, electrical devices, or unknown substances. If materials are unavailable, use the supplied model dataset in this lesson.

Explicit science instruction

Investigations are strongest when the measured outcome is defined before data collection. For a capillary-action investigation, one option is to measure how high water rises on equal-width paper strips after the same amount of time. The independent variable might be paper type; the dependent variable is rise height in millimetres; controlled variables include strip width, immersion depth, elapsed time, and starting water level.

Repeated trials help estimate variability. A single unusually high result should not be silently discarded. Record it, check the method, and decide transparently whether the analysis uses all trials. If you cannot perform the hands-on activity, analyze this synthetic teaching dataset for rise height after 120 seconds: Paper A = 22, 24, 23 mm; Paper B = 12, 15, 14 mm.

Surface-tension observations can also be made safely by counting how many similar water drops a clean coin can hold before spilling. That outcome is sensitive to drop size, cleanliness, and technique, so repeated trials and a clear method matter. Do not compare data collected with different droppers as though the procedure were identical.

Vocabulary: independent variable; dependent variable; controlled variable; repeated trial; mean; range; uncertainty; capillary action.

Worked example with reasoning

Using the synthetic capillary dataset, Paper A mean = (22 + 24 + 23) ÷ 3 = 23 mm and range = 24 − 22 = 2 mm. Paper B mean = (12 + 15 + 14) ÷ 3 ≈ 13.7 mm and range = 15 − 12 = 3 mm. The data support the statement that Paper A showed greater capillary rise under this model procedure. They do not show that Paper A will always outperform every other paper or reveal the exact molecular cause without additional evidence.

Guided question

Identify two variables that would make the Paper A/B comparison unfair if they changed between groups. Explain how each could influence rise height.

Independent work

  1. Write a one-sentence testable question and identify independent, dependent, and three controlled variables.
  2. If safe and feasible, run three water-only trials; otherwise use the synthetic dataset.
  3. Calculate mean and range for each condition.
  4. Make a small graph with units.
  5. Write one limitation and one concrete improvement for a second round.

Misconception check

A larger mean difference does not automatically prove a molecular mechanism. The investigation measures a bulk outcome influenced by material structure, procedure, adhesion, cohesion, and measurement uncertainty.

Formative understanding check

Why are three repeated trials more informative than one trial, even if all three do not match exactly?

Show the worked response

Repeated trials reveal variability and make the summary less dependent on one observation. Variation is expected; the goal is to characterize it, not to force identical results.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring the mentor your variable table and one limitation. Ask whether your proposed revision actually addresses that limitation without changing several variables at once.
