Information Flow Synthesis

Integrate transcription, RNA processing and translation while rejecting an overly rigid one-way slogan.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional DNA/RNA/protein sequences and standard codon tables. Do not use personal genomic data or infer disease from sequence examples. Sequence exercises are educational models, not clinical interpretation.

Explicit science instruction

The central information-flow model DNA→RNA→protein is useful for many genes, but biology also includes noncoding RNAs, RNA genomes, reverse transcription, RNA editing, protein modification, and regulation at every step. Models should capture the dominant logic without pretending exceptions do not exist.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional DNA change alters a splice site. The coding codon at that exact base may not be translated, yet mature RNA composition and protein sequence can still change. This shows why sequence-to-phenotype reasoning must include processing.

Guided question

What does the DNA→RNA→protein model explain well, and what does it omit?

Retrieval is formative. The goal is durable connection across units, not a separate grade.

Misconception check

Genes do not act as isolated “blueprints” that directly build traits; expression, regulation, cellular networks, environment and development intervene.

Formative understanding check

What is the strongest reason the genetic code links RNA sequence to protein sequence?

Worked response

Ribosomes and tRNAs interpret codons according to a biochemical mapping between codons and amino acids.
