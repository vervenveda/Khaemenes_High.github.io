RNA Processing & Transcript Diversity

Explain how eukaryotic RNA processing can change the mature transcript without changing the underlying DNA.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional DNA/RNA/protein sequences and standard codon tables. Do not use personal genomic data or infer disease from sequence examples. Sequence exercises are educational models, not clinical interpretation.

Explicit science instruction

Many eukaryotic primary transcripts are processed before translation. A 5′ cap and poly(A) tail affect stability and translation; splicing removes introns and joins exons. Alternative splicing can produce multiple mature RNA isoforms from one gene.

Processing is regulated and context dependent; “one gene = one protein” is therefore an oversimplification.

Worked example with reasoning

Primary transcript exons E1-E2-E3 with introns between them might yield mature E1-E2-E3 or, in an alternative isoform, E1-E3 if E2 is skipped. The DNA locus is unchanged; the RNA processing outcome differs.

Guided question

How can one DNA locus contribute to more than one protein product?

Independent work

  1. Mark introns/exons on a fictional transcript.
  2. Create two splice isoforms.
  3. Predict how an exon skip could alter a protein.
  4. State one limit of the simplified cartoon.

Misconception check

Introns are not simply “junk,” and alternative splicing does not mean cells randomly cut RNA without regulation.

Formative understanding check

Does alternative splicing require changing the DNA sequence?

Worked response

No. Different RNA segments can be joined from the same underlying gene.
