Gel Electrophoresis & Fragment Size

Interpret DNA band patterns and calibration curves without treating a band as a complete identity claim.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use paper models, public educational sequence examples, and fictional DNA. Do not perform unsupervised wet-lab amplification, collect personal DNA, upload personal genomes, or provide procedural gene-editing instructions. Privacy, consent, and dual-use risks are part of the science.

Explicit science instruction

Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments primarily by size through a matrix under an electric field. Smaller fragments generally migrate farther. A DNA ladder provides reference fragment sizes.

Band position estimates size; band intensity can reflect amount but depends on staining, imaging and loading. Matching bands support similarity at tested fragments, not proof of whole-genome identity.

Worked example with reasoning

A ladder has known fragments at 1,000, 700, 500 and 300 bp. An unknown band lies between 700 and 500 closer to 500. A calibration plot of migration distance versus log(fragment size) can estimate size more accurately than visual guessing.

Guided question

Why is a calibration curve stronger than simply saying “this band looks about 600 bp”?

Independent work

  1. Order sample fragments by migration distance.
  2. Estimate three sizes from a supplied ladder.
  3. Plot a simple calibration curve.
  4. State what a matching band can and cannot establish.

Misconception check

A band is not a gene “showing itself”; it represents DNA fragments of similar migration behavior under the assay.

Formative understanding check

Which generally travels farther in the same gel: 300 bp or 1,000 bp?

Worked response

The 300-bp fragment.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
