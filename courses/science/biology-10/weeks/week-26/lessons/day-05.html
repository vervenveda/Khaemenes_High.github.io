Genomics, Consent & Bioethics

Evaluate biotechnology decisions using evidence, privacy, equity, consent and uncertainty.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use paper models, public educational sequence examples, and fictional DNA. Do not perform unsupervised wet-lab amplification, collect personal DNA, upload personal genomes, or provide procedural gene-editing instructions. Privacy, consent, and dual-use risks are part of the science.

Explicit science instruction

Genomic information can reveal information about an individual and biological relatives. Ethical use therefore involves informed consent, data minimization, access control, secondary-use rules, bias, equity and the right not to know in some settings.

Scientific capability does not settle what should be done; ethical decisions require values, rights, stakeholder effects and governance alongside evidence.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional school proposes collecting student DNA to personalize nutrition lessons. Potential scientific value is weak, while privacy, consent, family inference and long-term data risks are substantial. A non-genetic educational alternative achieves the learning goal with far less risk.

Guided question

Why is “we can collect the data” not equivalent to “we should collect the data”?

Independent work

  1. Analyze one fictional biotech case.
  2. List stakeholders and rights.
  3. Separate scientific benefit from ethical justification.
  4. Propose a lower-risk alternative.
  5. Write a decision with uncertainty and revision criteria.

Spiral retrieval bridge

Try these from memory before reopening earlier lessons. Then check your reasoning and record one correction if needed.

  1. From Week 23: why does complementary sequence matter for primers or sequence recognition?
  2. From Week 1: what roles do positive and negative controls play in interpreting a biotechnology assay?

Retrieval is formative. The goal is durable connection across units, not a separate grade.

Misconception check

Genetic privacy is not solved by removing a name alone; genomic data can be inherently identifying and relational.

Formative understanding check

What is data minimization?

Worked response

Collecting only the data necessary for a justified purpose and avoiding unnecessary retention or secondary use.
