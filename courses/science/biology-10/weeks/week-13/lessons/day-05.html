Safety and evidence boundary

This week uses fictional signaling systems, published-style graphs, and card or digital models. It does not diagnose hormone, neurological, immune, or other medical conditions and does not require personal health information.

Explicit science instruction

Cell communication can be summarized as signal → detection → transduction → response → regulation, but each step contains biological choices. Target cells differ in receptor expression, downstream networks, transcriptional state, and context, so the same signal can generate different outcomes.

Quantitative dose–response evidence describes how response magnitude changes with concentration. Perturbation models help test causal pathway structure. Neither alone provides a complete explanation; together, multiple evidence types can strengthen a signaling model.

Communication also creates the foundation for feedback. A cellular or organismal response can alter the original stimulus, receptor sensitivity, signal production, or downstream pathway. Week 14 will extend this logic to homeostasis and dynamic regulation.

Worked example with reasoning

Claim: Cellular response depends on both extracellular signal and intracellular context. Evidence: different receptor/pathway models produce different responses to the same ligand; dose–response curves can differ among cell types; perturbations at specific pathway steps alter predicted output. Reasoning: signals must be detected and interpreted by cell-specific molecular networks before a response occurs. Limitation: simplified diagrams omit many interacting pathways and time-dependent effects.

Misconception check

Cells do not passively obey signals. They interpret signals through dynamic molecular systems. Response depends on receptor and pathway context, and regulation can change with prior exposure, developmental state, or other simultaneous signals.

Formative understanding check

What are the minimum conceptual steps needed to explain a cellular signaling response?

Show the worked response

A signal must be present, a compatible receptor must detect it, intracellular transduction must connect detection to effectors, a measurable response must occur, and regulatory mechanisms must shape or terminate the signal.
