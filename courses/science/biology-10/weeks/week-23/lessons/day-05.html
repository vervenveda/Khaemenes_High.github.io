DNA Replication Synthesis

Integrate structure, complementarity, directionality and repair into one evidence-based copying model.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use paper/digital molecular models and fictional DNA sequences. No biological samples, genetic testing, ancestry inference, or personal genomic data are required. Molecular models are representations and should not be treated as photographs of atoms.

Explicit science instruction

DNA's structure supports copying because each strand constrains a complement, but fidelity depends on enzymes, directionality, origins, proofreading, and repair. The double helix is therefore necessary context, not a self-executing copying machine.

Worked example with reasoning

A sequence-composition problem identifies complementarity; a fork model explains synthesis direction; old/new strand labeling demonstrates semiconservative replication; an error-rate model explains why copying is highly accurate but not perfect.

Guided question

What features of DNA structure enable faithful copying, and what additional cellular machinery is required?

Misconception check

Structure alone does not “cause” replication; enzymes and cellular energy/processes are essential.

Formative understanding check

What is the strongest link between base pairing and replication fidelity?

Worked response

Each template base constrains which complementary base can pair, providing information for polymerase-guided copying.
