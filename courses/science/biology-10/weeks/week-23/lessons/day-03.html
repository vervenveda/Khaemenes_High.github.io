Semiconservative Replication

Explain how each daughter DNA molecule receives one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use paper/digital molecular models and fictional DNA sequences. No biological samples, genetic testing, ancestry inference, or personal genomic data are required. Molecular models are representations and should not be treated as photographs of atoms.

Explicit science instruction

Before cell division, DNA is copied by a coordinated replication system. Helicase-like activities separate strands; primase supplies starting points; DNA polymerases extend new DNA using complementary templates; ligases join discontinuities. Replication is semiconservative: each product duplex contains one old and one new strand.

DNA polymerases synthesize in the 5′→3′ direction, so antiparallel templates create different continuous/discontinuous synthesis patterns.

Worked example with reasoning

Start with parental strands P1 and P2. After replication, duplex A contains P1 + new complement N1; duplex B contains P2 + new complement N2. The two daughter duplexes preserve sequence information by templated complementarity.

Guided question

Why does antiparallel DNA create different synthesis geometry on the two templates?

Independent work

  2. Label old/new strands.
  3. Mark 5′ and 3′ ends.
  4. Explain leading versus discontinuous synthesis conceptually.
  5. State one simplification of the diagram.

Misconception check

DNA polymerase does not copy an entire chromosome in one uninterrupted pass from one end to the other; replication begins at origins and uses coordinated machinery.

Formative understanding check

What does semiconservative mean?

Worked response

Each replicated DNA duplex contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized complementary strand.
