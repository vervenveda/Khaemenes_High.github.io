DNA Architecture: Nucleotides & Antiparallel Strands

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use paper/digital molecular models and fictional DNA sequences. No biological samples, genetic testing, ancestry inference, or personal genomic data are required. Molecular models are representations and should not be treated as photographs of atoms.

Explicit science instruction

DNA is a polymer of nucleotides, each containing deoxyribose, phosphate, and a nitrogenous base. Covalent phosphodiester bonds form a sugar-phosphate backbone. The two strands run in opposite 5′→3′ directions, making the double helix antiparallel.

Base pairing—A with T, G with C—allows sequence information on one strand to constrain the complementary strand while base stacking and backbone chemistry contribute to stability.

Vocabulary: nucleotide; phosphodiester bond; 5′ end; 3′ end; antiparallel; complementary; base pair; double helix.

Worked example with reasoning

Given 5′-A G T C C-3′, the complementary strand is 3′-T C A G G-5′. Written in its own 5′→3′ direction, it is 5′-G G A C T-3′.

Guided question

Why does strand direction matter when writing complementary DNA?

Independent work

  1. Label a nucleotide.
  2. Build a five-base antiparallel duplex.
  3. Write the complement both aligned and 5′→3′.
  4. Identify backbone versus information-bearing bases.

Misconception check

Hydrogen bonds between bases are not the covalent bonds that hold each DNA strand's backbone together.

Formative understanding check

Complement of 5′-ATGC-3′ written 5′→3′?

Worked response

5′-GCAT-3′.

Week 23 DNA-replication checkpoint

Phenomenon: DNA stores information in nucleotide sequence while its double-stranded architecture depends on a sugar-phosphate backbone, complementary base pairing, and antiparallel strand orientation.

Worked model: One strand written 5′-A G C T-3′ pairs with the complementary strand 3′-T C G A-5′. The strands are antiparallel; reversing direction changes how enzymes interact with the template.

Retrieval: Retrieve one chromosome or inheritance idea from Weeks 19–22 and connect it to DNA structure or replication.

Evidence revision: Revise your explanation so it distinguishes nucleotide sequence, strand orientation, complementary pairing, enzyme directionality, and fidelity.

Transfer: Predict what would happen if strand orientation, base pairing, proofreading, or repair function changed.

DNA-structure evidence extension

Structure safeguard: Antiparallel orientation means the two DNA strands run in opposite 5′→3′ directions. Connect this orientation to nucleotide chemistry, complementary base pairing, and why replication enzymes cannot treat both template strands identically.

DNA architecture transfer extension

Use a short paired-strand example to label 5′ and 3′ ends, identify hydrogen-bonded base pairs, and explain why complementarity preserves pairing information without making the two strands chemically identical.
