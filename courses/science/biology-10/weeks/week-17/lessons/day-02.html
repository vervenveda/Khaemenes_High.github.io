Design, Controls, Sources & Method Quality

Build a method that matches the research question and distinguishes background knowledge from evidence generated by the investigation.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use only teacher- or parent-approved low-risk investigations, supplied datasets, paper models, or simulations. No human-subject experimentation, personal health data, biological culturing, unknown environmental samples, hazardous chemicals, animal manipulation, ingestion studies, or medical claims are permitted. If a proposed investigation cannot meet these limits, convert it to a data-analysis or model-based study.

Explicit science instruction

A method should make the evidence relevant to the question. Identify the independent variable or comparison, dependent measure, constants, controls, replication, sampling plan, and measurement rules. When an experimental manipulation is inappropriate, a comparative observational design or supplied dataset can still support strong reasoning if its limits are explicit.

Background sources provide established concepts and methods; they do not substitute for the investigation's own evidence. Source quality depends on expertise, transparency, method, date, relevance, and whether claims can be checked.

Vocabulary: independent variable; dependent variable; control; constant; replicate; randomization; sampling; source credibility; method; confounder.

Worked example with reasoning

Suppose a student compares enzyme-rate data at four temperatures using a supplied dataset. The independent variable is temperature; rate is the dependent measure. A same-method reference condition acts as comparison. Replicates show variability. A source explaining enzyme structure helps interpret the pattern but is not itself the measured rate data.

Guided question

Why can a study use a reliable source and still have a weak method?

Independent work

  1. Write a method table: comparison, response measure, constants, replication, and safety boundary.
  2. Identify one confounder.
  3. Choose two background sources and state what each contributes.
  4. Separate source-derived information from investigation-derived evidence.
  5. Write one method revision that would improve trustworthiness.

Misconception check

More variables do not automatically make an investigation more sophisticated. Changing several factors at once can make causal interpretation weaker.

Formative understanding check

What is the difference between a control and a constant?

Show the worked response

A control is a comparison condition used to interpret change; a constant is a factor intentionally kept the same across conditions so it does not confound the comparison.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: use a method template with one variable and one control already identified. Core: justify each design choice and identify at least one confounder. Extended: compare experimental, observational, and simulation designs for the same question and defend the best feasible approach.
