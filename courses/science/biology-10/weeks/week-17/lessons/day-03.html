Choosing Statistics, Graphs & Uncertainty

Select quantitative summaries and visualizations that fit the data type instead of forcing every dataset into the same graph.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use only teacher- or parent-approved low-risk investigations, supplied datasets, paper models, or simulations. No human-subject experimentation, personal health data, biological culturing, unknown environmental samples, hazardous chemicals, animal manipulation, ingestion studies, or medical claims are permitted. If a proposed investigation cannot meet these limits, convert it to a data-analysis or model-based study.

Explicit science instruction

Data displays should match the question and the structure of the data. A bar chart can compare category summaries; a scatterplot can show relationships between paired quantitative variables; a line graph can show ordered or time-series change when connecting observations is meaningful. Tables remain important because graphs often hide exact values.

Useful summaries include counts, proportions, mean, median, range, percent change, rates, and measures of spread. The appropriate choice depends on distribution, sample size, outliers, and the question. Uncertainty should be reported rather than hidden.

Vocabulary: categorical data; quantitative data; mean; median; range; rate; percent change; scatterplot; time series; uncertainty; outlier.

Worked example with reasoning

Dataset A compares mean enzyme rate at four temperatures: a point or bar display with uncertainty may fit. Dataset B contains individual body-size and metabolic-rate pairs: a scatterplot is more informative because both variables are quantitative. Using four bars for Dataset B would discard the paired relationship.

Guided question

Why is choosing the graph after deciding what story you want to tell a threat to honest analysis?

Independent work

  1. Identify the data type in your investigation.
  2. Select a graph and justify the choice.
  3. Calculate at least one appropriate summary statistic.
  4. Mark missing data or uncertainty honestly.
  5. Write one pattern statement and one statement the graph cannot establish.

Misconception check

A mean is not automatically the “best” summary. Strongly skewed data or extreme outliers may make the median or a full distribution more informative.

Formative understanding check

When is a scatterplot preferable to a line graph?

Show the worked response

When the goal is to examine the relationship between paired quantitative variables without implying that observations form a continuous sequence in time or another ordered dimension.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
