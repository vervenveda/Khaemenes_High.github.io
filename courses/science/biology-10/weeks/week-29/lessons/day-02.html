Fitness, Relative Reproduction & Frequency Change

Quantify differential reproductive contribution without equating fitness with strength or health.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use paper-token simulations and published or fictional trait-frequency data. Do not intentionally expose organisms to harmful agents or breed animals for class. Avoid purpose-based language: populations change through differential reproduction, not because organisms need or want traits.

Explicit science instruction

In evolutionary biology, fitness refers to reproductive contribution relative to alternatives in a specified environment. It is not moral worth, athletic fitness or an absolute property.

Relative fitness can be modeled by multiplying genotype frequencies by survival/reproductive weights and renormalizing the population.

Worked example with reasoning

Fictional phenotypes A and B begin .5/.5. Relative reproductive weights are 1.0 and .8. Weighted contributions are .5 and .4; normalized next-generation parental contribution is .5/.9=.556 for A and .444 for B in the simplified model.

Guided question

Why must fitness always be interpreted relative to environment and alternatives?

Independent work

  1. Calculate weighted contributions for three trait classes.
  2. Normalize frequencies.
  3. Compare before/after.
  4. State assumptions about inheritance and reproduction.

Misconception check

“Fittest” does not mean strongest or best in every environment; fitness is context-dependent reproductive success.

Formative understanding check

If a trait improves survival but prevents reproduction, is high survival alone enough to imply high evolutionary fitness?

Worked response

No. Fitness concerns reproductive contribution, not survival alone.
