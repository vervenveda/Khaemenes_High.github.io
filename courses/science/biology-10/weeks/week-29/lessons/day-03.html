Directional, Stabilizing & Disruptive Selection

Interpret changes in trait distributions instead of memorizing three graph shapes.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use paper-token simulations and published or fictional trait-frequency data. Do not intentionally expose organisms to harmful agents or breed animals for class. Avoid purpose-based language: populations change through differential reproduction, not because organisms need or want traits.

Explicit science instruction

Selection can alter trait distributions. Directional selection shifts the mean toward one end; stabilizing selection reduces extremes around an intermediate region; disruptive selection can favor separated ranges over intermediates. These labels describe patterns under conditions, not permanent properties.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional bird beak distribution begins mean 10 mm. After a drought leaving mostly large hard seeds, survivors/reproducers average 12 mm: consistent with directional selection if beak size is heritable and other evidence supports differential reproduction.

Guided question

Why can't a before/after histogram alone prove selection caused the change?

Independent work

  1. Classify three distribution changes.
  2. Calculate mean shifts from supplied data.
  3. Identify heritability evidence needed.
  4. Propose drift or migration alternatives.

Misconception check

Stabilizing selection does not stop evolution, and disruptive selection does not automatically create new species.

Formative understanding check

Which pattern favors intermediate phenotypes relative to extremes?

Worked response

Stabilizing selection.

Week 29 natural-selection depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Selection can favor one extreme, intermediate phenotypes, or multiple extremes depending on the relationship between phenotype and reproductive success.

Vocabulary in context: directional selection, stabilizing selection, disruptive selection, phenotype distribution, variance, mean.

Worked reasoning: Directional selection can shift a trait mean, stabilizing selection can reduce variance around an intermediate optimum, and disruptive selection can increase representation of extremes under some conditions.

Evidence-quality task: Compare before-and-after phenotype distributions and state what pattern supports each mode while acknowledging that other processes could produce similar shifts.

Quantitative transfer: Use mean and variance changes to distinguish a likely directional vs stabilizing pattern.

Retrieval: Connect continuous variation from Week 22.

Selection-mode data safeguard

Common error: A change in a trait distribution does not uniquely identify a selection mode. Migration, drift, environmental measurement effects, or changing developmental conditions can also shift means or variances.

Quantitative extension: Compare a distribution whose mean shifts from 10 to 12 with unchanged variance to one whose mean remains 10 while variance falls by half. Explain why the first is more consistent with directional selection and the second with stabilizing selection, while noting alternative explanations.
