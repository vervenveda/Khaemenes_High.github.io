Selection Evidence & Synthesis

Use replicated trait-frequency evidence to argue for selection while keeping alternative mechanisms visible.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use paper-token simulations and published or fictional trait-frequency data. Do not intentionally expose organisms to harmful agents or breed animals for class. Avoid purpose-based language: populations change through differential reproduction, not because organisms need or want traits.

Explicit science instruction

Strong selection evidence combines measured variation, heritability, differential reproductive success and repeated population change. Field observations, experiments, genomic data and time series can contribute different evidence.

Observed evolutionary change does not require purpose; selection sorts existing/new heritable variation through differential reproduction.

Worked example with reasoning

Across five fictional populations exposed to the same environmental shift, trait T rises from ~20% to ~65%, while matched control populations remain near 20%. Parent–offspring data show heritability and T carriers leave more offspring. Convergence strengthens a selection explanation.

Guided question

Which evidence in the example is most diagnostic of natural selection rather than drift?

Retrieval is formative. The goal is durable connection across units, not a separate grade.

Misconception check

Selection is not a conscious process and does not generate needed mutations on demand.

Formative understanding check

What three evidence categories make a selection claim strong?

Worked response

Heritable variation, differential reproductive contribution, and repeated population change consistent with those differences.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
