Read and understand

Scientists connect claims to evidence

A claim states a concise answer to an investigable question. Evidence consists of specific observations, measurements, figures, or referenced scientific findings. Reasoning explains why the evidence supports the claim using accurate scientific principles. A persuasive answer also describes limits and acknowledges alternative explanations that remain plausible.

A model-specific example

Consider the simulated comparison at fixed pH and substrate level. The model may show a higher rate under a moderate temperature than at an extreme one. A defensible claim is “Within this simulation and these settings, model output was higher at the tested moderate temperature.” The evidence must name temperatures, output means and units. Reasoning can refer to the model’s assumed temperature-response curve; it should not pretend to establish protein denaturation empirically.

What a counterclaim adds

A counterclaim or competing explanation is not a statement of defeat. It may ask whether substrate availability, pH drift, limited sampling, model assumptions, or instrumental differences could explain the pattern. In a real experiment, matching controls and replicates make some alternatives less plausible but rarely remove all uncertainty. Explain what new evidence you would collect.

Vocabulary in context

claim
A statement that answers the investigation question.
evidence
Observed or measured information supporting a claim.
reasoning
The logical connection between evidence and a scientific principle.
counterclaim
A competing explanation or claim that deserves examination.
limitation
A restriction affecting a study, model, method, or conclusion.

Explain each term with an original example before your practice check.

Worked example

Claim: At fixed pH and substrate, the modeled rate was higher at 35°C than 45°C. Evidence: fictional Week 04 data mean 64 versus 47 relative units/minute (difference 17; 26.6% decrease from 35°C to 45°C). Reasoning: the model includes a high-temperature falloff. Limitation: five tested temperatures do not determine an exact optimum or molecular cause.

Common misconception: Observing a rate pattern in a simplified model does not establish a universal biological rule or molecular cause.
