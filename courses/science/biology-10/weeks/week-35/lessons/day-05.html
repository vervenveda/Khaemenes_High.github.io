Capstone Defense: Evidence, Ethics & Recommendation

Defend a responsible biological solution while making uncertainty, tradeoffs and ethical limits visible.

Safety, ethics and evidence boundary

Capstone work must remain low-risk and teacher/parent approved. No human-subject research, personal health/genetic data, biological culturing, animal manipulation, hazardous chemicals, mains electricity, weapons, or procedural gene editing. Public sources must be cited; private or sensitive locations/data must not be exposed.

Explicit science instruction

A strong capstone defense answers five questions: What problem was addressed? What evidence supports the model? How was performance measured? What tradeoffs and ethical issues remain? What would cause revision?

The final recommendation should distinguish measured results from projections and avoid claiming universal success from one prototype.

Worked example with reasoning

A conservation capstone improves modeled connectivity 30% at moderate cost but affects access for one stakeholder group. The defense recommends the design only with a co-designed access modification and monitoring trigger. That is stronger than hiding the tradeoff.

Guided question

What makes a recommendation scientifically responsible rather than merely persuasive?

Independent work

  1. Prepare a 5-part defense.
  2. Use at least three evidence types.
  3. Show one quantitative model.
  4. State two uncertainties and one ethical tradeoff.
  5. Define a revision trigger and next test.

Misconception check

A confident presentation is not stronger than transparent uncertainty. Scientific credibility increases when limitations and revision conditions are explicit.

Formative understanding check

What should a capstone recommendation include besides the preferred option?

Worked response

Evidence, criteria, constraints, uncertainty, tradeoffs, ethical considerations and conditions for revision.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
