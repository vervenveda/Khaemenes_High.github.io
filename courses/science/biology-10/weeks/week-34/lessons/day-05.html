Conservation Decision Matrix & Synthesis

Compare conservation strategies transparently and defend a revisable recommendation.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious Day

Safety, ethics and evidence boundary

Use public/de-identified conservation datasets and safe non-contact observation. Protect sensitive species locations and community privacy. Conservation choices involve ecological evidence plus rights, costs, access, and community values; science informs consequences but does not erase civic or ethical tradeoffs.

Explicit science instruction

A decision matrix makes criteria and tradeoffs visible. Possible criteria include biodiversity benefit, evidence strength, cost, feasibility, reversibility, community impact, time to benefit and uncertainty. Weighting criteria is a value choice and should be disclosed.

Strong conservation decisions connect each strategy to the causal pressure it is supposed to reduce.

Worked example with reasoning

Three strategies address habitat loss: purchase reserve, build corridor, restore degraded patch. Scores differ across ecological benefit, cost and community impact. Changing criterion weights can change the preferred option, revealing where value judgments—not hidden math—drive the result.

Guided question

Why should a decision matrix show criterion weights rather than hide them?

Independent work

  1. Build a three-strategy matrix.
  2. Use at least five criteria.
  3. Score evidence strength separately from expected benefit.
  4. Run one sensitivity test on weights.
  5. Write a recommendation with revision trigger.

Misconception check

A numerical score does not turn a value-laden decision into objective truth; the score is only as transparent and defensible as its criteria, weights and evidence.

Formative understanding check

What is sensitivity analysis?

Worked response

Testing whether the recommendation changes when uncertain values or criterion weights are varied.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
