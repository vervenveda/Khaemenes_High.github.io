Human Impacts, Ecosystem Services & Equity

Analyze environmental decisions without reducing nature or communities to one monetary metric.

Safety, ethics and evidence boundary

Use public/de-identified conservation datasets and safe non-contact observation. Protect sensitive species locations and community privacy. Conservation choices involve ecological evidence plus rights, costs, access, and community values; science informs consequences but does not erase civic or ethical tradeoffs.

Explicit science instruction

Human activities can alter habitat, harvest, pollution, climate, invasive species and disturbance regimes. Ecosystem services include provisioning, regulating, cultural and supporting functions, but nature also has intrinsic and relational values not captured by market price.

Environmental burdens and benefits can be unevenly distributed. Decision analysis should identify who gains, who bears risks, and whose knowledge and rights are represented.

Worked example with reasoning

A coastal project protects 200 hectares of marsh and reduces flood risk but restricts traditional access for a local community. A scientifically strong evaluation includes habitat and flood metrics plus access, rights and co-management alternatives.

Guided question

Why can the ecologically highest-scoring option still be an unjust or unstable policy?

Independent work

  1. Identify ecological and social indicators in a case.
  2. Map stakeholders.
  3. Separate measured consequence from value judgment.
  4. Propose one design revision improving both ecological outcome and access.

Investigation / Data Lab

Biodiversity and restoration-design analysis

Question: Which restoration option best improves biodiversity under ecological, financial, and community constraints?

  1. Use a supplied, non-sensitive species-abundance dataset to calculate richness and one diversity/evenness measure for two sites.
  2. Compare three restoration options for expected biodiversity benefit, cost, feasibility, reversibility, and community impact.
  3. Build a transparent decision matrix with stated weights.
  4. Run one sensitivity test by changing an uncertain score or weight.
  5. Protect exact locations of sensitive species and state a monitoring trigger that would cause plan revision.

Evidence standard: preserve the abundance table, diversity calculation, decision matrix, sensitivity result, and privacy/siting boundary.

Misconception check

Ecosystem-service valuation is a decision tool, not a claim that all ecological or cultural value can be priced.

Formative understanding check

What does stakeholder analysis add to ecological data?

Worked response

It identifies who is affected, whose rights and knowledge matter, and how benefits/risks are distributed.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
