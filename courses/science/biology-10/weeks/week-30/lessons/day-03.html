Character Matrices & Parsimony

Build and compare trees from shared derived characters while treating parsimony as one inference criterion.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied character matrices, public fossil/sequence data, and fictional taxa. No personal belief is graded. Scientific common-ancestry and phylogenetic models must be explained accurately; theological or philosophical claims may be discussed respectfully but are not interchangeable with empirical models unless they generate testable evidence.

Explicit science instruction

A character matrix records states across taxa. Shared derived characters can support clades. Parsimony favors trees requiring fewer character-state changes, but real phylogenetics also uses probabilistic models and large molecular datasets.

Character independence, homology, convergence and reversals affect inference.

Worked example with reasoning

Taxa A,B share derived character 1; B,C share character 2; A,B also share two additional derived states. A parsimonious tree may group A+B if that arrangement requires fewer independent origins overall. The full matrix—not one favored character—must be scored.

Guided question

Why can convergence mislead a character-based tree?

Independent work

  1. Score a four-taxon matrix.
  2. Propose two trees.
  3. Count changes for each.
  4. Choose the more parsimonious tree under stated assumptions.
  5. Identify one homoplasy.

Misconception check

Parsimony is not a law that nature always follows the fewest evolutionary changes; it is an inference method with assumptions.

Formative understanding check

What is homoplasy?

Worked response

A similar character state arising independently or by reversal rather than simple shared inheritance.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
