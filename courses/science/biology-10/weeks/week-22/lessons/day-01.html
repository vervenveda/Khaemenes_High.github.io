Polygenic Traits & Quantitative Variation

Explain why many traits form distributions when multiple loci and environments contribute.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms and de-identified/public datasets. Do not infer intelligence, personality, worth, disease, ancestry, race, or family traits from genetics. Complex-trait models are population-level scientific tools, not labels for individuals.

Explicit science instruction

A polygenic trait is influenced by variation at multiple loci. When many small genetic effects combine with environmental and developmental variation, phenotypes often form a continuous distribution rather than a few categories.

Genetic contribution does not imply fixed outcome. A quantitative trait can have substantial heritability in a population while individual outcomes remain influenced by environment and development.

Vocabulary: polygenic; quantitative trait; distribution; additive effect; variance; population; heritability.

Worked example with reasoning

In a fictional plant, three loci each contribute 0, 1, or 2 units to pigment intensity. Many genotype combinations yield the same total score, producing more intermediate than extreme values. Adding light variation broadens the phenotype distribution further.

Guided question

Why does a bell-shaped distribution not prove that a trait is purely genetic?

Independent work

  1. Build a three-locus additive model.
  2. List at least five genotype combinations producing the same score.
  3. Sketch the expected distribution.
  4. Add one environmental factor and predict how it could alter spread.

Misconception check

Polygenic does not mean genes act alone, and a population distribution cannot be used to rank individuals' value.

Formative understanding check

Why can different genotypes produce the same quantitative phenotype?

Worked response

Different combinations of allele effects can sum to similar values, and environmental/developmental factors can further overlap outcomes.
