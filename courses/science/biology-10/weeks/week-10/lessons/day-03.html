Mitochondria, Electron Transport & Chemiosmosis

Connect pyruvate oxidation, the citric-acid cycle, electron carriers, proton gradients, ATP synthase, and oxygen into one mitochondrial energy-transfer model.

Explicit science instruction

Under aerobic conditions, pyruvate-derived carbon enters mitochondrial pathways. Carbon oxidation in pyruvate processing and the citric-acid cycle releases CO₂ and transfers high-energy electrons to carriers such as NADH and FADH₂.

These carriers donate electrons to an electron-transport chain in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electron transfer is coupled to movement of protons across the membrane, creating an electrochemical gradient. Protons moving back through ATP synthase drive ATP formation. This coupling is called chemiosmosis.

Oxygen accepts electrons at the end of the chain and participates in water formation. ATP yield per glucose is an estimate rather than one universal fixed integer because transport costs, proton leak, shuttle systems, cell type, and experimental assumptions vary.

Vocabulary: citric-acid cycle; NADH; FADH₂; electron-transport chain; proton gradient; chemiosmosis; ATP synthase; oxidative phosphorylation.

Worked example with reasoning

Suppose Model A predicts 30 ATP per glucose and Model B predicts 32. The difference does not automatically mean one model is “wrong.” If the models assume different shuttle costs or proton-to-ATP coupling efficiencies, both may be reasonable approximations under different assumptions. A scientifically responsible answer states the model assumptions rather than memorizing one universal yield.

Guided question

Why would electron transport slow sharply if oxygen were unavailable in a typical aerobic mitochondrion?

Independent work

  1. Draw the inner mitochondrial membrane with electron flow, proton pumping, gradient, ATP synthase, and oxygen.
  2. Explain how redox reactions create a gradient.
  3. Explain how the gradient is coupled to ATP synthesis.
  4. List two reasons ATP-yield estimates can vary.

Misconception check

The citric-acid cycle does not produce most ATP directly, and oxygen is not used mainly by glycolysis. Much ATP production in aerobic eukaryotic cells is associated with oxidative phosphorylation driven by electron transport and chemiosmosis.

Formative understanding check

What directly powers ATP synthase during oxidative phosphorylation?

Show the worked response

The electrochemical proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Proton flow through ATP synthase drives conformational changes that support ATP formation.
