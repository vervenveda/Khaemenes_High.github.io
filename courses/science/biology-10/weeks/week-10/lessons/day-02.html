Glycolysis, Pyruvate & Fermentation

Explain glycolysis as a cytosolic pathway that begins glucose breakdown and compare fermentation with aerobic pathways without calling fermentation 'respiration without oxygen' too loosely.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied datasets or teacher-approved simulations unless a supervised laboratory procedure is explicitly provided. Do not seal living organisms in improvised containers or alter temperature, chemicals, or gas conditions without approved supervision.

Explicit science instruction

Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and converts one six-carbon glucose into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules through a sequence of enzyme-mediated reactions. The pathway includes an investment phase and payoff phase, producing a small net amount of ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation and reducing NAD⁺ to NADH.

When cells cannot sustain mitochondrial oxidation of NADH at a sufficient rate, fermentation pathways can regenerate NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue. Lactic-acid fermentation and alcoholic fermentation use different end products. Fermentation does not require oxygen directly and yields far less ATP per glucose than complete aerobic oxidation.

Glycolysis itself also does not directly require oxygen. Oxygen affects whether pyruvate and NADH can feed into aerobic pathways.

Vocabulary: glycolysis; pyruvate; NAD⁺; NADH; substrate-level phosphorylation; fermentation; lactate; ethanol.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional cell uses glycolysis to convert glucose to pyruvate and gains a net two ATP from the pathway. If oxygen is unavailable, NADH cannot simply accumulate indefinitely. In lactic fermentation, electrons are transferred so NAD⁺ is regenerated. The key function of fermentation in this context is therefore NAD⁺ recycling, allowing glycolysis to continue.

Guided question

Why would glycolysis stop if NAD⁺ were not regenerated, even though glycolysis itself does not directly use oxygen?

Independent work

  1. Create a flow chart: glucose → glycolysis → pyruvate.
  2. Label where ATP and NADH are produced conceptually.
  3. Compare aerobic continuation with fermentation.
  4. Explain why fermentation is not equivalent to complete aerobic respiration.

Misconception check

Lactate is not simply a poisonous “waste” that causes all muscle soreness, and fermentation is not a failed version of respiration. It is a biologically useful strategy for maintaining redox balance and ATP production through glycolysis under particular conditions.

Formative understanding check

What is the central redox purpose of fermentation after glycolysis?

Show the worked response

It regenerates NAD⁺ from NADH so glycolytic oxidation can continue. This supports continued substrate-level ATP production when mitochondrial oxidation cannot keep pace.
