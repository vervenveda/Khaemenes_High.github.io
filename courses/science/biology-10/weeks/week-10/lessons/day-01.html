Cellular Respiration: Energy Release Without 'Making Energy'

Build a system model for how cells transfer energy from organic molecules into ATP while conserving matter and releasing energy to the surroundings.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied datasets or teacher-approved simulations unless a supervised laboratory procedure is explicitly provided. Do not seal living organisms in improvised containers or alter temperature, chemicals, or gas conditions without approved supervision.

Explicit science instruction

Cellular respiration is a network of reactions that transfers energy from reduced organic molecules into forms cells can use, especially ATP. It does not create energy. Chemical energy is transferred through oxidation–reduction reactions, electron carriers, ion gradients, and phosphorylation.

A common net model for aerobic respiration is C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O plus energy transferred to ATP and heat. Like the photosynthesis equation, this is a system summary rather than a single reaction.

Carbon atoms from glucose can leave as CO₂, oxygen participates as a terminal electron acceptor in aerobic respiration, and much of the usable energy transfer occurs through multiple intermediate steps. ATP is an energy-transfer molecule, not a long-term energy warehouse.

Vocabulary: cellular respiration; ATP; oxidation; reduction; electron carrier; aerobic; substrate; energy transfer.

Worked example with reasoning

If one glucose molecule is fully oxidized aerobically, its six carbon atoms can ultimately appear in six CO₂ molecules in the net accounting. The atoms are conserved. The decrease in chemical potential energy is not “lost”; some is captured through ATP-producing processes and some is transferred as heat. The equation tracks matter, while energy accounting requires a separate statement.

Guided question

Why is “glucose turns into energy” scientifically inaccurate? Rewrite it so both matter and energy are described correctly.

Independent work

  1. Annotate the net respiration equation by atom type.
  2. Draw a matter-and-energy flow model from organic molecule to CO₂, H₂O, ATP transfer, and heat.
  3. Explain the role of oxygen without saying oxygen “contains energy.”
  4. Write one limitation of the net equation.

Misconception check

Cells do not “burn” glucose in one uncontrolled step, and ATP is not energy itself. Respiration uses regulated reaction pathways to transfer chemical energy; ATP is one molecular carrier used to couple energy-releasing and energy-requiring processes.

Formative understanding check

What does oxygen do in aerobic respiration?

Show the worked response

Oxygen serves as the terminal electron acceptor in the mitochondrial electron-transport system, allowing electron flow and associated proton-gradient formation to continue. It is reduced to water.
