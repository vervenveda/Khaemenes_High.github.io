Observed vs Expected: Chi-Square Concept

Use chi-square as a model-fit tool conceptually, emphasizing assumptions and interpretation over button pushing.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms, coins/cards, and published-style datasets. Do not require personal family histories, ancestry testing, fertility information, medical pedigrees, or personal genetic data. Trait inheritance never ranks human worth.

Explicit science instruction

A chi-square goodness-of-fit statistic summarizes how far observed category counts differ from expected counts relative to the expected counts. One form is Σ(O−E)²/E. Larger values mean greater discrepancy, but interpretation depends on degrees of freedom and a reference distribution.

The test does not measure biological importance, prove a model, or identify the cause of mismatch. Expected counts should be based on a specified model, and categories/observations should satisfy the test's assumptions.

Worked example with reasoning

A 3:1 model predicts 75 and 25 among 100 offspring. Observed counts are 70 and 30. χ² = (70−75)²/75 + (30−25)²/25 ≈ 0.33 + 1 = 1.33. The statistic quantifies discrepancy; judging whether it is unusual requires the appropriate reference threshold.

Guided question

Why can't a small chi-square value prove the genetic model is true?

Independent work

  1. Calculate expected counts for a 3:1 model with n=160.
  2. Compute χ² for an observed 115:45 result.
  3. State the degrees of freedom for two categories.
  4. List two assumptions.
  5. Explain what the test cannot identify.

Investigation / Data Lab

Fictional pedigree constraint analysis

Question: Which inheritance model is most consistent with a supplied fictional pedigree, and what remains ambiguous?

  1. Use only the supplied anonymous/fictitious pedigree. Do not substitute family medical history.
  2. Test autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, and one sex-linked model against the same relationships.
  3. Mark which genotypes are forced, possible, or excluded under each model.
  4. Choose the best-supported model and list at least two assumptions such as penetrance or complete reporting.
  5. Identify one additional observation that would discriminate between the remaining models.

Evidence standard: submit the annotated pedigree/model table, not a diagnosis or inference about real relatives.

Misconception check

A p-value is not the probability that the hypothesis is true, and “not significant” is not proof of no difference.

Formative understanding check

What happens to χ² when observed counts move farther from fixed expected counts?

Worked response

It generally increases because squared deviations grow.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
