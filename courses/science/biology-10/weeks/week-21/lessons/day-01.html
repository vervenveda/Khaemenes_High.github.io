Product & Sum Rules in Genetics

Use probability rules to combine independent or alternative genetic events without turning probability into certainty.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms, coins/cards, and published-style datasets. Do not require personal family histories, ancestry testing, fertility information, medical pedigrees, or personal genetic data. Trait inheritance never ranks human worth.

Explicit science instruction

The product rule multiplies probabilities for events that must both occur when the relevant events are independent or conditionally specified. The sum rule adds probabilities for mutually exclusive alternative outcomes.

Genetic reasoning must state assumptions about independence. Linked loci violate simple independent-assortment assumptions, and conditional information can change probabilities.

Vocabulary: product rule; sum rule; independent; mutually exclusive; conditional probability; outcome.

Worked example with reasoning

If two independently assorting loci each have a 1/2 probability of contributing a specified allele, the joint probability is 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. If a phenotype can arise from either of two mutually exclusive genotypes with probabilities 1/4 and 1/2, total probability is 3/4.

Guided question

Why must you check independence before multiplying probabilities?

Independent work

  1. Solve three product-rule examples.
  2. Solve two sum-rule examples.
  3. Identify one linked-locus case where simple multiplication is unsafe.
  4. Write assumptions beside each calculation.

Misconception check

Multiplying any two numbers associated with genetics is not the product rule; the events and conditioning must be defined.

Formative understanding check

If independent events have probabilities 1/2 and 1/4, what is the probability both occur?

Worked response

1/8.
