Pedigree Logic Without Personal Inference

Read fictional pedigrees as constrained probability models while preserving privacy and uncertainty.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms, coins/cards, and published-style datasets. Do not require personal family histories, ancestry testing, fertility information, medical pedigrees, or personal genetic data. Trait inheritance never ranks human worth.

Explicit science instruction

A pedigree records relationships and observed trait categories across generations. It can be used to test whether inheritance patterns are consistent with autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked, mitochondrial, or other simplified models.

Pedigrees rarely prove a mechanism by themselves. Small family size, missing information, adoption, non-paternity assumptions, variable penetrance, phenocopies, and classification errors can change interpretation. This course uses fictional or historical-public examples only.

Vocabulary: pedigree; proband; carrier; autosomal; dominant; recessive; inference; penetrance.

Worked example with reasoning

In a fictional pedigree, two unaffected parents have an affected child under a fully penetrant simple model. That is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance if both parents are carriers, but it is not uniquely proven without additional evidence.

Guided question

Why should a pedigree conclusion usually be phrased “consistent with” rather than “proves”?

Independent work

  1. Interpret a fictional three-generation pedigree.
  2. List two models ruled out by one relationship pattern.
  3. Identify ambiguous genotypes.
  4. State two real-world factors the simplified pedigree omits.

Misconception check

Pedigrees are not permission to infer relatives' health, paternity, ancestry, or carrier status from personal family information.

Formative understanding check

Can an unaffected person be a carrier in a recessive model?

Worked response

Yes, a heterozygous carrier can be unaffected if the model assumes a recessive phenotype.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
