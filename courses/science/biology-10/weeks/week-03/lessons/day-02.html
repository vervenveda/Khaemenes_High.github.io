Carbohydrates, Lipids, Proteins & Nucleic Acids

Compare the four major biomolecule classes by chemical structure and biological role without turning the comparison into a memorization-only list.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use teacher-approved diagrams or paper models. Do not perform chemical indicator tests or ingest materials. Food examples are used only as familiar labels, not as dietary recommendations.

Explicit science instruction

Carbohydrates include small sugars and larger structures built from sugar subunits. Their functions can include energy transfer, energy storage, and structural support. The category is chemically broad; not every carbohydrate tastes sweet or serves the same role.

Lipids include fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids, and other hydrophobic or partly hydrophobic molecules. Long hydrocarbon regions interact poorly with water. Phospholipids contain both water-interacting and water-avoiding regions, a property important to membranes.

Proteins are polymers of amino acids. Amino-acid sequence influences folding, and folding contributes to interaction with other molecules. Proteins can act as structural components, transporters, receptors, motors, antibodies, or catalysts. Week 04 will examine enzyme catalysis in depth.

Nucleic acids are nucleotide polymers. DNA stores hereditary information in nucleotide sequence; RNA can carry, regulate, or help use information in several ways. Nucleotides also appear in molecules involved in energy transfer and signaling, so the word “nucleotide” is not limited to DNA.

Vocabulary: sugar; polysaccharide; fatty acid; phospholipid; amino acid; protein folding; nucleotide; DNA; RNA.

Worked example with reasoning

A phospholipid has a polar region and nonpolar hydrocarbon tails. In water, many phospholipids can organize so the water-interacting regions face outward while hydrophobic regions are shielded. This behavior follows from molecular interactions; the phospholipid does not “decide” to make a membrane. The same structure–interaction reasoning is stronger than memorizing “phospholipids make membranes.”

Guided question

Compare a protein and a nucleic acid. Both are polymers, but what differs about their subunits and the kinds of information their sequence can encode?

Independent work

  1. For each biomolecule class, draw or describe one structural feature that matters for function.
  2. Explain why a triglyceride and a phospholipid can both be lipids but behave differently in water.
  3. Write one structure–function explanation for a protein without using enzymes as the example.
  4. State one reason a simple “one class = one function” chart would be misleading.

Misconception check

Biomolecule names are not job titles. Carbohydrates are not only “energy,” proteins are not only “muscle,” lipids are not only “fat storage,” and nucleic acids are not only “DNA.” Structure, location, and context matter.

Formative understanding check

What structural feature helps phospholipids organize at a water boundary?

Show the worked response

They contain both polar/water-interacting regions and nonpolar hydrophobic regions. This amphipathic structure leads to organized arrangements in water.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
