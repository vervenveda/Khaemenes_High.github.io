Macromolecule Structure–Function Synthesis

Use multiple lines of evidence to explain how molecular structure constrains biological function and prepare a clear conceptual bridge into Week 04 enzyme chemistry.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious Day

Safety and evidence boundary

This is a synthesis and writing lesson. Use only course diagrams, paper models, and synthetic quantitative data. Do not make personal dietary, diagnostic, or medical claims.

Explicit science instruction

The four major biomolecule classes illustrate a central biological principle: molecular structure constrains possible interactions. Carbohydrate structure can support energy transfer, storage, recognition, or structural roles. Lipid hydrophobic regions influence membrane organization and energy storage. Protein amino-acid sequences influence folding and interaction surfaces. Nucleic-acid sequences store and transmit information through specific base-pairing and molecular recognition.

Structure does not act alone. Temperature, pH, concentration, solvent conditions, other molecules, and cellular location can alter behavior. A complete explanation therefore connects composition and structure to interaction under stated conditions.

The transition to Week 04 is especially important for proteins. Some proteins act as enzymes. Their three-dimensional structures help create sites that interact with substrates, but enzyme activity also depends on conditions and molecular motion. Week 04 will test those ideas with reaction-rate evidence rather than treating “enzyme shape” as a complete explanation.

Vocabulary: structure–function; interaction; conformation; environment; evidence; mechanism; limitation; transfer.

Worked example with reasoning

Claim: Molecular structure constrains biological function by affecting which interactions are physically and chemically possible. Evidence: phospholipids contain both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions; proteins contain sequence-dependent folded structures; nucleic acids use ordered nucleotide sequences and complementary base interactions. Reasoning: different structural features create different interaction patterns. Limitation: structure alone does not predict behavior without environmental and cellular context.

Guided question

Choose one biomolecule class and identify the exact structural feature that supports your claimed function. If your sentence could apply equally to all four classes, make it more specific.

Independent work

  1. Build a final four-class concept map with one structural feature, one interaction, and one function for each class.
  2. Write a CER answering the week’s essential question.
  3. Include one quantitative evidence example from Wednesday and explain its limit.
  4. Include one model limitation from Thursday.
  5. Write one prediction about enzyme behavior that Week 04 could test.

Misconception check

“Structure determines function” should not be interpreted as a one-to-one deterministic rule. Molecular behavior is constrained by structure but also depends on environment, interacting partners, concentration, and dynamic changes.

Formative understanding check

What makes a structure–function explanation stronger than simply naming a biomolecule and its job?

Show the worked response

A strong explanation identifies the specific structural feature, the interaction or mechanism it enables or limits, evidence supporting that connection, and the conditions or limits of the claim.
