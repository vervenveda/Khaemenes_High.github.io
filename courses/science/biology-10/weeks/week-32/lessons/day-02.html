Carbon Cycle Across Biological & Geological Scales

Trace carbon through atmosphere, organisms, oceans, soils and rocks without reducing the cycle to photosynthesis alone.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use public system diagrams and aggregate environmental data. Field observations must remain non-contact and safe. Do not enter hazardous water, drains, industrial sites, disaster areas, or collect protected materials. Distinguish measured fluxes from arrows drawn in a model.

Explicit science instruction

Photosynthesis moves inorganic carbon into organic molecules; respiration and decomposition return carbon to inorganic forms. Oceans exchange carbon with the atmosphere and store dissolved inorganic/organic carbon. Sedimentation, weathering, volcanism and fossil-carbon processes operate over longer timescales.

Human combustion and land-use change alter fluxes faster than some reservoirs can equilibrate.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional forest takes up 120 units/year by photosynthesis, returns 70 by plant respiration and 35 by decomposition. Net biological storage in the simplified accounting is 15 units/year before disturbances or export are considered.

Week 32 biogeochemical depth checkpoint

Phenomenon: Carbon moves through biological reservoirs on short timescales and geological reservoirs on much longer ones, so the same element participates in processes with very different rates.

Vocabulary in context: carbon reservoir, photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion, sedimentation, sequestration, timescale.

Worked reasoning: Carbon fixed by photosynthesis can return to the atmosphere quickly through respiration, while carbon buried in sediments can remain stored for much longer periods before geological processes return it.

Evidence-quality task: Compare a short-term ecosystem carbon budget with a geological carbon-cycle model and identify which claims each timescale can support.

Quantitative transfer: Calculate a simple annual net carbon balance from inputs and outputs and state the sign convention used.

Retrieval: Connect photosynthesis/respiration from Weeks 9–10 with ecosystem production from Week 31.

Carbon-budget safeguard

Common error: A reservoir acting as a net sink over one interval can become a source over another. Sign depends on the chosen boundary and time window.

Quantitative extension: If inputs are 500 units/year and outputs are 460, net change is +40 units/year. If the following year outputs rise to 530 with the same inputs, the reservoir becomes a net source at −30 units/year.
