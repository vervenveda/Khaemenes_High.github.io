Reservoirs, Fluxes & Residence Time

Model biogeochemical cycles as transfers among reservoirs operating on different timescales.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use public system diagrams and aggregate environmental data. Field observations must remain non-contact and safe. Do not enter hazardous water, drains, industrial sites, disaster areas, or collect protected materials. Distinguish measured fluxes from arrows drawn in a model.

Explicit science instruction

A reservoir stores matter; a flux is a rate of transfer between reservoirs. Residence time can be approximated as reservoir amount divided by outgoing flux under steady assumptions. Fast biological cycles and slow geological cycles interact.

Vocabulary: reservoir; flux; residence time; pool; steady state; source; sink; cycle.

Worked example with reasoning

A reservoir contains 1,000 units and loses 100 units per year while approximately balanced by inputs. Approximate residence time is 10 years. If outputs change, the steady-state estimate may no longer apply.

Guided question

Why is a large reservoir not necessarily a fast-moving part of a cycle?

Independent work

  1. Label reservoirs and fluxes in a supplied system.
  2. Calculate three residence times.
  3. Identify one steady-state assumption.
  4. Separate stored amount from transfer rate.

Misconception check

A wide arrow can represent a large flux while a large box represents a large reservoir; these are different quantities.

Formative understanding check

Reservoir 500 units, output 25/year. Approximate residence time?

Worked response

20 years under the simple steady-state approximation.
