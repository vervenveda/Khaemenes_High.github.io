Biogeochemical Systems Synthesis

Integrate matter conservation, reservoir dynamics and biological processes into a bounded systems explanation.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use public system diagrams and aggregate environmental data. Field observations must remain non-contact and safe. Do not enter hazardous water, drains, industrial sites, disaster areas, or collect protected materials. Distinguish measured fluxes from arrows drawn in a model.

Explicit science instruction

Matter cycles because atoms move among reservoirs through physical, chemical and biological processes. Cycle diagrams compress complex three-dimensional, time-varying systems into arrows and boxes; strong models specify units, spatial boundary, timescale and omitted processes.

Worked example with reasoning

A combined carbon-nitrogen model shows plant growth moving both elements into biomass. Decomposition returns both, but gaseous losses differ. Coupling means changing one nutrient can alter the other cycle through biological demand.

Guided question

How can cycles be coupled even when their chemical transformations differ?

Independent work

  1. Write a Week 32 CER.
  2. Use one residence-time calculation and one mass balance.
  3. Connect at least two cycles through organisms.
  4. State two model boundaries.

Misconception check

A cycle diagram is not evidence that reservoir sizes remain constant; reservoirs can grow or shrink while matter remains conserved globally.

Formative understanding check

What should every quantitative flux arrow include?

Worked response

A rate with appropriate amount, area/volume if relevant, and time units.
