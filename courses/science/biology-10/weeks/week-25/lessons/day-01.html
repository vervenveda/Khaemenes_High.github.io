Promoters, Enhancers & Transcription Factors

Explain gene regulation as interactions among DNA regulatory regions, proteins and cellular context.

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional regulatory networks and de-identified expression datasets. Epigenetic examples are not permission to infer trauma, disease, behavior, or inheritance in individuals. Avoid deterministic or transgenerational claims not supported by evidence.

Explicit science instruction

A promoter helps recruit transcription machinery near a gene. Transcription factors bind specific DNA sequences or interact with other proteins to increase or decrease transcription. Enhancers and silencers can act over distance through chromosome folding.

Gene activity depends on combinations of regulators rather than a single universal on/off switch.

Vocabulary: promoter; enhancer; silencer; transcription factor; activator; repressor; regulatory network.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional gene has promoter P and enhancer E. Cell type A expresses activator X and transcribes strongly; Cell B lacks X and remains low despite identical DNA sequence. The difference lies in regulatory context.

Guided question

How can two cells with the same genome transcribe the same gene at different rates?

Independent work

  1. Draw a promoter-enhancer model.
  2. Add two activators and one repressor.
  3. Predict expression in three cell contexts.
  4. State one simplification.

Misconception check

An enhancer is not always physically adjacent to the gene it regulates, and “on/off” often oversimplifies graded expression.

Formative understanding check

What does a transcription factor do in this model?

Worked response

It binds regulatory DNA or regulatory complexes and influences transcriptional activity.

Week 25 gene-regulation checkpoint

Phenomenon: Promoters are DNA regions where transcription machinery assembles, while enhancers can influence transcription from a distance through DNA looping and regulatory proteins. A gene's expression depends on combinations of regulatory inputs, not one universal on/off switch.

Worked example: If a transcription factor activates an enhancer only in liver cells, the same gene can be expressed strongly in liver and weakly elsewhere even though the DNA sequence is present in both cell types.

Retrieval: Retrieve one information-flow idea from Week 24 and explain where regulation can alter the path from DNA to phenotype.

Evidence revision: Revise your explanation so regulatory element, mechanism, expression evidence, uncertainty, and alternative explanation are explicit.

Transfer: Predict how the interpretation changes in a different cell type, developmental stage, environment, or expression dataset.

Advanced transcription-control depth

Vocabulary in context: promoter, enhancer, silencer, transcription factor, regulatory element, chromatin accessibility, transcription rate.

Mechanism check: Enhancers can act at a distance because DNA folds in three dimensions, bringing regulatory proteins near promoter-associated machinery. Distance on a linear chromosome does not equal regulatory isolation.

Evidence-quality task: Compare promoter-sequence data, transcription-factor binding evidence, and expression data. Explain which combination most strongly supports a regulatory claim and why expression alone is insufficient.

Quantitative transfer: If reporter expression rises from 40 to 100 units after adding an enhancer, calculate fold change and percent increase, then state one control needed before attributing the change to the enhancer sequence.
