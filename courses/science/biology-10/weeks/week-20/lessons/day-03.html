Expected vs Observed Ratios

Explain why random sampling makes real offspring counts fluctuate around model expectations.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms, coins/cards, and published-style datasets. Do not require personal family histories, ancestry testing, fertility information, medical pedigrees, or personal genetic data. Trait inheritance never ranks human worth.

Explicit science instruction

Expected ratios are long-run model predictions. Observed counts are finite samples. Random sampling produces variation, especially in small samples, so a 3:1 expectation does not require every group of four offspring to contain exactly three of one phenotype.

Proportions become more stable with larger samples, but systematic departures may also reflect linkage, selection, classification error, incomplete dominance, nonrandom survival, or an incorrect model.

Vocabulary: expected; observed; sampling variation; proportion; sample size; deviation; model fit.

Worked example with reasoning

A simple cross predicts 75% dominant phenotype. In 20 offspring, 13 show it: 65%. The deviation is 10 percentage points. That difference alone is not enough to reject the model; interpretation depends on sample size and how unlikely the result is under the model.

Guided question

Why does a larger sample usually provide a stronger test of an expected ratio than a very small one?

Independent work

  1. Convert 3:1 to probabilities.
  2. Predict counts for n=40 and n=400.
  3. Compare an observed 28:12 sample with expectation.
  4. List two biological and two measurement reasons for deviation.

Misconception check

Deviation from an expected ratio is not automatically evidence that Mendel's laws are false; all models are tested against sampling and design uncertainty.

Formative understanding check

A 3:1 model predicts how many of 200 offspring in the dominant category?

Worked response

150 as an expectation, not a guarantee.
