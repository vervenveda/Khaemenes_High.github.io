Safety, privacy and evidence boundary

Use fictional organisms, coins/cards, and published-style datasets. Do not require personal family histories, ancestry testing, fertility information, medical pedigrees, or personal genetic data. Trait inheritance never ranks human worth.

Explicit science instruction

Mendelian models describe inheritance at loci where allele transmission can be approximated by segregation of homologous chromosomes. A diploid individual carries two allele copies at a locus; meiosis separates homologues so a gamete ordinarily receives one.

Dominance describes phenotype expression in a specified heterozygote; it does not mean an allele is stronger, more common, better, or more likely to be inherited. Genotype names allele combinations; phenotype describes measured outcomes.

Vocabulary: locus; allele; homozygous; heterozygous; genotype; phenotype; dominance; segregation.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional Aa individual produces gametes carrying A or a. Under a simple segregation model and equal gamete viability, each has probability 1/2. Crossing Aa × Aa gives AA, Aa, Aa, aa as equally likely genotype cells in a Punnett representation, yielding expected genotype probabilities 1/4, 1/2, 1/4.

Guided question

Why does segregation arise from chromosome behavior rather than from alleles “choosing” where to go?

Independent work

  1. Model an Aa parent through meiosis.
  2. State gamete probabilities.
  3. Distinguish genotype from phenotype.
  4. Explain what dominance does—and does not—mean.

Misconception check

Dominant does not mean common, healthy, evolutionarily superior, or guaranteed to appear in every child.

Formative understanding check

What probability does an Aa parent contribute allele a under the simple model?

Worked response

1/2, assuming ordinary segregation and no transmission distortion.
