Controls, Confounders & Experimental Comparison

Design comparisons that separate a focal biological variable from alternative explanations and use controls as evidence tools rather than ritual steps.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

This week emphasizes supplied datasets, simulations, and design reasoning. Do not improvise sealed-organism chambers, temperature stress, chemical gas absorbers, or invasive measurements. Use only teacher-approved procedures under appropriate supervision.

Explicit science instruction

A strong experiment changes or compares a focal variable while holding other relevant conditions as consistent as practical. A confounding variable changes with the focal condition and can offer an alternative explanation for the result.

Controls serve different purposes. A negative control can reveal background signal; a positive control can show that a method is capable of detecting an effect; a baseline comparison can show what happens before or without a treatment. Not every experiment requires every control type, but every comparison needs a reasoned reference condition.

Randomization, replication, consistent measurement intervals, apparatus calibration, and blinded scoring where appropriate can reduce bias and improve inference.

Vocabulary: control; baseline; confounder; replicate; randomization; calibration; bias; causal inference.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional dataset compares metabolic proxy rate at two temperatures, but the warmer group also contains samples twice as massive as the cooler group. Temperature and mass are confounded. A higher total rate in the warmer group cannot be attributed confidently to temperature until rate is normalized by mass or sample masses are matched.

Guided question

If a treatment group is measured with a different sensor than the control group, how could that create a false biological difference?

Independent work

  1. Identify the focal variable in a sample experiment.
  2. Name three possible confounders.
  3. Design a reference/control condition.
  4. Explain how replication strengthens the comparison.
  5. State what evidence would still be needed before making a causal claim.

Misconception check

A control is not simply “the group nothing happens to.” It is a comparison chosen to reveal background effects, baseline behavior, method performance, or the influence of a specific variable.

Formative understanding check

What is the difference between a control variable and a control group?

Show the worked response

A control variable is a condition kept consistent across groups when possible. A control group or reference condition is a comparison used to interpret the effect of the focal condition.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
