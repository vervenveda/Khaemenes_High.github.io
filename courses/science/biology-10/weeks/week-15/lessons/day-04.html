Prepared-Image Mitosis Investigation

Build a reproducible image-classification protocol and test whether independent observers produce similar cell-stage evidence.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use prepared images, paper models, supplied datasets, or teacher-approved classroom microscopy. Do not culture unknown cells or microbes, collect human tissue, handle stains or preservatives without qualified supervision, or use personal medical records. This course does not diagnose growth disorders or disease.

Explicit science instruction

Scientific image analysis requires explicit criteria. A strong mitosis investigation specifies what counts as a cell, how fields are chosen, which visual features define each stage, how uncertain cells are handled, and how totals are recorded. This reduces observer bias and improves reproducibility.

Prepared root-tip images are useful because many cells are actively cycling. Students should use supplied or teacher-approved images rather than preparing biological material with stains or preservatives unless a qualified laboratory protocol is available.

Vocabulary: classification criterion; observer bias; inter-rater agreement; replicate; field selection; uncertain category; reproducibility; protocol.

Worked example with reasoning

Observer A classifies 50 cells: 38 interphase, 4 prophase, 3 metaphase, 3 anaphase, 2 telophase. Observer B reviews the same field and disagrees on five cells. Instead of averaging blindly, the observers compare the five disputed cells against the written criteria, document revisions, and then repeat classification. The disagreement becomes evidence for improving the protocol.

Guided question

Why is “I know metaphase when I see it” a weaker scientific method than a written set of observable classification criteria?

Independent work

  1. Write one classification criterion for interphase and each major mitotic stage.
  2. Classify at least 30 prepared cells or use a supplied count set.
  3. Record uncertain cells separately.
  4. Compare with a second classification if available.
  5. Revise one rule that caused disagreement.
  6. Calculate the final mitotic index.

Misconception check

Disagreement between observers does not automatically mean the data are useless. It reveals classification uncertainty that should be measured, discussed, and reduced where possible.

Formative understanding check

What makes a cell-stage classification protocol reproducible?

Show the worked response

Another observer should be able to apply the same explicit criteria to the same type of images, record ambiguous cases consistently, and obtain reasonably comparable results.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.

Teacher guidance

Foundation: use a small labeled image set before independent classification. Core: classify an unlabeled image set, record uncertainty, and calculate index. Extended: compare observers quantitatively and propose a blinded or randomized image-selection improvement.
