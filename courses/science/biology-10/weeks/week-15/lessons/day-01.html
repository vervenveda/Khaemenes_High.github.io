The Cell Cycle: Growth, Replication & Division

Explain how G1, S, G2, mitosis, and cytokinesis coordinate cell growth with genetic continuity.

Explicit science instruction

The cell cycle is an ordered sequence of growth, DNA replication, chromosome segregation, and cell division. In many dividing eukaryotic cells, interphase includes G1, S, and G2. During G1 the cell grows and performs its specialized functions. During S phase DNA is replicated. During G2 the cell continues preparation and checks whether conditions support division. Mitosis separates duplicated chromosomes into two nuclei, and cytokinesis partitions the cell.

Replication changes DNA amount, but it does not automatically change chromosome number. After S phase, each replicated chromosome contains two sister chromatids joined at a centromeric region. Counting rules depend on what structure is being counted.

Vocabulary: cell cycle; interphase; G1; S phase; G2; mitosis; cytokinesis; chromosome; sister chromatid; centromere.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional diploid cell begins G1 with 6 chromosomes. After S phase, it still has 6 chromosomes, but each chromosome has been replicated into two sister chromatids. Just before mitosis there are 12 chromatids organized as 6 replicated chromosomes. After sister chromatids separate during mitosis, each daughter nucleus receives 6 chromosomes.

  1. G1: 6 chromosomes.
  2. After S: 6 replicated chromosomes, 12 chromatids.
  3. After segregation: each daughter nucleus has 6 chromosomes.

Guided question

Why does DNA replication double the amount of DNA without necessarily doubling the chromosome count?

Independent work

  1. Draw a four-panel cycle model for G1, S, G2, and M/cytokinesis.
  2. Track one chromosome through all four panels.
  3. For a cell beginning with 8 chromosomes, state chromosome and chromatid counts after S phase.
  4. Add one checkpoint where division could pause.

Misconception check

Interphase is not a resting stage. Cells can be metabolically active, grow, duplicate organelles, synthesize molecules, and replicate DNA during interphase.

Formative understanding check

A cell has 10 chromosomes in G1. Immediately after S phase, how many chromosomes and sister chromatids does it have?

Show the worked response

It still has 10 chromosomes, now replicated into 20 sister chromatids. Chromosome number is defined by centromeric units, not simply by DNA molecule count.
