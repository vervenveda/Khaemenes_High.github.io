Growth, Tissue Renewal & Genetic Continuity

Synthesize cell-cycle evidence to explain how mitosis supports growth and tissue maintenance without assuming all cells divide continuously.

Safety and evidence boundary

Use prepared images, paper models, supplied datasets, or teacher-approved classroom microscopy. Do not culture unknown cells or microbes, collect human tissue, handle stains or preservatives without qualified supervision, or use personal medical records. This course does not diagnose growth disorders or disease.

Explicit science instruction

Multicellular growth often involves increased cell number, increased cell size, extracellular material, and changes in tissue organization. Mitosis supports growth by producing daughter nuclei with corresponding chromosome sets, but organismal development also requires differentiation, regulated gene expression, cell movement, cell death, and tissue-level signaling.

Many mature cells divide infrequently or not at all under ordinary conditions. Other cell populations renew continuously or retain stem/progenitor capacity. Therefore “growth = mitosis” is incomplete even though cell division is central to many growth processes.

Vocabulary: growth; tissue renewal; differentiation; stem/progenitor cell; genetic continuity; cell fate; turnover; lineage.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional tissue begins with 1,000 cycling cells. Over time, some divisions produce cells that continue cycling, while others differentiate and stop dividing. Cell number can rise while the fraction actively cycling falls. A mitotic-index measurement therefore describes the sampled moment, not the full developmental history.

Guided question

How can daughter cells inherit corresponding chromosome sets yet later become structurally and functionally different?

Misconception check

Genetic continuity does not require every daughter cell to express the same genes at the same levels. Shared DNA and different gene-expression programs can coexist.

Formative understanding check

What evidence would support the statement that mitosis helps maintain chromosome continuity during ordinary tissue growth?

Show the worked response

Prepared images can show replicated chromosomes segregating to opposite poles, chromosome-counting models can track corresponding sets, and daughter nuclei can be compared for chromosome complement. These support continuity while still leaving room for mutation and biological variation.
