Membrane Structure & Selective Permeability

Explain how phospholipid bilayer structure and membrane proteins create selective rather than absolute permeability.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use diagrams, paper models, or digital simulations only. Do not use unknown biological membranes or chemicals. Any demonstration involving water must remain low-risk and teacher-approved.

Explicit science instruction

The plasma membrane separates the cell interior from its surroundings while still permitting exchange. A common model describes a phospholipid bilayer with hydrophilic heads facing watery environments and hydrophobic tails oriented away from water. This arrangement forms a dynamic boundary rather than a rigid wall.

Selective permeability means some substances cross more readily than others. Small nonpolar molecules can often move through the lipid region more easily than ions or large polar molecules. Membrane proteins create channels, carriers, pumps, receptors, and attachment sites that change what the membrane can do.

Membranes are not empty barriers. Lipids and proteins move laterally, composition differs among membranes, and cells regulate transport proteins. The fluid-mosaic model is useful because it emphasizes both structure and motion, but it is still a simplified representation.

Vocabulary: plasma membrane; phospholipid bilayer; hydrophilic; hydrophobic; selective permeability; channel protein; carrier protein; fluid mosaic.

Worked example with reasoning

Compare oxygen and sodium ions. Oxygen is small and nonpolar, so it can cross the lipid bilayer by simple diffusion relatively readily. A sodium ion carries charge and interacts strongly with water, so crossing the hydrophobic interior is energetically unfavorable without a protein pathway. The difference follows from chemical properties, not from the membrane “deciding” what to allow.

Guided question

Why can a membrane be described as both a boundary and a transport surface? Identify one lipid-based property and one protein-based property.

Independent work

  1. Draw a labeled membrane cross-section with phospholipids and at least two protein types.
  2. Predict which of three substances—O₂, Na⁺, or a large protein—would cross the bare lipid bilayer most readily and justify the ranking.
  3. Explain why selective permeability is more accurate than “semi-permeable lets small things through.”
  4. Identify one limitation of a flat textbook membrane diagram.

Misconception check

Membrane selectivity is not based only on particle size. Charge, polarity, lipid solubility, concentration gradients, and transport proteins all matter.

Formative understanding check

Why does a charged ion usually need a membrane protein pathway even if the ion is very small?

Show the worked response

Its electrical charge interacts strongly with water and poorly with the hydrophobic membrane interior, making direct passage through the lipid region unfavorable.
