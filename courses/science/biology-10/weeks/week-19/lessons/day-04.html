Meiosis Simulation: Sources of Variation

Run a repeatable chromosome-model simulation that separates independent assortment, crossing over, and random fertilization as distinct sources of variation.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and privacy boundary

Use fictional organisms, chromosome cards, beads, paper models, or teacher-approved simulations. Do not require family genetic histories, fertility information, ancestry testing, reproductive information, medical records, or personal genetic data. Human worth is never ranked by chromosome combinations or genetic variation.

Explicit science instruction

Models are strongest when each mechanism is represented separately before mechanisms are combined. Independent assortment changes which homologues enter a gamete. Crossing over changes allele combinations within homologous chromosome pairs. Random fertilization combines one gamete from each parent by chance.

A classroom model should record every randomization rule so results can be reproduced or compared. The goal is not to imitate every molecular detail but to test how mechanisms contribute to variation.

Vocabulary: simulation; randomization; replicate; independent assortment; recombination; fertilization; outcome frequency; model boundary.

Worked example with reasoning

In a 2-pair fictional species, Student A first randomizes homolog orientation and records the gamete. Then a crossover coin flip determines whether one pair recombines. Repeating the process 20 times produces a frequency table. Unequal frequencies in 20 trials do not disprove the underlying probability model because small samples fluctuate.

Guided question

Why should the simulation record independent assortment and crossing over as separate events rather than one unexplained shuffle?

Independent work

  1. Define the randomization rule for homolog orientation.
  2. Define a separate rule for whether crossing over occurs in the model.
  3. Run at least 20 simulated meioses or analyze a supplied 20-trial dataset.
  4. Tabulate gamete outcomes.
  5. Compare observed frequencies with simple expectations.
  6. List at least three ways the model simplifies real meiosis.

Misconception check

A simulation outcome is evidence about the behavior of the model. It is not direct evidence that a particular real person's gametes or children will have those exact combinations.

Formative understanding check

Why can repeated simulation runs produce different frequency tables even when the rules are unchanged?

Show the worked response

Random sampling produces variation between finite runs. Larger numbers of trials often produce more stable estimates of the model's expected probabilities.
