Meiosis I, Crossing Over & Recombination

Model homolog pairing and crossing over without implying that recombination creates entirely new genes from nothing.

Explicit science instruction

During early meiosis I, homologous chromosomes pair closely. Non-sister chromatids can exchange corresponding DNA segments through crossing over. This recombination creates chromosomes containing new combinations of alleles already present on the homologues.

In meiosis I, homologous chromosomes—not sister chromatids—separate. Sister chromatids remain joined until meiosis II. This distinction is central to understanding chromosome-number reduction.

Vocabulary: synapsis; tetrad; crossing over; recombination; non-sister chromatids; chiasma; meiosis I; homolog segregation.

Worked example with reasoning

Suppose one homolog carries A—B and the other carries a—b at two linked loci. A crossover between the loci can produce chromatids with A—b and a—B combinations. The genes remain at corresponding loci; recombination changes which allele combinations travel together.

Guided question

Why does crossing over usually change allele combinations without changing the number of chromosome types?

Independent work

  1. Build a two-homolog paper model with two loci.
  2. Model one crossover between non-sister chromatids.
  3. List parental and recombinant chromatid types.
  4. Explain what separates in meiosis I.
  5. State one limitation of the paper model.

Misconception check

Crossing over does not normally occur between sister chromatids as the main source of new allele combinations in standard meiosis models. It is modeled between homologous non-sister chromatids after homolog pairing.

Formative understanding check

What is the key segregation event of meiosis I?

Show the worked response

Homologous chromosomes move to opposite cells while sister chromatids remain associated.
