Homologous Chromosomes & the Purpose of Meiosis

Explain why sexual life cycles require chromosome-number reduction before fertilization and distinguish homologues from sister chromatids.

Explicit science instruction

Diploid cells contain chromosome pairs called homologous chromosomes. Homologues carry corresponding loci in corresponding regions, but they can carry different alleles. Before meiosis, DNA is replicated, so each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids.

Meiosis produces cells with one chromosome from each homologous pair. This reduction division is important because fertilization combines chromosome sets. Without reduction, chromosome number would tend to double each generation.

Vocabulary: diploid; haploid; homologous chromosomes; sister chromatids; locus; meiosis; gamete; fertilization; reduction division.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional species has 2n = 6. A diploid cell has three homologous pairs. After DNA replication it still has 6 chromosomes, now made of 12 sister chromatids. After meiosis is complete, each typical gamete contains n = 3 chromosomes—one from each homologous pair.

Guided question

Why are homologous chromosomes not the same thing as sister chromatids?

Independent work

  1. Draw a 2n = 4 cell before replication, after replication, after meiosis I, and after meiosis II.
  2. Label homologues and sister chromatids.
  3. Track chromosome number and chromatid number at each stage.
  4. Explain why fertilization restores diploidy.

Misconception check

Meiosis does not begin with a haploid cell. It begins with a diploid germ-line precursor whose chromosomes have replicated before the two meiotic divisions.

Formative understanding check

A species has 2n = 8. What is the usual chromosome number in a gamete?

Show the worked response

n = 4. The gamete receives one chromosome from each homologous pair.
