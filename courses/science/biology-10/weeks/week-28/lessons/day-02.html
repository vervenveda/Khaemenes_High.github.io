Hardy–Weinberg as a Baseline Model

Use Hardy–Weinberg expectations as a null/baseline model, not as a claim that real populations never evolve.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use fictional populations, cards, spreadsheets, or published aggregate data. Do not use population-genetic concepts to rank human groups or infer personal ancestry, intelligence, worth, or disease. Evolutionary mechanisms are population-level processes.

Explicit science instruction

For a two-allele locus under ideal assumptions—very large population, random mating, no selection, negligible mutation, no migration—allele frequencies remain stable and genotype expectations are p², 2pq, q².

The model is useful because deviations or changes motivate questions about mechanism, sampling and assumptions. It is a baseline, not a description of every real population.

Worked example with reasoning

If p=.6 and q=.4, expected genotype frequencies are AA=.36, Aa=.48, aa=.16. In N=500, expected counts are 180, 240, 80.

Guided question

Why is Hardy–Weinberg useful even though its assumptions are idealized?

Independent work

  1. Compute p², 2pq, q² for p=.8.
  2. Convert to counts for N=1,000.
  3. List five assumptions.
  4. Describe one cause of deviation and one cause of apparent deviation from sampling.

Misconception check

Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium is not “no change anywhere in biology”; it is a locus-specific population-genetic baseline under stated assumptions.

Formative understanding check

For p=.5, expected heterozygote frequency?

Worked response

2pq = .50.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
