Gene Flow, Mutation & Selection as Distinct Mechanisms

Compare mechanisms by the kind of change they introduce rather than labeling all frequency change “natural selection.”

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious DayNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use fictional populations, cards, spreadsheets, or published aggregate data. Do not use population-genetic concepts to rank human groups or infer personal ancestry, intelligence, worth, or disease. Evolutionary mechanisms are population-level processes.

Explicit science instruction

Gene flow moves alleles among populations through migration and reproduction. Mutation creates new sequence variants. Selection changes frequencies when heritable variants differ in reproductive contribution. Drift changes frequencies stochastically.

Several mechanisms can act simultaneously, and the same observed frequency change can have multiple plausible causes.

Worked example with reasoning

Population A has allele X=.2 and B has X=.8. Migration from B into A raises X in A before any fitness difference is measured: gene flow is sufficient to explain the immediate change. If X carriers later leave more offspring, selection may also contribute.

Guided question

What evidence would help distinguish gene flow from selection?

Independent work

  1. Match four datasets to mechanisms.
  2. For one dataset, propose two alternative mechanisms.
  3. Identify evidence needed to distinguish them.
  4. Explain mutation's special role in creating new alleles.

Misconception check

Natural selection is not the only evolutionary mechanism, and not every allele-frequency increase is adaptation.

Formative understanding check

Which mechanism directly introduces a new allele sequence?

Worked response

Mutation.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
