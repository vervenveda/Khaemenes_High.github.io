Allele & Genotype Frequencies

Calculate allele frequencies from genotype counts and distinguish population composition from individual genotype.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use fictional populations, cards, spreadsheets, or published aggregate data. Do not use population-genetic concepts to rank human groups or infer personal ancestry, intelligence, worth, or disease. Evolutionary mechanisms are population-level processes.

Explicit science instruction

Population genetics tracks how allele and genotype frequencies change across generations. For a diploid locus with alleles A and a, each individual contributes two allele copies. If counts are AA, Aa, aa, then frequency of A is (2AA + Aa)/(2N), and a is (2aa + Aa)/(2N).

Allele frequency belongs to a population sample; an individual does not have an “allele frequency.”

Vocabulary: allele frequency; genotype frequency; population; gene pool; diploid; generation.

Worked example with reasoning

In 100 fictional organisms: 36 AA, 48 Aa, 16 aa. A copies = 72+48=120 of 200, so p(A)=0.60; q(a)=0.40. Genotype frequencies are .36, .48, .16.

Guided question

Why do we divide allele counts by 2N in a diploid population?

Independent work

  1. Calculate frequencies for 25 AA, 50 Aa, 25 aa.
  2. Verify p+q=1.
  3. Distinguish allele and genotype frequency.
  4. State sampling limits.

Misconception check

A common allele is not necessarily dominant, advantageous, ancestral, or “better.” Frequency and dominance are different concepts.

Formative understanding check

If p=.7 in a two-allele model, q?

Worked response

.3.
