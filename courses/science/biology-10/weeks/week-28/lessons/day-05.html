Population-Genetics Simulation & Synthesis

Track allele frequencies across generations and defend mechanism claims with uncertainty.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesPrevious Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use fictional populations, cards, spreadsheets, or published aggregate data. Do not use population-genetic concepts to rank human groups or infer personal ancestry, intelligence, worth, or disease. Evolutionary mechanisms are population-level processes.

Explicit science instruction

A simulation can isolate mechanisms by changing one parameter at a time. Record initial frequencies, population size, migration, fitness assumptions, mutation setting and random seed/rule where possible. Multiple runs reveal stochastic variability.

Worked example with reasoning

Ten drift-only runs from p=.5 produce different final p values. Adding strong directional selection shifts the distribution of outcomes upward, but drift still creates variation among runs. Evidence comes from the distribution across replicates, not one lucky trajectory.

Guided question

Why are replicate simulation runs necessary for stochastic evolutionary models?

Independent work

  1. Run or analyze at least 10 trajectories.
  2. Graph allele frequency by generation.
  3. Compare one mechanism treatment with control.
  4. Write a CER naming mechanism and uncertainty.

Misconception check

A simulation demonstrates consequences of its rules, not proof that a real population followed the same history.

Formative understanding check

What does a spread of outcomes across identical simulation rules demonstrate?

Worked response

Stochastic variation generated by the model.

Open-book formative practice only; not an official mastery attempt or grade.
