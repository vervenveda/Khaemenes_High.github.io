Exponential Growth & Per-Capita Rate

Model rapid population change when proportional growth is approximately constant.

Weekly Classroom & EvidenceBiology CourseScience HallArchaemenesNext Day

Safety and evidence boundary

Use supplied long-term ecological datasets and low-risk simulations. Do not manipulate wild populations, introduce species, enter unsafe field sites, or infer a single cause from correlation alone. Population models are approximations with explicit scale and boundary assumptions.

Explicit science instruction

In an exponential model, population change is proportional to current population size. In continuous form dN/dt=rN; in discrete contexts growth can be represented by repeated multiplication. Exponential growth can describe short intervals with abundant resources but rarely persists indefinitely.

Vocabulary: population size; growth rate; per-capita rate; exponential; doubling time; birth; death; immigration; emigration.

Worked example with reasoning

A fictional population grows 10% per interval from N=100: 110, 121, 133.1... The absolute increase grows because the same proportion acts on a larger base.

Guided question

Why does constant percent growth produce increasing absolute additions?

Independent work

  1. Calculate five intervals at 5% growth.
  2. Graph N over time.
  3. Compare absolute and percent change.
  4. State two assumptions.

Misconception check

Exponential does not mean “fast” in every interval; it describes proportional growth structure.

Formative understanding check

100 organisms growing 20% gives how many after one interval?

Worked response

120.
