Unit 00 · Day 4

Evidence, Data & Models

Interpret patterns without overclaiming, investigate anomalies, distinguish correlation from causation, and evaluate model limitations.

45–60 minutes Tier 1 · Home-safe Portfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Write claims supported by a dataset
  • Identify what evidence does not justify
  • Evaluate a scientific claim and a model

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Provided seedling dataset
  • One scientific claim from media or advertising
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Follow the written procedure. Stop whenever materials, supervision, health needs, or environmental conditions differ from the lesson.

1. Patterns and anomalies

Data analysis identifies patterns without hiding variability. An anomalous result should be checked, repeated when possible, and documented rather than silently deleted.

Evidence statement

2. Correlation and causation

When two variables change together, they are correlated. Establishing causation requires stronger evidence, a defensible mechanism, control of alternative explanations, and often repeated studies.

Source check

Find a scientific claim in a news article, advertisement, video, or social post. Record the claim, the evidence offered, the original source if available, and one unanswered question.

3. Models are selective

A model may explain one feature well while omitting others. Evaluate what is represented, what is simplified, the scale, assumptions, evidence, and the conditions under which the model may fail.

Portfolio evidence

Revise yesterday’s model description by adding its assumptions and one way it could be tested or improved.
