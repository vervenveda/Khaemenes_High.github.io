Unit 00 · Day 1

Science as a Way of Knowing

Separate what is observed from what is inferred, and understand how questions, models, theories, and laws function in science.

45–60 minutes Tier 1 · Home-safe Portfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish observation from inference
  • Write a testable scientific question
  • Explain the different roles of models, theories, and laws

Materials

  • Science notebook or paper
  • Pencil or pen
  • One ordinary nonhazardous object
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Follow the written procedure. Stop whenever materials, supervision, health needs, or environmental conditions differ from the lesson.

1. Observation and inference

An observation reports information obtained through the senses or an instrument. An inference is a reasoned interpretation based on observations and prior knowledge.

Practice

Place an ordinary object in front of you. Write five observations without explaining why the object has those properties. Then write two inferences and label them clearly.

2. Questions and hypotheses

A scientific question must be answerable through evidence. A useful hypothesis proposes a testable relationship and does not need to be correct to be scientifically valuable.

Rewrite the question

Change “Which paper towel is best?” into a measurable question that identifies what “best” means.

3. Models, theories, and laws

  • A model represents selected features of a system and always has limitations.
  • A scientific theory is a broad, well-supported explanatory framework.
  • A scientific law describes a consistent relationship or pattern under stated conditions.

A theory does not become a law after receiving “enough proof”; theories and laws serve different scientific purposes.

Portfolio evidence

Draw or describe one model used in science. State one feature it represents well and one limitation.

4. Essential question & evidence ladder

Essential question: How does science move from what is directly observed to explanations that can be tested, challenged, and improved?

Scientific reasoning usually moves through several levels. An observation records what was detected. A pattern summarizes repeated observations. An inference proposes what those observations may mean. A model represents selected parts of a system. A broader explanation connects evidence, mechanisms, and tested ideas. Moving upward requires more reasoning, so the claim should become more cautious unless the supporting evidence also becomes stronger.

Evidence ladder

For the statement “The plant bent toward the window because it detected directional light,” identify which words describe an observation and which words are an inference or explanation. What additional evidence would strengthen the explanation?

5. Worked reasoning example

A student places two identical cups of water in the same room. One cup is covered and one is uncovered. After one day, the uncovered cup contains less water.

  1. Observation: the measured water level in the uncovered cup decreased more than the level in the covered cup.
  2. Inference: water likely left the uncovered cup through evaporation.
  3. Alternative explanation to consider: the cups may not have started with exactly the same volume, or one measurement may have been less precise.
  4. Better test: repeat the comparison with equal starting volumes, the same container type, repeated trials, and a consistent measuring method.

The inference may be reasonable, but good science distinguishes the measured result from the explanation and asks what evidence could challenge the explanation.

6. Vocabulary & misconception checks

TermUse it accurately
ObservationInformation obtained through senses or instruments.
InferenceAn interpretation supported by observations and prior knowledge.
HypothesisA testable proposed relationship or explanation.
ModelA selective representation used to explain, predict, or test ideas.
TheoryA broad explanatory framework supported by converging evidence.
LawA concise description of a consistent relationship under stated conditions.
Misconception check

Correct both statements: “A theory is just a guess.” “A theory becomes a law after scientists prove it.” Explain why each statement misrepresents how scientific knowledge is organized.

7. Retrieval, mentor conference & portfolio handoff

  1. Write one observation and one inference about the same ordinary object.
  2. Turn a vague question into a measurable scientific question.
  3. Name one limitation of a familiar scientific model.
  4. Explain the difference between a theory and a law without ranking one above the other.
