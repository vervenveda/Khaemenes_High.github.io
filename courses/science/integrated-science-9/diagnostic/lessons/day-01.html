Unit 00 · Day 1

Science as a Way of Knowing

Separate what is observed from what is inferred, and understand how questions, models, theories, and laws function in science.

45–60 minutes Tier 1 · Home-safe Portfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish observation from inference
  • Write a testable scientific question
  • Explain the different roles of models, theories, and laws

Materials

  • Science notebook or paper
  • Pencil or pen
  • One ordinary nonhazardous object
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Follow the written procedure. Stop whenever materials, supervision, health needs, or environmental conditions differ from the lesson.

1. Observation and inference

An observation reports information obtained through the senses or an instrument. An inference is a reasoned interpretation based on observations and prior knowledge.

Practice

Place an ordinary object in front of you. Write five observations without explaining why the object has those properties. Then write two inferences and label them clearly.

2. Questions and hypotheses

A scientific question must be answerable through evidence. A useful hypothesis proposes a testable relationship and does not need to be correct to be scientifically valuable.

Rewrite the question

Change “Which paper towel is best?” into a measurable question that identifies what “best” means.

3. Models, theories, and laws

  • A model represents selected features of a system and always has limitations.
  • A scientific theory is a broad, well-supported explanatory framework.
  • A scientific law describes a consistent relationship or pattern under stated conditions.

A theory does not become a law after receiving “enough proof”; theories and laws serve different scientific purposes.

Portfolio evidence

Draw or describe one model used in science. State one feature it represents well and one limitation.
