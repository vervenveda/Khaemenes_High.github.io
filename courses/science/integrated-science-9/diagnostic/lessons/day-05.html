Readiness & Learning Goals

Use diagnostic evidence to select supports, establish a portfolio, and set a personal scientific inquiry goal.

1. Complete the readiness diagnostic

The diagnostic identifies strengths and review needs. It is untimed, may be retaken, and is not a permanent grade.

2. Interpret results responsibly

  • Use domain results to select supports, not to label ability.
  • Begin with the pathway that provides an appropriate level of support.
  • Change pathways by skill when needed.
  • Correct misconceptions and retain evidence of growth.

The portfolio will preserve investigations, graphs, source evaluations, revisions, projects, assessments, and reflections.

4. Essential question & responsible diagnostic interpretation

Essential question: How can diagnostic results guide learning without becoming a label about intelligence or future ability?

A diagnostic is a snapshot of performance on selected skills under particular conditions. It can identify what is already secure and what deserves review, but it cannot measure every scientific strength. A learner may show strong reasoning while needing more measurement practice, or strong content knowledge while needing help interpreting graphs. The useful question is not “What kind of science student am I forever?” but “What evidence tells me what to practice next?”

Pathways are therefore supports, not identities. A student may use Foundation support for one skill, Core work for another, and Extended work where evidence shows readiness.

5. Worked learning-plan example

Consider a fictional diagnostic record:

DomainResultReasonable next step
Safety reasoning90%Maintain Core work and keep using written risk scans.
Measurement60%Review units, tool choice, repeated measurement, and range before advancing.
Data interpretation70%Practice graph reading and claim-evidence reasoning with guided examples.
Scientific vocabulary85%Continue using terms in explanations rather than memorizing definitions alone.

The learner is not “a 60% science student.” The evidence instead identifies a specific measurement skill that can be strengthened. A later check should be used to see whether the targeted practice changed performance.

6. Build an evidence-based goal

A useful goal names the skill, the practice, and the evidence that will show improvement.

Weak goal

“Get better at science.”

Stronger goal

“During the next two weeks, I will record units on every measurement, complete two repeated-measurement tables, and explain range and one source of variation without a model answer.”

Goals may be revised when new evidence appears. Revision is not failure; it is part of scientific learning.

Misconception check

Correct this statement: “My diagnostic score tells me how good I am at science.” Explain what a diagnostic can legitimately tell you, what it cannot tell you, and what evidence would show growth later.

7. Retrieval, mentor planning conference & portfolio launch

  1. Why should a diagnostic result not be treated as a permanent label?
  2. What is one current strength supported by your evidence?
  3. What is one specific skill that deserves review?
  4. What artifact or performance will show that the skill has improved?
