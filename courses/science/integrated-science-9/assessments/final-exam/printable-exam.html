Unit 00

1. [Safety] A planned investigation has a serious risk that cannot be controlled with available equipment. What should happen first?

  1. Revise or replace the method before beginning.
  2. Proceed with fewer trials.
  3. Ask participants to accept the risk.
  4. Continue if the scientific question is important.

2. [Personal protection] Which action is appropriate when an approved activity requires splash goggles?

  1. Share one pair while materials are being handled.
  2. Wear correctly fitting splash goggles for the entire exposure period.
  3. Wear ordinary eyeglasses instead.
  4. Remove the goggles whenever they fog.

3. [Chemical habits] What is the safest rule for an unlabeled substance?

  1. Taste a tiny amount.
  2. Mix it with water to see what happens.
  3. Do not handle or use it; inform the supervising adult.
  4. Smell it closely to identify it.

4. [Emergency response] A liquid splashes into a student’s eye during a supervised laboratory activity. What is the correct immediate response?

  1. Finish recording data before responding.
  2. Rub the eye with a dry towel.
  3. Wait to see whether pain develops.
  4. Use the approved eyewash procedure immediately and alert the supervisor.

5. [Data integrity] A result does not match the other trials. What is the most responsible response?

  1. Document it, check the method and equipment, and repeat if appropriate.
  2. Delete it without explanation.
  3. Change it to equal the mean.
  4. Conclude that all other trials are wrong.

6. [Ethics] Which study plan requires informed consent and additional ethical safeguards?

  1. Timing a paper helicopter.
  2. Collecting identifiable health information from human participants.
  3. Measuring the length of classroom pencils.
  4. Using a published anonymous dataset.

7. [Environmental responsibility] Which field practice is appropriate?

  1. Remove rare specimens for a personal collection.
  2. Publish exact locations of vulnerable species.
  3. Observe from a permitted stable location and minimize disturbance.
  4. Enter a protected nesting area for a closer photograph.

8. [Scientific habits] Which notebook entry best supports reproducibility?

  1. Only the final conclusion.
  2. A copied procedure with no changes recorded.
  3. A list of results without units.
  4. Date, materials, exact method, measurements, changes, and unexpected events.

Constructed Responses

Units 00–01

Scientific Inquiry, Safety & Evidence

Design a safe investigation of how one controllable factor affects the cooling rate of water. Define the independent, dependent, and at least four controlled variables; write an operational definition; specify equipment resolution, repeated trials, a data table, graph choice, and analysis method; explain how you would handle anomalies and uncertainty; and identify safety, consent, privacy, and environmental boundaries. Conclude with an example claim, evidence statement, and scientific reasoning statement.

Units 02–04

Cells, Homeostasis & Inheritance

A multicellular organism experiences dehydration and must maintain cell function. Explain how membrane transport, surface-area-to-volume ratio, specialized cells, circulatory transport, kidney function, nervous/endocrine coordination, and negative feedback contribute to homeostasis. Then explain how mitosis supports repair, how meiosis and fertilization create variation, and why genotype does not determine phenotype with certainty. Include one calculation or quantitative model and two ethical limits on biological or genetic data.

Units 05–07

Ecosystems, Atoms & Chemical Change

A wetland receives nutrient-rich runoff and a proposed treatment material is evaluated. Construct a food-web and matter-cycle explanation for possible ecological effects; distinguish observation from evolutionary or historical inference; calculate one biodiversity or population measure; use atomic structure and periodic patterns to explain the treatment material; write and balance one relevant reaction; analyze conservation, energy transfer, reaction rate, and equilibrium; and evaluate safety, community, biodiversity, and source-quality tradeoffs.

Units 08–09

Motion, Energy, Circuits, Waves & Information

Design a low-voltage sensor system mounted on a 4.0 kg cart. The cart changes velocity from 1.0 m/s to 5.0 m/s in 2.0 s; calculate acceleration, net force, initial and final kinetic energy, and momentum change.
