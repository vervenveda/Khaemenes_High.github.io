Science Foundations Mastery Assessment

Demonstrate readiness across the six-week bridge. This assessment measures transferable scientific reasoning—not memory of lesson wording. A score of 80% or higher, with required reasoning corrections, releases Official Unit 1.

30 scored prompts6 strands80% masteryNAIB readiness record
Assessment integrity: No answer key appears on the student-facing page. Reasoning items require evidence or mechanism, not keyword matching. Preserve stable IDs when this assessment is converted to interactive scoring.

Assessment architecture

Five scored prompts represent each bridge strand. Use 1 point per prompt for a 30-point total. For multi-part reasoning prompts, award the point only when the required reasoning is substantially demonstrated. 24/30 = 80% overall mastery.

Overall mastery does not hide strand gaps. NAIB should report both total score and six strand percentages. Essential reasoning gaps should trigger targeted refresh even when the total score reaches 80%.

Strand A · Scientific Thinking & Evidence

  1. ID SF-A01. A student writes, “The liquid is cloudy, so a new substance definitely formed.” Separate the observation from the inference and explain why the conclusion is too strong.
  2. ID SF-A02. Rewrite “Does light help plants?” as a measurable, testable question containing an independent variable and dependent variable.
  3. ID SF-A03. In an investigation changing water temperature and measuring dissolving time, identify the independent variable, dependent variable, and one relevant control.
  4. ID SF-A04. Explain the difference among a scientific claim, evidence, and reasoning.
  5. ID SF-A05. Two variables increase together in an observational dataset. Explain why this does not by itself establish causation and propose one competing explanation.

Strand B · Measurement & Quantitative Reasoning

  1. ID SF-B01. Convert 2.75 m to cm using a conversion factor and show unit cancellation.
  2. ID SF-B02. A sample has mass 63 g and volume 21 cm³. Calculate density with units.
  3. ID SF-B03. A temperature rises from 18 °C to 30 °C in 4 minutes. Calculate the average rate of temperature change with units.
  4. ID SF-B04. A measurement increases from 50 to 65. Calculate the absolute change and percent change.
  5. ID SF-B05. A calculator gives 7.3170731707 g/cm³ from measurements recorded only to tenths. Explain why reporting every calculator digit is misleading.

Strand D · Matter & Energy Foundations

  1. ID SF-D01. Distinguish a compound from a mixture using particle-level reasoning.
  2. ID SF-D02. Ice melts in a sealed container. Explain why this is a physical change and what happens to particle arrangement without changing chemical identity.
  3. ID SF-D03. A sealed reaction vessel has mass 150.2 g before reaction and 150.2 g after bubbles form. Explain what the measurement supports about conservation of matter.
  4. ID SF-D04. Explain why a warm bathtub may contain more total thermal energy than a much hotter cup of water.
  5. ID SF-D05. Trace energy through a battery-powered fan and correct the statement “the fan uses up the energy until it disappears.”

Strand E · Cells, Scale & Biological Organization

  1. ID SF-E01. State one core idea of cell theory and explain why it matters for biological organization.
  2. ID SF-E02. Explain diffusion as net movement and state why particles still move at dynamic equilibrium.
  3. ID SF-E03. Explain osmosis across a selectively permeable membrane without describing water as moving intentionally.
  4. ID SF-E04. Cubes with side lengths 1, 2, and 3 units have SA:V ratios 6:1, 3:1, and 2:1. Describe the trend and one biological implication.
  5. ID SF-E05. Build a functional chain from specialized cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. Explain one relationship between adjacent levels.

Strand F · Systems, Models & Scientific Literacy

  1. ID SF-F01. Define a useful system boundary for a potted plant investigation and identify one matter input, one matter output, and one energy transfer.
  2. ID SF-F02. Explain why “exercise → breathing” is an incomplete causal model and add at least one intermediate mechanism or measurable state change.
  3. ID SF-F03. Distinguish negative feedback from a simple one-way causal chain and explain where a feedback loop must close.
  4. ID SF-F04. Explain the difference among a statement directly supported by a source (S), a paraphrase (P), and a student inference (I).
  5. ID SF-F05. A new dataset conflicts with your original model. Describe the scientifically appropriate response, including what should happen to the model and what further evidence may be needed.

Integrated readiness performance

This performance is required as qualitative evidence but is not an extra point bonus that can conceal weak foundational mastery.

The Closed Habitat

A fictional transparent habitat contains a plant, moist soil, air, and sensors. After the habitat is moved closer to a light source, supplied records show rising daytime temperature, changing humidity, altered gas concentrations, and increased plant growth over several days.

Construct a concise scientific explanation that: defines the system boundary; distinguishes matter from energy; uses at least three quantitative observations; includes one rate or percent change; connects cell-level and organism-level processes; identifies one possible feedback; states one limitation; proposes a competing explanation; and identifies the next measurement needed to distinguish explanations.

Reasoning correction protocol

  1. Name the stable ID and skill that needs repair.
  2. Explain the original reasoning—not merely the selected answer.
  3. Identify the evidence, unit, model, or mechanism that changes the reasoning.
  4. Write the corrected explanation.
  5. Complete a new parallel problem that tests the same skill in a different context.
  6. Record the correction as complete only after the learner demonstrates the reasoning independently.

Readiness release

Mastery demonstrated: NAIB records the Science Foundations Bridge as complete and routes the learner directly to Official Unit 1. The six bridge weeks remain part of the supported 42-week pathway record.
Mastery still developing: Do not restart all six weeks automatically. Use the strand profile and stable IDs to prescribe the smallest effective refresh, then provide a parallel reassessment.
