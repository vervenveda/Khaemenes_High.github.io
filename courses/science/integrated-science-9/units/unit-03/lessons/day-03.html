The Heart, Vessels & Blood

Explain how biological structure creates physical consequences that support circulation, exchange, transport, and homeostasis.

Heart structure → pressure → flow

Atria receive blood and ventricles pump it. Valves reduce backflow, helping preserve directional movement through the heart. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right because it must generate the greater pressure needed to move blood through systemic circulation.

Reasoning pattern: do not stop at naming a structure. Ask what physical consequence the structure produces, then explain how that consequence supports the biological function.
Heart reasoning check

Use the pattern structure → physical consequence → biological function to explain the left ventricular wall and one heart valve. For each arrow, state the mechanism rather than writing only “causes.”

Vessels: structure creates different flow conditions

VesselStructurePhysical consequenceBiological function
ArteryThick, elastic, muscular wallWithstands changing pressure; elastic tissue can stretch and recoilSupports blood flow away from the heart under relatively high pressure
VeinThinner wall, relatively large lumen, valves in many veinsAccommodates lower-pressure return; valves reduce backward movementSupports return of blood toward the heart
CapillaryMicroscopically thin exchange barrier and very small diameterCreates a short diffusion distance and close contact with tissuesSupports exchange of gases, nutrients, water, ions, wastes, and other substances
Transfer from Unit 02

Why does reducing diffusion distance matter at a capillary? Connect your answer to the particle/transport reasoning you used for membranes. Then identify one additional factor besides distance that can influence net exchange.

Blood is a transport tissue, not just a liquid

Plasma transports dissolved substances and thermal energy. Red blood cells are specialized for oxygen transport. White blood cells contribute to defense. Platelets and clotting proteins participate in processes that limit blood loss after vessel damage.

Portfolio evidence

Choose an artery, a capillary, and a red blood cell. For each, write a complete structure → physical consequence → biological function explanation. Then predict one system consequence if the selected structure were substantially different.

Critique the pump-and-pipe model

A pump-and-pipe analogy can help represent pressure-driven flow and directional transport, but it is incomplete. Blood vessels are living tissues; vessel diameter can change; capillaries exchange matter with tissues; blood contains cells and signaling molecules; and circulation participates in regulation rather than simply moving an inert fluid through rigid tubes.

Model-limits challenge

Identify two features the pump-and-pipe model represents reasonably well, two important biological features it leaves out, and one observation that would require you to revise a too-simple version of the model.
