Excretion follows internal matter

Excretion removes metabolic waste products or substances present in excess after they have entered or arisen within internal body processes. The lungs excrete carbon dioxide; the kidneys remove urea and help regulate water and ions. Egestion removes material that remained within the digestive tract and was not absorbed.

Continuity from Days 06–07

Trace nitrogen from a fictional protein-rich meal through digestion → amino-acid absorption → portal blood → liver metabolism/deamination → urea formation → systemic blood → kidney. Explain why the final removal of urea is excretion rather than egestion.

A nephron is more than a passive filter

A simplified nephron model includes several distinct processes. Filtration moves water and many small dissolved substances from blood into nephron fluid at the renal corpuscle. Selective reabsorption moves selected substances from nephron fluid back toward the blood. Secretion can move selected substances from blood or surrounding tissue into tubular fluid. Excretion is the final removal of substances in urine.

Boundary: “the kidney filters blood and everything filtered becomes urine” is incorrect. Filtration is followed by extensive, selective transport.

Daily word · reabsorption

Reabsorption is movement of selected substances from nephron fluid back into the blood or internal fluid compartments. It is not “absorbing a substance for the first time”; the prefix re- reflects recovery after filtration.

Precision challenge

Correct this statement: “The kidney absorbs glucose from the urine because glucose was never in the blood.” Use blood, filtration, filtrate, reabsorption, and excretion in your repair.

Nephron sorting challenge

Think of the nephron as a dynamic sorting-and-regulation system rather than a sieve. The scientific question is not just “Was it filtered?” but “What happened to it after filtration?”

Portfolio evidence

Create four labeled pathways—glucose, water, an ion, and urea. For each, show: blood → filtration status → nephron-fluid route → reabsorption or secretion if relevant → final destination. Label each arrow as bulk transport, filtration, membrane transport, regulated reabsorption, secretion, or excretion.

Model consequence

For each substance, predict one consequence if selective reabsorption failed substantially. Mark each answer as an inference from the model, not an observation from an individual person.

Water balance is a feedback problem

Water balance depends on intake, losses, circulation, nervous/endocrine signaling, and kidney responses. When body water is low in a simplified model, changes in internal fluid conditions are detected and increased ADH signaling promotes greater water reabsorption in parts of the kidney, producing a smaller volume of more concentrated urine.

Negative-feedback loop

Build a mechanism-labeled loop using: body water falls → internal concentration/osmotic conditions change → receptors/control centers detect deviation → ADH signaling increases → kidney water reabsorption increases → urine water loss decreases → internal conditions move toward the regulated range.

Important model limit: conserving water is not the same as replacing water already lost. A feedback response can reduce further loss while the original deficit still exists.

Generalization challenge

Rewrite the water-balance loop using only the abstract terms variable → receptor/detector → control/signal → effector → response → changed variable. Then explain which parts would remain the same in a different negative-feedback system and which would change.

Urine is evidence, not a diagnosis

Urine composition and concentration can contain useful physiological information, but interpretation depends on timing, intake, environment, medicines, activity, kidney function, measurement method, and many other variables.

Evidence boundary: one urine observation cannot by itself diagnose hydration status, kidney disease, endocrine disorders, or overall health.
Claim-strength challenge

A fictional student observes a darker urine sample in a model dataset and concludes, “This proves severe dehydration.” Rewrite the conclusion so its certainty matches the evidence. Then list at least three additional observations or controls needed before making a stronger causal claim.

Model limits

The nephron model used here is intentionally simplified. Real kidney function involves different nephron regions, transport proteins, countercurrent processes, blood-pressure effects, hormonal regulation, acid-base balance, secretion, and interactions with many other systems.

Identify one feature the simplified model explains well, two mechanisms it leaves out, and one reason a more detailed model might change a prediction.
