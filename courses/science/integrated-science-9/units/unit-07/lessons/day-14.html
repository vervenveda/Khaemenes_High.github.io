Unit 07 · Day 14

Reversible Reactions, Equilibrium & Industrial Tradeoffs

Model dynamic equilibrium and evaluate changes in conditions.

45–60 minutesReal evidenceModel limits

Learning objectives

  • Define dynamic equilibrium in a closed system
  • Predict qualitative effects of condition changes when information is supplied
  • Explain why catalysts change rate but not equilibrium position

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table and supplied reference data
  • Calculator where required
Chemical safety boundary

Wear properly fitting splash goggles for any approved hands-on activity. Never mix household cleaners, heat sealed containers, taste substances, burn materials, handle strong acids or bases, or use unknown chemicals. The effervescent-tablet option requires adult approval, water only, open containers, and careful handling of warm—not boiling—water.

Evidence standard

Separate measured properties, observed changes, calculated atom counts, published thermochemical values, and explanatory models. A simulation result is not independent experimental evidence.

Dynamic equilibrium

At equilibrium, forward and reverse rates are equal while reactant and product concentrations remain constant—not necessarily equal.

Changing conditions

Temperature, pressure, and concentration can shift equilibrium position according to the system response.

Industry

Haber-process conditions balance rate, yield, energy, equipment limits, safety, and economics.

Portfolio evidence

Evaluate one Haber-process condition using rate, equilibrium, safety, and resource evidence.

Model boundary

Dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick, lattice, particle-collision, and energy-pathway diagrams are useful representations. They distort scale, motion, electron density, and complexity.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can reversible reactions reach dynamic equilibrium, and why do industrial choices involve tradeoffs?

In a closed system, a reversible reaction can reach dynamic equilibrium when forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. Concentrations then remain constant on average, though reactions continue microscopically. Changing temperature, pressure, or concentration can shift equilibrium position. Catalysts speed both directions and shorten time to equilibrium without changing equilibrium position.

Worked reasoning example

For a gas reaction with fewer gas particles on the product side, increasing pressure can favor the side with fewer gas particles in a qualitative Le Chatelier model. In an industrial process, higher pressure may improve equilibrium yield but increase equipment cost, energy demand, and safety requirements.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: reversible reaction · dynamic equilibrium · equilibrium position · closed system · Le Chatelier principle · industrial tradeoff

Misconception check: At equilibrium, reactions have not stopped; forward and reverse rates are equal.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check formulas, charge balance, atom counts, units, energy direction, evidence quality, and the limits of each model.

  1. Explain dynamic equilibrium microscopically.
  2. Predict one qualitative effect of concentration or pressure change.
  3. Explain why a catalyst does not shift equilibrium position.
  4. Identify one rate–yield–cost tradeoff in an industrial process.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your structure model, formula, balanced equation, rate calculation, or equilibrium explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which change affects equilibrium position and which affects only rate?
  • Is your system closed enough for equilibrium reasoning?
  • What engineering cost accompanies the condition that improves yield?
