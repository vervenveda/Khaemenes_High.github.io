Unit 07 · Day 3

Ionic Formulae & Charge Balance

Derive neutral empirical formulas from ion charges.

45–60 minutesReal evidenceModel limits

Learning objectives

  • Use total positive and negative charge to construct formulas
  • Reduce subscripts to the lowest whole-number ratio
  • Use parentheses for repeated polyatomic ions

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table and supplied reference data
  • Calculator where required
Chemical safety boundary

Wear properly fitting splash goggles for any approved hands-on activity. Never mix household cleaners, heat sealed containers, taste substances, burn materials, handle strong acids or bases, or use unknown chemicals. The effervescent-tablet option requires adult approval, water only, open containers, and careful handling of warm—not boiling—water.

Evidence standard

Separate measured properties, observed changes, calculated atom counts, published thermochemical values, and explanatory models. A simulation result is not independent experimental evidence.

Neutrality

The total charge represented by an ionic formula must equal zero.

Smallest ratio

Formula subscripts show the lowest whole-number ratio of ions, not a molecular count.

Polyatomic ions

Parentheses are required when more than one of the same polyatomic ion appears, as in Ca(NO₃)₂.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Ionic Formula Laboratory and explain five formulas using charge totals.

Model boundary

Dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick, lattice, particle-collision, and energy-pathway diagrams are useful representations. They distort scale, motion, electron density, and complexity.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can ionic formulas be derived from charge balance rather than memorized?

An ionic formula represents the simplest whole-number ratio of ions giving net charge zero. Charges must be balanced algebraically. Subscripts describe ratios and must not carry charge signs. Polyatomic ions should be treated as intact charged groups when they remain unchanged in the compound.

Worked reasoning example

For Al³⁺ and O²⁻, the least common total charge magnitude is 6: two Al³⁺ ions give +6 and three O²⁻ ions give -6, producing Al₂O₃. For Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻, two nitrate ions are needed: Ca(NO₃)₂.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: charge balance · subscript · formula unit · polyatomic ion · neutral compound · ratio

Misconception check: The criss-cross method is not a scientific rule; it is a shortcut that only works when charges are interpreted and simplified correctly.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check formulas, charge balance, atom counts, units, energy direction, evidence quality, and the limits of each model.

  1. Derive formulas for Na⁺/O²⁻, Mg²⁺/Cl⁻, Al³⁺/O²⁻, and Ca²⁺/NO₃⁻.
  2. Explain why subscripts are simplified to lowest whole-number ratio.
  3. State why charges disappear from the final neutral formula.
  4. Check one formula by summing total positive and negative charge.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your structure model, formula, balanced equation, rate calculation, or equilibrium explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Can you justify the ratio without using a memorized shortcut?
  • Did you preserve a polyatomic ion correctly?
  • What charge check proves your formula is neutral?
