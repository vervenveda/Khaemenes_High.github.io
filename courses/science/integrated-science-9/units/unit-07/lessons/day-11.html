Unit 07 · Day 11

Exothermic & Endothermic Reactions

Interpret measured temperature changes and energy pathways.

45–60 minutesReal evidenceModel limits

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish system from surroundings
  • Interpret signs of ΔH
  • Read exothermic and endothermic pathway diagrams

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table and supplied reference data
  • Calculator where required
Chemical safety boundary

Wear properly fitting splash goggles for any approved hands-on activity. Never mix household cleaners, heat sealed containers, taste substances, burn materials, handle strong acids or bases, or use unknown chemicals. The effervescent-tablet option requires adult approval, water only, open containers, and careful handling of warm—not boiling—water.

Evidence standard

Separate measured properties, observed changes, calculated atom counts, published thermochemical values, and explanatory models. A simulation result is not independent experimental evidence.

Energy transfer

Exothermic reactions transfer thermal energy to surroundings; endothermic reactions take in thermal energy from surroundings.

Enthalpy sign

At constant pressure, negative ΔH denotes exothermic change and positive ΔH denotes endothermic change.

Measurement limits

Temperature change depends on heat capacity, insulation, amounts, mixing, and heat loss—not only ΔH.

Portfolio evidence

Use the published reaction-energy table and draw two labeled pathway diagrams.

Model boundary

Dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick, lattice, particle-collision, and energy-pathway diagrams are useful representations. They distort scale, motion, electron density, and complexity.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How should exothermic and endothermic reactions be described using system and surroundings?

In an exothermic process, the reacting system transfers energy to the surroundings; in an endothermic process, the system absorbs energy from the surroundings. Measured temperature changes depend on the experimental boundary and heat capacity, so temperature change is evidence about energy transfer rather than identical to the reaction's total energy change.

Worked reasoning example

If a reaction mixture warms its surroundings from 20°C to 28°C in an insulated cup, the observation supports energy transfer from system to surroundings, consistent with exothermic behavior. If the mixture cools, endothermic behavior may be supported, but heat loss/gain from the environment must still be considered.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: exothermic · endothermic · system · surroundings · enthalpy change · temperature change

Misconception check: Exothermic does not mean a reaction must feel hot in every setup; observed temperature depends on amounts, heat capacities, and heat exchange.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check formulas, charge balance, atom counts, units, energy direction, evidence quality, and the limits of each model.

  1. Classify energy direction for exothermic and endothermic processes.
  2. Explain why measured temperature change is not exactly the same as ΔH.
  3. Identify the system and surroundings in one experiment.
  4. Describe one source of heat-transfer error.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your structure model, formula, balanced equation, rate calculation, or equilibrium explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • What evidence supports your energy-direction claim?
  • Where might heat exchange with the room alter the observation?
  • Have you defined the system boundary clearly?
