Unit 07 · Day 12

Bond Energy, Activation Energy & Catalysts

Explain energy changes and catalytic pathways without implying that catalysts change ΔH.

45–60 minutesReal evidenceModel limits

Learning objectives

  • Explain bond breaking and bond making energetically
  • Define activation energy
  • Compare catalyzed and uncatalyzed pathways

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table and supplied reference data
  • Calculator where required
Chemical safety boundary

Wear properly fitting splash goggles for any approved hands-on activity. Never mix household cleaners, heat sealed containers, taste substances, burn materials, handle strong acids or bases, or use unknown chemicals. The effervescent-tablet option requires adult approval, water only, open containers, and careful handling of warm—not boiling—water.

Evidence standard

Separate measured properties, observed changes, calculated atom counts, published thermochemical values, and explanatory models. A simulation result is not independent experimental evidence.

Bond energy model

Breaking bonds requires energy; making bonds releases energy. Overall reaction energy depends on the balance.

Activation barrier

Reacting particles require sufficient energy and suitable orientation to follow a successful pathway.

Catalysts

A catalyst provides a lower-activation-energy pathway and is regenerated; it does not change the energy difference between reactants and products.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Energy and Rate Laboratory and annotate one catalyst diagram.

Model boundary

Dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick, lattice, particle-collision, and energy-pathway diagrams are useful representations. They distort scale, motion, electron density, and complexity.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do bond energies, activation energy, and catalysts describe different aspects of a reaction pathway?

Breaking bonds requires energy; forming bonds releases energy. Overall reaction energy depends on the balance between these processes. Activation energy is the barrier that must be overcome for a pathway to proceed at an appreciable rate. A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy but does not change the overall energy difference between reactants and products.

Worked reasoning example

If breaking required bonds needs 500 kJ per modeled amount and forming new bonds releases 620 kJ, the net model change is 500−620 = -120 kJ, exothermic. A catalyst can lower the highest barrier along the pathway, increasing rate, while the -120 kJ overall difference remains unchanged.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: bond energy · activation energy · catalyst · reaction pathway · transition state · energy profile

Misconception check: Catalysts do not make an endothermic reaction exothermic or change equilibrium ΔH.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check formulas, charge balance, atom counts, units, energy direction, evidence quality, and the limits of each model.

  1. Use supplied bond-energy totals to estimate reaction energy.
  2. Distinguish activation energy from overall energy change.
  3. Explain how a catalyst changes rate.
  4. Sketch or describe catalyzed and uncatalyzed energy profiles.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your structure model, formula, balanced equation, rate calculation, or equilibrium explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which part of your diagram is the barrier and which is the overall energy difference?
  • What does the catalyst change?
  • Which simplification limits average bond-energy calculations?
