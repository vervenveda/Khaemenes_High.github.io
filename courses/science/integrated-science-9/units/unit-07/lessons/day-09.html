Unit 07 · Day 9

Balancing Equations & Conservation of Atoms

Use mathematical representations to show that atoms are conserved.

45–60 minutesReal evidenceModel limits

Learning objectives

  • Balance equations with whole-number coefficients
  • Verify atom counts on both sides
  • Explain why coefficients may change but subscripts may not

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table and supplied reference data
  • Calculator where required
Chemical safety boundary

Wear properly fitting splash goggles for any approved hands-on activity. Never mix household cleaners, heat sealed containers, taste substances, burn materials, handle strong acids or bases, or use unknown chemicals. The effervescent-tablet option requires adult approval, water only, open containers, and careful handling of warm—not boiling—water.

Evidence standard

Separate measured properties, observed changes, calculated atom counts, published thermochemical values, and explanatory models. A simulation result is not independent experimental evidence.

Conservation

Chemical reactions rearrange atoms; ordinary chemical reactions do not create or destroy atomic nuclei.

Balancing method

Count atoms, adjust coefficients, reduce to the smallest whole-number ratio, and verify.

Closed systems

Measured mass is conserved in a closed system. Apparent mass loss can occur when gas escapes an open system.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Equation and Conservation Laboratory with an atom-count audit.

Model boundary

Dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick, lattice, particle-collision, and energy-pathway diagrams are useful representations. They distort scale, motion, electron density, and complexity.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How does balancing a chemical equation demonstrate conservation of atoms?

Chemical reactions rearrange atoms. In a closed system, atoms of each element are conserved. Balancing adjusts coefficients so the number of each atom type matches on both sides. An atom audit is stronger evidence than visual guessing because it makes conservation explicit.

Worked reasoning example

For Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃, balance iron and oxygen systematically: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. Atom audit: Fe left 4, right 4; O left 6, right 6. Coefficients are the smallest whole-number ratio.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: conservation of atoms · balanced equation · coefficient · atom audit · closed system · stoichiometric ratio

Misconception check: Balanced equations conserve atoms, not necessarily the number of molecules or formula units.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check formulas, charge balance, atom counts, units, energy direction, evidence quality, and the limits of each model.

  1. Balance three simple equations and perform an atom audit.
  2. Explain why coefficients can change but subscripts cannot.
  3. Describe how conservation of mass follows from atom conservation in a closed system.
  4. Identify one equation that is balanced but chemically impossible because a formula is wrong.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your structure model, formula, balanced equation, rate calculation, or equilibrium explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which element was most efficient to balance first?
  • How does your atom audit prove conservation?
  • Could the equation be mathematically balanced yet chemically miswritten?
