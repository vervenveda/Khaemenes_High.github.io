Unit 09 · Day 7

Magnetic Fields, Electromagnets & Motors

Relate current, magnetic fields, and forces in devices.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Describe magnetic fields around magnets and current-carrying conductors
  • Explain factors affecting an electromagnet
  • Describe the motor effect qualitatively

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Fields

Magnetic field diagrams represent direction and relative strength; field lines are not physical threads.

Electromagnets

Current, coil turns, core material, and geometry affect field strength.

Motor effect

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field can experience a force; direction depends on current and field.

Portfolio evidence

Analyze an electromagnet design using published or teacher-supplied data.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How does electric current create magnetic effects that can produce forces and motion?

Electric current produces a magnetic field around a conductor. Coils concentrate and shape this field, forming electromagnets. A current-carrying conductor in an external magnetic field can experience a force, which is the basis of motor action. Device behavior depends on field direction, current direction, geometry, and current magnitude.

Worked reasoning example

If current in a coil is increased while geometry and core remain unchanged, the magnetic effect generally becomes stronger in the simple model. Reversing current reverses the coil's magnetic polarity. In a motor, reversing either current or external field direction reverses the force direction; reversing both can leave the force direction unchanged.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: magnetic field · electromagnet · coil · motor effect · current direction · field direction

Misconception check: Magnetism in a circuit is not caused by charge simply 'running out' of the wire; it is associated with moving charge/current and field interactions.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, units, diagrams, model limits, and any claim that is broader than the evidence supports.

  1. Describe the field around a current-carrying conductor qualitatively.
  2. Predict one change that can strengthen an electromagnet.
  3. Explain why reversing current can reverse motor-force direction.
  4. Distinguish a magnetic field from a magnetic force on a chosen object.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your circuit model, graph, wave calculation, or communication-system explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which interaction actually produces the force in your motor model?
  • What variable would you change to test electromagnet strength?
  • Where does a simple field-line diagram hide three-dimensional geometry?
