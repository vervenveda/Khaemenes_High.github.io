Electricity, Waves & Information Synthesis

Integrate circuit evidence, electromagnetic devices, wave behavior, and communication engineering.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Complete all four laboratories
  • Pass the mastery quiz and assessment
  • Submit the communication engineering case
  • Evaluate models, evidence, risks, and access

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Synthesis

A complete explanation identifies the system, energy source, signal carrier, encoding method, receiver, losses, noise, and safety controls.

Assessment sequence

Pass four laboratory checks at 7/8, submit the case, pass the quiz at 16/20, pass the assessment at 24/30, and complete reflection.

Safety boundary

No mains wiring, battery shorting, laser exposure, high-volume sound, strong-magnet experiments near medical devices, or improvised transmitters are required.

Portfolio evidence

Submit the engineering case, corrections, constructed responses, and final reflection.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can one systems model connect electrical energy transfer, waves, encoded information, and engineering tradeoffs?

A communication or electrical system can be traced from source through transformation, transmission, reception, and output. Electrical circuits move charge and transfer energy; electromagnetic or mechanical waves can carry energy and encoded information; devices use fields, sensors, transmitters, channels, receivers, and decoders. Strong explanations distinguish energy flow from information content and evaluate losses, noise, uncertainty, safety, privacy, and accessibility.

Worked reasoning example

Consider a simple radio communication chain: electrical energy powers an encoder/transmitter; information modulates a carrier; electromagnetic waves carry energy through space; a receiver detects the signal; electronics decode it; a speaker transforms electrical energy into sound. Noise can corrupt the encoded signal even though energy still reaches the receiver.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: system · encoder · transmitter · channel · receiver · decoder

Misconception check: Information is not a conserved energy substance flowing unchanged through the system. The same information can be represented by different physical signals and energy transfers.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, units, diagrams, model limits, and any claim that is broader than the evidence supports.

  1. Draw a source → encoder → transmitter → channel → receiver → decoder → output model.
  2. Trace energy transfer separately from information representation.
  3. Solve one circuit calculation and one wave calculation from the unit.
  4. Identify one safety, privacy, noise, or accessibility tradeoff.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your circuit model, graph, wave calculation, or communication-system explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Where in your system is information transformed into a different physical representation?
  • Which loss affects energy and which affects information quality?
  • Which unit objective still needs a fresh calculation or evidence explanation before mastery assessment?
