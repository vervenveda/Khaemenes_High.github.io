Unit 09 · Day 6

Electrical Power, Energy Use & Efficiency

Calculate the rate and total amount of electrical energy transfer.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Use P = VI, P = I²R, and P = V²/R within valid conditions
  • Use E = Pt
  • Evaluate efficiency and energy-cost claims

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Power

Electrical power is the rate at which energy is transferred.

Energy

Electrical energy may be calculated from power multiplied by time.

Claims

Efficiency, cost, and environmental impact depend on system boundaries, operating time, energy source, and measurement conditions.

Portfolio evidence

Calculate appliance energy use from supplied safe data without connecting to mains equipment.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do electrical power and energy separate rate from total transfer?

Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer. In a circuit, P = VI. Total electrical energy transferred over time is E = Pt. A high-power device transfers energy quickly; it does not necessarily use more energy overall unless it operates long enough. Efficiency compares useful output with total input for a stated purpose.

Worked reasoning example

A device operates at 12 V and 0.50 A. Power = VI = 6.0 W. If it runs for 10 min = 600 s, energy transferred = Pt = 6.0 × 600 = 3600 J. A 3 W device operating for twice as long can transfer the same total energy.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: electrical power · watt · energy · joule · efficiency · rate

Misconception check: Power and energy are not interchangeable. Power tells how fast energy is transferred.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, units, diagrams, model limits, and any claim that is broader than the evidence supports.

  1. Calculate power from V and I.
  2. Calculate energy from power and time.
  3. Compare two devices that transfer the same energy at different powers.
  4. Explain why higher power does not automatically mean higher efficiency.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your circuit model, graph, wave calculation, or communication-system explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which quantity answers 'how fast' and which answers 'how much'?
  • Did you convert minutes to seconds before using joules and watts?
  • What losses or non-target outputs should be included in an efficiency claim?
