Unit 09 · Day 14

Analog, Digital Signals, Noise & Information Transfer

Model how information is encoded, transmitted, corrupted, checked, and recovered.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Compare analog and digital representations
  • Encode information using binary patterns
  • Evaluate sampling, noise, error detection, storage, security, and accessibility

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Analog and digital

Analog signals vary continuously; digital systems represent information with discrete symbols.

Reliability

Thresholding, repeaters, error checks, and redundant encoding can improve recovery from noise.

Tradeoffs

Digital systems support copying and error detection but introduce sampling limits, data size, energy use, security, privacy, and access concerns.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Information Signal Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse guided circuit tables, diagrams, and one-step equations.
CoreComplete series/parallel, wave, signal, and energy calculations.
ExtendedAnalyze transformer ratios, non-ohmic curves, sampling limits, and model tradeoffs.
