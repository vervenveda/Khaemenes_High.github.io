Unit 09 · Day 1

Charge, Current, Potential Difference & Electrical Energy

Define electrical quantities through charge transfer and energy per charge.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish charge, current, potential difference, and electrical energy
  • Use Q = It and V = E/Q
  • State conventional-current direction and model limits

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Charge and current

Electric charge is measured in coulombs. Current is the rate at which charge passes a point.

Potential difference

Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge between two points.

Models

Conventional current points from positive to negative outside a source; electron drift in metals is opposite. Neither arrow depicts individual electron speed.

Portfolio evidence

Create a quantity–symbol–unit table and solve four charge/current/energy records.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do charge flow and energy-per-charge define current and potential difference?

Electric current is the rate of charge flow, I = Q/t. Potential difference describes energy transferred per unit charge, V = E/Q. Current is not 'used up' by a component; charge is redistributed through the circuit while energy is transferred from the source to components and surroundings. A complete explanation separates charge flow from energy transfer.

Worked reasoning example

If a current of 0.50 A flows for 20 s, charge transferred is Q = It = 0.50 × 20 = 10 C. If 24 J of energy is transferred when 6 C of charge passes through a component, potential difference = E/Q = 24/6 = 4.0 V.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: charge · current · ampere · potential difference · volt · electrical energy

Misconception check: Current is not the same as electrical energy, and components do not consume charge in ordinary steady circuits.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, units, diagrams, model limits, and any claim that is broader than the evidence supports.

  1. Calculate charge from current and time.
  2. Calculate potential difference from energy and charge.
  3. Explain the difference between charge flow and energy transfer.
  4. State the unit for current, charge, energy, and potential difference.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your circuit model, graph, wave calculation, or communication-system explanation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which quantity in your explanation measures flow rate and which measures energy per charge?
  • Where does energy go after leaving the source?
  • How would you detect a calculation that mixes coulombs and joules incorrectly?
