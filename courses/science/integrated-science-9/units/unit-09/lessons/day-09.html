Wave Quantities & the Wave Equation

Relate wave speed, frequency, wavelength, period, and amplitude.

45–60 minutesEvidence & modelsLow-voltage safety

Learning objectives

  • Use v = fλ
  • Use T = 1/f
  • Distinguish amplitude, frequency, wavelength, speed, and period

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and supplied reference tables
  • Optional approved low-voltage circuit kit with adult supervision
Electrical, sound, light, and magnet safety

Use only approved battery-powered circuits of 6 V or less with current limiting. Never access wall outlets, mains wiring, chargers, power supplies, internal appliance parts, or damaged batteries. Do not short batteries, heat wires, use laser pointers, create loud headphone or speaker exposure, place strong magnets near medical implants, or build improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Separate measured values, published records, calculations, circuit and wave models, digital simulations, and engineering inferences. State units, conditions, uncertainty, system boundary, and signal assumptions.

Wave equation

Wave speed equals frequency multiplied by wavelength for a specified medium and wave type.

Frequency and period

Frequency counts cycles per second; period is time per cycle.

Amplitude

Amplitude measures maximum displacement or field magnitude in the model and often relates to transferred energy, not wave speed.

Complete the Wave Equation Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Circuit diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, sinusoidal waves, photons, binary channels, and ideal transformers are purposeful simplifications. They do not show literal scale, all microscopic interactions, every loss mechanism, or complete quantum behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How are wave speed, frequency, wavelength, period, and amplitude related but not interchangeable?

Wave speed is related to frequency and wavelength by v = fλ. Period is the time for one cycle, T = 1/f. Frequency describes cycles per second; wavelength describes spatial separation between corresponding points; amplitude describes the size of an oscillation and often relates to energy or intensity depending on the wave system. Changing one variable does not necessarily change all others independently.

Worked reasoning example

A sound wave travels at 340 m/s with frequency 170 Hz. λ = v/f = 340/170 = 2.0 m. Period T = 1/170 ≈ 0.0059 s. If frequency doubles in the same medium while speed remains approximately constant, wavelength halves.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: frequency · wavelength · period · amplitude · wave speed · hertz

Misconception check: Amplitude is not the same quantity as frequency, and higher frequency does not automatically mean faster wave speed in the same medium.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, units, diagrams, model limits, and any claim that is broader than the evidence supports.

  1. Calculate wavelength from v and f.
  2. Calculate period from frequency.
  3. Predict wavelength change when frequency changes but speed stays constant.
  4. Distinguish amplitude from wavelength.
