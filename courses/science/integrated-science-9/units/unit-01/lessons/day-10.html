Unit 01 · Day 10

Design Challenge & Mastery

Demonstrate the complete inquiry cycle independently: design before data, defend measurement and comparison choices, communicate evidence honestly, complete mastery assessments, correct misconceptions, and leave Unit 01 with a documented plan for stronger scientific reasoning.

75–100 minutes + retakes as neededTier 1 · Home-safe designIndependent Transfer80% Mastery GateMentor ConferencePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Transfer Unit 01 inquiry principles to a new investigation without copying the helicopter design.
  • Define variables, measurements, controls, repetitions, safety boundaries, data structure, analysis, and limitations before collecting evidence.
  • Defend graph, source, and CER choices using the reasoning developed across Days 01–09.
  • Complete objective and constructed-response mastery evidence independently.
  • Use error analysis and corrections to repair misconceptions rather than merely repeat answers.
  • Reflect on growth in question design, measurement, data integrity, uncertainty, causal reasoning, and scientific communication.

Materials

  • Investigation Design Task
  • Science notebook and Unit 01 portfolio
  • Khaemenes Scientific Calculator when calculation verification is permitted
  • Dataset Lab, Evidence Citation Studio, and PROSE only where the task explicitly permits them
  • Mastery Quiz and Unit Assessment
  • Student Record / Reflection
Tier 1 · design-first boundary

The independent task must remain within the written low-risk conditions. Do not taste experimental materials, use unknown substances, heat materials, climb, use mains electricity, involve animals/people as test subjects, or improvise procedures outside the stated task. If the environment, equipment, supervision, allergies, accessibility needs, or learner conditions differ materially from the written activity, stop and redesign rather than improvising.

1. Unit 01 inquiry map

Before beginning the transfer task, reconstruct the scientific workflow from memory:

  1. Phenomenon → investigable question
  2. Variables → operational definitions
  3. Fair comparison → controls, sampling/repetition, confounders
  4. Measurement → instrument fitness, resolution, variation, bias
  5. Preregistered design → protocol, table, prediction, safety
  6. Data collection → raw-data integrity and deviations
  7. Analysis → summaries, anomalies, graphing
  8. Inference → uncertainty, association, causation, alternatives
  9. Communication → CER, sources, revision
Retrieval check

Without reopening earlier lessons, identify the stage where your own reasoning is currently strongest and the stage most likely to introduce error. Explain why.

2. Independent investigation design challenge

Design a fair test of how paper type affects the mass of water absorbed by a fixed-size sheet. The scientific product today is the design and defense. Do not collect data unless the linked task explicitly instructs you to do so and the safety conditions are satisfied.

Your design must include:

  • a precise investigable question;
  • an operational definition of “water absorbed” using measurable quantities;
  • independent/dependent variables and controlled factors;
  • paper-condition selection and repeated-trial plan;
  • a measurement method, instrument resolution, and likely uncertainty/bias;
  • a reproducible procedure and raw-data table created before results;
  • safety/stop conditions and spill management;
  • a graph choice with axes and units;
  • a prediction stated separately from evidence;
  • at least two plausible limitations or confounders;
  • a plan for what would count as evidence supporting, weakening, or failing to distinguish the prediction.

5. Mastery Gate A — 15-question quiz

  1. Complete the mastery quiz under its stated assessment conditions.
  2. Submit before opening explanations or correction resources.
  3. Score 12/15 or higher (80%).
  4. If below 80%, do not simply memorize answer positions. Identify the concept behind each miss, return to the relevant lesson/evidence, write a correction explanation, then attempt a fresh retake when available.
Open Mastery Quiz
Correction record

For each missed concept, record: my original reasoning → why it failed → corrected principle → one new example.

6. Mastery Gate B — objective unit assessment

After the quiz gate and required corrections, complete the 24-question objective assessment under its stated conditions.

  • Mastery threshold: 20/24 or higher (at least 80%; 19/24 is below the course gate).
  • Assessment completion alone is not mastery.
  • A below-gate score triggers targeted review and reassessment rather than progression by participation.
Open Unit Assessment

7. Constructed-response evidence

Complete both constructed responses using the published rubric. These responses should demonstrate reasoning that multiple-choice items cannot fully capture: experimental design, evidence evaluation, uncertainty, and scientific communication.

Self-audit before submission

Highlight your claim/question, evidence/design choices, reasoning, uncertainty/limitation, and safety or source-integrity consideration where applicable. If one component is missing, revise before submission.

8. Mentor conference — after independent evidence

Use the Science Mentor after completing the required independent mastery evidence, or at the point explicitly permitted by the assessment instructions. Bring your correction record and design task—not hidden answer keys.

Be prepared to discuss:

  • Which misconception survived longest during Unit 01?
  • What evidence changed your thinking?
  • Which design decision in the paper-absorbency task is hardest to defend?
  • Where did you appropriately limit a claim?
  • What would you change before running an independent replication?

Mentor support is for explanation, correction, and reflection. It does not convert assisted work into independent assessment evidence.

10. Final scientific reflection

Complete the Student Record and respond with specific evidence from your work:

  1. How did your approach to scientific questions change?
  2. What do you now check before trusting a measurement?
  3. How do you decide whether an unusual data point should remain?
  4. What graphing choice are you now more cautious about?
  5. How has your causal language changed?
  6. How do you distinguish a strong source from a persuasive-looking source?
  7. What is one scientific habit you will carry into Unit 02?
