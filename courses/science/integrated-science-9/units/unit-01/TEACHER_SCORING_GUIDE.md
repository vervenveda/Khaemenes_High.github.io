# Unit 01 Teacher / Parent-Educator Scoring Guide

This guide supports the human-reviewed components of **Integrated Science 9 · Unit 01: Scientific Inquiry, Measurement & Evidence**.

## Mastery thresholds

- Objective Mastery Quiz: **12/15 or higher**
- Objective Unit Assessment: **20/24 or higher**
- Independent Investigation Design Task: **32/40 or higher**
- Constructed Response 1: **16/20 or higher**
- Constructed Response 2: **16/20 or higher**

Passing an objective quiz or marking pages complete does not replace the required performance evidence.

---

## A. Independent Investigation Design Task — 40 points

Score the student's **first independent submission** before Mentor/PROSE-assisted revision.

### 1. Question, prediction, and variables — 8 points
Full credit requires a focused investigable question; measurable independent/dependent variables; planned levels or comparison conditions; a preregistered prediction separate from future evidence; and controls that logically address alternative explanations.

### 2. Operationalization and measurement — 8 points
Full credit requires the correct absorbency definition **water absorbed (g) = wet mass (g) − dry mass (g)**; suitable units; appropriate instruments; stated resolution; repeatable measurement rules; and recognition of measurement uncertainty or bias.

### 3. Fair and reproducible method — 8 points
Full credit requires equal-size sheets, consistent water exposure/contact time, consistent drainage/blotting rule, consistent weighing method, at least five trials per paper type, condition-order consideration, and a procedure another learner could follow without hidden verbal instructions.

### 4. Data and analysis plan — 8 points
Full credit requires a raw-data table designed before results; paper type, trial, dry mass, wet mass, calculated absorbed mass, and notes/deviations; an appropriate graph; axes/units; variation analysis; and an anomaly/exclusion rule that does not permit result-based deletion.

### 5. Safety, limitations, and improvements — 8 points
Full credit requires spill/electronics protections, stop conditions, low-risk materials, at least two scientifically meaningful limitations/confounders, and improvements matched to those limitations rather than generic “be more careful” statements.

**Mastery: 32/40 or higher.**

## B. Constructed Response 1 — Investigation Design — 20 points

Prompt: design an investigation of how drop height affects the mean bounce height of the same ball on the same surface.

### 1. Scientific task completion — 4 points
Testable question, directional hypothesis, correct independent/dependent variables, and operational definitions.

### 2. Fair comparison and specificity — 4 points
At least five relevant controls, five trials per height, consistent release and measurement method, and a reproducible comparison plan.

### 3. Measurement/data reasoning — 4 points
Raw-data headings, units, graph choice, repeated-trial summaries, and recognition of measurement variation.

### 4. Safety and limitations — 4 points
Safe setup plus two concrete limitations and, where appropriate, corresponding improvements.

### 5. Communication/integrity — 4 points
Clear organization, no invented results, prediction kept separate from evidence, and language appropriately limited to the proposed design.

**Mastery: 16/20 or higher.**

## C. Constructed Response 2 — Dataset Reasoning — 20 points

Dataset: mean pendulum periods of 0.90 s at 20 cm, 1.10 s at 30 cm, 1.28 s at 40 cm, 1.43 s at 50 cm, and 1.55 s at 60 cm.

### 1. Claim — 4 points
States that period increases as pendulum length increases **within the tested range**, without universalizing the exact values.

### 2. Evidence — 4 points
Uses multiple supplied numerical values accurately rather than one cherry-picked pair.

### 3. Reasoning — 4 points
Connects the observed pattern to a scientifically appropriate relationship between pendulum length and period while keeping the reasoning proportional to what Unit 01 has established.

### 4. Limitation and improvement — 4 points
Identifies a specific limitation such as timing resolution/reaction time, release consistency, limited number of tested lengths, or lack of repeated-trial variation data, and proposes a matched improvement.

### 5. Scientific communication — 4 points
Separates observation from explanation, uses units where appropriate, avoids “proves,” and does not claim every possible pendulum must follow the exact dataset.

**Mastery: 16/20 or higher.**

## D. Verification rules

The reviewer should verify that:

1. the paper-helicopter raw observations exist and calculated summaries trace back to them;
2. anomaly/exclusion decisions have documented reasons;
3. the Dataset Laboratory contains the student's own graph and causal-reasoning work;
4. the first Design Task submission was independently produced;
5. objective corrections address concepts rather than merely answer positions;
6. both constructed responses are actually scored with the published criteria;
7. Mentor/ProTool-assisted revisions remain distinguishable from independent mastery evidence;
8. the final reflection is substantive and tied to actual Unit 01 work.

Do not record Unit 01 mastery verification merely because every page has been visited.

## E. Public static-course answer-key boundary

The objective quiz and assessment are client-side JavaScript applications hosted in a public static repository. The interface withholds correct answers until submission, but the browser must receive scoring logic in order to grade locally. Therefore these objective keys are **not cryptographically secret** from a learner who deliberately inspects source code.

For ordinary formative/mastery use, independent-attempt instructions plus post-submission correction cycles provide reasonable classroom integrity. For a proctored/high-stakes examination requiring truly secret keys, use a protected teacher-controlled or server-side assessment environment. Do not represent client-side answer hiding as secure key storage.
