Unit 05 · Comparative evidence assessment

Origins Evidence Hearing

Present a reasoned position while distinguishing empirical evidence, scientific reconstruction, uncertainty, and worldview.

No compelled belief

You may defend an evolutionary common-descent, young-earth creation, old-earth creation, intelligent-design, evolutionary-creation, another responsibly sourced position, or an undecided conclusion. Scoring is based on evidence accuracy, classification, reasoning, source fairness, limitations, and respectful communication.

Hearing question

What do ecological change, observed selection, speciation evidence, fossils, equid anatomy, genetics, and human-origins records establish directly; what relationships are scientifically inferred; and how do competing origins worldviews interpret the evidence?

Required structure

  1. Definitions and scope
  2. Direct ecological and biological observations
  3. Fossil and genetic evidence
  4. Historical reconstruction and its assumptions
  5. Creation, intelligent-design, evolutionary-creation, and naturalistic interpretations
  6. Strongest evidence and strongest limitation for your position
  7. Fair response to a competing position
  8. Stewardship conclusion

Submission criteria

Source Dossier
Evaluation standard

Fair comparison does not require pretending every claim is equally testable or equally supported. It requires accurate evidence classification, honest representation, and conclusions proportional to the evidence.
