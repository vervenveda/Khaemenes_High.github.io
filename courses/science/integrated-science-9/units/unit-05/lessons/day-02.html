Unit 05 · Day 2

Food Chains, Food Webs & Trophic Roles

Trace feeding relationships while recognizing that real ecosystems form networks rather than simple ladders.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Identify producers, consumers, decomposers, and detritivores
  • Read arrows as the direction of energy and matter transfer
  • Construct a food web from documented feeding records
  • Predict direct and indirect consequences cautiously

Materials

  • Notebook
  • Food-web evidence table
  • Source dossier
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Trophic roles

Producers capture energy and build organic matter. Consumers obtain organic matter by feeding. Decomposers chemically break down dead material and wastes, while detritivores ingest fragments of detritus.

Arrow convention

In a food web, an arrow normally points from the organism or material being consumed toward the consumer. The arrow represents transfer of matter and chemical energy, not friendship or movement.

Networks and indirect effects

A predator may influence prey, competitors, vegetation, and nutrient movement. Removing one species can have several effects, but the outcome depends on alternative foods, migration, disease, weather, and density.

Evidence quality

A documented stomach-content study, camera record, stable-isotope analysis, or repeated feeding observation can support a link. A plausible link is not automatically a documented link.

Evidence standard

Build the web only from cited feeding records or from a clearly labeled instructional relationship table. Distinguish documented links from hypothesized links.

Portfolio evidence

Construct a minimum eight-node food web, identify three trophic roles, and write one direct-effect and one indirect-effect prediction with stated uncertainty.

Boundary and limitation

Trophic level is sometimes approximate because omnivores feed at more than one level and diets change through life stages and seasons.

Deep learning: networks, not ladders

Essential question: How can a food web represent energy transfer without implying that ecosystems are simple linear chains?

In a food-web diagram, arrows conventionally point from the resource or food toward the organism receiving energy and matter. Producers capture energy into biomass; consumers obtain biomass from other organisms; decomposers process dead organic matter and wastes. Many organisms occupy more than one trophic role across life stages or food sources.

Because ecosystems are networks, changing one population can create indirect effects. A predator can influence plants by changing herbivore abundance or behavior, and an omnivore can connect multiple trophic pathways.

Worked food-web reasoning

Suppose grass is eaten by rabbits and grasshoppers; grasshoppers are eaten by frogs; rabbits and frogs are eaten by hawks. Correct arrows are grass → rabbit, grass → grasshopper, grasshopper → frog, rabbit → hawk, and frog → hawk. Removing frogs may increase grasshoppers, which can increase pressure on grass, but the actual outcome depends on other food sources and population responses.

Misconception and retrieval check

  • Arrows do not point toward what is eaten; they point toward the receiver of transferred energy/matter.
  • “Top predator” does not mean an organism has no ecological constraints.
  • Food webs describe feeding relationships, not every interaction in an ecosystem.
  1. What does an arrow from algae to zooplankton mean?
  2. Why can an omnivore occupy more than one trophic level?
  3. Give one possible indirect effect of removing a predator.
