Unit 05 · Day 13

Fossils, Equids & Historical Reconstruction

Examine real horse-family evidence while distinguishing specimens, classifications, phylogenetic hypotheses, and worldview interpretations.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Identify directly measured equid fossil features
  • Explain why modern equid history is modeled as branching rather than a ladder
  • Distinguish fossil species assignment from witnessed ancestry
  • Compare common-descent and created-kind interpretations

Materials

  • Florida Museum Fossil Horses sources
  • Evidence classification lab
  • Notebook
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Physical evidence

Museums hold equid teeth, jaws, skulls, limb bones, feet, and skeletons. Researchers measure toe structure, tooth crown height, enamel pattern, body size, limb proportions, geological location, and, for some later material, ancient DNA.

Branching, not a march

The older straight-line diagram from a small multi-toed form to the modern horse is misleading. Equid history is reconstructed as branching diversification with contemporaneous species and many extinct branches.

What fossils cannot label

A fossil does not carry a tag naming its parents or descendants. Researchers infer species and relationships from anatomy, stratigraphy, statistics, and sometimes molecular evidence. Classifications can be revised.

Interpretations

The standard evolutionary interpretation describes branching descent, speciation, extinction, and anatomical change within Equidae. A creation-based interpretation may describe extensive diversification within a created equid kind while disputing unrestricted common ancestry.

Evidence standard

Use the Florida Museum fossil horse collection and “Amazing Feets” evidence. Record specimen or feature, measurement, classification, proposed relationship, and uncertainty separately.

Portfolio evidence

Evaluate one equid diagram. Identify at least four physical observations, three inferences, two uncertainties, and how a branching diagram differs from a ladder.

Boundary and limitation

The equid record contains extensive physical evidence. It is not a filmed generation-by-generation sequence and does not show an instantaneous horse-to-nonhorse jump.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse prepared definitions, labeled evidence categories, and guided calculations.
CoreComplete the full evidence analysis and defend conclusions within stated limits.
ExtendedCompare primary sources, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate alternative models.
