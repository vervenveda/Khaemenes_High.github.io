Unit 05 · Day 11

Variation, Natural Selection & Observed Change

Explain natural selection as differential survival and reproduction acting on inherited variation—not purposeful self-change.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish inherited variation from acquired change
  • Explain selection without need-based language
  • Interpret allele or trait-frequency data
  • Use antimicrobial resistance as observed population change

Materials

  • Evidence classification lab
  • CDC NARMS source
  • Notebook
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Conditions for selection

A population must vary; some variation must be heritable; individuals differ in survival or reproduction under stated conditions; and these differences alter trait or allele frequencies across generations.

No purposeful mutation

Individuals do not produce a useful mutation because they need it. Variation exists or arises through mutation and recombination; environmental conditions influence which variants leave more descendants.

Resistance

Antimicrobial use can favor resistant variants already present or newly arising. Resistant organisms then become a larger fraction of the population. Resistance evolves in microbial populations; a person does not become genetically resistant to an antibiotic.

Limits of one example

Observed resistance demonstrates inherited population change and selection. By itself, one resistance case does not establish every proposed historical relationship among all organisms.

Evidence standard

Use CDC NARMS tables or reports to identify organism, drug, sampling population, year, measured resistance percentage, and uncertainty or sampling limitation.

Portfolio evidence

Write an observation–mechanism–inference analysis of one real resistance trend. Remove all phrases suggesting organisms changed because they wanted or needed to.

Boundary and limitation

Natural selection is one evolutionary mechanism. Drift, migration, mutation, recombination, and changing population structure also influence frequencies.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse prepared definitions, labeled evidence categories, and guided calculations.
CoreComplete the full evidence analysis and defend conclusions within stated limits.
ExtendedCompare primary sources, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate alternative models.
