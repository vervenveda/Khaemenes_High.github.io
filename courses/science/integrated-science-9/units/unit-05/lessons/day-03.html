Unit 05 · Day 3

Energy Flow, Biomass & Ecological Efficiency

Quantify energy transfer and explain why energy flow differs from matter cycling.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish energy flow from matter cycling
  • Calculate transfer efficiency
  • Interpret biomass and energy pyramids
  • Explain why fixed percentages are approximations

Materials

  • Calculator
  • Energy-transfer dataset
  • Notebook
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Energy enters and leaves

Most ecosystems receive energy through photosynthesis. Organisms transfer some chemical energy through feeding, while much is used in respiration and released as heat. Energy therefore flows through ecosystems rather than cycling indefinitely.

Transfer efficiency

Transfer efficiency is the energy available at one trophic level divided by the energy available at the preceding level, multiplied by 100 percent.

Why energy decreases

Not all biomass is eaten, not all consumed material is digested, and organisms use energy for movement, maintenance, growth, reproduction, and temperature regulation.

Pyramid limits

An energy pyramid represents energy transfer per area and time. A biomass pyramid is a snapshot and can be inverted in aquatic systems when rapidly reproducing producers have low standing biomass.

Evidence standard

Use measured productivity or energy values from a documented study when available. The common ten-percent statement is a teaching approximation, not a universal law.

Portfolio evidence

For producer energy of 18,000 kJ and primary-consumer energy of 2,160 kJ, calculate efficiency. Then calculate efficiency from 2,160 kJ to 270 kJ and compare.

Boundary and limitation

Energy estimates depend on area, time, sampling, water content, caloric conversion, and which organisms were included.

Deep learning: energy flow is not matter cycling

Essential question: Why does usable energy generally decrease across trophic transfers even though matter is continually recycled?

Energy enters many ecosystems through photosynthesis and leaves as dispersed heat through metabolism. Matter atoms can be reused, but energy is transformed and dissipated. Ecological efficiency compares energy or production available at one trophic level with that available at the preceding level.

Worked quantitative example

A producer level stores 10,000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ of new biomass energy and primary consumers store 1,200 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹.

Transfer efficiency = (1,200 ÷ 10,000) × 100 = 12%. The remaining energy was not “destroyed”; much was used in metabolism, lost as heat, remained in uneaten material, or entered detrital pathways.

If secondary consumers store 120 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹, efficiency from primary to secondary consumers is (120 ÷ 1,200) × 100 = 10%.

Misconception and retrieval check

  • The “10% rule” is a rough teaching generalization, not a universal constant.
  • Energy pyramids and biomass pyramids are related but not identical measurements.
  • Decomposers are not outside energy flow; detrital pathways can be substantial.
  1. Calculate efficiency from 5,000 units to 600 units.
  2. Why does energy not cycle in the same sense as carbon atoms?
  3. Name two destinations for energy not transferred to the next trophic level.
Check

600 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 12%. Energy is transformed and dissipated as heat; atoms can be reused. Metabolism, heat, uneaten biomass, waste, and detritus are valid destinations.
