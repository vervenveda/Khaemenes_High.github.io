Unit 05 · Day 15

Evidence Hearing, Stewardship & Mastery

Synthesize ecology, biodiversity, population change, origins evidence, worldviews, and conservation responsibility.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Present a claim with accurately classified evidence
  • Respond fairly to a competing interpretation
  • Complete the mastery quiz and unit assessment
  • Propose biodiversity stewardship grounded in evidence and community rights

Materials

  • Origins evidence hearing
  • Mastery quiz
  • Unit assessment
  • Student record
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Two-part hearing standard

Part A — science mastery: explain the scientific models taught in this unit accurately, classify evidence correctly, use data and uncertainty, and distinguish direct observations from historical inference. For evolution/origins topics, students must demonstrate understanding of natural selection, population change, speciation, phylogenetic reasoning, and common ancestry as they are used in modern biology.

Part B — worldview literacy: students may describe personal, religious, or philosophical interpretations if they choose. They are assessed only on accurate representation, respectful reasoning, and correct separation of scientific versus non-scientific claims—not on adopting a particular belief.

No compelled belief — and no substitute for science mastery

No student is required to affirm or reject a religious belief, divine action, philosophical naturalism, creation theology, or intelligent-design belief. Personal faith is not graded.

At the same time, a personal worldview position cannot replace the scientific learning target. A student who disagrees personally with common ancestry must still be able to explain the scientific model, the evidence scientists use to test it, and the model's uncertainties accurately—just as a student who accepts common ancestry must accurately distinguish scientific inference from philosophical or theological claims.

Stewardship common ground

Different origins views can still support protection of habitats, responsible resource use, humane treatment of organisms, pollution prevention, and accountability to future generations.

Mastery sequence

Complete required investigations, submit the hearing, pass the quiz at 16/20, pass the assessment at 24/30, complete constructed responses and corrections, and record a final reflection.

Evidence standard

Cite direct data and primary sources. Do not use an advocacy article as though it were a fossil, measurement, or population record.

Portfolio evidence

Submit: (1) an ecology/biodiversity evidence analysis, (2) a population-change or natural-selection dataset interpretation, (3) a scientific origins evidence brief demonstrating the modern biological model accurately, (4) an optional separate worldview reflection, and (5) a stewardship proposal with target, baseline, action, indicator, community consideration, uncertainty, and revision rule.

Boundary and limitation

A fair hearing does not require treating every claim as equally testable or equally supported. It requires accurate classification and honest evaluation.

Mastery retrieval and correction protocol

  1. Explain why energy flows through ecosystems while matter cycles.
  2. Distinguish population density from population growth rate.
  3. Explain why carrying capacity can change.
  4. Use inherited variation, differential reproductive success, and changing frequencies to explain natural selection without need-based language.
  5. Distinguish observed evolutionary change, speciation evidence, and deeper phylogenetic inference.
  6. Explain why a worldview claim and a scientific claim can address related questions while being evaluated by different methods.

After the quiz or unit assessment, correct every missed essential concept by naming the error, explaining the correct model, and completing one fresh transfer example before reassessment.

Mentor synthesis conference

Use Archaemenes to rehearse evidence classification and correction reasoning. The mentor may challenge explanations but may not provide protected assessment answers or replace mastery evidence.
