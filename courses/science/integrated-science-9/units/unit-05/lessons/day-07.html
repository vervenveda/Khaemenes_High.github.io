Unit 05 · Day 7

Population Growth, Carrying Capacity & Limits

Interpret population change without treating carrying capacity as a fixed permanent number.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Calculate absolute and percentage population change
  • Compare exponential and logistic models
  • Explain density-dependent and density-independent limits
  • Interpret predator–prey data without claiming one-cause certainty

Materials

  • Population evidence lab
  • Calculator
  • Notebook
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Population balance

Population size changes through births, deaths, immigration, and emigration. A growth rate summarizes these processes but may conceal different causes.

Exponential model

With abundant resources and constant proportional growth, a population can approximate exponential increase. Real populations rarely maintain the same rate indefinitely.

Logistic model and carrying capacity

Logistic growth slows near a modeled carrying capacity. Carrying capacity depends on food, habitat, climate, disease, competitors, predators, and human activity; it can shift over time.

Predator and prey

Predator and prey populations can influence one another, but weather, vegetation, disease, age structure, migration, and sampling also matter. A time-series association alone does not isolate causation.

Evidence standard

Use the NPS Isle Royale series as a real long-term record. Identify years of increase and decline, then compare ecological explanations with the limitations stated by the source.

Portfolio evidence

Calculate the percentage change from 1,500 moose in 2018 to 2,060 in 2019. Explain why the change cannot be attributed to one factor from counts alone.

Boundary and limitation

Exponential and logistic curves are idealized models. Real populations fluctuate, cross boundaries, experience delays, and change age structure.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse prepared definitions, labeled evidence categories, and guided calculations.
CoreComplete the full evidence analysis and defend conclusions within stated limits.
ExtendedCompare primary sources, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate alternative models.
