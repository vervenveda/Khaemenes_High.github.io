Unit 05 · Day 8

Species Interactions & Food-Web Change

Evaluate competition, predation, herbivory, parasitism, mutualism, and commensalism as context-dependent interactions.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Classify major ecological interactions
  • Explain resource competition and niche overlap
  • Predict food-web effects with alternative explanations
  • Avoid assuming that an interaction is permanently beneficial or harmful

Materials

  • Food-web laboratory
  • Notebook
  • Source dossier
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Interaction signs

Competition is often represented −/−, predation and herbivory +/−, parasitism +/−, mutualism +/+, and commensalism +/0. These symbols summarize effects on fitness under stated conditions.

Competition and niches

Competition occurs when organisms use a limiting resource. Resource partitioning can reduce overlap through differences in location, timing, size, or method of use.

Context matters

A relationship may change with life stage, abundance, climate, or resource level. A pollinator can benefit a plant, while excessive herbivory by the same species can have a negative effect.

Food-web interventions

Removing a predator, adding nutrients, introducing a species, or fragmenting habitat can produce indirect effects. Predictions should identify assumptions and alternative pathways.

Evidence standard

Support each claimed interaction with a documented observation or source. Do not infer mutualism merely because two species occur together.

Portfolio evidence

Choose one documented interaction and write a claim, two pieces of evidence, mechanism, boundary conditions, and one alternative explanation.

Boundary and limitation

Interaction labels summarize average effects and may not describe every individual, season, or environmental condition.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse prepared definitions, labeled evidence categories, and guided calculations.
CoreComplete the full evidence analysis and defend conclusions within stated limits.
ExtendedCompare primary sources, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate alternative models.
