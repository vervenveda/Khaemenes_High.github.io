Unit 05 · Day 6

Population Size, Density & Sampling

Estimate populations while accounting for imperfect detection and sampling uncertainty.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Calculate population density
  • Compare census, quadrat, transect, and mark–recapture methods
  • Use the Lincoln–Petersen estimator cautiously
  • Identify assumptions that can bias an estimate

Materials

  • Calculator
  • Population evidence lab
  • Notebook
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Population properties

Population size is the number of individuals; density is individuals per unit area or volume; distribution may be clumped, uniform, or random at the scale measured.

Sampling instead of counting all

A complete census may be impossible. Ecologists estimate abundance from representative samples, repeated detection, distance sampling, camera records, or capture histories.

Mark–recapture model

A simple estimate is N ≈ M×C/R, where M is initially marked, C is captured later, and R is marked individuals recaptured. It assumes a closed population, durable marks, mixing, similar capture probability, and no mark effect.

Uncertainty

A single estimate can be unstable when recaptures are few. Repeated sessions and models that estimate detection probability are stronger than one simple calculation.

Evidence standard

Analyze the documented Isle Royale wolf–moose record in the population laboratory and identify what was counted versus what must still be inferred.

Portfolio evidence

Calculate density for 420 organisms across 35 hectares. Then estimate N when M=40, C=50, and R=10, and list four assumptions.

Boundary and limitation

Population estimates are model-dependent. A precise-looking number may still have broad uncertainty.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse prepared definitions, labeled evidence categories, and guided calculations.
CoreComplete the full evidence analysis and defend conclusions within stated limits.
ExtendedCompare primary sources, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate alternative models.
