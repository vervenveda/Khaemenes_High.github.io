Unit 05 · Day 1

Ecology Begins with Observation

Define ecological levels and build explanations from field observations rather than assumptions.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish organism, population, community, ecosystem, biome, and biosphere
  • Separate biotic and abiotic observations
  • Distinguish habitat from ecological niche
  • Record an observation before proposing an explanation

Materials

  • Notebook or field sheet
  • Optional camera or binoculars
  • Safe observation location or public biodiversity record
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

What ecology studies

Ecology examines relationships among organisms and their physical surroundings. An organism is one individual; a population contains members of one species in an area; a community includes interacting populations; an ecosystem includes the community and abiotic environment.

Habitat and niche

A habitat is where an organism lives. A niche describes its ecological role, resources, tolerances, timing, and interactions. Two organisms may share a habitat while occupying different niches.

Observation before interpretation

A student may directly observe leaf damage, insect abundance, temperature, shade, or soil moisture. Saying that one factor caused another is an interpretation that requires comparison, controls, repeated evidence, or a suitable natural experiment.

Field evidence protocol

Record date, time, general site type, weather, observation method, sampling effort, organisms or signs observed, abiotic measurements, and uncertainties. Do not publish precise locations of threatened species.

Evidence standard

Use a local, state, or federal biodiversity observation source, or conduct a non-contact observation from a safe public location. Label photographs, measurements, and counts as observations; label ecological explanations as inferences.

Portfolio evidence

Create a six-level ecological scale diagram and a two-column record containing at least five direct observations and five possible interpretations. Mark each interpretation as supported, uncertain, or unsupported.

Boundary and limitation

Ecological categories are models. Real systems have moving boundaries, seasonal change, migration, and interactions across scales.

Deep learning: observation before explanation

Essential question: How can an ecologist move from a field observation to a defensible explanation without smuggling assumptions into the data?

Ecology operates across nested levels: organism, population, community, ecosystem, biome, and biosphere. A habitat describes where an organism lives; a niche describes how it uses resources, tolerates conditions, and interacts with other organisms. Niche is therefore more than an “address.”

Separate three layers in every record: observation (what was directly seen or measured), pattern (a repeated relationship in observations), and inference (an explanation proposed for the pattern). Strong ecological reasoning also records time, location scale, weather, sampling method, and what may have gone undetected.

Worked field-evidence example

You observe 14 pill bugs beneath four shaded logs and 2 beneath four sun-exposed boards during one survey. The observation is the count under stated conditions. A pattern is that more were found in the shaded samples. “Pill bugs prefer shade because it reduces water loss” is an inference requiring additional controlled evidence; it is not contained in the count itself.

Misconception and retrieval check

  • A habitat is not identical to a niche.
  • Failure to detect an organism does not prove absence when detection is imperfect.
  • A one-time correlation does not establish a mechanism.
  1. Name the ecological level represented by all individuals of one species in one area.
  2. Why should a field notebook record search effort?
  3. Rewrite one inference as a direct observation.
