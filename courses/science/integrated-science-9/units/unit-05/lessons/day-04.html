Unit 05 · Day 4

Matter Cycles Through Ecosystems

Track carbon, water, nitrogen, and phosphorus through organisms and Earth systems.

45–60 minutesEvidence firstPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Model reservoirs and transfers in biogeochemical cycles
  • Explain roles of photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, and combustion
  • Distinguish matter conservation from energy dissipation
  • Evaluate model boundaries and human influences

Materials

  • Cycle-model template
  • Notebook
  • Source dossier
Field and organism safety

Use non-contact observation unless a qualified protocol explicitly permits otherwise. Do not handle wildlife, collect protected organisms, disturb nests or dens, culture unknown microbes, trespass, or work in unsafe weather or terrain.

Location, identity, and worldview respect

Do not publish precise locations of sensitive species. Do not require students to disclose a personal religion or family belief. Evaluate evidence classification and reasoning rather than compelled agreement.

Reservoirs and fluxes

A reservoir stores matter; a flux transfers matter. Atmosphere, oceans, soil, rocks, organisms, and sediments can serve as reservoirs with very different residence times.

Carbon and water

Photosynthesis moves carbon into organic molecules. Respiration and decomposition return carbon dioxide. Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and transpiration move water.

Nitrogen and phosphorus

Nitrogen fixation converts atmospheric nitrogen into biologically available forms. Nitrification, assimilation, decomposition, and denitrification move nitrogen. Phosphorus commonly cycles through rocks, soils, water, and organisms without a large atmospheric reservoir.

Human influences

Fossil-fuel combustion, land change, fertilizer use, wastewater, mining, and dams alter rates and reservoirs. The effect depends on location, magnitude, and time scale.

Evidence standard

Use measured atmospheric, watershed, soil, or nutrient records when evaluating a claim. A cycle diagram is a model and does not itself measure a flux.

Portfolio evidence

Create one integrated cycle model showing at least three reservoirs, six transfers, one human influence, and two limitations.

Boundary and limitation

Matter is conserved in ordinary ecological processes, but local reservoir amounts can change greatly and cycle diagrams often omit slow geological processes.

Pathway choices
