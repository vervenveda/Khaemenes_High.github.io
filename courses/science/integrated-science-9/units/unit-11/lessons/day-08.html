Unit 11 · Day 8

Light, Spectra & Telescopes

Use electromagnetic spectra, absorption/emission lines, and instrument design to infer cosmic properties.

45–60 minutesModels & cosmic evidenceObservation safety

Learning objectives

  • Explain continuous, absorption, and emission spectra
  • Relate spectral lines to atomic composition and motion
  • Compare optical, radio, infrared, ultraviolet, X-ray, and gamma-ray observations

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, supplied tables, spectra, and light curves
  • Optional night-sky observations only from permitted stable locations with adult approval
Astronomy and observation safety

Never look directly at the Sun or through binoculars, telescopes, cameras, viewfinders, magnifiers, or improvised filters unless a certified solar system is used exactly as designed under qualified supervision. Do not observe alone from roofs, roadsides, restricted land, unstable terrain, or severe weather. Do not use lasers, launch devices, high-voltage equipment, or improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Label raw data, calibrated data, composite image, false-color image, simulation, model, catalog value, inference, projection, artist concept, and public claim accurately. State wavelength band, scale, time, instrument, processing, uncertainty, and whether the signal is direct or indirect.

Spectra

Atoms and ions interact with characteristic wavelengths, allowing composition to be inferred from calibrated spectra.

Doppler shift

Motion along the line of sight can shift observed wavelengths; redshift and blueshift describe wavelength changes, not object color alone.

Telescopes

Different wavelengths reveal different processes, and many bands require space-based instruments because Earth’s atmosphere absorbs them.

Portfolio evidence

Annotate a supplied spectrum and compare two telescope bands and their limitations.

Model boundary

Celestial spheres, orbit diagrams, scale models, color–temperature relations, stellar tracks, transit curves, galaxy classifications, and expansion graphs simplify three-dimensional geometry, selection effects, calibration, relativity, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do spectra and telescopes convert incoming radiation into evidence about objects that cannot be sampled directly?

Telescopes collect electromagnetic radiation and instruments separate or measure it by wavelength, time, direction, or polarization. Spectral lines can identify atomic or molecular transitions; continuum shape can constrain temperature; brightness depends on intrinsic output and distance; wavelength shifts can indicate line-of-sight motion. Different wavelength bands reveal different physical processes, so astronomy is not limited to visible light.

Worked reasoning example

If a known hydrogen line has laboratory wavelength 656.3 nm and is observed at 656.9 nm, the line is shifted to longer wavelength. The fractional shift z ≈ (656.9−656.3)/656.3 ≈ 9.1 × 10^-4. For a small shift, line-of-sight speed magnitude can be approximated by v≈zc ≈ 2.7 × 10^5 m/s.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: spectrum · absorption line · emission line · redshift · telescope · wavelength band

Misconception check: A telescope image is not always a literal-color photograph; many astronomical images map nonvisible wavelengths or processed intensities into visible displays.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check units, observation frames, model assumptions, uncertainty, and whether each conclusion is directly observed or inferred from remote evidence.

  1. Explain how a spectral line can indicate composition.
  2. Calculate a small fractional wavelength shift.
  3. Name two reasons astronomers observe outside visible wavelengths.
  4. Distinguish apparent brightness from intrinsic luminosity.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your calculation, spectrum, orbital model, or evidence claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your assumptions and scope but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which spectral feature is measured and which physical property is inferred?
  • What assumption enters a small-redshift speed estimate?
  • How can image processing improve visibility without inventing the underlying data?
