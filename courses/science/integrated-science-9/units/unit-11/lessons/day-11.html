Exoplanets: Detection, Bias & Habitability Claims

Evaluate transit, radial-velocity, direct-imaging, microlensing, and astrometric evidence.

45–60 minutesModels & cosmic evidenceObservation safety

Learning objectives

  • Calculate transit depth from radius ratio
  • Explain period and repeated-transit evidence
  • Distinguish habitable-zone location, potential habitability, biosignature evidence, and confirmed life

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, supplied tables, spectra, and light curves
  • Optional night-sky observations only from permitted stable locations with adult approval
Astronomy and observation safety

Never look directly at the Sun or through binoculars, telescopes, cameras, viewfinders, magnifiers, or improvised filters unless a certified solar system is used exactly as designed under qualified supervision. Do not observe alone from roofs, roadsides, restricted land, unstable terrain, or severe weather. Do not use lasers, launch devices, high-voltage equipment, or improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Label raw data, calibrated data, composite image, false-color image, simulation, model, catalog value, inference, projection, artist concept, and public claim accurately. State wavelength band, scale, time, instrument, processing, uncertainty, and whether the signal is direct or indirect.

Transit method

A planet passing in front of its star can produce a small periodic brightness decrease.

Bias

Large planets close to small stars are often easier to detect, so discovery catalogs are not unbiased samples.

Habitability

Habitable-zone location does not prove habitability or life.

Being in a habitable zone does not establish atmosphere, surface conditions, biological activity, or inhabitants.

Portfolio evidence

Evaluate an exoplanet claim using method, signal, uncertainty, bias, and alternative explanations.

Model boundary

Celestial spheres, orbit diagrams, scale models, color–temperature relations, stellar tracks, transit curves, galaxy classifications, and expansion graphs simplify three-dimensional geometry, selection effects, calibration, relativity, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do exoplanet detection methods reveal indirect evidence, and what biases shape the planets we find?

Most exoplanets are detected indirectly. Transit surveys measure repeated brightness dips when orbital geometry carries a planet across the star. Radial-velocity methods detect line-of-sight stellar motion caused by gravitational interaction. Direct imaging, microlensing, and astrometry sample different parameter ranges. Every method has selection effects, and false positives must be tested. Detection does not by itself establish habitability or life.

Worked reasoning example

A transit depth of 1% means ΔF/F = 0.01. In the simple no-limb-darkening model, transit depth ≈ (R_p/R_s)^2, so R_p/R_s≈√0.01=0.10. The planet's radius is about 10% of the star's radius in that simplified model.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: transit · radial velocity · selection bias · false positive · habitability · detection threshold

Misconception check: Finding a planet in a star's 'habitable zone' does not mean the planet is inhabited or even necessarily habitable.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check units, observation frames, model assumptions, uncertainty, and whether each conclusion is directly observed or inferred from remote evidence.

  1. Explain what a transit measures directly.
  2. Explain what radial velocity measures directly.
  3. Calculate a radius ratio from transit depth.
  4. Name one detection bias for each method.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your calculation, spectrum, orbital model, or evidence claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your assumptions and scope but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which conclusion is direct evidence and which is inference?
  • What false positive could mimic a transit?
  • What additional evidence would be needed before making a habitability claim?
