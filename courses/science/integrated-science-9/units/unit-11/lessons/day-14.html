Unit 11 · Day 14

Cosmic Expansion, Big Bang Evidence & Model Limits

Use redshift, expansion relationships, cosmic microwave background, and composition evidence to explain the Big Bang model.

45–60 minutesModels & cosmic evidenceObservation safety

Learning objectives

  • Calculate a simple Hubble-law recession speed or model distance
  • Explain redshift as evidence for expansion at large scales
  • Combine expansion, cosmic microwave background, and elemental-abundance evidence

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, supplied tables, spectra, and light curves
  • Optional night-sky observations only from permitted stable locations with adult approval
Astronomy and observation safety

Never look directly at the Sun or through binoculars, telescopes, cameras, viewfinders, magnifiers, or improvised filters unless a certified solar system is used exactly as designed under qualified supervision. Do not observe alone from roofs, roadsides, restricted land, unstable terrain, or severe weather. Do not use lasers, launch devices, high-voltage equipment, or improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Label raw data, calibrated data, composite image, false-color image, simulation, model, catalog value, inference, projection, artist concept, and public claim accurately. State wavelength band, scale, time, instrument, processing, uncertainty, and whether the signal is direct or indirect.

Expansion

Galaxies are not generally expanding through a pre-existing empty center; the large-scale model describes increasing separation as space expands.

Evidence

Redshift trends, the cosmic microwave background, and primordial-abundance evidence support an evolving hot early universe.

Limits

The Big Bang model describes early expansion and evolution; it does not by itself answer why there is a universe or what, if anything, preceded the modeled early state.

Portfolio evidence

Analyze a distance–velocity graph and distinguish observation, model, extrapolation, and open question.

Model boundary

Celestial spheres, orbit diagrams, scale models, color–temperature relations, stellar tracks, transit curves, galaxy classifications, and expansion graphs simplify three-dimensional geometry, selection effects, calibration, relativity, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: What evidence supports an expanding universe and a hot early cosmic state, and what does the Big Bang model not claim?

The Big Bang model describes an early universe that was hotter and denser and has expanded and evolved. Evidence includes systematic cosmological redshift with distance, the cosmic microwave background, primordial light-element abundances, and the success of expansion models in connecting these observations. Expansion is not modeled as an explosion from one location into preexisting empty space; on large scales, distances between unbound regions increase as space expands.

Worked reasoning example

For modest cosmological distances, Hubble–Lemaître reasoning uses v≈H₀d as an approximate relationship. If a model uses H₀=70 km/s/Mpc and a galaxy is 100 Mpc away, recession speed estimate ≈7000 km/s. This is a simplified relation; local motions and cosmological-model details matter.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: cosmic expansion · redshift · cosmic microwave background · Big Bang model · Hubble–Lemaître relation · primordial abundance

Misconception check: The Big Bang is not a conventional explosion with a central point in ordinary space.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check units, observation frames, model assumptions, uncertainty, and whether each conclusion is directly observed or inferred from remote evidence.

  1. Name three independent evidence lines for the Big Bang model.
  2. Explain what cosmological redshift indicates on large scales.
  3. Use a supplied Hubble constant in a simple model calculation.
  4. State one limitation of the linear Hubble-law approximation.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your calculation, spectrum, orbital model, or evidence claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your assumptions and scope but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which observation most directly supports expansion?
  • What would be an overstatement of what the CMB alone proves?
  • How would you correct the phrase 'the universe exploded into empty space'?
