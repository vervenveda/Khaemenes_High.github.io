Exoplanet Transit Laboratory

Calculate transit depth, estimate radius ratio, and test whether repeated signals support a planet interpretation.

45–60 minutesModels & cosmic evidenceObservation safety

Learning objectives

  • Use transit depth=(Rp/Rs)²
  • Estimate radius ratio from a measured depth
  • Evaluate noise, starspots, grazing eclipses, cadence, and confirmation

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, supplied tables, spectra, and light curves
  • Optional night-sky observations only from permitted stable locations with adult approval
Astronomy and observation safety

Never look directly at the Sun or through binoculars, telescopes, cameras, viewfinders, magnifiers, or improvised filters unless a certified solar system is used exactly as designed under qualified supervision. Do not observe alone from roofs, roadsides, restricted land, unstable terrain, or severe weather. Do not use lasers, launch devices, high-voltage equipment, or improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Label raw data, calibrated data, composite image, false-color image, simulation, model, catalog value, inference, projection, artist concept, and public claim accurately. State wavelength band, scale, time, instrument, processing, uncertainty, and whether the signal is direct or indirect.

Depth

For a simple central transit with uniform stellar brightness, fractional depth approximates area ratio.

Period

Repeated, consistent spacing supports periodic motion but does not by itself prove a planet.

Confirmation

Independent methods, additional transits, and false-positive analysis strengthen a claim.

Model boundary

Celestial spheres, orbit diagrams, scale models, color–temperature relations, stellar tracks, transit curves, galaxy classifications, and expansion graphs simplify three-dimensional geometry, selection effects, calibration, relativity, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can repeated transit data support an exoplanet interpretation while still requiring uncertainty and false-positive checks?

A transit laboratory should preserve the time-series brightness data before fitting a model. Repeated dips with consistent depth, duration, and spacing can support a periodic orbit interpretation. Transit depth constrains radius ratio in a simplified model, while spacing constrains orbital period. Stellar variability, eclipsing binaries, instrumental artifacts, and blended sources can imitate or distort signals.

Worked reasoning example

A star dims from normalized brightness 1.000 to 0.996 during each event. Transit depth = 0.004 = 0.4%. Radius ratio ≈ √0.004 ≈ 0.063. If events repeat every 8.0 days, that spacing is evidence for an approximately 8-day orbital period, subject to missed events or aliases.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: light curve · transit depth · orbital period · radius ratio · false positive · baseline

Misconception check: A single dip is not enough to establish a planet.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check units, observation frames, model assumptions, uncertainty, and whether each conclusion is directly observed or inferred from remote evidence.

  1. Calculate transit depth from normalized brightness.
  2. Estimate radius ratio from a transit depth.
  3. Infer period from repeated event spacing.
  4. List two nonplanet explanations that should be tested.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your calculation, spectrum, orbital model, or evidence claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge your assumptions and scope but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • How many repeated events support your period estimate?
  • Which assumption links transit depth to radius ratio?
  • What follow-up observation would reduce false-positive risk?
