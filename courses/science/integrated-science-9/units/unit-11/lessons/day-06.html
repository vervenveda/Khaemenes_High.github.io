Unit 11 · Day 6

Solar-System Formation & Planetary Diversity

Use accretion evidence to explain shared patterns and differences among planets and smaller bodies.

45–60 minutesModels & cosmic evidenceObservation safety

Learning objectives

  • Describe collapse, rotation, disk formation, condensation, accretion, and clearing
  • Compare terrestrial planets, giant planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets
  • Use meteorites, crater records, orbital patterns, and composition as evidence

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, supplied tables, spectra, and light curves
  • Optional night-sky observations only from permitted stable locations with adult approval
Astronomy and observation safety

Never look directly at the Sun or through binoculars, telescopes, cameras, viewfinders, magnifiers, or improvised filters unless a certified solar system is used exactly as designed under qualified supervision. Do not observe alone from roofs, roadsides, restricted land, unstable terrain, or severe weather. Do not use lasers, launch devices, high-voltage equipment, or improvised radio transmitters.

Evidence standard

Label raw data, calibrated data, composite image, false-color image, simulation, model, catalog value, inference, projection, artist concept, and public claim accurately. State wavelength band, scale, time, instrument, processing, uncertainty, and whether the signal is direct or indirect.

Nebular model

A rotating cloud of gas and dust collapsed under gravity and flattened into a disk around the forming Sun.

Diversity

Temperature, material availability, collisions, migration, atmospheric loss, and later impacts shaped different worlds.

Evidence limits

The solar-system formation model reconstructs past processes from surviving materials, patterns, ages, and simulations.

Portfolio evidence

Build an evidence table connecting one observed solar-system pattern to one formation inference.

Model boundary

Celestial spheres, orbit diagrams, scale models, color–temperature relations, stellar tracks, transit curves, galaxy classifications, and expansion graphs simplify three-dimensional geometry, selection effects, calibration, relativity, and uncertainty.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse guided diagrams, unit cards, and one-step calculations.
CoreComplete orbital, spectral, transit, and expansion analyses.
ExtendedEvaluate approximations, selection bias, distance ladders, and competing models.
