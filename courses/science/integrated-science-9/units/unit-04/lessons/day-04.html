Growth, Repair & Differentiation

Use fictional organisms, supplied datasets, paper models, or digital tools. No biological samples or medical tests are required.

Genetic privacy and respect

Do not collect family medical history, ancestry, paternity evidence, reproductive information, or identifiable genetic data. Human worth is never ranked by genes.

From one cell to many

A multicellular organism develops through repeated division, movement, signaling, selective cell death, and differentiation.

Same genome, different expression

Most body cells contain similar genetic information, but different genes are active in different cell types.

Stem cells and limits

Stem cells can self-renew and produce differentiated descendants; potential depends on type and context.

Build a lineage map from zygote to two specialized cells.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: If most body cells contain essentially the same genome, how do tissues become structurally and functionally different?

Growth depends on both cell division and changes in cell size, organization, and specialization. Differentiation occurs when cells develop different structures and functions through regulated patterns of gene expression. Most differentiated cells do not need to delete unused genes; instead, different genes are activated, repressed, or regulated at different levels.

Signals from neighboring cells, hormones, developmental history, transcription factors, chromatin state, and environmental conditions can all influence gene expression. Stem cells are defined by their ability to self-renew and produce one or more differentiated cell types, but different stem cells have different developmental potentials.

Worked reasoning example

A neuron and a skeletal-muscle cell can contain the same chromosome set while producing very different proteins. The neuron expresses networks supporting electrical signaling and synaptic function; the muscle cell strongly expresses proteins involved in contraction. The difference does not require the neuron to “lose the muscle genes.” It reflects regulation and developmental state.

During tissue repair, a stem or progenitor cell may divide, and some descendants may differentiate. Mitosis supplies cells; differentiation helps establish specialized function.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Differentiation: development of specialized cell state. Gene expression: use of genetic information to produce RNA or functional products. Stem cell: cell capable of self-renewal and producing specified descendant cell types. Lineage: developmental history of cells descended from earlier cells.

  • Misconception: Different cell types normally have completely different genomes.
  • Misconception: Differentiation means DNA sequence must be rewritten for every tissue.
  • Misconception: All stem cells can automatically produce every cell type or cure any disease.

Retrieval and explanation practice

  1. How can cells with the same chromosome set have different functions?
  2. What roles do mitosis and differentiation play in growth?
  3. Why is “stem cell” not one single level of developmental potential?
  4. Name one type of evidence that could show two tissues express different genes.
Practice explanations — open after attempting
  1. They can regulate and express different sets of genes and proteins.
  2. Mitosis increases or maintains cell number; differentiation produces specialized cell states.
  3. Stem-cell populations differ in which descendant cell types they can generate.
  4. Examples include RNA-expression data, protein measurements, or carefully controlled reporter studies.

Mentor conference and portfolio evidence

Defend the statement: “Different cell identity usually reflects differential gene expression, not a completely different genome.”
