Unit 04 · Day 6

Asexual Reproduction & Cloning

Compare binary fission, budding, vegetative propagation, fragmentation, spores, and cloning.

45–60 minutesModel and data basedPortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Describe asexual methods
  • Explain similarity without claiming perfection
  • Evaluate context-dependent advantages

Materials

  • Notebook
  • Comparison table
Safe genetics learning

Use fictional organisms, supplied datasets, paper models, or digital tools. No biological samples or medical tests are required.

Genetic privacy and respect

Do not collect family medical history, ancestry, paternity evidence, reproductive information, or identifiable genetic data. Human worth is never ranked by genes.

One-parent continuity

Asexual reproduction uses genetic information from one parent. Examples include binary fission, budding, runners, tubers, and fragmentation.

Similarity and variation

Mutation, development, cytoplasmic differences, and environment can produce variation among clones.

Context matters

Asexual reproduction can be rapid in stable conditions; lower variation can be limiting under change.

Compare one microbial and one plant example.
