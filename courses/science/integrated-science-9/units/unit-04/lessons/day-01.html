DNA, Genes, Alleles & Chromosomes

Relate genetic information across scales and distinguish DNA sequence, gene, allele, chromosome, genome, genotype, and phenotype.

45–60 minutesModel and data basedPortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Order genome, chromosome, gene, and nucleotide by scale
  • Define allele accurately
  • Distinguish genotype and phenotype

Materials

  • Notebook
  • Genetic scale template
Safe genetics learning

Use fictional organisms, supplied datasets, paper models, or digital tools. No biological samples or medical tests are required.

Genetic privacy and respect

Do not collect family medical history, ancestry, paternity evidence, reproductive information, or identifiable genetic data. Human worth is never ranked by genes.

Genetic information across scales

A genome is the complete genetic material. Eukaryotic genomes are organized into chromosomes—long DNA molecules packaged with proteins. Genes are DNA regions involved in functional products or regulation.

Boundary: Many traits involve multiple genes, regulation, development, environment, and chance.

Alleles and homologues

Alleles are variants of a gene. Homologous chromosomes carry corresponding loci but may carry different alleles.

Portfolio evidence

Create a nested diagram: organism → cell → nucleus → chromosome → DNA → gene → nucleotide. Add one limitation.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can the same hereditary information be described accurately at different biological scales?

DNA is a polymer built from nucleotides. A gene is a region of DNA whose sequence contributes to a functional product or regulated biological process. An allele is one sequence version of a gene or genetic locus. A chromosome is a long DNA molecule packaged with proteins and containing many genes plus other functional DNA regions. The genome is the complete DNA content considered for an organism or cell.

These terms are nested but they are not interchangeable. A chromosome is not “one gene,” and a gene is not a free-floating object separate from DNA. In a diploid organism, homologous chromosomes carry corresponding loci in corresponding positions, but the alleles at those loci may differ.

Genotype describes genetic information at one or more loci. Phenotype describes an observable or measurable characteristic that results from genetic information interacting with gene regulation, development, environment, and chance.

Worked reasoning example

Suppose a fictional organism has a homologous chromosome pair. At the same locus, one chromosome carries allele A and the other carries allele a. The organism's genotype at that locus is Aa. That does not tell you the entire genome, nor does it guarantee one visible phenotype without knowing how the alleles function and what environmental conditions apply.

  1. Scale from smallest to largest: nucleotide sequence region → gene/locus → chromosome → genome.
  2. The letters A and a represent allele versions at one locus, not entire chromosomes.
  3. A phenotype claim requires an additional biological model linking genotype, regulation, and environment.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Locus: a position or region on a chromosome. Allele: a sequence version at a locus. Homologous chromosomes: chromosome partners carrying corresponding loci. Genotype: genetic constitution at specified loci. Phenotype: observable or measurable outcome.

  • Misconception: “One gene equals one trait.” Many traits involve multiple genes, regulation, environment, and development.
  • Misconception: “Dominant or common alleles are better.” Frequency and dominance do not measure biological worth.
  • Misconception: “A chromosome is made only of genes.” Chromosomes also contain regulatory and structural DNA regions.

Retrieval and explanation practice

  1. Why is an allele not the same thing as a chromosome?
  2. What makes two chromosomes homologous?
  3. Can two cells from one person have the same genome but different phenotypes? Explain.
  4. Why should a phenotype never be treated as a direct readout of one allele without evidence?
Practice explanations — open after attempting
  1. An allele is one sequence version at a locus; a chromosome is a much larger DNA molecule containing many loci.
  2. They carry corresponding genes/loci in corresponding positions and derive from the same chromosome type, though their alleles may differ.
  3. Yes. Different cell types regulate different genes and develop different structures and functions.
  4. Expression, interactions among genes, environment, and development can all affect phenotype.
