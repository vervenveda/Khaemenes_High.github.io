Meiosis II & Gamete Variation

Use fictional organisms, supplied datasets, paper models, or digital tools. No biological samples or medical tests are required.

Do not collect family medical history, ancestry, paternity evidence, reproductive information, or identifiable genetic data. Human worth is never ranked by genes.

Second division

DNA is not normally replicated again between meiosis I and II. Sister chromatids separate during meiosis II.

Independent combinations

With n chromosome pairs, independent assortment alone gives 2ⁿ combinations before crossing over and mutation.

n=2 gives 4; n=3 gives 8.

Model limits

Real organisms include linked genes, many pairs, multiple crossovers, mutation, and unequal gamete survival.

Calculate n=1,2,3,5,10 and explain why this underestimates variation.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How does meiosis II complete chromosome segregation, and how can probability quantify only part of the resulting genetic variation?

Meiosis II begins with haploid cells whose chromosomes are still replicated. Its chromosome-separation pattern resembles mitosis: sister chromatids separate and become individual chromosomes in descendant cells. No second round of DNA replication normally occurs between meiosis I and meiosis II.

Independent assortment alone predicts 2ⁿ possible chromosome combinations, where n is the haploid chromosome number, when each homologous pair is treated as two equally likely orientation choices. This count does not include the enormous additional variation produced by crossing over, new mutation, or random fertilization.

Worked combination example

For a fictional species with n = 3 independently assorting chromosome pairs:

  1. Each pair contributes two possible homolog choices.
  2. Multiply the independent choices: 2 × 2 × 2 = 2³ = 8 chromosome combinations.
  3. If n = 4, the model gives 2⁴ = 16 combinations.
  4. The number is a model of chromosome-origin combinations only; crossing over means the real number of possible genetically distinct gametes is much larger.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Meiosis II: second meiotic division in which sister chromatids separate. Combinatorial variation: different combinations generated from independent choices. Gamete variation: genetic differences among gametes arising from multiple mechanisms.

  • Misconception: DNA replicates again before meiosis II.
  • Misconception: 2ⁿ counts every possible gamete genotype. It ignores recombination and other sources of variation.
  • Misconception: Four meiotic products must always be equally sized or functionally equivalent in every organism.

Retrieval and explanation practice

  1. What separates during meiosis II?
  2. For n = 5, how many chromosome combinations does 2ⁿ predict?
  3. Why does 2ⁿ underestimate total gamete variation?
  4. What is one important difference between meiosis I and meiosis II?
Practice explanations — open after attempting
  1. Sister chromatids.
  2. 2⁵ = 32 combinations.
  3. It does not include crossing over, mutation, or later random fertilization.
  4. Meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes and reduces chromosome-set number; meiosis II separates sister chromatids.
