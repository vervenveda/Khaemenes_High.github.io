Unit 02 · Day 7 · Quantitative Transport

Diffusion & Concentration Gradients

Connect random molecular motion to measurable net change. Use graphs, rates, controls, and uncertainty to investigate diffusion while separating molecular diffusion from bulk fluid movement such as convection.

60–80 minutesTier 1 · ≤40°C WaterRequired InvestigationQuantitative EvidenceMastery ≥80%

Learning objectives

  • Explain diffusion as net movement arising from random molecular motion under a concentration gradient.
  • Distinguish dynamic equilibrium from cessation of molecular motion.
  • Calculate and compare average observed spreading rates from time-series data.
  • Explain how temperature, gradient, distance, surface area, medium, and particle properties can affect observed transport.
  • Identify convection and measurement method as possible confounders in a warm-water diffusion investigation.

Materials & tools

  • Required Diffusion Investigation page
  • Notebook or printed laboratory record
  • Graph paper or ecosystem graphing/data tool
  • Calculator for checking rate calculations
  • Optional: two identical clear cups, cool/warm tap water, food coloring, ruler, timer
ProTool/data connection: graphing and calculator tools may verify plots, slopes, and arithmetic after the student defines variables and shows the relationship being calculated. Use the graph to interrogate evidence—not merely decorate the lab report.
Tier 1 · warm tap water only

Direct observation is optional. Use cool and warm tap water only, with warm water at or below 40°C. Never use boiling water. Protect clothing/surfaces from food coloring, keep liquids away from electronics and walkways, clean spills promptly, and use the provided dataset whenever the direct pathway is unsuitable.

1. Random motion can produce directional net change

Individual particles do not need a goal or awareness of a gradient. They move through continual molecular motion. When more particles occupy one region than another, random crossings occur in both directions but, statistically, more particles can leave the higher-concentration region than return to it. The population therefore shows net movement down the concentration gradient.

Do not personify diffusion: particles do not “want to spread out.” Diffusion is an emergent statistical result of molecular motion and unequal concentration.
Micro → macro explanation

Explain how motion that is random at the individual-particle level can produce a predictable net trend at the population level.

2. Dynamic equilibrium

As a concentration difference decreases, the imbalance in opposing particle fluxes decreases. At dynamic equilibrium, particles continue moving and crossing available boundaries, but opposing rates balance sufficiently that there is no net concentration change at the scale being observed.

Misconception repair

Correct: “Diffusion ends when every molecule stops at equilibrium.” Include both molecular motion and net change in your correction.

4. Required investigation: cool versus warm water

The investigation asks how water temperature relates to the diameter of a food-coloring cloud over 120 seconds while cup shape, water volume, drop size, and observation method are controlled. Students may collect observations or analyze the provided dataset.

Open Diffusion Investigation
Interpretation boundary: visible dye-cloud spreading is an indirect classroom proxy for molecular transport. In warm water, convection and other bulk fluid motion can contribute to visible spreading. Therefore the activity does not isolate molecular diffusion perfectly.

5. Quantitative evidence studio

For the provided dataset, the 0–120 s cloud diameter changes from 0.0 to 3.4 cm in cool water and 0.0 to 6.8 cm in warm water. Calculate the average observed diameter increase:

average observed spreading rate = change in measured cloud diameter ÷ elapsed time

Calculate and compare

Calculate both average rates in cm/s. Then calculate the warm:cool rate ratio. State exactly what these values measure—and why they should not automatically be called molecular diffusion coefficients.

6. Graph for shape, not just endpoints

Plot time on the horizontal axis and cloud diameter on the vertical axis. Include units, an honest scale, a legend, and all measured points. Examine whether the relationship is approximately linear across this short interval or whether the apparent rate changes with time.

Graph interrogation

What additional information can the full graph reveal that comparing only the 0 s and 120 s endpoints cannot?

Data-tool challenge: if the graphing tool offers a trend line, do not accept it automatically. Ask whether a linear model is justified over the observed interval and inspect how the points depart from it.

7. Confounding: diffusion versus convection

A warmer cup can develop density differences and fluid circulation. Such bulk motion can transport dye much faster than molecular diffusion alone. Stirring, unequal drop placement, temperature gradients, cup geometry, and residual currents can therefore confound the comparison.

Design repair

Propose two changes that would reduce unwanted fluid-motion differences between conditions. Then explain why “the warm dye spread faster, therefore molecular diffusion alone caused the entire difference” is too strong a conclusion.

8. Measurement quality

Cloud diameter is easy to observe but imperfect: the boundary may be diffuse, clouds may become asymmetric, and deciding where color “ends” can vary among observers. Repeated trials and a defined measurement rule improve reliability.

Operational definition

Write an operational rule for measuring dye-cloud diameter consistently. Include what you would do if the cloud is not circular.

9. Mentor evidence defense

After completing your graph and calculations, present the conclusion to the Science Mentor. Ask the Mentor to challenge whether you have confused diffusion with convection or correlation with an isolated causal mechanism. Revise your claim to match the evidence.

Mentor supports post-analysis critique. The student's calculations, graph interpretation, and mastery check remain independent evidence.

Pathway choices
FoundationAnalyze the supplied dataset using a graph frame and rate equation.
CoreCollect or analyze repeated observations, graph the data, compare rates, and evaluate confounding.
ExtendedDesign a revised method intended to minimize convection and explain what evidence would be needed to estimate a true diffusion coefficient.

Exit mastery check

  1. Define diffusion without saying particles “want” to spread.
  2. Explain dynamic equilibrium at both particle and population levels.
  3. Calculate the average observed spreading rate for 6.8 cm over 120 s.
  4. Name two variables that should be controlled in the investigation.
  5. Explain why convection threatens a simple causal interpretation of the warm-water result.
  6. Give one limitation of cloud diameter as a measurement and one improvement.

Mastery target: demonstrate at least 80% understanding and complete corrections before treating Day 7 as secure.
