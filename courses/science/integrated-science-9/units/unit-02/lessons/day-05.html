Unit 02 · Day 5 · Evidence Laboratory

Virtual Microscopy Laboratory

Use six model-based microscopy stations to classify cellular evidence, calculate actual size, audit diagrams, separate observation from inference, and defend what the evidence can—and cannot—support.

60–75 minutesTier 1 · Virtual Lab6 Stations5/6 = 83.3%Portfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Classify cell evidence using visible features while distinguishing direct observation from inference.
  • Calculate actual biological size from compatible image units and magnification.
  • Evaluate simplified biological diagrams for artificial color, scale distortion, missing information, and false universality.
  • Use uncertainty language when a model cannot uniquely establish identity or function.
  • Construct an evidence statement that includes observation, inference, limitation, and a next-evidence request.

Materials & tools

  • Virtual Microscopy Laboratory
  • Notebook or laboratory record sheet
  • Calculator for checking arithmetic after dimensional reasoning is shown
  • Optional Evidence Citation Studio for Extended comparison with a reputable real micrograph
Important provenance: the six laboratory images are simplified instructional SVG models, not direct microscope photographs. Treat them as models deliberately designed to test interpretation skills.
Tier 1 · virtual laboratory

No biological specimen collection, culturing, staining, glass slides, chemicals, or live microscopy is required. Do not substitute an unsupervised wet-lab activity for this virtual investigation.

1. Laboratory protocol

Before opening the stations, create a six-row record with these headings: station, direct observation, classification/answer, reasoning, uncertainty or model limitation, additional evidence desired.

  1. Inspect the visual before reading the answer choices.
  2. Record at least one directly represented feature.
  3. Answer the station question independently.
  4. For numerical work, show unit reconciliation and calculation in your notebook.
  5. State one uncertainty or limitation where appropriate.
  6. Submit only after all six stations are answered.
Observation ≠ interpretation: “green oval structures are drawn” is an observation about the model. “They are chloroplasts” is an interpretation supported by the instructional context. Keep those reasoning levels distinguishable.

2. Complete all six stations

The laboratory includes bacterial classification, onion epidermal tissue, cheek epithelial cells, photosynthetic leaf cells, a magnification calculation, and model evaluation.

Open Virtual Microscopy
Before submitting

Which station depends most heavily on quantitative reasoning? Which depends most heavily on model-literacy reasoning? Explain why.

3. Quantitative audit

At the magnification station, do not rely only on selecting the correct option. Record the complete dimensional calculation:

actual size = image size ÷ magnification

Then convert the result to micrometers and perform a reverse check by multiplying actual size by magnification.

Precision rule: a calculator display does not determine biological measurement precision. Report only precision justified by the supplied values/model.

4. Classification confidence

Not every visible feature has equal diagnostic value. A represented membrane-bound nucleus strongly supports eukaryotic classification; a nucleoid model supports prokaryotic organization. Cell shape or color alone is generally weaker evidence because diagrams often use stylized geometry and artificial colors.

Evidence ranking

Rank these from stronger to weaker evidence for cellular classification in this lab: represented nucleus/nucleoid organization, cell boundary shape, artificial color. Defend your ranking.

5. Model forensics

Every station is a representation. Ask what has been exaggerated, omitted, recolored, flattened, or made static. A useful model can still be scientifically limited.

Model featureUseful forPotential limitation
Artificial colorSeparating structures visuallyMay imply colors that are not observed directly
Enlarged organellesRecognition and labelingCan distort relative scale
Clean boundariesIntroductory classificationReal images may have blur, overlap, preparation artifacts, or low contrast
Single representative cellFocusing attentionCan hide biological variation within tissues/species

6. Laboratory result and correction cycle

The virtual station score requires 5 of 6, which is 83.3% and therefore satisfies the course's ≥80% threshold for this laboratory. A score below 5/6 requires conceptual correction before a new attempt.

For every missed station, record: your original reasoning, the evidence you overlooked or misinterpreted, the corrected principle, and one new example.

Mastery integrity: the station score measures objective responses. Your laboratory record and evidence statement provide additional evidence that you can explain the reasoning rather than memorize answer positions.

7. Evidence statement

Choose one station and write a four-part scientific evidence statement:

  1. Observation: what is directly represented?
  2. Inference: what classification or interpretation follows?
  3. Limitation: what can this model not establish?
  4. Next evidence: what additional observation, measurement, or source would strengthen the conclusion?
Portfolio evidence

Write the four-part statement and include confidence language appropriate to the evidence.

8. Mentor laboratory defense

After completing your independent station attempt, present one classification and the magnification calculation to the Science Mentor. Ask the Mentor to challenge one assumption and one uncertainty statement rather than reveal answers before submission.

Mentor support belongs in review and revision. The first station attempt remains independent mastery evidence.

Pathway choices
FoundationComplete all six stations using the laboratory record prompts and unit-conversion frame.
CoreComplete all six independently, then produce the four-part evidence statement.
ExtendedUse Evidence Citation Studio to compare one instructional model with a reputable real light- or electron-micrograph source and identify two differences caused by representation or imaging method.

Exit mastery check

  1. Why does a represented membrane-bound nucleus support eukaryotic classification?
  2. Why is artificial color weak evidence for biological identity?
  3. Explain why ordinary onion bulb epidermal cells are not expected to contain chloroplasts.
  4. Show how 24 mm at ×400 corresponds to 60 µm actual size.
  5. Name two limitations of simplified cell diagrams.
  6. Distinguish an observation from an inference using one laboratory station.

Mastery target: ≥80% conceptual understanding plus a laboratory station score of at least 5/6. Correct missed reasoning before treating Day 5 as secure.

Portfolio evidence

Submit the six-station laboratory record, shown magnification calculation with reverse check, correction notes if needed, and one four-part observation → inference → limitation → next-evidence statement.
