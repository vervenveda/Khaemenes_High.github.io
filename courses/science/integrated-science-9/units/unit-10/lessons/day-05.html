Earth Interior & Plate Evidence Laboratory

Analyze seismic timing and boundary evidence using transparent model assumptions.

45–60 minutesSystems & evidenceSafe field boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Calculate S–P arrival-time lag
  • Use a labeled practice relation to estimate station distance
  • Classify plate-boundary evidence and evaluate model limits

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, maps, and supplied data
  • Optional safe observations from stable public locations with adult approval
Earth-science field safety

Do not enter floodwater, storm drains, caves, mines, construction sites, wildfire areas, disaster zones, unstable slopes, cliffs, sinkholes, restricted shorelines, or fast-moving streams. Do not taste minerals, inhale dust, use acids on unknown rocks, climb road cuts, or collect protected materials.

Evidence standard

Identify direct measurement, mapped observation, laboratory evidence, calculation, model, historical inference, projection, scenario, and decision separately. State scale, units, system boundary, uncertainty, and source conditions.

Timing evidence

The S–P lag increases with distance from an earthquake because P and S waves travel at different speeds.

Practice relation

A classroom linear conversion can support arithmetic practice but is not a global travel-time model.

Inference

A single station estimates distance, not direction or an exact epicenter.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Earth Interior & Plate Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Earth cross-sections, plate maps, water budgets, reservoir boxes, flux arrows, energy balances, climate projections, and hazard maps simplify scale, heterogeneity, time variability, interactions, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can seismic timing and mapped plate evidence be analyzed without hiding model assumptions?

A plate/interior evidence laboratory should preserve raw or supplied observations before applying a model. Travel-time differences can be used to infer distance or interior structure only if assumptions about wave speeds and paths are stated. Boundary identification similarly depends on combining earthquake depths, volcano locations, seafloor age, topography, and motion data rather than one feature alone.

Worked reasoning example

Suppose two seismic phases arrive 40 s apart at one station and 70 s apart at another. The larger separation can indicate greater source distance when interpreted with an appropriate travel-time model. The time difference alone is not a direct distance unless the model or calibration relating time difference to distance is supplied.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: travel time · seismic phase · triangulation · model assumption · epicenter · boundary evidence

Misconception check: A measured arrival-time difference is data; the converted distance is a model-dependent result.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check budgets, units, system boundaries, model assumptions, uncertainty, and the scope of every causal claim.

  1. Separate raw seismic observations from calculated or inferred quantities.
  2. Name at least three mapped features that can support a plate-boundary interpretation.
  3. Explain why one station cannot uniquely locate an earthquake epicenter.
  4. Identify one assumption in a travel-time conversion.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your map, budget, data interpretation, or Earth-system model to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and evidence quality but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which step of your laboratory depends most heavily on a model?
  • What additional station or dataset would reduce ambiguity?
  • Did you preserve an observation that seems inconsistent with the expected pattern?
