Unit 10 · Day 13

Atmosphere, Solar Energy & the Greenhouse Effect

Use an energy-flow model to explain Earth’s atmospheric and surface temperatures.

45–60 minutesSystems & evidenceSafe field boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish weather from climate
  • Calculate reflected and absorbed solar energy in a simple model
  • Explain the natural greenhouse effect without claiming the atmosphere is a solid blanket

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, maps, and supplied data
  • Optional safe observations from stable public locations with adult approval
Earth-science field safety

Do not enter floodwater, storm drains, caves, mines, construction sites, wildfire areas, disaster zones, unstable slopes, cliffs, sinkholes, restricted shorelines, or fast-moving streams. Do not taste minerals, inhale dust, use acids on unknown rocks, climb road cuts, or collect protected materials.

Evidence standard

Identify direct measurement, mapped observation, laboratory evidence, calculation, model, historical inference, projection, scenario, and decision separately. State scale, units, system boundary, uncertainty, and source conditions.

Energy balance

Long-term climate depends on incoming solar energy, reflection, absorption, outgoing infrared radiation, and internal redistribution.

Greenhouse effect

Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation, changing the altitude and conditions from which energy escapes to space.

Weather and climate

Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes statistical patterns over longer periods.

Portfolio evidence

Construct an atmospheric energy-flow diagram and calculate a simple absorbed-energy value.

Model boundary

Earth cross-sections, plate maps, water budgets, reservoir boxes, flux arrows, energy balances, climate projections, and hazard maps simplify scale, heterogeneity, time variability, interactions, and uncertainty.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How does Earth's energy balance and the natural greenhouse effect connect incoming solar energy with outgoing infrared radiation?

Earth receives shortwave solar energy, reflects part of it, and absorbs the remainder in the atmosphere and surface. The Earth system emits infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation at specific wavelength ranges, altering where and how efficiently energy escapes to space. The natural greenhouse effect raises surface temperature relative to an atmosphere transparent to infrared radiation. Energy balance is about rates of incoming, reflected, absorbed, and outgoing energy.

Worked reasoning example

Use a normalized 100-unit solar budget. If 30 units are reflected, 70 are absorbed by the Earth system. In long-term balance, outgoing energy to space must average about 70 units. If outgoing energy temporarily falls below 70 while incoming absorbed energy remains 70, the system gains energy until temperatures and other processes adjust.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: albedo · shortwave radiation · infrared radiation · greenhouse effect · energy balance · radiative imbalance

Misconception check: Greenhouse gases do not act like a solid roof 'trapping' all heat. They alter infrared absorption and emission within the atmosphere.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check budgets, units, system boundaries, model assumptions, uncertainty, and the scope of every causal claim.

  1. Calculate absorbed energy from incoming and reflected energy.
  2. Explain why long-term energy balance requires outgoing and absorbed rates to match on average.
  3. Distinguish the natural greenhouse effect from additional forcing.
  4. Explain why increasing albedo would tend to reduce absorbed solar energy, all else equal.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your map, budget, data interpretation, or Earth-system model to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and evidence quality but does not provide protected assessment answers.

  • Which terms in your energy budget are inputs, outputs, or internal transfers?
  • What observation supports greenhouse-gas interaction with infrared radiation?
  • How does an energy imbalance differ from ordinary short-term weather variability?
