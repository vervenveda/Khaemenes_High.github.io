Unit 10 · Day 10

Carbon Cycle: Reservoirs, Fluxes & Residence Time

Develop a quantitative carbon-cycle model across Earth’s spheres.

45–60 minutesSystems & evidenceSafe field boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Identify major carbon reservoirs and fluxes
  • Calculate reservoir change and simple residence time
  • Distinguish fast and slow carbon-cycle pathways

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator, maps, and supplied data
  • Optional safe observations from stable public locations with adult approval
Earth-science field safety

Do not enter floodwater, storm drains, caves, mines, construction sites, wildfire areas, disaster zones, unstable slopes, cliffs, sinkholes, restricted shorelines, or fast-moving streams. Do not taste minerals, inhale dust, use acids on unknown rocks, climb road cuts, or collect protected materials.

Evidence standard

Identify direct measurement, mapped observation, laboratory evidence, calculation, model, historical inference, projection, scenario, and decision separately. State scale, units, system boundary, uncertainty, and source conditions.

Fast pathways

Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, fire, and air–sea exchange can transfer carbon relatively quickly.

Slow pathways

Burial, sedimentation, rock formation, weathering, volcanism, and tectonics operate over longer timescales.

Accounting

A reservoir grows when inputs exceed outputs and shrinks when outputs exceed inputs.

Portfolio evidence

Calculate three reservoir changes and explain one fast and one slow pathway.

Model boundary

Earth cross-sections, plate maps, water budgets, reservoir boxes, flux arrows, energy balances, climate projections, and hazard maps simplify scale, heterogeneity, time variability, interactions, and uncertainty.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse guided diagrams, vocabulary cards, and one-step budgets.
CoreComplete system models, maps, flux accounts, and evidence evaluations.
ExtendedAnalyze residence time, feedbacks, uncertainty, and scenario tradeoffs.
