Unit 08 · Day 1

Physical Quantities, SI Units, Scalars & Vectors

Build a disciplined measurement language for motion, force, momentum, work, and energy.

45–60 minutesMeasurement & modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Use SI units and prefixes accurately
  • Distinguish scalar and vector quantities
  • Report measurements with units, direction, precision, and uncertainty

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and ruler
  • Optional low-speed tabletop or walking measurements with adult approval
Motion and impact safety

Use clear indoor or enclosed spaces, low speeds, light objects, stable surfaces, and adult approval. Do not enter roads, use motor vehicles, drop heavy objects from height, launch hard projectiles, test impacts on people or animals, improvise restraints, or stand beneath suspended masses.

Evidence standard

Separate direct measurements, calculated quantities, graph interpretations, idealized models, and engineering inferences. Record units, direction, reference frame, system boundary, and uncertainty.

Measurement language

Distance, speed, time, mass, energy, and temperature are scalars. Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum, and impulse are vectors.

Units

Use metres, seconds, kilograms, metres per second, metres per second squared, newtons, joules, watts, and kilogram metres per second.

Direction matters

A velocity of 5 m/s east is different from 5 m/s west even though the speeds are equal.

Portfolio evidence

Create a quantity–symbol–unit–scalar/vector reference table and evaluate three measurement records.

Model boundary

Point-mass, constant-acceleration, free-body, closed-system, frictionless, and ideal-machine models simplify shape, rotation, deformation, air resistance, and material behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How does precise measurement language prevent confusion when motion and forces have both size and direction?

Physics begins by defining what is measured, the unit used, and whether direction matters. A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction. Distance, speed, time, energy, mass, and temperature are treated as scalars in this course. Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, and momentum are vectors. SI units make comparisons traceable: metres, seconds, kilograms, newtons, joules, and watts. A numerical answer without a unit or a vector answer without a direction is incomplete evidence.

Worked reasoning example

A runner travels 120 m east in 30 s. Distance = 120 m, a scalar. Displacement = 120 m east, a vector. Average speed = 120/30 = 4.0 m/s. Average velocity = 4.0 m/s east. The same number appears in this simple path, but speed and velocity are still different quantities because only velocity includes direction.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: SI unit · scalar · vector · magnitude · direction · measurement uncertainty

Misconception check: A quantity does not become a vector just because it changes. Direction must be part of the defined quantity.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then use the lesson to correct units, signs, diagrams, calculations, and any claim that is broader than the evidence.

  1. Classify distance, displacement, speed, velocity, mass, force, energy, and momentum as scalar or vector.
  2. Convert 2.5 km to metres and 450 ms to seconds.
  3. Explain why '5 m/s' and '5 m/s north' are not identical statements.
  4. State one reason units are part of scientific evidence rather than decoration.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your diagram, graph, calculation, or engineering claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not supply protected assessment answers or replace independent mastery evidence.

  • Which quantity in your work loses important information if direction is omitted?
  • How would a unit error affect a later calculation?
  • Where does measurement uncertainty enter even when arithmetic is exact?
