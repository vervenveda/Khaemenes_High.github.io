Unit 08 · Day 3

Position–Time and Distance–Time Graphs

Use graph slope to interpret motion and compare intervals.

45–60 minutesMeasurement & modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Plot position or distance against time
  • Interpret rest, constant motion, and changing motion
  • Calculate slope with correct units

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and ruler
  • Optional low-speed tabletop or walking measurements with adult approval
Motion and impact safety

Use clear indoor or enclosed spaces, low speeds, light objects, stable surfaces, and adult approval. Do not enter roads, use motor vehicles, drop heavy objects from height, launch hard projectiles, test impacts on people or animals, improvise restraints, or stand beneath suspended masses.

Evidence standard

Separate direct measurements, calculated quantities, graph interpretations, idealized models, and engineering inferences. Record units, direction, reference frame, system boundary, and uncertainty.

Graph meaning

The horizontal axis is time. A position–time graph can show direction; a distance–time graph cannot decrease.

Slope

For a straight position–time segment, slope equals velocity. Steeper magnitude means greater speed.

Curves

A changing slope indicates changing velocity. Do not describe a curved graph as a physical hill.

Portfolio evidence

Construct and annotate one motion graph from measured or supplied practice data.

Model boundary

Point-mass, constant-acceleration, free-body, closed-system, frictionless, and ideal-machine models simplify shape, rotation, deformation, air resistance, and material behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: What information is encoded in the slope of a position–time graph?

A position–time graph records position on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis. The slope Δposition/Δtime gives velocity over an interval. A horizontal segment means constant position and therefore zero velocity. A steeper slope means greater magnitude of velocity. A negative slope represents motion in the negative direction of the chosen coordinate system. Curved position–time graphs indicate changing velocity.

Worked reasoning example

Between 2 s and 6 s, a position changes from 3 m to 15 m. Slope = (15 - 3)/(6 - 2) = 12/4 = 3.0 m/s. If position later changes from 15 m to 11 m in 2 s, slope = (11 - 15)/2 = -2.0 m/s. The negative sign indicates direction, not 'negative speed.'

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: position–time graph · slope · interval · constant velocity · instantaneous trend · reference position

Misconception check: A higher point on a position–time graph does not mean the object is moving faster. Speed is related to slope, not height alone.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then use the lesson to correct units, signs, diagrams, calculations, and any claim that is broader than the evidence.

  1. Calculate the slope between two points on a position–time graph.
  2. Interpret a horizontal segment.
  3. Explain the meaning of a negative slope.
  4. Distinguish graph height from graph steepness.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your diagram, graph, calculation, or engineering claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not supply protected assessment answers or replace independent mastery evidence.

  • Which graph feature supports your velocity claim?
  • Where are you describing position rather than motion?
  • How would measurement scatter affect confidence in a slope estimate?
