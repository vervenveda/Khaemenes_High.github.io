Unit 08 · Day 4

Speed–Time and Velocity–Time Graphs

Use slope and area to connect acceleration, velocity change, and displacement.

45–60 minutesMeasurement & modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Interpret velocity–time and speed–time graphs
  • Calculate acceleration from slope
  • Calculate displacement or distance from area

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and ruler
  • Optional low-speed tabletop or walking measurements with adult approval
Motion and impact safety

Use clear indoor or enclosed spaces, low speeds, light objects, stable surfaces, and adult approval. Do not enter roads, use motor vehicles, drop heavy objects from height, launch hard projectiles, test impacts on people or animals, improvise restraints, or stand beneath suspended masses.

Evidence standard

Separate direct measurements, calculated quantities, graph interpretations, idealized models, and engineering inferences. Record units, direction, reference frame, system boundary, and uncertainty.

Slope and area

The slope of a velocity–time graph is acceleration. Signed area under it is displacement.

Negative values

Negative velocity indicates direction, not necessarily slowing down. Negative acceleration indicates acceleration in the chosen negative direction.

Units

Graph slope units reduce to m/s²; area units reduce to metres.

Portfolio evidence

Calculate two slopes and two areas and explain the signs.

Model boundary

Point-mass, constant-acceleration, free-body, closed-system, frictionless, and ideal-machine models simplify shape, rotation, deformation, air resistance, and material behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do slope and area on velocity–time graphs represent different physical quantities?

On a velocity–time graph, slope gives acceleration: Δvelocity/Δtime. Area between the velocity curve and the time axis gives displacement. Positive and negative areas contribute with opposite signs because velocity is directional. On a speed–time graph, area gives distance when speed remains nonnegative. Students must therefore read the axis label before interpreting area.

Worked reasoning example

A car increases velocity uniformly from 2 m/s to 8 m/s over 3 s. Acceleration = (8 - 2)/3 = 2.0 m/s². Displacement during the interval is the trapezoid area: ((2 + 8)/2) × 3 = 15 m. If the graph were velocity below zero, area would represent negative displacement rather than negative distance.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: velocity–time graph · acceleration · slope · area · displacement · trapezoid

Misconception check: The area under a velocity–time graph is not acceleration. Slope is acceleration; area is displacement.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then use the lesson to correct units, signs, diagrams, calculations, and any claim that is broader than the evidence.

  1. Find acceleration from a velocity change over a time interval.
  2. Calculate displacement from a rectangular or trapezoidal velocity–time segment.
  3. Explain what negative graph area means on a velocity–time graph.
  4. State why a speed–time graph and velocity–time graph cannot always be interpreted identically.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your diagram, graph, calculation, or engineering claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not supply protected assessment answers or replace independent mastery evidence.

  • Which operation—slope or area—answers the question you were actually asked?
  • How does the sign of velocity affect the accumulated displacement?
  • What approximation error might appear if a curved graph is replaced by rectangles?
