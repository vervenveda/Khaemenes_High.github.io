Unit 08 · Day 5

Motion Data Laboratory

Collect or analyze time-position evidence without inventing observations.

45–60 minutesMeasurement & modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Record repeated position and time measurements
  • Calculate interval and overall motion quantities
  • Evaluate precision, reaction time, and graph limitations

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and ruler
  • Optional low-speed tabletop or walking measurements with adult approval
Motion and impact safety

Use clear indoor or enclosed spaces, low speeds, light objects, stable surfaces, and adult approval. Do not enter roads, use motor vehicles, drop heavy objects from height, launch hard projectiles, test impacts on people or animals, improvise restraints, or stand beneath suspended masses.

Evidence standard

Separate direct measurements, calculated quantities, graph interpretations, idealized models, and engineering inferences. Record units, direction, reference frame, system boundary, and uncertainty.

Real data option

Use a walking person or slowly rolling object in a clear, level area with adult approval. Mark positions and record times without entering roads or using motor vehicles.

Practice option

The application may generate labeled practice scenarios for calculation, but practice values are not experimental evidence.

Evaluation

Discuss timing resolution, start/stop judgment, path alignment, surface variation, and repeatability.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Motion Graph Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Point-mass, constant-acceleration, free-body, closed-system, frictionless, and ideal-machine models simplify shape, rotation, deformation, air resistance, and material behavior.

Pathway choices
FoundationUse guided diagrams, unit tables, and one-step equations.
CoreComplete graphs, forces, momentum, and energy calculations.
ExtendedAnalyze signed vectors, multi-stage energy accounts, and uncertainty.
