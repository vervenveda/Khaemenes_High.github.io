Unit 08 · Day 6

Forces, Free-Body Diagrams & Net Force

Represent interactions and determine resultant force.

45–60 minutesMeasurement & modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Identify contact and field forces
  • Construct free-body diagrams
  • Calculate one-dimensional net force

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Calculator and ruler
  • Optional low-speed tabletop or walking measurements with adult approval
Motion and impact safety

Use clear indoor or enclosed spaces, low speeds, light objects, stable surfaces, and adult approval. Do not enter roads, use motor vehicles, drop heavy objects from height, launch hard projectiles, test impacts on people or animals, improvise restraints, or stand beneath suspended masses.

Evidence standard

Separate direct measurements, calculated quantities, graph interpretations, idealized models, and engineering inferences. Record units, direction, reference frame, system boundary, and uncertainty.

Forces are interactions

Pushes, pulls, normal forces, tension, friction, drag, gravity, electric, and magnetic forces arise from interactions.

Free-body diagrams

Draw only forces acting on the chosen object. Arrow direction shows force direction; arrow length may represent magnitude.

Net force

Add forces as vectors. Balanced forces mean zero net force, not necessarily zero motion.

Portfolio evidence

Draw four free-body diagrams and calculate each net force.

Model boundary

Point-mass, constant-acceleration, free-body, closed-system, frictionless, and ideal-machine models simplify shape, rotation, deformation, air resistance, and material behavior.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do free-body diagrams turn interactions into a testable net-force model?

A free-body diagram isolates one chosen object and shows external forces acting on that object. Each arrow represents a force with direction; arrow length may represent relative magnitude when a scale is stated. Net force is the vector sum of those forces. Forces acting on other objects do not belong on the same diagram. Balanced forces produce zero net force, but zero net force does not require zero velocity.

Worked reasoning example

A box experiences 10 N right from a push and 4 N left from friction. Net force = 10 - 4 = 6 N right. If an upward normal force of 50 N and downward weight of 50 N also act, the vertical net force is zero. The overall acceleration is horizontal because the vertical forces balance.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: free-body diagram · external force · net force · normal force · friction · weight

Misconception check: A free-body diagram is not a picture of every interaction in the entire scene. It represents forces acting on one selected object.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then use the lesson to correct units, signs, diagrams, calculations, and any claim that is broader than the evidence.

  1. Draw or describe a free-body diagram for a book resting on a table.
  2. Calculate one-dimensional net force from opposing forces.
  3. Explain why balanced forces can exist while an object moves at constant velocity.
  4. Identify one force that belongs on the object and one interaction force that acts on a different object.

Mentor evidence conference

Bring your diagram, graph, calculation, or engineering claim to the Science Mentor. The mentor may question assumptions and reasoning but does not supply protected assessment answers or replace independent mastery evidence.

  • Which object is your free-body diagram actually about?
  • Did you include any force that acts on another object instead?
  • How does your net-force result constrain the possible acceleration?
