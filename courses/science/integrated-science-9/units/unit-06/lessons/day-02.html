Unit 06 · Day 2

How Atomic Models Changed

Trace how evidence revised Dalton, Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr, and quantum models.

45–60 minutesEvidence and modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Place major model revisions in evidence-based sequence
  • Explain what cathode-ray, scattering, and spectral evidence contributed
  • Identify strengths and limits of historical models

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table or supplied dataset
  • Calculator where required
Chemistry and radiation safety

Do not handle reactive alkali metals, concentrated halogens, unknown salts, open flames, radioactive minerals, smoke-detector sources, or unknown electrical equipment. Use published data, safe simulations, or a qualified laboratory protocol.

Evidence and source standard

Use NIST, CIAAW, IUPAC, official curriculum sources, or a documented laboratory record. Distinguish a measured spectrum or isotope abundance from an instructional model.

Dalton and chemical regularity

Dalton’s model helped explain fixed composition and whole-number combining patterns, but the atom was later shown to have internal structure.

Thomson and Rutherford

Cathode-ray deflection supported negatively charged particles. Rutherford scattering supported a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus and mostly empty space at the scale of the experiment.

Bohr and later models

Bohr’s quantized levels explained key hydrogen patterns. Modern quantum models retain quantized energy but do not assign electrons exact planetary paths.

Portfolio evidence

Create a five-column timeline: model, evidence, claim supported, limitation, and later revision.

Model boundary

Atom and shell diagrams are not literal scale drawings or photographs. They simplify size, distance, probability, and electron behavior.
