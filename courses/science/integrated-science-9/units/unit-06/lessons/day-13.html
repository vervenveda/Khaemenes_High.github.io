Unit 06 · Day 13

Group Trends: Alkali Metals, Halogens & Noble Gases

Analyze real group patterns without unsafe chemical handling.

45–60 minutesEvidence and modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Describe Group 1, 17, and 18 patterns
  • Predict a property from group data
  • Explain why reactive substances require controlled evidence sources

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table or supplied dataset
  • Calculator where required
Chemistry and radiation safety

Do not handle reactive alkali metals, concentrated halogens, unknown salts, open flames, radioactive minerals, smoke-detector sources, or unknown electrical equipment. Use published data, safe simulations, or a qualified laboratory protocol.

Evidence and source standard

Use NIST, CIAAW, IUPAC, official curriculum sources, or a documented laboratory record. Distinguish a measured spectrum or isotope abundance from an instructional model.

Group 1

Lithium, sodium, and potassium are soft metals with one outer electron; reactivity generally increases down the group.

Group 17

Halogens have seven outer electrons; reactivity generally decreases down the group. Their physical states and colors also change.

Group 18

Noble gases have filled outer levels in the simple model and low ordinary reactivity. Some heavier noble-gas compounds are known, so 'inert' is not absolute.

Portfolio evidence

Graph one sourced property down each group and write a bounded prediction.

Model boundary

Atom and shell diagrams are not literal scale drawings or photographs. They simplify size, distance, probability, and electron behavior.
