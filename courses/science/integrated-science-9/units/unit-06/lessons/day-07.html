Unit 06 · Day 7

Relative Atomic Mass & Abundance

Calculate abundance-weighted atomic masses from real isotope records.

45–60 minutesEvidence and modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Convert percentages to fractions
  • Calculate a weighted mean
  • Distinguish isotope mass, mass number, and standard atomic weight

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table or supplied dataset
  • Calculator where required
Chemistry and radiation safety

Do not handle reactive alkali metals, concentrated halogens, unknown salts, open flames, radioactive minerals, smoke-detector sources, or unknown electrical equipment. Use published data, safe simulations, or a qualified laboratory protocol.

Evidence and source standard

Use NIST, CIAAW, IUPAC, official curriculum sources, or a documented laboratory record. Distinguish a measured spectrum or isotope abundance from an instructional model.

Weighted mean

Multiply each isotope’s relative mass by its fractional abundance and add the products.

Why decimals appear

A periodic-table atomic weight represents an isotope mixture, not the mass number of every individual atom.

Natural variation

CIAAW lists intervals for some elements because isotope composition can vary measurably in normal materials.

Portfolio evidence

Complete chlorine and magnesium calculations, report appropriate rounding, and compare with a reference value.

Model boundary

Atom and shell diagrams are not literal scale drawings or photographs. They simplify size, distance, probability, and electron behavior.
