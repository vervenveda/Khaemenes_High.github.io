Unit 06 · Day 8

Electron Energy Levels & Spectral Evidence

Use line spectra as evidence for quantized electron-energy differences.

45–60 minutesEvidence and modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Explain what a line spectrum records
  • Connect photon energy to electron transitions qualitatively
  • Distinguish evidence from a shell-model picture

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table or supplied dataset
  • Calculator where required
Chemistry and radiation safety

Do not handle reactive alkali metals, concentrated halogens, unknown salts, open flames, radioactive minerals, smoke-detector sources, or unknown electrical equipment. Use published data, safe simulations, or a qualified laboratory protocol.

Evidence and source standard

Use NIST, CIAAW, IUPAC, official curriculum sources, or a documented laboratory record. Distinguish a measured spectrum or isotope abundance from an instructional model.

Characteristic lines

Excited atoms can emit light at specific wavelengths. Different elements produce characteristic patterns.

Energy differences

A photon’s energy corresponds to the difference between allowed electron energy states. The pattern supports quantization.

Limits

A spectrum does not provide a photograph of an electron path. Models infer energy structure from measured wavelengths.

Portfolio evidence

Annotate one published spectrum: direct measurement, pattern, model inference, and uncertainty.

Model boundary

Atom and shell diagrams are not literal scale drawings or photographs. They simplify size, distance, probability, and electron behavior.
