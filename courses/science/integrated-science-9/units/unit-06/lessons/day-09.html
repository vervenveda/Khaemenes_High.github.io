Unit 06 · Day 9

Electron Configurations for Elements 1–20

Represent electrons in simplified shells and connect configurations to atomic number.

45–60 minutesEvidence and modelsPortfolio evidence

Learning objectives

  • Write shell configurations for elements 1–20
  • Check that electron totals equal atomic number for neutral atoms
  • Recognize the limits of the simplified shell model

Materials

  • Notebook or digital record
  • Periodic table or supplied dataset
  • Calculator where required
Chemistry and radiation safety

Do not handle reactive alkali metals, concentrated halogens, unknown salts, open flames, radioactive minerals, smoke-detector sources, or unknown electrical equipment. Use published data, safe simulations, or a qualified laboratory protocol.

Evidence and source standard

Use NIST, CIAAW, IUPAC, official curriculum sources, or a documented laboratory record. Distinguish a measured spectrum or isotope abundance from an instructional model.

Simplified distribution

For the first 20 elements in this course, use shell totals such as 2,8,3. This is a curriculum model for pattern recognition.

Neutrality check

A neutral atom’s electron total equals its proton number.

Beyond the model

Actual electronic structure uses orbitals and quantum states. The shell model is useful but incomplete.

Portfolio evidence

Write and verify configurations for all first-20 elements; mark period and valence count.

Model boundary

Atom and shell diagrams are not literal scale drawings or photographs. They simplify size, distance, probability, and electron behavior.
