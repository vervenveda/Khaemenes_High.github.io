Unit 12 · Day 6

Generating Concepts, Biomimicry & Systems Thinking

Generate diverse solution concepts before selecting or combining them.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Use decomposition, analogy, biomimicry, modularity, passive design, and circular strategies
  • Avoid premature commitment to the first idea
  • Combine strong features from different concepts

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Divergence

Early concept generation values variety before narrowing.

Biomimicry

Biological structures or processes may inspire functions, but analogies must be tested rather than treated as proof.

Systems thinking

A local improvement can shift impacts elsewhere in the system.

Portfolio evidence

Generate at least six concepts and identify the scientific principle behind each.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can concept generation avoid premature fixation and use biology or natural systems responsibly?

Early design should generate multiple concepts before selection. Decomposition can separate a system into functions, and concepts can be mixed across functions. Biomimicry uses principles observed in living systems as inspiration, but copying a biological shape without understanding its mechanism is weak reasoning. Biological analogies must be translated into testable engineering functions.

Worked reasoning example

If the function is passive cooling, a team might explore shading, reflective surfaces, ventilation, thermal mass, evaporative cooling, or combinations. A termite-mound analogy is useful only if the team identifies the airflow/thermal mechanism being borrowed and tests whether it applies at the new scale and climate.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: concept generation · functional decomposition · biomimicry · morphological combination · design fixation · mechanism

Misconception check: Biomimicry is not simply making a product look like an organism.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Generate at least three distinct concepts for one function.
  2. Decompose your system into subfunctions.
  3. Identify one biological principle and the mechanism behind it.
  4. Combine components from two concepts into a hybrid option.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Are your concepts genuinely different or cosmetic variations of one idea?
  • What mechanism makes the biological analogy relevant?
  • Which function has too few alternatives and needs another concept round?
