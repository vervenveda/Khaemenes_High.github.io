Unit 12 · Day 3

Criteria, Constraints, Requirements & Tradeoffs

Translate needs into measurable success criteria and non-negotiable constraints.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish criteria, constraints, assumptions, and preferences
  • Quantify performance targets and verification methods
  • Prioritize criteria without hiding tradeoffs

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Criteria

Criteria are measurable features used to judge how well a solution succeeds.

Constraints

Constraints are limits or requirements such as budget, safety, time, law, materials, access, or environmental protection.

Tradeoffs

Improving one criterion may worsen another; weights and priorities must be transparent.

Portfolio evidence

Produce a numbered specification with at least six criteria and six constraints.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How do criteria, constraints, requirements, and tradeoffs turn a need into an auditable design target?

Criteria describe how success will be judged and should be measurable where possible. Constraints are hard limits that cannot be violated. Requirements translate needs into specific conditions the design must meet. Tradeoffs occur when improving one criterion makes another worse. Good engineering keeps hard constraints separate from weighted preferences.

Worked reasoning example

A portable shelter concept might require mass ≤12 kg, setup time ≤10 min, and no exposed sharp edges as hard requirements. Thermal performance and cost could be weighted criteria. A concept scoring very high on insulation but weighing 18 kg should be rejected if the mass limit is truly non-negotiable, even if its weighted score is otherwise high.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: criterion · constraint · requirement · tradeoff · verification method · threshold

Misconception check: A weighted decision score cannot legitimately override a hard safety or legal constraint.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Classify five design statements as criteria, constraints, or requirements.
  2. Convert one vague goal such as “easy to use” into a measurable criterion.
  3. Explain why a hard constraint should be checked before weighted scoring.
  4. Describe one likely tradeoff in your capstone problem.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Which design condition is truly non-negotiable?
  • How will you verify each requirement objectively?
  • Which criterion is at risk of being weighted simply because it favors your preferred concept?
