Unit 12 · Day 2

Problem Definition, Stakeholders & Need Statements

Convert a broad challenge into a precise, ethical, testable engineering problem.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Identify users, affected communities, maintainers, and decision-makers
  • Write a need statement without embedding a preferred solution
  • Define spatial, temporal, legal, cultural, and ecological boundaries

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Needs and wants

A need statement describes the desired improvement and affected users without assuming one specific technology.

Stakeholders

People may experience benefits, costs, risks, maintenance burdens, or exclusion differently.

Boundaries

A clear design boundary states what the project includes, excludes, controls, and cannot control.

Portfolio evidence

Write a stakeholder map, need statement, system boundary, and conflict-of-interest note.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can a broad challenge be converted into a precise, ethical, testable engineering problem?

A strong problem definition identifies users and stakeholders, the undesirable current condition, the desired improvement, the operating context, and the evidence that the problem exists. It avoids prescribing a favorite solution before alternatives are explored. Need statements should be measurable enough to guide testing and broad enough to allow multiple concepts.

Worked reasoning example

Weak statement: “Build a solar fan.” Stronger statement: “Reduce heat exposure in a small study area for users without grid access while limiting noise, cost, electrical hazards, and material waste.” The stronger statement defines a need and constraints without locking the team into one device.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: need statement · stakeholder · user requirement · problem definition · baseline · scope

Misconception check: A product idea is not the same as an engineering problem definition.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Rewrite a solution-first statement as a need statement.
  2. Identify primary users and at least two other stakeholders.
  3. State one ethical boundary that belongs in the problem definition.
  4. Name one baseline measurement needed before designing.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Whose need is centered in your problem statement?
  • Which stakeholder could experience a cost or risk not visible to the primary user?
  • Have you accidentally chosen the solution before defining the need?
