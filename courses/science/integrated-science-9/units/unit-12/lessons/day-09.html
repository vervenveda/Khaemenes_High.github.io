Unit 12 · Day 9

Prototype Testing, Uncertainty & Optimization Laboratory

Analyze baseline and prototype trial data, improvement, spread, reliability, and pass/fail performance.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Calculate mean, range, percent improvement, and success rate
  • Compare performance with a threshold
  • Distinguish random variation from systematic bias and redesign decisions

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Repeated trials

Multiple trials reveal variation that a single test cannot show.

Improvement

Percent improvement requires an explicit baseline and direction of better performance.

Optimization

The best tested design is not necessarily a universal optimum; it is best within the explored design space and constraints.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Prototype Testing Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can repeated prototype trials distinguish meaningful improvement from normal variation?

Repeated trials reveal variation that one measurement hides. Mean summarizes central performance; range shows spread; percent improvement compares baseline and redesign; success rate measures how often a requirement is met. Optimization should consider both average performance and reliability. A design with excellent mean performance but frequent failures may be unacceptable.

Worked reasoning example

Baseline trial times are 12, 11, 13, 12 s: mean 12.0 s, range 2 s. Prototype times are 9, 8, 10, 9 s: mean 9.0 s, range 2 s. Percent improvement in time = (12−9)/12 ×100%=25%. If requirement is ≤10 s, prototype success rate is 4/4=100%.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: mean · range · percent improvement · success rate · repeatability · systematic bias

Misconception check: A lower mean does not guarantee better reliability if variation or failure rate worsens.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Calculate mean and range for repeated trials.
  2. Calculate percent improvement from baseline to prototype.
  3. Calculate a pass/fail success rate.
  4. Identify one possible systematic bias in a test setup.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Did performance improve consistently or only on average?
  • Which result would change most with one additional trial?
  • What evidence suggests bias rather than random variation?
