Unit 12 · Day 15

Capstone Integration, Exhibition & Course Completion

Present, defend, and reflect on a sustainable engineering solution that integrates the full Grade 9 science course.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Complete all four investigations
  • Pass the mastery quiz and assessment
  • Submit the sustainable engineering capstone and final reflection
  • Connect biology, chemistry, physics, Earth systems, space systems, data, safety, and ethics

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Synthesis

A strong capstone defines the need, system boundary, science basis, criteria, constraints, concepts, model, tests, life cycle, risks, revision, stakeholders, and monitoring plan.

Assessment sequence

Pass four laboratory checks at 7/8, submit the capstone, pass the quiz at 16/20, pass the assessment at 24/30, and complete reflection.

Course completion

Completion records demonstrated evidence; it does not replace local legal, accreditation, credit, or professional requirements.

Portfolio evidence

Submit the capstone portfolio, corrections, constructed responses, presentation, and final course reflection.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: What evidence is required to defend a Grade 9 sustainable-engineering recommendation honestly?

A capstone recommendation should connect the need statement, criteria, constraints, scientific principles, concept generation, decision rationale, test data, uncertainty, life-cycle impacts, risk controls, iteration history, stakeholder impacts, maintenance, monitoring, and end-of-life plan. Strong engineering communication includes alternatives and limitations rather than presenting the selected concept as inevitable.

Worked reasoning example

A final claim such as “Prototype B is preferred” should be followed by evidence: it met all hard constraints, improved the primary performance metric by 25%, achieved 100% success across four trials, had lower lifetime-normalized material impact in the chosen boundary, and reduced the highest-priority risk. The report should also note small trial count, assumed lifetime, and any criteria where another concept performed better.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: technical recommendation · verification · validation · monitoring · maintenance plan · design rationale

Misconception check: A polished presentation is not evidence of engineering quality unless the underlying decisions and tests are reproducible.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Write a one-paragraph recommendation with quantitative evidence.
  2. List three limitations or uncertainties honestly.
  3. Identify one alternative concept and why it was not selected.
  4. Explain how biology, chemistry, physics, Earth science, or data reasoning contributed to the capstone.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Which sentence in your recommendation has the weakest evidence?
  • What limitation should be visible to a decision-maker before adoption?
  • If new monitoring data contradict your prediction, what would trigger redesign?
