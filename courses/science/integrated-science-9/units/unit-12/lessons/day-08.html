Unit 12 · Day 8

Prototypes, Models & Test Plans

Select an appropriate model or prototype and design a fair, safe test.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish sketch, mathematical model, simulation, proof of concept, scale model, and functional prototype
  • Define independent, dependent, and controlled variables
  • Specify trials, instruments, calibration, acceptance thresholds, and stop conditions

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Prototype purpose

A prototype answers a defined design question and is not automatically a deployable product.

A prototype should answer a specific question; it need not resemble the final product in every way.

Fair test

A valid comparison controls relevant variables and uses repeat trials.

Stop conditions

Testing stops when safety, equipment, environmental, animal, human-subject, or legal boundaries are reached.

Portfolio evidence

Write a test plan with hypothesis, variables, trials, measurement resolution, and safety controls.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How should an engineer choose between a mathematical model, simulation, physical prototype, or field test?

Different models answer different questions. Mathematical models are efficient for relationships that can be expressed quantitatively. Simulations can explore many conditions safely. Physical prototypes reveal geometry, human interaction, manufacturing, and unexpected failure modes. Field tests provide realism but can increase risk and reduce control. A test plan should match the uncertainty that matters most.

Worked reasoning example

If the main uncertainty is whether a beam will deflect excessively, a calculation or simulation may screen designs quickly. If the uncertainty is whether users can operate a latch with limited hand strength, a physical prototype and accessibility test are more appropriate.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: prototype · simulation · test plan · independent variable · controlled variable · verification

Misconception check: A more realistic prototype is not always the best first test; early low-cost models can answer focused questions efficiently.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Choose the simplest model capable of answering one design question.
  2. Define a fair comparison between two concepts.
  3. Identify controlled variables in a prototype test.
  4. Write a pass/fail verification rule.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • What uncertainty is your test actually trying to reduce?
  • Could a cheaper model answer the same question first?
  • Which safety boundary must be established before testing?
