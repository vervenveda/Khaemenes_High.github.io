Unit 12 · Day 5

Criteria, Constraints & Decision-Matrix Laboratory

Compare three concepts using weighted criteria while enforcing hard constraints.

45–75 minutesEngineering evidenceSafe capstone boundaries

Learning objectives

  • Normalize criterion weights
  • Calculate weighted scores
  • Reject concepts that fail non-negotiable constraints even if their preference score is high

Materials

  • Notebook or digital portfolio
  • Calculator, supplied records, and low-risk modeling materials
  • Only teacher- or parent-approved prototype materials and procedures
Capstone safety and authorization

No mains electricity, combustion, pressure vessels, structural loading of people, medical testing, human-subject experiments, private-data collection without consent, road testing, water-entry, hazardous chemicals, biological culturing, weapons, drones, lasers, radio transmitters, construction-site access, or deployment into public infrastructure is required. Use models, simulations, supplied data, and low-risk prototypes.

Evidence and accountability standard

Record source, stakeholder, baseline, unit, method, calibration, trial count, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts of interest, system boundary, impacts, and who is authorized to accept residual risk. Distinguish measured, calculated, modeled, estimated, assumed, projected, and preferred values.

Decision matrix

A weighted matrix makes priorities visible but does not replace evidence or stakeholder judgment.

Hard constraints

A design that violates a safety, legal, ethical, or feasibility limit is not acceptable merely because it scores well elsewhere.

Sensitivity

Changing weights can change the ranking, so weight choices should be documented and tested.

Portfolio evidence

Complete the Decision-Matrix Laboratory and its 8-question check.

Model boundary

Decision matrices, prototypes, simulations, life-cycle estimates, risk scores, and optimization results depend on selected boundaries, data quality, assumptions, stakeholder priorities, and explored alternatives. They support decisions but do not certify a product or eliminate professional review.

Essential question and conceptual model

Essential question: How can a decision matrix compare concepts transparently without hiding constraints or bias?

A weighted decision matrix assigns criteria weights and scores concepts against them. Weights should reflect stakeholder priorities before scores are known. Hard constraints should be checked separately. A matrix helps make reasoning visible, but it does not remove judgment; results depend on weights, score definitions, and evidence quality.

Worked reasoning example

Three concepts are scored on performance (weight 0.40), cost (0.25), durability (0.20), and accessibility (0.15), using 1–5 scores. If Concept A scores 5,3,4,2, weighted score = 5(.40)+3(.25)+4(.20)+2(.15)=3.85. If A violates a hard safety constraint, it is still rejected despite the 3.85 score.

Vocabulary and misconception checks

Vocabulary: decision matrix · weight · score · weighted total · hard constraint · sensitivity

Misconception check: A decision matrix is not an objective truth machine; changing weights or score definitions can change the ranking.

Retrieval and explanation practice

Answer from memory first. Then check calculations, requirements, evidence quality, risk assumptions, stakeholder effects, and whether each recommendation is reproducible.

  1. Calculate one weighted concept score.
  2. Check hard constraints before ranking concepts.
  3. Change one weight and observe whether the ranking changes.
  4. Explain why weights should be justified before seeing final totals.

Mentor design review

Bring your need statement, decision matrix, prototype data, risk analysis, or final recommendation to the Science Mentor. The mentor may challenge assumptions and design reasoning but does not replace independent work or protected assessment evidence.

  • Which weight has the greatest influence on your result?
  • Would a reasonable stakeholder choose a different weighting?
  • Does your highest-scoring concept violate any hard limit?
