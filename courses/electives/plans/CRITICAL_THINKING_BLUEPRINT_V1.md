# Critical Thinking — 13-Week Elective Blueprint

**State:** curriculum architecture complete · student production pending  
**Authority:** none until the elective promotion gate passes  
**Target:** Grades 9–12 · 13 weeks · 65 lessons · 80% mastery where scored

## Purpose

Critical Thinking teaches students to inspect claims, evidence, assumptions, alternatives, statistics, sources, persuasion, disagreement, and uncertainty without turning the course into ideological sorting or personality judgment.

The permanent reasoning routine is:

**Question → Claim → Evidence → Assumption → Alternative → Limitation → Conclusion → Revision**

## Weekly arc

1. Claims, Questions & Evidence
2. Reasoning, Assumptions & Warrants
3. Deduction, Induction & Abduction
4. Logical Fallacies & Argument Repair
5. Source Credibility & Lateral Reading
6. Media Literacy, Framing & Persuasion
7. Numbers, Graphs & Statistical Claims
8. Correlation, Causation & Competing Explanations
9. Bias, Heuristics & Decision-Making
10. Disagreement, Steelmanning & Counterargument
11. Ethics, Values & Trade-offs
12. Research Synthesis & Evidence Weight
13. Public Reasoning & Evidence Defense

## Daily contract

Each week uses five recurring moves:

- Day 1 — Retrieve / Notice
- Day 2 — Learn / Model
- Day 3 — Practice / Compare
- Day 4 — Apply / Test
- Day 5 — Revise / Defend

Each lesson should eventually include retrieval, explicit instruction, vocabulary in context, worked reasoning, evidence/source inspection, guided comparison, independent application, uncertainty/limitation, revision, and a short transfer reflection.

## Assessment

- Weekly checks remain open-book.
- Weeks 4, 8 and 12 are cumulative transfer checkpoints.
- Week 13 culminates in an evidence defense rather than a giant recall final.
- Objective/scored components use the Academy 80% threshold.
- Below mastery requires targeted correction followed by a parallel-form check or substantive artifact revision.
- A student must be able to state what evidence would weaken or change a conclusion.

## Neutrality and dignity

The course may use civic, scientific, historical, media, commercial, and everyday claims, but it must not grade political identity, religion, worldview, family background, or conformity to a preferred opinion.

Students are evaluated on:
- accuracy of claim representation;
- source/evidence quality;
- reasoning;
- treatment of alternatives;
- statistical/data literacy;
- uncertainty and limitations;
- counterargument;
- revision.

## Existing resource reuse

Useful supporting material already exists across Language Arts, Mathematics, and Social Studies. Those resources may be adapted or linked during production, but the Critical Thinking course will own its own lesson sequence, assessments, mastery evidence, and records.

There is intentionally **no live course route yet**. Production begins only after this architecture is accepted.
