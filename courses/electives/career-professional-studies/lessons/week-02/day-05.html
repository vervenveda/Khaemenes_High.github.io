2 · Core Lesson

Career research & source credibility

Career information can be distorted by advertising, stereotypes, unusually successful examples, or outdated assumptions. Reliable research compares multiple source types: official occupational data, employer descriptions, professional associations, education providers, worker interviews, and independent reporting. Each source has a purpose and therefore possible limits. A training provider may accurately describe its program while still emphasizing benefits. A salary website may aggregate useful data while hiding variation by region, experience, schedule, or industry. Career research is stronger when claims are cross-checked and when uncertainty is visible. Students should never treat a single salary figure, influencer video, or job posting as a universal description of a career.

4 · Worked Model

Reasoning you can inspect

A learner compares an occupational handbook, two current job postings, and an interview with a worker. They note that all four sources agree on communication demands but disagree on entry credentials, so they record the disagreement rather than hiding it.

Common misconception

If a website looks professional, its career claims are trustworthy.

Correction

Credibility depends on evidence, provenance, purpose, recency, and corroboration—not appearance alone.
