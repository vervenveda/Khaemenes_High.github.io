2 · Core Lesson

Resumes, portfolios & evidence of work

A resume is a concise evidence summary; a portfolio is a curated set of artifacts that can show the work behind the summary. Neither should exaggerate. Strong accomplishment statements describe an action, a context, and an outcome that can be supported. Students may use academic and community evidence when formal work history is limited. A portfolio should preserve provenance: what the artifact is, when it was created, what role the learner had, and what was revised. Design matters, but readability and truthfulness matter more. A polished document that makes unverifiable claims is weaker than a simple document backed by evidence.

4 · Worked Model

Reasoning you can inspect

Rather than “Excellent at research,” write “Compared five sources for a science investigation, documented credibility criteria, and revised the conclusion after finding conflicting evidence.”

Common misconception

A resume should make every experience sound impressive, even if wording stretches the truth.

Correction

Strong resumes translate real evidence into concise language without inventing responsibility or results.

7 · Test & Revise

Stress-test the work

  1. What claim or decision does your artifact make?
  2. What evidence supports it?
  3. What constraint or alternative could change it?
  4. What would a skeptical but fair reviewer ask?
  5. What revision improves accuracy, usefulness, or ethics?
