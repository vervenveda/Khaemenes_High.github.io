Sign Forge

Determine the sign, forge the magnitude, model repeated signed change, and use patterns to explain why integer products and quotients behave the way they do.

Lesson sync: same nonzero signs produce a positive product or quotient; different signs produce a negative result. Operate on magnitudes, then apply the sign rule. Zero times any value is zero; zero divided by a nonzero value is zero; division by zero is undefined.

The sign pattern has only two cases.

Rather than memorizing four unrelated rules, group them by whether the factors have the same sign or different signs.

Same → positive. Different → negative.

Separate magnitude from sign.

Multiplication can mean repeated signed change.

Four hourly decreases of 3°C are four equal changes of −3: −3 + −3 + −3 + −3 = −12.

Odd number of negative factors → negative product

Extended: count negative factors.

Every pair of negative signs contributes a positive sign. So an even number of negative factors gives a positive product; an odd number gives a negative product.

Parity predicts the sign before any multiplication.
