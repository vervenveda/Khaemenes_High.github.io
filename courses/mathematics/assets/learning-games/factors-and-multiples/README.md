The game portion is also broader now. A round mixes:

Factor Pair Builder missions
Students physically construct every rectangle for numbers such as 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, and more. The game detects reversed duplicates, tracks completeness, renders the array, and teaches the square-root stopping rule.

Factor vs. Multiple reasoning
For example, students determine why 8 is a factor of 56 and why 56 is a multiple of 8.

Common Factors and Common Multiples
Students identify shared divisors and shared repeated products.

Factor completeness
Questions explicitly test whether all pairs have been found and why the search can stop after passing √n.

Perfect-square reasoning
Students learn why numbers like 25, 36, and 16 have odd factor counts.

Equal-group applications
Problems connect factors to real grouping situations, matching the lesson’s community/team application.

I also synchronized the three pathways:

Foundation — arrays, straightforward factor pairs, skip-counting, basic common values.
Core — completeness reasoning, square-root boundary, common factors/multiples, perfect squares.
Extended — larger values, deeper factor-count reasoning, synchronization applications, and structural explanations.

The actual lesson explicitly uses those Foundation/Core/Extended support levels.
