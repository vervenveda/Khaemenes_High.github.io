Decimal points align equal place values.

Adding and subtracting decimals is not about lining up the final digits—it is about combining matching base-ten units.

Tenths combine with tenths; hundredths with hundredths.

Scaling both dividend and divisor preserves the quotient.

Multiplying both by the same power of ten produces an equivalent ratio while removing awkward decimal places.

7.2/0.09 = 720/9 = 80.

The reduced denominator predicts the decimal form.

Prime factors 2 and/or 5 fit into powers of ten. Other prime factors create repeating decimal patterns.

Reduced denominator only 2s and 5s → terminating.
1/3 = 0.333…exact repeating notation
2/3 = 0.666…exact
2/3 ≈ 0.67rounded to nearest hundredth
Exact ≠ approximateprecision should match the context

Repeating notation can be exact; rounding is approximate.

Use exact notation when mathematical identity matters. Use an appropriate rounded precision when reporting measurements or practical results.
