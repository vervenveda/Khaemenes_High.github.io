# High School Mathematics · Exceptional Standard v1
**Date:** 2026-11-17

“Exceptional” is an evidence standard, not a label. A course dimension earns Exceptional only when the learner can repeatedly demonstrate it through substantial, varied, recoverable evidence.

## Exceptional dimensions

1. **Conceptual depth** — explanations connect definitions, structure, boundary cases, and why methods work.
2. **Procedural fluency** — sufficient varied practice supports accurate, flexible execution without replacing reasoning.
3. **Reasoning / proof** — learners justify, analyze errors, defend claims, and identify invalid reasoning.
4. **Modelling / application** — authentic problems require assumptions, units, validation, limitations, revision, and communication.
5. **Multiple representations** — symbolic, graphical, tabular, verbal, numerical, spatial, or contextual forms are explicitly connected.
6. **Differentiation** — Foundation / Core / Extended materially change scaffolding, difficulty, quantity, or reasoning demand while preserving the target.
7. **Practice abundance** — there is enough aligned practice to learn without memorizing one form.
8. **Retake validity** — new attempts prioritize unseen or meaningfully varied evidence.
9. **Retention** — delayed retrieval is present between first learning and cumulative exams.
10. **Constructed / defended evidence** — selected response is not the sole proof of mastery where explanation, proof, modelling, or communication matters.
11. **Teacher / evaluator reliability** — scoring criteria are explicit enough that two reasonable evaluators should reach similar judgments.
12. **Learner route clarity** — required, recommended, extension, and recovery work are distinguishable.
13. **Accessibility and cognitive load** — content is rigorous without forcing unnecessary visual, navigation, or resource overload.

## Minimum evidence expectations

- Mastery threshold remains **80%**.
- Missing evidence is never treated as zero or mastery.
- Retakes preserve prior attempts and corrections.
- High-stakes forms should not be trivially memorized from a tiny pool.
- Major function / geometry / statistics concepts use multiple representations.
- Projects require validation and limitations, not decorative “real-world” context.
- Course breadth must not outrun consolidation and retention.

## Current use

Algebra II Unit 01 is the first implementation pilot built explicitly against this standard. The same benchmark will be used to upgrade Algebra II Units 02–13, then to close the smaller Pre-Algebra and Geometry gaps identified in the learning-depth audit. Algebra I should be protected from unnecessary scope expansion and refined only where the benchmark reveals a real evidence gap.
