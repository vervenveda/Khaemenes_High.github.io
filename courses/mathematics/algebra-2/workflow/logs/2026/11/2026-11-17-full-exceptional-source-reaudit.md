## Executive result

Algebra II now presents one coherent 36-week architecture from readiness through capstone:

- Week 1: Algebra I readiness diagnostic and bridge planning.
- Weeks 2–17: Units 01–06.
- Week 18: midpoint synthesis, corrections, portfolio review, and 60-question midterm.
- Weeks 19–35: Units 07–12.
- Week 36: Unit 13 advanced modelling capstone, oral defense, comprehensive final, and record review.

The course contains 13 units, 92 detailed lessons, 180 planned study sessions, three differentiated pathways, 13 analytic applied investigations, a 60-question midterm, a 100-question comprehensive final, and a 40-question readiness diagnostic.

## Lesson depth and representation

All 92 lessons have a dedicated depth layer in addition to the core lesson architecture.

- Unit 01 uses the Unit 01 depth companion.
- Units 02–03 use `algebra2-exceptional-lesson-depth-units02-03-v1.js`.
- Units 04–05 use the established exceptional lesson-depth layer.
- Units 06–07, 08–09, 10–11, and 12–13 use their dedicated exceptional lesson-depth assets.

Exceptional lesson extensions add varied worked examples, representation changes, proof/justification, transfer, and retrieval bridges. These assets are instructional only and do not write grades, progression, mastery, or learner records.

## Differentiation

Every unit now uses the same explicit three-pathway practice architecture:

- Foundation: 12 items.
- Core: 16 items.
- Extended: 20 items.

Pathways are bound explicitly in assessment configuration rather than being labels only.

Unit 13 has five lessons and therefore ten explicit difficulty-1 depth questions. The assessment engine now supplements a short Foundation pool only with legacy unclassified/basic course-bank items from the same unit. It does not raise the learner into Core or Extended content merely to fill the requested 12-item Foundation form.

## Unit mastery

All 13 unit mastery checks now contain 20 questions and use the five-dimension depth blueprint.

Formal mastery remains **80%**.

Retakes are novelty-aware and prioritize questions not used on the immediately prior form. Correction evidence remains part of the mastery process.

## Cumulative examinations

The 60-question midterm now uses both:
- unit balance across Units 01–06; and
- five-dimension reasoning balance.

The 100-question final now uses both:
- unit balance across all 13 units; and
- five-dimension reasoning balance.

The assessment engine includes a combined `balancedDepthByUnit` selection path so cumulative examinations do not have to choose between content coverage and reasoning coverage.

## Retrieval and retention

The retrieval metadata and student-visible labels are reconciled to the official 36-week pacing calendar.

Instructional retrieval covers Weeks 2–17 and 19–36. Week 1 is readiness/bridge planning and Week 18 is the midpoint milestone.

Retrieval forms are non-gating. They deliberately mix current learning with delayed prior learning and do not unlock or block progression.

Legacy retrieval filenames retain the original sequence-week number for backward compatibility, while displayed labels, record keys, question week metadata, teacher guides, and retrieval mixes use the official calendar.

## Applied investigations and modelling

All 13 units retain a 100-point analytic investigation rubric with explicit evidence for:

- question, context, and ethics;
- mathematical structure;
- computation and technology;
- validation and uncertainty;
- communication and defense;
- revision and reflection.

The Unit 13 capstone explicitly includes ethical question framing, data provenance, model selection, reproducibility, holdout/residual validation, sensitivity, fairness, uncertainty, extrapolation limits, oral defense, and documented revision.

## Readiness and pacing

The 40-question readiness diagnostic samples Algebra I prerequisites and is explicitly used for bridge planning rather than permanent learner labeling.

The canonical course data contains a complete 36-week map from Week 1 through Week 36. The teacher pacing guide and curriculum map agree with that schedule.

## Learner record and storage resilience

The shared High School course-entry contract now loads the canonical mathematics hardening layer on Pre-Algebra, Algebra I, Geometry, and Algebra II routes.

The hardening chain loads:

1. verified storage adapter;
2. canonical learner record;
3. read-only progress contract;
4. visible storage-health layer;
5. evidence authority;
6. progression ledger.

Persistent writes are not reported as saved until read-back verification succeeds. When persistence cannot be verified, the system exposes memory-only/read-only state and recommends export.

The completion-record page now uses the verified storage adapter when available, verifies fallback local writes, and captures completion evidence into the canonical recovery record.

## Evidence authority and progression integrity

The evidence layer distinguishes practice, reviewed, mastered, retained, pending-review, and evaluator-verified states.

Missing evidence is not silently converted to a zero or a mastery result.

Legacy unscoped evidence may be preserved for recovery, but it is not treated as proof of learner ownership.

Browser-local evidence is explicitly distinguished from an official transcript and from Account Cloud synchronization.

## Accessibility and readability

The Algebra II shared stylesheet now enforces a 16px minimum readability floor across body text, controls, labels, summaries, metadata, and category eyebrows, including print output.

This higher-specificity course rule supersedes the historical embedded 14px body / 55px eyebrow presentation policy without changing assessment logic.

Shared compatibility guards provide responsive single-column fallbacks, minimum touch targets, overflow protection, and tablet-landscape safeguards.
