Angle Measure, Radians & Coterminal Angles

Radians measure angle by arc length relative to radius. Coterminal angles differ by full rotations, and reference angles connect positions to exact values.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of angle measure, radians & coterminal angles using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do angle measure, the unit circle, graphs, and periodic functions model rotation, waves, cycles, and repeated behavior?

Governing relationship

180°=π radians; s=rθ for θ in radians.

Radians measure angle by arc length relative to radius. Coterminal angles differ by full rotations, and reference angles connect positions to exact values.

Worked examples

Read each example, close it, and reconstruct the reasoning independently. Then verify by substitution, graph, table, or alternate method.

Reasoning workshop · Radians, coterminal rays and arc length

Study the governing rule, follow every step, and verify the conclusion before attempting independent work.

Investigation A · Convert 225° and list two coterminal angles.

  1. Multiply by π/180 to obtain 225π/180=5π/4 radians.
  2. Add or subtract 360° to obtain 585° and −135°; all three rays coincide.
  3. Subtract 2π from 5π/4 to check that −3π/4 is a coterminal radian angle.

Verification: Angles separated by 360° (or 2π) share a terminal ray.

Investigation B · Find arc length for r=12 and central angle 5π/4.

  1. The formula s=rθ requires radians; 5π/4 already has the correct units.
  2. s=12(5π/4)=15π distance units.
  3. The arc is 5/8 of a full revolution; (5/8)(24π)=15π.

Verification: The answer lies between a semicircle's 12π and a full circumference's 24π.

Concept diagram · Radians, coterminal rays and arc length

Independent transfer · check after solving

Complete these without the game or answer key first. Give exact values where possible and show a short verification.

  1. Convert 330° to radians.
  2. Normalize −30° and 870° into [0°,360°).
  3. Find arc length for r=6 and θ=5π/3.
Reveal independent answer reasoning
  1. 330°=11π/6 radians.
  2. −30°→330°; 870°−720°=150°.
  3. s=6(5π/3)=10π distance units.

Misconception to correct: Substituting θ=225 into s=rθ as though 225 were radians gives an arc longer than the entire circle. Convert degrees first.

Compare your solution to the verified reasoning, correct one mistaken step, and then use optional game practice. Official grading stays separate.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Using s=rθ with θ measured in degrees.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.

Mastery evidence

  • Correct domain, interval, unit, and precision statements.
  • At least one independent verification.
  • A correction explaining why an incorrect approach fails.
