The Unit Circle & Coordinate Definitions

The unit circle defines sine and cosine as coordinates and all other trigonometric functions as ratios. Symmetry and reference angles reduce memorization.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of the unit circle & coordinate definitions using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do angle measure, the unit circle, graphs, and periodic functions model rotation, waves, cycles, and repeated behavior?

Governing relationship

Point at angle θ is (cosθ,sinθ).

The unit circle defines sine and cosine as coordinates and all other trigonometric functions as ratios. Symmetry and reference angles reduce memorization.

Worked examples

Read each example, close it, and reconstruct the reasoning independently. Then verify by substitution, graph, table, or alternate method.

At θ=2π/3, coordinates are (−1/2,√3/2).Thus cosθ=−1/2 and sinθ=√3/2.
At θ=3π/2.Coordinates (0,−1); tangent is undefined.

Reasoning workshop · Unit-circle coordinates and quotient restrictions

Study the governing rule, follow every step, and verify the conclusion before attempting independent work.

Investigation A · Find exact coordinates and tangent at θ=2π/3.

  1. 2π/3=120° is in Quadrant II, reference angle 60°.
  2. The absolute coordinate magnitudes are (1/2,√3/2). In Quadrant II cosine is negative.
  3. Therefore P=(−1/2,√3/2) and tanθ=sinθ/cosθ=−√3.

Verification: Check (−1/2)²+(√3/2)²=1.

Investigation B · Identify undefined ratios at θ=3π/2.

  1. The unit-circle point at 270° is P=(0,−1).
  2. Thus sinθ=−1 and cosθ=0.
  3. tanθ=sinθ/cosθ=−1/0 is undefined, not zero.

Verification: The point exists even when a coordinate quotient does not.

Concept diagram · Unit-circle coordinates and quotient restrictions

Independent transfer · check after solving

Complete these without the game or answer key first. Give exact values where possible and show a short verification.

  1. Give the coordinate at θ=7π/6.
  2. Compute sin(−π/3) and cos(−π/3).
  3. Explain why tan(π/2) is undefined.
Reveal independent answer reasoning
  1. P=(−√3/2,−1/2) in Quadrant III.
  2. sin(−π/3)=−√3/2; cos(−π/3)=1/2.
  3. cos(π/2)=0; tan(π/2)=1/0 has no real value.

Misconception to correct: The first coordinate is cosine, not sine. Swapping them can produce incorrect quadrant signs and invalid tangent ratios.

Compare your solution to the verified reasoning, correct one mistaken step, and then use optional game practice. Official grading stays separate.

Guided and independent practice

  1. Find coordinates at standard angles.
  2. Determine signs by quadrant.
  3. Use symmetry for related angles.
  4. Create one original example involving the unit circle & coordinate definitions and solve it completely.
  5. Use the Khaemenes Scientific Calculator to verify one result, then document the setup and reasonableness check.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Swapping sine and cosine coordinates.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.
