Inverse Trigonometric Functions & Restricted Domains

arcsin, arccos, and arctan return principal angles. They reverse trig functions only within specified ranges.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of inverse trigonometric functions & restricted domains using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do angle measure, the unit circle, graphs, and periodic functions model rotation, waves, cycles, and repeated behavior?

Governing relationship

Inverse trig functions use restricted domains to be one-to-one.

arcsin, arccos, and arctan return principal angles. They reverse trig functions only within specified ranges.

Worked examples

Read each example, close it, and reconstruct the reasoning independently. Then verify by substitution, graph, table, or alternate method.

Reasoning workshop · Inverse principal values versus equation roots

Study the governing rule, follow every step, and verify the conclusion before attempting independent work.

Investigation A · Evaluate arcsin(1/2) and solve a related equation.

  1. arcsin(1/2)=π/6 because π/6 lies in the principal range [−π/2,π/2].
  2. For sinθ=1/2 on [0,2π), sine is positive in Quadrants I and II.
  3. Thus the full interval solutions are π/6 and 5π/6.

Verification: sin(π/6)=sin(5π/6)=1/2.

Investigation B · Distinguish cosine and tangent principal ranges.

  1. arccos(−√2/2)=3π/4 in the range [0,π].
  2. arctan(−1)=−π/4 in the range (−π/2,π/2).
  3. Solving tanθ=−1 on [0,2π) yields 3π/4 and 7π/4.

Verification: The inverse's principal answer is not necessarily an interval solution.

Independent transfer · check after solving

Complete these without the game or answer key first. Give exact values where possible and show a short verification.

  1. Evaluate arcsin(sin(5π/6)).
  2. Solve tanθ=−1 on [0,2π).
  3. Evaluate arccos(cos(5π/3)).
Reveal independent answer reasoning
  1. π/6, the principal arcsine value.
  2. θ=3π/4 and 7π/4.
  3. π/3, the principal arccosine value.

Misconception to correct: sin⁻¹ denotes inverse function, not 1/sin. Cosecant is sine's reciprocal.

Compare your solution to the verified reasoning, correct one mistaken step, and then use optional game practice. Official grading stays separate.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Treating inverse trig notation as reciprocal trig notation.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.
