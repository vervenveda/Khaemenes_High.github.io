Trigonometric Functions & Periodic Models

How do angle measure, the unit circle, graphs, and periodic functions model rotation, waves, cycles, and repeated behavior?

Detailed Lessons

Lessons include objectives, formulas, concept development, worked examples, guided and independent practice, differentiated pathways, application, misconception analysis, mastery evidence, reflection, and local completion.

Lesson 01

Angle Measure, Radians & Coterminal Angles

Radians measure angle by arc length relative to radius. Coterminal angles differ by full rotations, and reference angles connect positions to exact values.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 02

The Unit Circle & Coordinate Definitions

The unit circle defines sine and cosine as coordinates and all other trigonometric functions as ratios. Symmetry and reference angles reduce memorization.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 03

Exact Trigonometric Values & Reference Angles

Exact trigonometric values come from special triangles and unit-circle symmetry. Keep radicals exact unless approximation is requested.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 04

Six Trigonometric Functions & Reciprocal Relationships

The six trigonometric functions are connected by quotient and reciprocal relationships. Undefined values occur when denominators are zero.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 05

Graphs of Sine and Cosine

Sine and cosine graphs encode periodic change. Key points at quarter-period intervals support accurate sketches and interpretation.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 06

Amplitude, Period, Phase & Vertical Shifts

A controls vertical scale, B controls period, C phase shift, and D midline. Context determines which features have physical meaning.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 07

Graphs of Tangent, Cotangent, Secant & Cosecant

Tangent and cotangent have vertical asymptotes and no amplitude. Secant and cosecant are reciprocal graphs with branches outside |y|≥1.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 08

Inverse Trigonometric Functions & Restricted Domains

arcsin, arccos, and arctan return principal angles. They reverse trig functions only within specified ranges.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 09

Sinusoidal Regression & Periodic Data

Periodic regression estimates amplitude, cycle length, phase, and midline. Data span should cover enough cycles to support the model.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 10

Vectors, Bearings & Component Models

Vectors represent magnitude and direction. Components add, scalar multiplication changes magnitude and possibly direction, and bearings require careful angle conventions.
