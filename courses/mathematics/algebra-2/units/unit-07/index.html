Lesson 01

Logarithms as Inverses of Exponentials

A logarithm answers an exponent question. Domain requires x>0, and base requires b>0, b≠1.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 02

Common, Natural & Other Logarithmic Bases

Common and natural logarithms are standard tools, but any valid base may be used. Base choice reflects context or computational convenience.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 03

Product, Quotient & Power Properties

Logarithm properties come from exponent laws and apply only to valid positive arguments. They expand products and powers or condense sums and differences.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 04

Solving Exponential Equations with Logarithms

Logarithms solve exponential equations with unmatched bases. Keep exact log forms before approximating and interpret time or quantity units.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 05

Solving Logarithmic Equations & Domain Checks

Use log properties or convert to exponential form. Algebraic candidates may violate the domain, so substitution into the original is mandatory.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 06

Change of Base, Technology & Estimation

The change-of-base formula lets technology evaluate any valid logarithm and supports mental estimation by comparing nearby powers.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 07

Logarithmic Scales, Data & Model Interpretation

Log scales describe orders of magnitude, sound intensity, acidity, and other multiplicative phenomena. Differences on a log scale represent ratios in the original quantity.
