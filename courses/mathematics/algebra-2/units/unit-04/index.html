Rational Expressions, Equations & Functions

How do excluded values, asymptotes, holes, and rates of change shape rational expressions and models?

Detailed Lessons

Lessons include objectives, formulas, concept development, worked examples, guided and independent practice, differentiated pathways, application, misconception analysis, mastery evidence, reflection, and local completion.

Lesson 01

Simplifying Rational Expressions & Restrictions

Rational expressions simplify by factoring numerator and denominator and canceling common nonzero factors. Original denominator restrictions remain even after cancellation.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 02

Multiplying and Dividing Rational Expressions

Factoring before multiplication often exposes common factors and reduces work. Division means multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor, with all original and reciprocal restrictions recorded.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 03

Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions

Rational terms add only after they share a denominator. Factor denominators first, construct the least common denominator, and combine numerators carefully.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 04

Solving Rational Equations & Extraneous Solutions

Clearing denominators converts a rational equation to an algebraic equation, but candidate solutions must be checked against the original equation and its restrictions.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 05

Reciprocal Functions, Asymptotes & Holes

A rational graph’s domain, intercepts, holes, vertical asymptotes, and end behavior come from its factored structure. Horizontal or slant asymptotes describe long-run behavior, not forbidden lines.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 06

Transformations and Features of Rational Functions

Transformed reciprocal functions use h and k to locate asymptotes and a/b to control orientation and scale. Feature analysis should be tied to the original equation.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 07

Direct, Inverse, Joint & Combined Variation

Variation equations encode proportional structure. Determine k from known values, preserve units, and test whether the model form is reasonable.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 08

Rational Models, Rates & Work Problems

Rational models arise when rates, densities, resistances, or per-unit quantities combine. Define quantities and units before manipulating equations.

Open detailed lesson
