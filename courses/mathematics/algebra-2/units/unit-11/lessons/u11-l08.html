Unit 11 · Lesson 08 · Open-Age

Navigation, Vectors & Multi-Step Triangle Models

Multi-step trigonometric models require a diagram, angle convention, units, and reasonableness checks. Rounding should be delayed.

← Previous LessonUnit 11 HomeNext Lesson →

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of navigation, vectors & multi-step triangle models using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do identities and triangle laws transform expressions, solve periodic equations, and determine unknown measurements?

Governing relationship

Combine bearings, vectors, triangle laws, and area formulas.

Multi-step trigonometric models require a diagram, angle convention, units, and reasonableness checks. Rounding should be delayed.

Worked examples

First decide whether you are comparing two endpoints from one origin or combining successive travel legs. The models are different. A compass bearing is measured clockwise from north.

Investigation A · Direct separation between two endpoints.
  1. Two straight routes leave the same origin, with lengths 12 km and 18 km and an included angle of 70° between them.
  2. The segment between their endpoints is opposite the included 70° angle, so d²=12²+18²−2(12)(18)cos70°.
  3. Calculate d²=468−432cos70°≈320.25, hence d≈17.90 km.
  4. Reasonableness check: |18−12|<17.90<18+12, i.e. 6<d<30. A diagram and triangle inequality support the result.

Interpretation: This is the distance between two destination points, not the sum of two consecutive travel legs. Do not assume 12+18=30 km is the straight-line separation.

Investigation B · Add successive compass legs by components.
  1. Travel 12 km on bearing N30°E, then 8 km due east. N30°E means clockwise 30° from north, not a standard x-axis angle of 30°.
  2. Use east and north components: leg 1 has east=12sin30°=6 and north=12cos30°=6√3≈10.39 km. Leg 2 has east=8 and north=0.
  3. Combine: total east=6+8=14 km, total north=6√3 km.
  4. Resultant straight-line distance is √[14²+(6√3)²]=√304=4√19≈17.44 km.
  5. Its bearing clockwise from north is atan2(east,north)=atan2(14,6√3)≈53.41° (equivalently N53.41°E).
  6. Verification: resolving 17.44 km at bearing 53.41° returns approximately 14 km east and 10.39 km north.

Model limit: Bearings are idealized as flat-plane directions here; over long distances, Earth curvature, terrain, and navigation corrections must be considered.

Visual model · two-leg bearing displacement

Guided and independent practice

  1. Draw a north arrow and label 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west before calculating a bearing.
  2. For two straight routes from a common start of 6 km and 8 km separated by 60°, compute the distance between endpoints using the Law of Cosines.
  3. For a successive route of 10 km north then 10 km east, find resultant magnitude and initial bearing from north.
  4. For 6 km N60°E followed by 4 km south, find signed east/north components, displacement, and bearing. Explain the quadrant.
  5. Compare your methods in Coordinated Mission Control and Triangle Law Expedition; use your private note to describe why the angle convention changes the components.

Independent transfer · check only after solving

  1. Two endpoints are 6 km and 8 km from the same point, with a 60° included angle. What is their separation?
  2. Travel 10 km due north then 10 km due east. Give exact resultant length and compass bearing.
  3. Travel 6 km N60°E then 4 km due south. Calculate signed components and the resultant magnitude; is the final bearing north or south of east?
Reveal worked verification and answers
  1. d²=6²+8²−2×6×8cos60°=52, so d=2√13≈7.21 km. These are endpoints, not consecutive legs.
  2. East=10, north=10; resultant=√200=10√2≈14.14 km, bearing=45° clockwise from north.
  3. East=6sin60°=3√3≈5.20 km, north=6cos60°−4=−1 km; distance=√(27+1)=2√7≈5.29 km. Bearing≈100.89° clockwise from north, so the final displacement is south of east.

Differentiated pathways

Foundation

Review prerequisite vocabulary, annotate each worked step, complete the first four prompts, and use a table or graph to support symbolic work.

Core

Complete every prompt, explain domain and verification, correct errors, and submit the applied task with clear mathematical communication.

Extended

Generalize the relationship, compare methods, use technology strategically, analyze sensitivity, and create a proof, counterexample, or advanced model.

Application laboratory

Defend a navigation, surveying, force, or accessibility model.

Required evidence: defined quantities and units; at least two representations; transparent calculations; verification; one limitation; and a defensible conclusion.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Using an unlabeled sketch and losing track of which angle is included or opposite.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.

Mastery evidence

  • Accurate symbolic work with preserved equivalence.
  • Correct domain, interval, unit, and precision statements.
  • At least one independent verification.
  • A correction explaining why an incorrect approach fails.
  • A clear application conclusion that distinguishes mathematical result from recommendation.

Reflection

What structure did you recognize? Which representation was most useful? What assumption or restriction mattered most? What will you do differently on the next problem?
