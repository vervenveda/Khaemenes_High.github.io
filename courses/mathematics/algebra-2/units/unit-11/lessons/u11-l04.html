Double-Angle and Half-Angle Identities

Double- and half-angle identities transform frequency and powers. Choose the form matching available information and domain.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of double-angle and half-angle identities using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do identities and triangle laws transform expressions, solve periodic equations, and determine unknown measurements?

Governing relationship

sin2x=2sinx cosx; cos2x has several equivalent forms.

Worked examples

Read each example, close it, and reconstruct the reasoning independently. Then verify by substitution, graph, table, or alternate method.

Reasoning workshop · Double angles and signed half angles

Study the governing rule, follow every step, and verify the conclusion before attempting independent work.

Investigation A · Verify sin2x when x=π/6.

  1. Directly, sin(2x)=sin(π/3)=√3/2.
  2. By the identity, 2sin(π/6)cos(π/6)=2(1/2)(√3/2)=√3/2.
  3. The methods agree, giving a check on the identity.

Verification: At x=0, both methods also produce 0.

Investigation B · Find cos(θ/2) for θ=240°.

  1. θ/2=120° lies in Quadrant II, so cosine must be negative.
  2. cosθ=−1/2; the half-angle magnitude √[(1+cosθ)/2]=√[(1−1/2)/2]=1/2.
  3. Choose the sign from the half-angle quadrant: cos120°=−1/2.

Verification: Unit-circle cosine at 120° verifies the negative value.

Concept diagram · Double angles and signed half angles

Independent transfer · check after solving

Complete these without the game or answer key first. Give exact values where possible and show a short verification.

  1. Evaluate sin2x at x=π/6.
  2. If sinx=5/13, find cos2x using 1−2sin²x.
  3. For θ=300°, use the half-angle rule to find sin(θ/2).
Reveal independent answer reasoning
  1. sinπ/3=√3/2.
  2. 1−2(25/169)=119/169.
  3. θ/2=150° is in Quadrant II: sin(θ/2)=+√[(1−cos300°)/2]=+1/2.

Misconception to correct: Half-angle square-root formulas give absolute value until the quadrant fixes the required sign.

Compare your solution to the verified reasoning, correct one mistaken step, and then use optional game practice. Official grading stays separate.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Forgetting the ± sign in half-angle square-root forms.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.
