Fundamental, Reciprocal & Pythagorean Identities

Fundamental identities follow from the unit circle and reciprocal or quotient definitions. They permit equivalent rewrites and simplification.

01 · Recall

Recall the prerequisite structure

Before starting Fundamental, Reciprocal & Pythagorean Identities, identify the identity family, interval, known triangle data, side–angle correspondence, or vector/navigation constraints that control the problem.

Check

Name the relationship, quantities, and restrictions that must remain valid before calculating.

02 · See

See the governing relationship

sin²x+cos²x=1; 1+tan²x=sec²x; 1+cot²x=csc²x.

Fundamental identities follow from the unit circle and reciprocal or quotient definitions. They permit equivalent rewrites and simplification.

03 · Worked

Follow complete examples

Simplify (1−sin²x)/cos x.cos²x/cos x=cos x where cos x≠0.
If tan x=3/4, find sec²x.1+9/16=25/16.

Reasoning habit: preserve equivalence and match every side with its opposite angle before selecting a method.

04 · Explain

Explain why the method works

Connect identities, equivalent transformations, interval restrictions, triangle laws, ambiguity conditions, or vector geometry to the conclusion, and justify every transformation.

05 · Guided

Try one with support

Use a Pythagorean identity.

  1. Identify the controlling identity, interval, triangle relationship, or vector model.
  2. Carry out one justified step at a time.
  3. Verify with identity substitution, interval checks, triangle consistency, alternate formulas, or vector/component reconstruction.

06 · Independent

Complete the practice independently

  1. Rewrite with sine and cosine.
  2. State denominator restrictions.
  3. Create one original example involving fundamental, reciprocal & pythagorean identities and solve it completely.
  4. Use the Khaemenes Scientific Calculator to verify one result, then document the setup and reasonableness check.

07 · Error

Find and repair the misconception

Common misconception: Canceling terms across a sum instead of factoring or using an identity.

Locate the first invalid step, name the identity, condition, or theorem that corrects it, and verify the repair.

Choose identities strategically to simplify a model expression.
