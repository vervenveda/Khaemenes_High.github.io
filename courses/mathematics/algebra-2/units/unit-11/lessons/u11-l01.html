Fundamental, Reciprocal & Pythagorean Identities

Fundamental identities follow from the unit circle and reciprocal or quotient definitions. They permit equivalent rewrites and simplification.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the central structure of fundamental, reciprocal & pythagorean identities using precise mathematical language.
  • Apply the governing relationship and verify results with an alternate representation or substitution.
  • State domain, unit, precision, and context restrictions whenever they affect the result.
  • Analyze a misconception and communicate a correction with evidence.

Essential question: How do identities and triangle laws transform expressions, solve periodic equations, and determine unknown measurements?

Governing relationship

sin²x+cos²x=1; 1+tan²x=sec²x; 1+cot²x=csc²x.

Fundamental identities follow from the unit circle and reciprocal or quotient definitions. They permit equivalent rewrites and simplification.

Worked examples

Read each example, close it, and reconstruct the reasoning independently. Then verify by substitution, graph, table, or alternate method.

Simplify (1−sin²x)/cos x.cos²x/cos x=cos x where cos x≠0.
If tan x=3/4, find sec²x.1+9/16=25/16.

Reasoning workshop · Pythagorean identities and domain preservation

Study the governing rule, follow every step, and verify the conclusion before attempting independent work.

Investigation A · Simplify (1−sin²x)/cosx without losing restrictions.

  1. Use sin²x+cos²x=1, so the numerator becomes cos²x.
  2. Then cos²x/cosx=cosx, provided cosx≠0.
  3. State the original excluded inputs x=π/2+kπ even though cosx itself is defined there.

Verification: At x=0 the original equals 1 and the simplified form cos0=1.

Investigation B · Use a tangent ratio to find secant squared.

  1. Given tanx=3/4, square: tan²x=9/16.
  2. By 1+tan²x=sec²x, sec²x=25/16.
  3. Therefore |secx|=5/4. The sign of secx requires more information about the quadrant.

Verification: Squared secant is positive regardless of the sign of cosine.

Concept diagram · Pythagorean identities and domain preservation

Independent transfer · check after solving

Complete these without the game or answer key first. Give exact values where possible and show a short verification.

  1. Simplify (sec²x−1)/tanx and state restrictions.
  2. If sinx=5/13 in Quadrant I, find cosx and secx.
  3. Does sin²x+cos²x=1 hold at x=π/2?
Reveal independent answer reasoning
  1. It equals tanx where cosx≠0 and tanx≠0; retain both original restrictions.
  2. cosx=12/13, secx=13/12.
  3. Yes. sin²(π/2)=1 and cos²(π/2)=0; the identity remains true even where some quotients fail.

Misconception to correct: Canceling a term across addition is invalid; use identities to factor first, and retain all original denominator restrictions.

Compare your solution to the verified reasoning, correct one mistaken step, and then use optional game practice. Official grading stays separate.

Guided and independent practice

  1. Use a Pythagorean identity.
  2. Rewrite with sine and cosine.
  3. State denominator restrictions.
  4. Create one original example involving fundamental, reciprocal & pythagorean identities and solve it completely.
  5. Use the Khaemenes Scientific Calculator to verify one result, then document the setup and reasonableness check.
  6. Write a one-paragraph explanation connecting symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.

Differentiated pathways

Foundation

Review prerequisite vocabulary, annotate each worked step, complete the first four prompts, and use a table or graph to support symbolic work.

Core

Complete every prompt, explain domain and verification, correct errors, and submit the applied task with clear mathematical communication.

Extended

Generalize the relationship, compare methods, use technology strategically, analyze sensitivity, and create a proof, counterexample, or advanced model.

Application laboratory

Choose identities strategically to simplify a model expression.

Required evidence: defined quantities and units; at least two representations; transparent calculations; verification; one limitation; and a defensible conclusion.

Misconception and correction

Common misconception: Canceling terms across a sum instead of factoring or using an identity.

Write the first invalid step, the governing rule that corrects it, and one counterexample or verification that demonstrates the correction.

Mastery evidence

  • Accurate symbolic work with preserved equivalence.
  • Correct domain, interval, unit, and precision statements.
  • At least one independent verification.
  • A correction explaining why an incorrect approach fails.
  • A clear application conclusion that distinguishes mathematical result from recommendation.

Reflection
