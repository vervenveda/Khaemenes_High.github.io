Lesson 01

Rational Exponents & Radical Equivalence

Rational exponents unify roots and powers. Exponent properties remain valid when expressions are defined, but even roots require care in the real numbers.

Lesson 02

Simplifying Radical Expressions

Simplifying a radical means factoring out perfect powers while preserving absolute-value conditions where required, such as √(x²)=|x|.

Lesson 03

Operations with Radicals & Rationalizing

Radical addition requires like radicands after simplification. Multiplication uses distribution, and conjugates rationalize binomial radical denominators.

Lesson 04

Solving Radical Equations & Checking Solutions

Powering both sides may introduce extraneous solutions. Every candidate must be substituted into the original radical equation.

Open detailed lesson

Lesson 05

Radical Functions, Domains & Transformations

Radical graphs begin at or approach a domain boundary. Transformations determine the endpoint, orientation, and scale, while inverse relationships connect radicals and powers.

Lesson 06

Radical Models, Geometry & Measurement Error

Radical models require exact structure, dimensional consistency, and sensitivity awareness because measurement error can be amplified or reduced by roots.
