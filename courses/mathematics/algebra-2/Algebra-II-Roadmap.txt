Khaemenes High School — Algebra II Roadmap

Course ID: KH-MATH-A2
Length: 36 weeks
Mastery target: 80%
Architecture: 13 units
Pathways: Foundation · Core · Extended
Course philosophy: concept → representation → reasoning → practice → application → mastery → remediation

13-unit academic map
Unit	Approx. Weeks	Core Study
01 · Algebra II Foundations & Function Language	1–2	Algebra I diagnostic bridge, expressions, equations, inequalities, function notation, domain/range, transformations
02 · Linear Functions, Systems & Advanced Modeling	3–5	Linear models, systems, inequalities, matrices as system tools, regression, constraints, optimization
03 · Quadratic Functions & Complex Numbers	6–8	Parabolas, forms of quadratics, factoring, completing square, quadratic formula, discriminant, complex solutions
04 · Polynomial Functions	9–11	Polynomial structure, operations, graphs, zeros, multiplicity, division, remainder/factor theorems, Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
05 · Rational Expressions & Rational Functions	12–14	Rational expressions, equations, restrictions, asymptotes, holes, graph behavior, inverse variation and modeling
06 · Radical Functions & Rational Exponents	15–16	Radicals, rational exponents, radical equations, extraneous solutions, transformations and models
07 · Exponential Functions	17–19	Growth/decay, compound interest, continuous growth, half-life, transformations, model selection
08 · Logarithmic Functions	20–22	Logs as inverses, properties, equations, exponential/logarithmic modeling, scales and applications
09 · Sequences, Series & Recursive Models	23–25	Arithmetic/geometric sequences, recursive/explicit rules, sigma notation, finite/infinite geometric series
10 · Advanced Function Analysis & Inverses	26–27	Composition, inverse functions, one-to-one behavior, piecewise functions, transformations, model comparison
11 · Trigonometric Foundations & Functions	28–30	Radians, unit circle, sine/cosine/tangent, graphs, transformations, basic identities and equations
12 · Probability, Statistics & Data Modeling	31–33	Conditional probability, independence, counting, distributions, expected value, regression and interpreting evidence
13 · Integrated Mathematical Modeling & Capstone	34–36	Multi-family model selection, data, uncertainty, financial/scientific/social applications, portfolio, oral/written defense

That gives us a genuine Algebra II progression rather than simply “Algebra I with harder questions.”

Assessment architecture

I would carry forward the strongest system we've developed:

Every lesson

substantive instructional reading;
worked examples;
visual/model representations;
misconception checks;
20 meaningful practice problems where appropriate;
Foundation/Core/Extended routing;
printable work;
local progress evidence.

Every unit

lessons;
vocabulary;
three-tier practice;
mastery assessment;
teacher answer key with explanations;
teacher guide;
family guide;
standards map;
applied lab/project;
remediation path;
meaningful interactive component.

I would retain 80% as the mastery threshold.

Major assessments

Beginning: Algebra I readiness diagnostic — diagnostic only, not punitive.

Midterm: after Unit 06, with balanced representation of Units 01–06 rather than allowing one topic to dominate.

Final: cumulative across all 13 units.

Capstone: Unit 13 mathematical-modeling portfolio and defense.

For consistency with Algebra I, we can provisionally retain:

40% coursework · 20% midterm · 30% final · 10% capstone

but explicitly document automated versus human-reviewed evidence from day one.

Preventive forensic standard

This is where Algebra II can start considerably ahead of our previous courses.

The validator should exist before we build Unit 01, and continuously check duration, 13-unit inventory, expected lesson counts, required unit-package files, question IDs, normalized prompt uniqueness, choice uniqueness, valid answer indexes, correct keyed answers, explanations, mathematical-content flags, question depth per lesson, Foundation/Core/Extended parity, project/lab presence, standards metadata, navigation, accessibility, print behavior, offline references, records, grading weights, mastery threshold, and broken/duplicate/development files.

In other words:

We don't wait until Audit #2 to discover what a validator could have caught during construction.

One additional Algebra II improvement

I would make function families the longitudinal spine of the course.

Instead of students learning polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions as disconnected chapters, every major family repeatedly asks:

What is its parent behavior? → What are its domain/range restrictions? → How do parameters transform it? → How do we solve equations involving it? → What does its inverse mean? → What real phenomena can it model? → When is this model inappropriate?

By Unit 13, the student isn't merely being told which equation to use. They're given a situation and must decide:

linear, quadratic, polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric—or none of them?

That is the mathematical maturity I would want Algebra II to produce.

So our larger mathematics progression becomes:

Pre-Algebra → Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II

with each generation inheriting the forensic lessons of the previous one.

And then Algebra II positions us naturally for Precalculus.
