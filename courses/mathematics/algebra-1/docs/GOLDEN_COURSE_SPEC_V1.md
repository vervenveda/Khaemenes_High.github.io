# Khaemenes High School · Algebra I
## Golden Course Specification v1.0 · First Native A+++ Course

This document governs the first-draft reconstruction of Algebra I. It is intentionally stricter than the historical Pre-Algebra implementation while preserving compatibility with the existing 36-week / 13-unit course architecture.

## 1. Academic authority

- The course engine is the authority for course mastery evidence.
- Completion controls, review buttons, drafts, family notes, or mentor recommendations may never manufacture mastery.
- Mastery threshold is **80%** unless a future formally documented course policy explicitly changes it.
- A later voluntary lower retake may update the latest score but may not erase a previously demonstrated best score at or above mastery.

## 2. Attempt evidence

Every scored lesson, practice set used as mastery evidence, unit mastery check, diagnostic, midterm, and final should support:

- first score;
- latest score;
- best score;
- attempt count;
- mastery threshold;
- mastery state;
- mastery timestamp when available;
- bounded attempt history where practical.

Legacy flat scores may be migrated into one-attempt histories when their meaning is unambiguous.

## 3. Readiness and placement

The Algebra I readiness diagnostic is support-oriented, not punitive. It should identify prerequisite skill evidence in areas including number systems, signed arithmetic, fractions/decimals/percent, proportional reasoning, expressions, equations, inequalities, coordinate reasoning, functions, slope, units, estimation, and evidence interpretation.

Readiness outcomes should support four instructional states:

1. Ready — proceed normally.
2. Ready with targeted refreshers — proceed while strengthening identified prerequisites.
3. Prerequisite gap — route to exact refreshers, tools, or Pre-Algebra resources before dependent work.
4. Significant foundational gap — recommend an earlier pathway with a clear route back to Algebra I.

No readiness label should be represented as a permanent judgment of ability.

## 4. Diagnostic signal

Scored instructional evidence should support the hierarchy:

`course → unit → lesson → domain/category → skill → item → outcome`

A curriculum-quality/adaptive-support signal may contain identifiers, skill/category outcomes, difficulty, and correctness, but should not contain learner names, family identifiers, credentials, answer keys, or free-response text.

This signal is designed for future Creative Spark / AIPCore / NAIB support routing. It does not grant those systems authority to alter grades or mastery.

## 5. Remediation loop

When mastery is below 80%, the learner should receive a specific path back:

`missed item → weak skill → explanation/review → targeted practice/tool/game → reassessment`

Students may revisit lessons and retake assessments. The system should show what needs work rather than merely blocking progression.

## 6. Progression

- Lesson-to-lesson advancement should rely on evidence-backed mastery where sequential dependency is intended.
- Review access remains available even when forward progression is locked.
- Unit-level mastery should be distinguishable from lesson completion.
- Administrative or family controls may not bypass mastery silently.

## 7. Records and trust

Browser-local records are learning evidence, not automatically authenticated institutional records.

Record types should distinguish at minimum:

- unfinished drafts;
- scored course-engine evidence;
- learner-created reflections/evidence;
- local learner association;
- future independently validated institutional records.

Exports should state their trust class and source. Drafts may never grant mastery.

## 8. Multilingual and accessibility design

- Vocabulary and explanatory support should preserve multilingual capability already present in the course.
- Translation is instructional support; mathematical notation and scoring authority remain canonical.
- Public-facing pages should be keyboard accessible, responsive, printable where appropriate, and readable without crowding.
- Accommodations may change access method without lowering the mastery standard.

## 9. Input and mathematical tools

Typing, handwriting/drawing, graphing, calculator use, manipulatives, and interactive tools should be introduced where they improve the mathematics rather than as decoration.

Tools may teach, visualize, scaffold, or provide practice. They do not independently certify mastery unless the course explicitly treats a scored activity as canonical assessment evidence.

## 10. Presentation standard

- restrained CINZEL-style headings where available without compromising sovereign/offline behavior;
- clean geometric body typography with Brandon-Grotesque-like proportions through local/system-safe fallbacks;
- centered, breathable composition;
- elegant symmetric rectangular controls;
- consistent hierarchy and spacing;
- sacred-number rhythm may inform measurements and timing only when accessibility/usability remain primary.

## 11. Integrity and QA

Protected shared engines and high-authority assessment components should receive deterministic component validation. A changed protected hash should fail validation until the change is reviewed and deliberately certified.

The first-draft sequence is:

`inventory → shared contracts → readiness → Units 01–13 → formal assessments → records → teacher/family surfaces → offline/accessibility → first-pass validation`

Only after the full first draft is coherent does the separate forensic audit cycle begin.

## 12. Preservation rule

Strong existing Algebra I work is preserved unless there is concrete evidence that changing it improves correctness, learning, accessibility, trust, maintainability, or coherence. This course is an upgrade of the existing architecture, not a replacement for replacement's sake.
