Begin with evidence

Start Algebra I intentionally.

There are two readiness surfaces, and they have different jobs. The Week 1 diagnostic is the learner’s Algebra I starting assessment. The Readiness Bridge simply carries useful evidence forward from Pre-Algebra and toward later mathematics.

Step 1 · Algebra I starting assessment

Begin with the Week 1 diagnostic.

The 36-question Algebra I diagnostic is untimed, saves locally, and guides Foundation, Core, or Extended support. It is readiness evidence, not part of the official course grade.

  1. 1Take the diagnostic.
    Work independently enough to make the evidence useful.
  2. 2Review the breakdown.
    Use prerequisite skill evidence to choose support intentionally.
  3. 3Begin Unit 01.
    From Unit 01 onward, normal 80% mastery progression controls advancement.

Course architecture

A complete instructional cycle—not a list of topics.

Each phase has a different role. Readiness evidence guides support; lessons teach and check mastery; weekly checks preserve spaced retrieval; unit mastery validates synthesis; cumulative assessments require broader evidence.

Readiness

Week 1 diagnostic plus optional continuity evidence. Diagnostic results guide support but do not enter the course grade.

Daily Lessons

87 instructional lessons with worked reasoning, practice, error analysis, verification, and an 80% lesson gate.

Weekly Mastery

Weeks 02–36 use 10-question graded checks. Every required lesson and weekly check must reach 80% before normal progression.

Unit & Cumulative Evidence

13 unit mastery checks, a reviewed Midterm, and a reviewed Final preserve breadth, transfer, and human-reviewed reasoning.

Applied Modelling

Six laboratory experiences and the Unit 13 integrated portfolio/oral defence connect Algebra I to engineering, finance, systems, data, and decision making.

13-unit progression

The Algebra I pathway

Unit cards remain the canonical mastery-gated course sequence. The lesson directory below gives direct access only when the same evidence gates allow it.

01 · Real Numbers & Precision

Reasoning, notation, properties, units, estimation, and verification.

02 · Expressions & Polynomial Foundations

Structure, equivalence, distribution, polynomial language, and factoring foundations.

03 · Linear Equations & Models

Equality, equations, formulas, proportions, rates, and contextual models.

04 · Inequalities & Absolute Value

Solution sets, compound constraints, absolute value, tolerances, and feasible ranges.

05 · Functions & Representations

Function notation, domain/range, graphs, transformations, sequences, and piecewise rules.

06 · Linear Functions & Regression

Slope, line forms, parallel/perpendicular lines, regression, residuals, and modelling.

07 · Systems

Graphing, substitution, elimination, system classification, feasible regions, and optimization.

08 · Exponents & Exponential Functions

Exponent laws, scientific notation, sequences, growth/decay, and compound interest.

09 · Polynomial Operations & Factoring

Polynomial structure, operations, special products, factoring, zeros, and applications.

10 · Quadratic Functions & Equations

Parabolas, forms, factoring, completing the square, formula, systems, and optimization.

11 · Radical & Rational Relationships

Radicals, rational exponents, rational expressions/equations, variation, and restrictions.

12 · Statistics, Data & Evidence

Distributions, sampling, bivariate data, regression, probability, evidence, and data ethics.

13 · Integrated Modelling & Capstone

Integrated model design, sensitivity, communication, fairness, portfolio, and oral defence.
