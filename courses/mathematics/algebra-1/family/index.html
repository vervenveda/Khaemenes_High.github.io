Family & learning-support guide

How Algebra I Works

A mastery-based 36-week course designed so a learner can study, practice, receive exact corrective guidance, reassess, and continue after demonstrating at least 80% mastery.

1 · Learn

Work through the unit lessons, examples, assignments, practice, labs, and projects. Progress is stored locally where supported.

2 · Demonstrate mastery

Formal progression uses an 80% mastery threshold. A first attempt below 80% is evidence for what to study next, not the end of the learning path.

3 · Correct exactly

Assessment evidence identifies the unit, skill, and submitted item needing review. The recovery layer routes the learner to Foundation, Core, or Extended work.

4 · Try again

Learners may return to instruction and practice and repeat assessments. Attempt history is preserved, and previously earned mastery is not erased by a later lower attempt.

5 · Apply

Labs and projects connect Algebra I to modeling, data, finance, geometry, and decision-making. They strengthen portfolio evidence without silently replacing required assessment mastery.

6 · Document

Course records preserve unit mastery, midterm and final evidence, capstone review, instructional information, and a parent/program-issued completion record.

Family checkpoints

  • Check that the learner can explain the idea, not only select an answer.
  • When mastery is below 80%, use the exact skill feedback before another attempt.
  • Keep representative worksheets, lab/project work, assessment exports, and corrections in the learner portfolio.
  • Use accommodations that preserve the mathematical target while improving access.
  • Celebrate improvement and durable mastery rather than speed alone.

Privacy & trust

This sovereign course can operate without a student account or external AI service. Browser-local evidence is useful educational evidence but is not independently authenticated identity evidence. NAIB may later enhance recommendations, but it is not required for completion.

Families remain responsible for any home-education, transcript, evaluation, credit, testing, or graduation requirements that apply in their jurisdiction.
