Unit 05 · Functions and representations

A function assigns exactly one output to every input in its domain. The input-output relationship can be expressed as an equation, table, graph, or verbal rule. For f(x)=2x−3, the inputs −1, 0, and 2 give outputs −5, −3, and 1.

Domain matters: a ticket-price function might only allow nonnegative whole-number inputs even when its equation accepts any real x. The vertical line test identifies when a drawn graph fails to define a function of x.

Common error: two different x-values can share an output and still define a function; one x-value assigned two different outputs cannot.

Unit 06 · Slope, residuals and model limits

For the line y=2x+1, the slope 2 represents a two-unit increase in y per one-unit increase in x, and 1 is the y-intercept. Rate must be interpreted with the quantities' units.

When observed values are (0,1), (1,3), and (2,6), the model predicts 1, 3, and 5. Residuals (observed minus predicted) are 0, 0, and +1. A positive residual means the observation lies above the model's prediction.

Remember: a line can describe association without establishing causation. Predictions far outside the observed domain require a separate justification.

Unit 07 · Systems and constraints

The system x+y=7 and x−y=1 has the solution (4,3). Adding the equations eliminates y: 2x=8, so x=4 and y=3. Substituting verifies both original equations.

Constraints: x≥0, y≥0 and x+y≤7 define a triangular feasible region in the first quadrant, including its boundary. The feasible region represents solutions satisfying every inequality simultaneously.

Common error: a point on one line is not necessarily a solution to a system; it must meet every condition.
