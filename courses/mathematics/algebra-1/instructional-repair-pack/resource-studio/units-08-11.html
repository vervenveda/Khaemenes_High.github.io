Reading and mathematical reasoning

08 · Exponents and growth

For a nonzero base a, a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ. With like bases, multiply powers by adding exponents: 2³ × 2⁻⁵=2⁻²=1/4. A zero exponent gives 1 for nonzero bases.

Growth uses a factor above 1, such as 100(1.05)ᵗ; decay uses a factor between 0 and 1, such as 100(0.95)ᵗ. A factor of 1.05 represents five-percent growth per period, not five additional units.

Check: negative-exponent reciprocals cannot use a zero base.

09 · Polynomial structure

Expanding (x−2)(x−3) gives x²−5x+6; factoring the polynomial reverses the product. The zero-product property gives x=2 and x=3 when the product equals zero.

Check: the zero-product property does not let us set individual factors equal to zero when their product equals a nonzero number. Rearrange to zero first.

10 · Quadratic forms

The equivalent forms x²−4x+3, (x−1)(x−3), and (x−2)²−1 reveal different features of the same parabola: standard form, zeros at 1 and 3, and vertex (2,−1).

Check: completing the square must preserve equality. The vertex is a turning point, not automatically a zero.

11 · Radicals and coordinate geometry

√25 is the principal, nonnegative square root 5; solving x²=25 gives two solutions ±5. For √(x−2)=3, real-number inputs require x≥2; squaring yields x=11, which must be checked in the original.

The distance from (1,2) to (4,6) follows a 3–4–5 right triangle: √(3²+4²)=5.

Check: squaring both sides can introduce candidates that fail the original equation.
