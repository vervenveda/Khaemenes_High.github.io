## Unit 02 — Expressions and structure
**Gap:** Worked examples exist, but base lesson data has no structured visual examples.
**Repair demonstration:** An expression tree for `3(2x - 5) + 4x`: multiplication node at `3(2x - 5)`, distribution to `6x - 15`, then collect `6x + 4x = 10x - 15`. Annotate that `3(2x - 5) ≠ 6x - 5`; the factor applies to both terms. Compare a verbal statement (“fifteen less than ten times a number”) with `10x - 15`.
**Student defence:** substitute x = 2 into original and simplified forms (both 5). Do not claim testing one value proves the identity: distribution and like-term rules do that.

## Unit 03 — Equality and equations
**Gap:** Equation-solving lessons need more explicit equivalence and extraneous-solution interpretation.
**Repair demonstration:** A balance diagram for `3x + 5 = 20`. Remove 5 *from both pans*, then partition the remaining 15 into three equal groups: `x = 5`. For `2(x - 3) = x + 4`: expand, subtract x and add 6 → `x = 10`; substitute both sides (14 each). Contrast an identity `2(x + 1)=2x+2` with a contradiction `2(x+1)=2x+3`.
**Student defence:** label every equality-preserving operation and verify in the original.

## Unit 04 — Inequalities
**Gap:** Negative-factor reversals and intersections benefit from diagrams plus boundary tests.
**Repair demonstration:** `-2x < 6`; dividing by -2 reverses the sign, yielding `x > -3`. Test x = 0 (valid) and x = -4 (invalid). Draw an open circle at -3 with shading rightward. Show `x ≥ 1 AND x < 4` as `[1,4)`, while OR means union.
**Student defence:** test a point on either side of a boundary; justify inclusion of endpoints.

## Unit 05 — Functions
**Gap:** Strengthen synchronized graphs, tables and algebra.
**Repair demonstration:** `f(x)=2x-3` with table `(-1,-5),(0,-3),(2,1)` and plotted points. Distinguish a rule valid for all reals from a contextual domain such as whole ticket counts. Explain that each x has one y, even when multiple x values share a y.
**Student defence:** identify slope, intercept, independent variable, valid context domain and units.

## Unit 06 — Linear models
**Gap:** Make residuals and model limits tangible.
**Repair demonstration:** observed y at x=0,1,2: 1, 3, 6; prediction `y=2x+1` gives 1, 3, 5; residuals `observed - predicted` are 0, 0, +1. Draw points, model line and vertical residual at x=2.
**Student defence:** interpret the residual sign, distinguish fit from causation, and state when extrapolation is not justified.

## Unit 07 — Systems
**Gap:** Stronger method-selection rationale and feasible-region interpretation.
**Repair demonstration:** `x+y=7`, `x-y=1`; add equations → `2x=8` → `x=4`, `y=3`; verify both originals. Graph both lines crossing at (4,3). For `x≥0,y≥0,x+y≤7`, shade the triangular feasible region and identify boundary inclusion.
**Student defence:** choose substitution/elimination/graphing with a reason, then verify constraints.

## Unit 08 — Exponents and exponential models
**Gap:** Rules need derivations and contextual limits.
**Repair demonstration:** `2³ × 2⁻⁵ = 2⁻² = 1/4`, using exponent subtraction through cancellation of equal base factors. Illustrate growth `y=100(1.05)^t` versus decay `y=100(0.95)^t` over nonnegative time. State that negative exponents require nonzero base where reciprocals are used.
**Student defence:** describe multiplicative change and units/time domain.

## Unit 09 — Polynomials
**Gap:** Link factoring to zeros and check by expansion.
**Repair demonstration:** `x² - 5x + 6 = (x-2)(x-3)`; expand factors to confirm. Solve `x²-5x+6=0` → x=2 or x=3. Contrast with `x²-5x+6=4`, where setting each factor equal to 4 is invalid; rearrange the equation first.
**Student defence:** show multiplication verification and describe a zero on a graph.

## Unit 10 — Quadratics
**Gap:** Coordinate key features with algebraic forms.
**Repair demonstration:** `y=x²-4x+3=(x-1)(x-3)=(x-2)²-1`. The forms reveal roots x=1,3, axis x=2 and vertex (2,-1). Draw one labelled parabola with all features; verify that y(2)=-1.
**Student defence:** explain which form is most useful for each question and why.

## Unit 11 — Radicals and coordinate geometry
**Gap:** Only two structured visual examples in base lessons; reinforce domain and exact values.
**Repair demonstration:** `√(x-2)=3` requires x≥2. Squaring gives x-2=9 and x=11; check in the original: √9=3. For distance between (1,2) and (4,6), a right-triangle sketch has legs 3 and 4, distance 5. Contrast `√25=5` with solutions to `x²=25` (x=±5).
**Student defence:** state domain restrictions and check candidate roots in the original.

## Unit 12 — Statistics and finance
**Gap:** Two structured visual examples in the base data; needs more complete data displays (check additional runtime content before implementation).
**Repair demonstration:** dataset `2,4,4,6,9`: mean 5, median 4, range 7. Label the points on a dot plot, compute absolute deviations from the mean `3,1,1,1,4`, and discuss why a single measure of center does not describe spread. In a finance comparison, calculate simple annual interest on $1,000 at 5% ($50) and contrast with one year's compound total ($1,050); do not generalize one-year equality to multi-year growth.
**Student defence:** explain variation, appropriate graph choice, denominators and limitations of sampling claims.

## Unit 13 — Modelling capstone
**Gap:** Base lesson data contains no structured visual worked examples; supplemental runtime layers require comparison before implementation.
**Full exemplar:** A club compares two transport quotes for a trip of n students: A costs `$180+$8n`, B costs `$80+$12n`. Assume both quotes serve the same trip with equal capacity and service. Set equal: `180+8n=80+12n` → `n=25`. At n=20, A=$340 and B=$320 so B is cheaper; at n=30, A=$420 and B=$440 so A is cheaper. Chart both lines for 0≤n≤40. Add a sensitivity check: if A's fixed fee increases to $200, break-even becomes n=30. State that capacity, taxes and accessibility may invalidate the simplified choice.
**Student defence:** identify assumptions, units, domain, decisions at two data points, sensitivity result, equity considerations and limits. Provide a model-revision paragraph, not merely an answer.
