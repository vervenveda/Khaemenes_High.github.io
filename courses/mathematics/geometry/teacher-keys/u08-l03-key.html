Inscribed Angles & Intercepted Arcs

Teacher reference for the inscribed-angle theorem and its diameter and cyclic-quadrilateral consequences.

Core theorem

m∠ = ½(m intercepted arc)m arc = 2(m inscribed angle)

Instructional boundary: Keep this lesson focused on angles with vertices on the circle. Central angles were established in L02; tangent/secant angle and product relationships belong later in the unit.

L03 Answer & Reasoning Key

U08 · L03
1

An inscribed angle intercepts an arc measuring 124°.

Answer: 62°.
Reasoning: An inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc: 124/2=62.
2

Inscribed angle ∠XYZ measures 46°. Find intercepted arc XZ.

Answer: 92°.
Reasoning: The arc is twice the inscribed angle: 2(46)=92.
3

Two inscribed angles intercept the same arc PQ. One measures 58°.

Answer: 58°.
Reasoning: Inscribed angles intercepting the same arc each equal half that same arc, so they are congruent.
4

AB is a diameter and C is another point on the circle. Find ∠ACB.

Answer: 90°.
Reasoning: Diameter AB intercepts a 180° semicircle; half of 180° is 90°.
5

A cyclic quadrilateral has one angle of 112°. Find its opposite angle.

Answer: 68°.
Reasoning: Opposite inscribed angles intercept arcs totaling 360°, so the angles total half of 360° = 180°. Thus 180−112=68.
6

An inscribed angle is 3x+4 degrees and its intercepted arc is 8x−8 degrees.

Answer: x=8; angle=28°; arc=56°.
Reasoning: 3x+4=½(8x−8)=4x−4, so x=8. Substitute to obtain 28° and 56°.

Human-review look-fors

  • Student identifies the correct intercepted arc rather than an adjacent arc.
  • Student distinguishes the central-angle equality from the inscribed-angle one-half relationship.
  • For explanations, student cites the theorem or derives the special case rather than relying on appearance.
