Coordinate Intersections & Geometric Systems · Teacher Key

Common-solution reasoning for coordinate geometric conditions.

Instructional focus

Every reported intersection must satisfy every equation. Connect algebraic solution counts to geometry: unique intersection, parallel/no intersection, coincident/infinite intersection, and multiple intersections for nonlinear loci.

1
Answer: (3,5).
3x−4=−x+8 → 4x=12 → x=3; y=5. Both equations give 5.
2

3

Answer: (3,4) and (3,−4).
Substitute x=3 into x²+y²=25: 9+y²=25 → y²=16.
5
System: y=x+1 and x²+y²=25.
Answer: x=(-1±7)/2, so x=3 or −4. Points are (3,4) and (−4,−3). Both are 5 units from the origin.
6
Expected reasoning: Substitute each candidate's x- and y-values into every governing equation or condition. A candidate is an actual intersection only if every condition is true simultaneously. Reject any point that fails even one condition.

Teacher prompt

Ask “What does this coordinate have to make true?” before students calculate. That keeps the geometry of simultaneous conditions visible beneath the algebra.
