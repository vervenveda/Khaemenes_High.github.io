Cross-Sections & Solids of Revolution

Instructional anchor

Require students to name the geometric operation, not merely the resulting shape. For cross-sections, the orientation of the cutting plane matters. For solids of revolution, the axis of rotation determines the resulting dimensions.

1
Answer: A circle congruent to either base. Its size stays constant as a parallel cutting plane moves along a right cylinder because the cylinder has constant radius.
2
Answer: A great circle. A plane through the center reaches the sphere's full radius; planes displaced from the center intersect in smaller circles.
3
Answer: A rectangle congruent to the face to which the plane is parallel, assuming the slice remains within the prism. Corresponding side lengths are unchanged.
4
Answer: A right circular cone with radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. The 12 cm leg is the axis; the 5 cm leg sweeps the base radius.
5
Answer: A sphere of radius 7 cm. Rotating the semicircular region around its diameter sweeps out the full sphere.
6
Answer: About a 3 cm side: cylinder height 3 cm, radius 8 cm. About an 8 cm side: cylinder height 8 cm, radius 3 cm. They are not congruent because their radius-height pairs differ.

Misconception watch

  • Assuming every cylinder cross-section is a circle regardless of plane orientation.
  • Calling every sphere slice a great circle; only a central plane produces one.
  • Ignoring the named axis when rotating a region.
  • Swapping radius and height without noticing that the resulting solid changes.

Mastery: 80% plus corrections. Strong evidence includes a sketch or verbal explanation connecting the plane/axis to the resulting geometry.
