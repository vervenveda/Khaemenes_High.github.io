Perpendicular and Angle Bisector Loci · Teacher Key

Equidistance theorems, constructions, coordinate bisectors, and converse reasoning.

Teacher KeyConstruction + proofMastery target 80%

Instructional focus

  • Equidistant from two points ⇔ perpendicular-bisector locus.
  • Equidistant from two intersecting lines ⇔ internal or external angle-bisector locus.
  • Distance from a point to a line is measured perpendicularly.
  • Coordinate construction requires midpoint plus perpendicular slope.

Watch: Students often name only the internal angle bisector. For two complete intersecting lines, the complete equidistance locus contains both internal and external bisectors.

Bisector Loci
1

Points equidistant from C and D.

Answer: The perpendicular bisector of segment CD.
2

A(2,−3), B(8,−3).

Answer: x=5.
Reasoning: Midpoint is (5,−3); AB is horizontal, so its perpendicular bisector is vertical.
3

A(−4,2), B(2,8).

Answer: Midpoint (−1,5); slope AB=1; perpendicular slope=−1. One equation is y−5=−(x+1), or y=−x+4.
4

Points equally distant from two intersecting road centerlines.

Answer: The two angle-bisector lines—internal and external. Every point on either has equal perpendicular distance to the two lines.
5

P is 7 cm from each side of an angle.

Answer: P lies on an angle bisector of the two side-lines. This uses the converse of the angle-bisector equidistance theorem.
6

Facility equidistant from three noncollinear towns.

Answer: Construct perpendicular bisectors of two town-to-town segments. Their intersection is automatically equidistant from all three vertices; it is the triangle's circumcenter. A third perpendicular bisector passes through the same point.

Teacher prompts

Ask students to say what is equal before naming the locus. For coordinate problems, insist on midpoint first and perpendicular direction second. For angle loci, ask whether the objects are rays forming one angle or complete intersecting lines; this clarifies whether one or two bisectors are relevant.
