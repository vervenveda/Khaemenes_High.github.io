Pyramids & Cones

Answer key for the one-third volume relationship, perpendicular height, and exact cone/pyramid volume.

Mastery target 80%Corrections required

Instructional anchor

For a pyramid or cone, V = ⅓Bh. Compare with a prism or cylinder having the same base area B and perpendicular height h: its volume is Bh, so the pointed solid is one-third as large.

Watch for: slant height substituted for perpendicular height, missing ⅓, premature decimal approximations of π, and square units instead of cubic units.

U11 L02 · Answer Key

Pyramids & Cones
1

Square pyramid: side 8 cm, h = 15 cm.

Answer: 320 cm³.
Reasoning: B=8²=64; V=⅓(64)(15)=320.
2

Cone: r = 5 m, h = 12 m.

Answer: 100π m³.
Reasoning: V=⅓π(25)(12)=100π.
3

Same B=54 cm² and h=10 cm.

Answer: Prism = 540 cm³; pyramid = 180 cm³; ratio pyramid:prism = 1:3.
Reasoning: Bh=540 and ⅓Bh=180.
4

Cone: V=96π in³, r=4 in.

Answer: h = 18 in.
Reasoning: 96π=⅓π(16)h=(16π/3)h; h=18.
5

Slant height 13 cm, r=5 cm, perpendicular h=12 cm.

Answer: 100π cm³.
Reasoning: Volume uses perpendicular height, not slant height: V=⅓π(25)(12)=100π.
6

Matching cylinder holds 750 cm³.

Answer: 250 cm³.
Reasoning: Equal radius and perpendicular height give the same B and h; cone volume is one-third cylinder volume, so 750÷3=250.

Mastery decision

A learner reaching 80% should also correct any missed item and explain the one-third relationship in words. If the learner repeatedly substitutes slant height, revisit the perpendicular-height diagram before advancing.
