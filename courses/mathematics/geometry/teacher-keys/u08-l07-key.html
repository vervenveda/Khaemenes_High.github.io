Sector Area, Segment Area & Annuli · Teacher Key

Answer key aligned to circular-region decomposition and exact-area reasoning.

Teacher KeyExact form firstMastery target 80%

Instructional focus

  • Sector area is the central-angle fraction of the full circle area.
  • Minor segment area is sector area minus the central triangle area.
  • Annulus area is outer-circle area minus inner-circle area.
  • Students should distinguish area from arc length and preserve exact forms until approximation has a purpose.
Asector=(θ/360°)πr²Asegment=Asector−AtriangleAannulus=π(R²−r²)

Boundary: This lesson assesses circular regions, not power theorems, tangent theorems, or coordinate-circle equations.

Sectors · Segments · Annuli
1

Radius 9 cm; 120° sector.

Answer: 27π cm².
Reasoning: (120/360)π(9²)=(1/3)(81π)=27π.
2

Radius 10 m; sector area 25π m².

Answer: 90°.
Reasoning: (θ/360)(100π)=25π, so θ/360=1/4 and θ=90°.
3

Radius 8 cm; 90° minor segment.

Answer: 16π−32 cm².
Reasoning: Quarter-circle sector area=(1/4)π(64)=16π. The central triangle is right with legs 8 and 8, so its area is 32. Subtract.
4

Annulus with R=13 cm and r=5 cm.

Answer: 144π cm².
Reasoning: π(13²−5²)=π(169−25)=144π.
5

Garden R=12 m; pond r=7 m.

Answer: 95π m² ≈ 298.5 m².
Reasoning: π(12²−7²)=π(144−49)=95π. Decimal approximation is useful for interpreting planting area.
6

Compare 90° sector r=14 cm with annulus R=8 cm, r=3 cm.

Answer: Annulus is greater.
Sector: (1/4)π(14²)=49π cm².
Annulus: π(8²−3²)=55π cm².
Comparison: 55π>49π, a difference of 6π cm².

Teacher prompts

Ask students to shade the target region before calculating. For segment problems, ask what must be removed from the sector. For annuli, ask why subtracting radii first and then squaring gives a different—and generally incorrect—area.
