Unit 13 · Lesson 03 · Teacher Key

Test Measurements, Sensitivity & Limitations

Require numerical stress testing and honest limits before the capstone advances to portfolio production.

Math Word: Sensitivity

Expected retrieval: A sensitive model changes substantially when an input changes; an insensitive model changes relatively little over the tested range.

Worked-example check

For 10.0 m × (6.0±0.2) m, areas are 58.0, 60.0, and 62.0 m². The ±2.0 m² change is approximately ±3.33% of the base area. Whether that matters depends on the decision threshold.

1
Expected: A measured input comes from observation/measurement or verified source data; an assumed input is adopted for the model without direct verification. Require a capstone-specific example.
2
Expected: Base plus defensible low/high values. Accept measurement tolerance, instrument precision, source range, design allowance, or justified scenario bounds. Reject arbitrary ranges with no rationale.
3
Expected: Three auditable calculations using the same governing relationship, correct units, and clearly identified low/base/high inputs.
4
Expected: Robust if the decision/conclusion survives the tested range; fragile if plausible changes reverse or materially weaken it. Require numerical evidence rather than the label alone.
5
Expected: A bounded claim such as “The model assumes the measured width represents the entire site; variation elsewhere has not been verified.” It must also identify evidence that could reduce the limitation.
6
Expected: A revision tied directly to the stress-test evidence, or a justified no-revision decision demonstrating that the tested variation does not alter the relevant conclusion.

Instructional checkpoint

Do not accept “limitations: none.” Every real model has scope, assumptions, measurement precision, omitted variables, or conditions under which its claim may not hold. The goal is responsible mathematical communication, not manufactured weakness.

Mastery

80% plus corrections. Before L04, require at least two tested inputs/assumptions, low/base/high results, a robustness judgment, one precise limitation, and a documented revision or justified no-revision decision.
