Unit 08 · Lesson 01 · Teacher Key

Circle Vocabulary, Radius, Diameter & Chords

Keyed evidence for circle structure, segment classification, and radius-diameter relationships.

Instructional focus

Students should distinguish structural terms before later circle theorems are introduced. Accept equivalent wording when the mathematical classification and justification are correct.

Watch for: students saying that every chord is a diameter. Require the center condition.

Circle Structure Lab · Teacher Key

U08 · L01
1

A circle has radius 7.5 cm. Find its diameter.

Answer: 15 cm.
Reasoning: d=2r=2(7.5)=15.
2

A circle has diameter 26 m. Find its radius.

Answer: 13 m.
Reasoning: r=d/2=26/2=13.
3

Explain why every diameter is a chord but not every chord is a diameter.

Answer: A chord joins two points on the circle. A diameter is a chord that also passes through the center; chords that miss the center are not diameters.
4

In circle O, OA and OB are radii. If OA=11, what is OB? State the justification.

Answer: OB=11.
Reasoning: All radii of the same circle are congruent.
5

Segment XY has both endpoints on a circle and passes through its center. Classify XY as precisely as possible.

Answer: XY is a diameter, and therefore also a chord.
6

A chord is 12 units long in a circle with diameter 12 units. What special kind of chord must it be? Explain.

Answer: A diameter.
Reasoning: No chord can be longer than the diameter; a chord equal to the circle's diameter has maximum chord length and passes through the center.
