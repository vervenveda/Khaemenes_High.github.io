Equation of a Circle & Coordinate Applications · Teacher Key

Answer key aligned to center-radius interpretation, distance reasoning, and coordinate-circle applications.

Teacher KeyCoordinate reasoningMastery target 80%

Instructional focus

  • Standard form encodes center (h,k) and radius r.
  • The signs inside the squared binomials are opposite the center-coordinate signs.
  • A radius can be determined with the distance/Pythagorean relationship.
  • Comparing a point's squared distance from the center with r² classifies it as inside, on, or outside.
(x−h)²+(y−k)²=r²d²=(Δx)²+(Δy)²d²<r² insided²=r² ond²>r² outside

Boundary: This lesson focuses on standard-form circle equations and coordinate interpretation; it does not require expanding to general quadratic form.

Circle Equations
1

(x−4)²+(y+7)²=81.

Answer: Center (4,−7); radius 9.
Reasoning: h=4, k=−7, and r²=81.
2

Center (−5,2), radius 6.

Answer: (x+5)²+(y−2)²=36.
3

Center (1,−3), point (5,0).

Answer: Radius 5; equation (x−1)²+(y+3)²=25.
Reasoning: r²=(5−1)²+(0+3)²=16+9=25.
4

Point (6,2); circle (x−2)²+(y+1)²=25.

Answer: On the circle.
Reasoning: Squared distance=(6−2)²+(2+1)²=16+9=25=r².
5

Diameter endpoints A(−2,4), B(8,−2).

Answer: Center (3,1); r²=34; equation (x−3)²+(y−1)²=34.
Reasoning: Midpoint=((−2+8)/2,(4−2)/2)=(3,1). From center to B, Δx=5 and Δy=−3, so r²=25+9=34.
6

Safety zone (x−12)²+(y−5)²=144; sensor (20,14).

Answer: Center (12,5), radius 12 m; sensor is outside.
Reasoning: Sensor squared distance=(20−12)²+(14−5)²=64+81=145. Since 145>144, it lies just outside the boundary.

Teacher prompts

Ask why a center y-coordinate of −7 appears as (y+7)². Encourage students to compare squared distances with r² rather than taking unnecessary square roots. For diameter endpoints, require midpoint reasoning before distance calculation.
