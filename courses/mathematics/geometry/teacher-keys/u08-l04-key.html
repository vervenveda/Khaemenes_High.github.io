Chords, Perpendicular Bisectors & Distances · Teacher Key

Instructional focus

  • A perpendicular from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • Radius, center-to-chord distance, and half-chord form a right triangle.
  • Congruent chords in the same circle are equidistant from the center, and conversely.
  • Among chords in the same circle, the chord closer to the center is longer.
AM=MB=AB/2r²=d²+(c/2)²equal chords ⇔ equal distances

Boundary: This lesson does not assess arc length, inscribed angles, tangent-radius theorems, or coordinate-circle equations.

Chords & Distances
1

OM ⟂ chord AB at M; AB=18 cm.

Answer: AM=9 cm and MB=9 cm.
Reasoning: A perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects the chord, so each half is 18/2=9.
2

Radius 10 cm; chord 16 cm.

Answer: 6 cm.
Reasoning: Half-chord=8. Then 10²=d²+8², so d²=36 and d=6.
3

Radius 17 m; chord distance 8 m.

Answer: 30 m.
Reasoning: Half-chord x satisfies 17²=8²+x², so x²=225 and x=15. Full chord=30.
4

Two 14 cm chords in the same circle; one is 6 cm from center.

Answer: 6 cm.
Reasoning: Congruent chords in the same circle are equidistant from the center.
5

Chord CD is closer to the center than chord EF.

Answer: CD is longer.
Reasoning: In the same circle, the longer chord lies closer to the center; equivalently, the chord closer to the center is longer.
6

Circular window radius 25 cm; support chord 14 cm from center.

Answer: 2√429 cm ≈ 41.42 cm.
Reasoning: Half-chord x satisfies 25²=14²+x², so x²=429 and x=√429. Double for the full chord: 2√429. A calculator may verify the fabrication decimal after the exact result is established.

Teacher prompts

Ask: Why must the center-to-chord segment be perpendicular before we can claim it bisects the chord? Why do we use half the chord in the Pythagorean relationship? How does moving a chord toward the center change its length?
