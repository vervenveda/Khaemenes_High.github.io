Unit 09 · Lesson 05

Circle-Line Intersections & Tangency · Teacher Key

Distance classification and system solutions for circle-line relationships.

Core relationship

d<r gives a secant and two intersections; d=r gives a tangent and one intersection; d>r gives an external line and no intersections.

1
Answer: Tangent at (0,6). The center-to-line distance is 6, equal to radius 6. Substitution gives x²+36=36 → x=0.
2
Answer: 0 intersections; external. Center-to-line distance from (0,0) to x=7 is 7>5.
3
Answer: (3,4) and (−4,−3). Substitute y=x+1: x²+(x+1)²=25 → 2x²+2x−24=0 → x²+x−12=0 → (x+4)(x−3)=0.
4
Answer: Tangent at (−2,−1). Center is (2,−1), radius 4; distance to x=−2 is 4. Substitution leaves (y+1)²=0.
5
Answer: External.
d=|3(3)+4(2)−42|/5=25/5=5, so actually d=r and the line is tangent.
Correct classification: tangent.
6
Expected explanation: Substitution reduces the system to a quadratic. Two distinct real roots correspond to two circle-line intersections (secant); one repeated real root corresponds to one intersection (tangent); no real roots correspond to no real intersection (external).

Verification habit

Problem 5 intentionally requires calculation rather than trusting an initial visual impression: the distance is exactly 5, so the line is tangent. Require students to compare d and r explicitly.
