Unit 09 · Lesson 06

Parabolas as Distance Loci · Teacher Key

Core definition

A parabola is the locus of points equidistant from a fixed point (focus) and a fixed line (directrix).

1
Answer: The set of all points whose distance from a fixed focus equals their perpendicular distance from a fixed directrix.
2
Answer: Vertex (0,0), p=3, opens upward, equation x²=12y.
3
Answer: 4p=12 → p=3. Vertex (0,0), focus (0,3), directrix y=−3, axis x=0.
4
Answer: 4p=−8 → p=−2. Vertex (2,1), focus (0,1), directrix x=4, opens left.
5
Answer: PF=√((4−0)²+(4−2)²)=√20. Distance from P to y=−2 is |4−(−2)|=6. Since √20≠6, P does not lie on the parabola. The prompt's proposed membership is false; verification should reject it.
6
Answer: p=1.5, so x²=6y. The focus is the fixed point whose distance from every point on the parabola equals that point's perpendicular distance to the directrix y=−1.5.

Teacher note

Problem 5 is a verification trap by design: students must compare distances rather than accept “lies on” as guaranteed. A future wording sweep may change “Show that” to “Determine whether” for maximum clarity.
