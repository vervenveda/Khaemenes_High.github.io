Unit 12 · Lesson 03 · Teacher Key

Layers, Objects & Geometric Organization

Instructional anchor

A layer organizes objects by function and supports controls such as hide/show/lock. A group binds selected objects so they can be handled together. Students should not treat those concepts as interchangeable.

1
Expected: Walls, Dimensions, and Annotations/Labels on separate layers. Separation allows independent visibility, editing, styling, checking, and output.
2
Expected: A layer is a functional category across a drawing; a group is a selected set of objects handled together. Example: all dimensions on one layer; a desk and chair grouped as one furniture arrangement.
3
Answer: Hide the Dimensions layer temporarily. Deleting destroys useful technical information and may require reconstruction later.
4
Answer: Lock the Walls/Structure layer so finished geometry cannot be moved while other objects are edited.
5
Examples: difficult selective printing; accidental edits to finished geometry; inability to hide clutter; poor inspection; harder collaboration/revision; inconsistent styling.
6
One strong plan: Walls/Structure; Openings; Furniture; Dimensions; Electrical; Notes/Annotations. Other defensible four-plus-layer structures are acceptable if categories serve distinct technical purposes.

Vocabulary check

Word: layer. If students can repeat the definition but cannot explain why hiding, locking, or independently exporting objects matters, the vocabulary is not yet functional.

Mastery: 80% plus corrections and a defensible layer plan.
