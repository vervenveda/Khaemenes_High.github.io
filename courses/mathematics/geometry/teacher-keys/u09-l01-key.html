Locus as a Set of Points Meeting a Condition · Teacher Key

Answer key aligned to locus definition, boundary language, and fixed-distance conditions.

Teacher KeyBoundary reasoningMastery target 80%

Instructional focus

  • A locus is the complete set of points satisfying a condition.
  • Fixed distance from one point produces a circle boundary.
  • Fixed distance from a line produces two parallel lines.
  • Equal distance from two fixed points produces a perpendicular-bisector locus.
  • Words such as exactly, less than, greater than, at least, and at most determine whether a boundary or region is included.

Boundary: This lesson introduces locus language only. Formal angle-bisector loci, point-to-line distance formulas, circle-line intersection algebra, parabolas, and Voronoi regions belong to later Unit 09 lessons.

Loci & Conditions
1

Exactly 6 units from point P.

Answer: A circle centered at P with radius 6 units.
Reasoning: Every point on that circle is exactly 6 units from P, and points inside or outside are not.
2

Exactly 3 cm from straight line ℓ.

Answer: Two lines parallel to ℓ, one on each side, each 3 cm away.
Reasoning: Perpendicular distance to ℓ is constant on each parallel line.
3

Equidistant from fixed points A and B.

Answer: The perpendicular bisector of AB.
Reasoning: Every point on the perpendicular bisector has equal distance to A and B.
4

Stay at least 5 m from machine M.

Answer: The exterior of the circle of radius 5 centered at M, including the circle.
Reasoning: “At least” means distance ≥5, so the boundary is included.
5

Points less than 4 m from sprinkler S.

Answer: The interior of the circle of radius 4 centered at S; the circular boundary is excluded.
Reasoning: “Less than” means distance <4.
6

Four sample points each 2 units from x-axis.

Answer: The four samples are not the locus. The complete locus is the two horizontal lines y=2 and y=−2.
Reasoning: Infinitely many points on those two lines are exactly 2 units from the x-axis.

Teacher prompts

Ask what single word in a condition controls boundary inclusion. Require learners to distinguish “points I tested” from “all possible points.” Encourage sketches before symbolic notation.
