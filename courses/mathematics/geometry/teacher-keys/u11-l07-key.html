Unit 11 · Lesson 07 · Teacher Key

Similarity, Scale & Volume Relationships

Instructional anchor

For similar solids only: linear measures scale by k, surface areas by k², and volumes by k³. Ask for the linear factor first so students choose the exponent intentionally rather than pattern-match numbers.

1
Answer: Surface area ×9; volume ×27. With k=3, k²=9 and k³=27.
2
Answer: k=10/4=5/2; area factor=25/4; volume factor=125/8.
3
Answer: Linear ratio 6:11; volume ratio 216:1331. Take square roots of 36:121, then cube 6:11.
4
Answer: 27:216 simplifies to 1:8, so linear ratio=1:2 and surface-area ratio=1:4.
5
Answer: 160 L. Volume factor=4³=64, so 2.5×64=160. Multiplying by 4 treats a three-dimensional measure as if it were linear.
6
Answer: Adding 2 cm does not generally double a length; scale factor is multiplicative. If the original cube edge were 2 cm, the new edge would be 4 cm and the scale factor would indeed be 2.

Misconception watch

  • Using k for area or volume.
  • Using k² for volume.
  • Applying similarity power rules when only one dimension changes.
  • Confusing additive change with multiplicative scale.
  • Taking a square root of a volume ratio instead of a cube root.

Mastery: 80% plus corrections and an explanation of why the exponent matches the dimension.
