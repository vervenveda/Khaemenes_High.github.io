Scale Plans, Rooms & Openings

Keep the lesson centered on proportional scale and spatial constraints. Export formats belong in L07.

Math Word: Scale

Expected retrieval: At 1:100, 3.2 cm represents 320 cm = 3.2 m.

Instructional anchor

Require compatible units before applying a scale ratio. A geometrically accurate scale drawing can still be a poor design if openings, clear paths, or object interference violate the stated constraints.

1
Answer: 12 cm.
Reasoning: 6.0 m=600 cm; 600÷50=12 cm.
2
Answer: 7.4 m.
Reasoning: 7.4 cm×100=740 cm=7.4 m.
3
Answer: 12 cm × 9 cm.
Reasoning: 4.8 m=480 cm and 3.6 m=360 cm; divide each by 40.
4
Answer: 1.8 cm minimum on the plan.
Reasoning: 90÷50=1.8. The swing must be checked because a correctly sized opening may still be obstructed or interfere with circulation.
5
Answer: No.
Reasoning: 1.6 cm×50=80 cm=0.80 m, which is 0.10 m less than the stated 0.90 m requirement.
6
Answer: Scale accuracy only verifies proportional dimensions; it does not guarantee usability. Acceptable checks include door swing, clear passage width, collision/interference, access route, or required clearance around objects.

Misconception watch

  • Applying the scale denominator before converting units.
  • Reversing actual-to-drawing and drawing-to-actual operations.
  • Treating a visually open path as measured clearance.
  • Assuming a correctly scaled plan automatically satisfies design constraints.

Mastery: 80% plus corrections and one CAD plan demonstrating a declared scale and a checked circulation constraint.
