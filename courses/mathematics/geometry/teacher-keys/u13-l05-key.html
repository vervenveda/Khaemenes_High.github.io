Unit 13 · Lesson 05 · Teacher Key

Present, Defend & Revise

Assess mathematical responsiveness: students should neither surrender a supported claim casually nor defend an error after evidence overturns it.

Math Word: Rebuttal

Expected retrieval: An evidence-based response showing why a challenge does not overturn a claim, or how the claim must be qualified. A rebuttal is inappropriate when the challenge exposes a verified error.

1
Expected: Concise question + principal claim + strongest evidence + known limitation. The opening should match the L01 brief and L04 portfolio rather than introducing a new project.
2
Expected: A real audience question/request and a traceable response citing a figure, calculation, proof, sensitivity test, source, or new evidence.
3
Expected: A substantive mathematical challenge and evidence checked before answering. Encourage measurement verification, recalculation, theorem conditions, model assumptions, or sensitivity evidence.
4
Expected: Classification supported by evidence. “I disagree” is not a classification rationale. Partly valid and unresolved are legitimate outcomes.
5
Expected: Valid objection → revise; existing evidence resolves it → rebut with that evidence; unresolved → specify the additional test/evidence needed. Reward intellectual flexibility.
6
Expected: Transparent revision record identifying change, cause, and effect on the conclusion. Accept “no change” only when the student demonstrates that the challenge was genuinely answered by evidence.

Defense protocol

Use questions such as: “Which assumption does that depend on?” “Where is that measurement verified?” “What theorem condition justifies that step?” “Would your conclusion survive this changed input?” “What evidence would make you change your conclusion?”

Instructional checkpoint

Do not grade confidence, charisma, speed, accent, or performance style as mathematical mastery. Grade the quality of reasoning, evidence use, response to critique, and documented revision. Allow written or supported defense formats when needed while preserving the same mathematical standard.

Mastery

80% plus corrections. Before L06, require an evidence-based defense and a revision log. The student must demonstrate willingness to correct a verified error and ability to defend a supported claim.
