Instructional focus

  • A radius to a point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • Tangent segments from the same external point are congruent.
  • Radius, tangent segment, and center-to-external-point segment form a right triangle.
  • Tangent verification requires a 90° relationship at the contact point.

Boundary: This lesson does not assess circumference, area, arc length, inscribed-angle theorems, or coordinate-circle equations.

Tangent Relationships
1

Tangent line ℓ at T and radius OT.

Answer: 90°.
Reasoning: A radius drawn to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent line.
2

PA and PB are tangent segments from P; PA=17 cm.

Answer: PB=17 cm.
Reasoning: Tangent segments from the same external point are congruent.
3

OT=8 m, OP=17 m, PT tangent.

Answer: 15 m.
Reasoning: 17²=8²+PT², so PT²=225 and PT=15.
4

PT=24 cm and OT=7 cm.

Answer: OP=25 cm.
Reasoning: OP²=7²+24²=625, so OP=25.
5

PA=3x+5 and PB=5x−9.

Answer: x=7; common tangent length=26.
Reasoning: Set tangent lengths equal: 3x+5=5x−9. Then 14=2x, so x=7. Substitute: 3(7)+5=26 and 5(7)−9=26.
6

Verify a straight support is tangent to a circular feature.

Answer: Draw or identify the radius from the center to the contact point and verify that it forms a 90° angle with the support. If the support is perpendicular to that radius at a point on the circle, it is tangent there.

Teacher prompts

Ask students why the center-to-external-point segment becomes the hypotenuse, why equal tangents must begin at the same external point, and how perpendicularity can be used as a construction test for tangency.
