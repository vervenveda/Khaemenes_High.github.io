Angles of Elevation and Depression

Model line-of-sight situations with horizontal reference lines and right triangles.

ModelingLine of sightMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Distinguish elevation from depression, transfer equal angles formed by parallel horizontals, and include observer height when the context requires it.

Success evidence

  • Draw a horizontal reference through the observer.
  • Place the angle at the correct line of sight.
  • Choose a trig ratio from the modeled triangle.
  • Add or subtract eye/instrument height only when it is outside the triangle being solved.

Visual model

tanθ=vertical/horizontalelevation: look updepression: look down

Worked reasoning

Example: A surveyor's eye is 1.6 m high and sees the top of a tree at 36° from 20 m away.

  1. The triangle's vertical leg is the rise above eye level.
  2. tan36°=x/20, so x≈14.5 m.
  3. Add eye height: 14.5+1.6≈16.1 m.
  4. State that the modeled tree height is about 16.1 m.

Misconception check: The measured angle is from a horizontal reference, not from the vertical object.
