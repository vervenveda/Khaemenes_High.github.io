Geometric Mean & Right-Triangle Altitudes

Use the three similar triangles created by an altitude to the hypotenuse to solve geometric-mean relationships.

SimilarityExact radicalsMastery target 80%

Learning intention

When an altitude from the right angle meets the hypotenuse, use similarity to justify h²=pq, a²=c·p, and b²=c·q.

Success evidence

  • Identify the two hypotenuse segments p and q.
  • Recognize the altitude as a geometric mean of p and q.
  • Recognize each leg as a geometric mean of the whole hypotenuse and its adjacent segment.
  • Choose the correct relationship from the diagram rather than memorizing symbols without meaning.

Visual model

h²=pqa²=c·pb²=c·q

Worked reasoning

Example: The altitude divides the hypotenuse into segments 4 and 9. Find the altitude.

  1. Use h²=pq because h is the geometric mean of the two segments.
  2. h²=(4)(9)=36.
  3. h=6, taking the positive length.
  4. Check that 6 lies between 4 and 9, as a geometric mean should.

Misconception check: The altitude is not the arithmetic mean of the two hypotenuse segments.
