Unit 06 · Lesson 01 · Right Triangles, Trigonometry & Bearings

Pythagorean Theorem & Its Converse

Use side-length relationships to solve right triangles and use the converse to classify triangles from three side lengths.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Apply a²+b²=c² only when the triangle is right, identify the hypotenuse correctly, and use the converse to decide whether three side lengths form a right triangle.

Success evidence

  • Identify the hypotenuse as the side opposite the right angle and the longest side.
  • Solve for an unknown side and preserve exact radical form when appropriate.
  • Use a²+b²=c² as a theorem and a²+b² ? c² as a classification test.
  • Explain why a chosen value of c must be the largest side in converse problems.

Visual model

a²+b²=c²c=√(a²+b²)a²+b²=c² ⇒ right triangle

Worked reasoning

Example: Determine whether side lengths 8, 15, 17 form a right triangle.

  1. Choose the largest side as c: c=17.
  2. Compute 8²+15²=64+225=289.
  3. Compute 17²=289.
  4. Because the values are equal, the converse of the Pythagorean theorem proves the triangle is right.

Misconception check: Do not automatically add the squares of the first two numbers given. In converse problems, the largest side must be tested as c.
