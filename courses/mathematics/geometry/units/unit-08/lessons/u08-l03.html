Inscribed Angles & Intercepted Arcs

Use the inscribed-angle theorem to move between angle measure and intercepted-arc measure, then extend the relationship to diameters and cyclic quadrilaterals.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Identify inscribed angles and their intercepted arcs, apply the one-half relationship, and justify special cases created by diameters and cyclic quadrilaterals.

Success evidence

  • Distinguish a central angle from an inscribed angle by vertex location.
  • Match an inscribed angle to the arc lying between its endpoints.
  • Use m∠ = ½(m intercepted arc) in both directions.
  • Explain why an angle subtending a diameter is 90° and why opposite angles of an inscribed quadrilateral are supplementary.

Visual model

The vertex of an inscribed angle lies on the circle. Its rays are chords, and the angle intercepts the arc between the other two endpoints.

m∠ACB = ½m⌢ABm⌢AB = 2m∠ACBdiameter arc = 180° ⇒ angle = 90°

Worked reasoning

Example: Inscribed angle ∠ACB measures 37°. Find its intercepted minor arc AB.

  1. The vertex C is on the circle, so ∠ACB is an inscribed angle.
  2. An inscribed angle equals half its intercepted arc.
  3. 37° = ½(m⌢AB), so m⌢AB = 74°.
  4. Check: the intercepted arc must be twice the angle, not equal to it.

Misconception check: The one-half rule applies to an inscribed angle. A central angle has the same measure as its intercepted arc.

Vocabulary

inscribed angle — vertex on the circle with chord sides.
intercepted arc — arc between the chord endpoints opposite the angle's vertex.
cyclic quadrilateral — quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on one circle.
subtend — to form an angle from the endpoints of an arc or segment.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Color-code angle endpoints and intercepted arcs.

Core: Solve both angle-from-arc and arc-from-angle problems.

Extended: Explain diameter and cyclic-quadrilateral consequences from the theorem.
