Unit 08 · Lesson 01 · Circles, Arcs & Tangency

Circle Vocabulary, Radius, Diameter & Chords

Build the structural language of circles before applying arc, angle, tangent, secant, and coordinate theorems.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Identify and distinguish center, radius, diameter, chord, semicircle, minor arc, major arc, and congruent radii using correct notation and diagrams.

Success evidence

  • Identify each segment by its endpoints and relationship to the center.
  • Use d=2r and r=d/2 accurately.
  • Recognize that every diameter is a chord, but not every chord is a diameter.
  • Use equal radii to justify simple congruence statements.

Visual model: one circle, different segment jobs

AB=dOC=rd=2rr=d/2

AB passes through O, so it is both a diameter and a chord. OC is a radius. DE is a chord because both endpoints lie on the circle, but it is not a diameter because it does not pass through O.

Worked reasoning

A circle has diameter 18 cm. Segment PQ joins two points on the circle but does not pass through the center. Segment OR joins the center to the circle.

  1. Radius: r=18/2=9 cm.
  2. OR is a radius, so OR=9 cm.
  3. PQ is a chord because both endpoints lie on the circle.
  4. PQ is not a diameter because it does not pass through the center.

Misconception check: A diameter is a special chord through the center. The words are related, not interchangeable.
