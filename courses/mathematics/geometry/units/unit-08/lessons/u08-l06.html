Secant, Chord & Tangent Power Theorems

Use product relationships for intersecting chords, two secants, and tangent-secant configurations.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Distinguish inside-circle and outside-circle power configurations and apply the correct product theorem with labeled segment lengths.

Success evidence

  • Identify whether the relevant point lies inside or outside the circle.
  • Use chord·chord for intersecting chords inside a circle.
  • Use external·whole for two secants from the same external point.
  • Use tangent² = external·whole for tangent-secant configurations.

Three power structures

a·b=c·de₁w₁=e₂w₂t²=e·w

Worked reasoning

Example: From external point P, a tangent has length 12 and a secant has external part 8. Find the whole secant length.

  1. The point is outside and one path is tangent, so use tangent-secant power.
  2. 12² = 8·w.
  3. 144 = 8w, so w=18.
  4. The inside portion is 18−8=10 if requested.

Misconception check: For an outside secant, multiply the external part by the whole secant—not external by internal.

Vocabulary

power of a point — common product associated with secant, tangent, or chord segments from a point.
external segment — part of a secant outside the circle.
whole secant — external plus internal length.
intersecting chords — two chords meeting inside a circle.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Sort diagrams into inside vs outside before calculating.

Core: Solve all three theorem types.

Extended: Diagnose incorrect product setups and explain why they fail.
