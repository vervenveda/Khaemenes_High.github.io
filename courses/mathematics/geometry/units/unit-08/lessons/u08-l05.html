Unit 08 · Lesson 05 · Circles, Arcs & Tangency

Tangents and Radius Relationships

Use the perpendicular radius–tangent theorem and equal tangent segments to reason about lengths, angles, and constructions.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Recognize and justify tangent relationships at a circle, then use right-triangle and congruent-segment reasoning to solve problems.

Success evidence

  • Identify a tangent and its point of tangency.
  • Use the fact that a radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • Use tangent segments drawn from the same external point as congruent segments.
  • Combine tangent theorems with Pythagorean reasoning when appropriate.

Visual model

At point T, radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent line. If a second tangent from the same external point P touches the circle at another point, the two tangent segments from P have equal length.

OT ⟂ tangent at T∠OTP = 90°PA = PB for tangents from POP² = OT² + PT²

Worked reasoning

Example: From external point P, PT is tangent to a circle centered at O. OT=9 cm and OP=15 cm. Find PT.

  1. OT is perpendicular to tangent PT, so triangle OTP is right.
  2. Use OP as the hypotenuse: 15²=9²+PT².
  3. PT²=225−81=144, so PT=12 cm.
  4. Check: 9-12-15 is a right triangle and the tangent length is positive.

Misconception check: A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point locally; it does not pass through the circle like a secant.

Vocabulary

tangent — line meeting a circle at one point.
point of tangency — the contact point.
external point — point outside the circle from which tangents may be drawn.
tangent segments — segments from an external point to points of tangency.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Mark the right angle before calculating.

Core: Solve tangent-length and equal-tangent problems.

Extended: Justify tangent verification in a construction or design context.
