Chords, Perpendicular Bisectors & Distances

Use perpendicular relationships from a circle's center to analyze chord length, chord midpoint, and distance from the center.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Apply chord theorems and right-triangle reasoning to determine missing chord lengths and distances from the center.

Success evidence

  • Explain why a radius perpendicular to a chord bisects that chord.
  • Use half a chord, the center-to-chord distance, and the radius as a right triangle.
  • Relate congruent chords to equal distances from the center.
  • Check that calculated distances and lengths are geometrically possible.

Visual model

If OM is perpendicular to chord AB, then M is the midpoint of AB. The radius OB, distance OM, and half-chord MB form a right triangle.

AM = MB = AB/2OM ⟂ ABr² = d² + (c/2)²equal chords ⇔ equal center distances

Worked reasoning

Example: A circle has radius 13 cm. A chord is 10 cm long. Find the perpendicular distance from the center to the chord.

  1. The perpendicular from the center bisects the chord, so the half-chord is 5 cm.
  2. The radius is the hypotenuse of a right triangle: 13²=d²+5².
  3. d²=169−25=144, so d=12 cm.
  4. Check: 5-12-13 is a right triangle and 12<13, so the distance is possible.

Misconception check: Use half the chord in the right triangle, not the entire chord.

Vocabulary

chord — segment with endpoints on a circle.
perpendicular bisector — a line perpendicular to a segment through its midpoint.
distance from center to chord — shortest, perpendicular center-to-chord distance.
congruent chords — chords of equal length.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Identify midpoint and right triangle before calculating.

Core: Solve chord-length and center-distance problems.

Extended: Explain and apply equal-chord/equal-distance relationships.
