Central Angles, Arcs & Arc Length

Connect a central angle to its intercepted arc, then use that fraction of a full turn to determine arc length.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Relate central-angle measure, intercepted-arc measure, circumference, and arc length using proportional reasoning.

Success evidence

  • Identify the center, radii, central angle, and intercepted minor or major arc.
  • Explain why a central angle and its intercepted arc have the same degree measure.
  • Use the fraction θ/360° to calculate exact arc length.
  • Reverse the relationship to determine a missing central angle and justify the result.

Visual model

A central angle has its vertex at the center. Its degree measure equals the degree measure of its intercepted arc. A 90° central angle therefore intercepts one quarter of the circle.

m∠AOB = m⌢ABarc fraction = θ/360°C = 2πrs = (θ/360°)(2πr)

Worked reasoning

Example: A circle has radius 12 cm. Find the length of the minor arc intercepted by a 75° central angle.

  1. The arc is 75/360 of the full circle.
  2. The circumference is 2π(12)=24π cm.
  3. s=(75/360)(24π)=5π cm.
  4. Check: 75° is a little more than one fifth of 360°, so 5π is a little more than one fifth of 24π. The scale is reasonable.

Misconception check: Arc measure is measured in degrees; arc length is a distance. Keep exact answers in terms of π unless a decimal approximation is requested.

Vocabulary

central angle — vertex at the center.
intercepted arc — arc between the angle's rays.
minor arc — less than 180°.
major arc — more than 180°.
arc measure — degree measure.
arc length — distance along the circle.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Begin with quarter-, half-, and three-quarter circles.

Core: Solve forward and reverse arc-length problems in exact form.

Extended: Compare arcs across different radii and defend conclusions proportionally.

Calculation check

Do the geometry first and keep exact π-form answers. Then use the calculator only to verify decimal approximations when useful.
