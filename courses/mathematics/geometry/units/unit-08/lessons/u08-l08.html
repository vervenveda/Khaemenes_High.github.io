Unit 08 · Lesson 08 · Circles, Arcs & Tangency

Equation of a Circle & Coordinate Applications

Read, build, and verify circle equations from centers, radii, points, and coordinate geometry.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Connect the geometric definition of a circle—points a fixed distance from a center—to its standard coordinate equation.

Success evidence

  • Identify center and radius from standard form.
  • Write standard form from a known center and radius.
  • Use the distance relationship to find a radius from a center and a point.
  • Test whether a point lies inside, on, or outside a circle.

Coordinate model

The horizontal and vertical changes from (h,k) to (x,y) form a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the radius. The Pythagorean theorem gives the circle equation.

(x−h)²+(y−k)²=r²center=(h,k)radius=√(r²)distance²=(Δx)²+(Δy)²

Worked reasoning

Example: Write the equation of the circle centered at (3,−2) and passing through (9,6).

  1. Find radius squared from the center to the point: r²=(9−3)²+(6−(−2))².
  2. r²=6²+8²=36+64=100, so r=10.
  3. Insert h=3, k=−2, and r²=100.
  4. The equation is (x−3)²+(y+2)²=100.

Misconception check: Standard form contains opposite-looking signs: center (3,−2) produces (x−3)²+(y+2)².

Vocabulary

standard form — (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r².
center — fixed point (h,k).
radius — fixed distance from center to circle.
locus — set of points satisfying a condition.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Decode signs and identify center/radius.

Core: Build equations from geometric information.

Extended: Use midpoint and distance reasoning in coordinate applications.
