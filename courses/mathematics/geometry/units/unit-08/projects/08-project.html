Applied investigation · Unit 08

Circle-Theorem Investigation & Radial Design

Create an inspectable circular design or investigation in which geometry, evidence, and revision all matter.

Design brief

Choose a circular object, radial design, site plan, wheel, ring, window, pattern, or original construction. Your work must be understandable without opening any digital tool.

  1. State the question, audience, constraints, units, and success criteria.
  2. Create a labeled planning sketch or construction showing center, radii, chords/arcs, and any tangents, secants, or coordinate information used.
  3. Demonstrate at least three different Unit 08 relationships, drawn from at least three lessons.
  4. Include at least one exact result using π, a radical, or an exact product relationship before any decimal approximation.
  5. Verify one result using a second method, construction, measurement, or the scientific calculator.
  6. Record one mathematical claim and its supporting evidence. The Evidence & Citation Studio may be used for sources, theorem notes, or provenance when outside research is included.
  7. Explain assumptions, precision, limitations, and one revision made after checking the design.
  8. Submit a printable one- to three-page portfolio plus optional source/export files.

Suggested investigation pathways

  • Radial design: arcs, central/inscribed angles, sectors, segments, annuli, symmetry.
  • Tangent construction: radius-tangent perpendicularity, equal tangent segments, power relationships.
  • Coordinate model: center/radius, diameter endpoints, standard-form equation, point classification.
  • Chord investigation: perpendicular bisectors, center distance, congruent chords, power of a point.

Tool-use boundary

Digital tools may support exploration, verification, design, or documentation. They do not replace the labeled diagram, governing theorem/formula, exact mathematical work, or written justification required in the portfolio.
