Unit 12 · Extended Pathway

CAD, Scale Drawing & Spatial Design

Generalize, audit, optimize, and defend. Extended work uses the same seven lesson concepts at greater depth rather than merely adding larger numbers.

1 · L01

Develop a rule for choosing a snap increment from a project's smallest meaningful dimension. Explain the tradeoff between precision and drafting efficiency.

2 · L01

Audit a hypothetical CAD file whose objects were drawn under mixed unit assumptions. Propose a verification sequence that could detect whether geometry was rescaled or merely relabeled.

3 · L02

Derive a general pass condition for target dimension T with symmetric tolerance ±t and measured value m. Express it as an inequality and apply it to T=125, t=0.8, m=124.1.

4 · L02

Two measurements have absolute errors 0.5 mm: one measures a 10 mm feature and one a 500 mm feature. Compare relative errors and explain why absolute error alone can mislead.

5 · L03

Design a layer naming and locking convention for a multi-user plan. Include a rule that prevents annotation geometry from being mistaken for physical geometry.

6 · L03

Explain how selective visibility can function as a mathematical audit tool rather than merely a visual convenience. Give two examples.

7 · L04

A plan must fit a usable drawing width of 24 cm while representing an actual 18 m width. Determine the smallest common integer scale denominator that will fit, then compute the resulting drawing width.

8 · L04

A designer proposes widening a doorway from 0.90 m to 1.05 m on a 1:50 plan. Quantify the plan change and explain how a small drawing change can represent a meaningful spatial change.

9 · L05

Create a general expression for purchased area when base area is A, waste rate is w, package coverage is c, and only whole packages can be bought. Explain why a ceiling operation is required.

10 · L05

Compare two flooring layouts for the same 42 m² room: Layout A uses 6% waste and Layout B 11%. If material costs $28/m² before package rounding, quantify the waste-cost difference and identify what evidence could justify Layout B.

11 · L06

Prove using the distance formula that translation by vector ⟨a,b⟩ preserves the distance between any two points P(x₁,y₁) and Q(x₂,y₂).

12 · L06

A modular design has order-n rotational symmetry. Derive the smallest positive rotational angle in terms of n. Then explain what changes if every module is dilated by k about its own center.

13 · L07

Derive a formula for the actual print-scale denominator N when an actual length A and printed length p are expressed in the same units. Use it to audit an 8 m feature printing at 15.6 cm.

14 · L07

Write a compact design-defense rubric with four mathematical criteria. Explain how each criterion distinguishes evidence from aesthetic preference.

Extended evidence

Strong responses generalize procedures, expose assumptions, verify with a second representation or method, and connect CAD operations to mathematical invariants and constraints. Course mastery remains 80% plus corrections.
