Unit 12 · Family Guide

CAD, Scale Drawing & Spatial Design

Learners use geometry to create technical drawings that are measurable, organized, scaled, and defensible—not simply attractive.

What your learner will study

The seven lessons move through CAD setup, precision and tolerance, layers and object organization, scale plans and openings, material quantities, transformations and modular design, and finally export/print verification and mathematical design defense.

The daily Math Words are snap, tolerance, layer, scale, quantity, transformation, and vector.

Helpful questions at home

  • “What units and scale are you using?”
  • “How precise is that measurement, and what variation is acceptable?”
  • “Why did you place those objects on separate layers?”
  • “Does the door or passage actually have enough clear space?”
  • “Does this material need length, area, or a package quantity?”
  • “Which transformation created that repeated pattern?”
  • “How did you verify that the printed/PDF drawing is really at the intended scale?”

Practice & mastery

Foundation, Core, and Extended are temporary support/challenge pathways. The Unit-12 mastery check contains 14 questions—two from each lesson. Mastery target is 80% plus corrections.

If the first attempt is below mastery, the learner reviews the specific lesson concepts, corrects missed work with reasoning, completes targeted practice, and then reassesses.

Portfolio

The OHMIC CAD Spatial Design Portfolio asks learners to produce a real technical design with documented setup, scale, precision, layers, material quantities, transformations, output verification, and a short mathematical design defense. A satisfactory portfolio also uses an 80% mastery threshold with correction of material errors.

Positive reinforcement

Some successful lesson checkpoints unlock optional Geometry Arcade or logic challenges. These are celebrations and extra practice only; they do not raise or lower grades and never replace required work or corrections.
