📚 Math Word of the Day: Transformation

Technical definition: A transformation is a rule that maps every point of a figure to an image point.

Student definition: A transformation moves or changes a figure according to a precise rule.

Example: Rotating a tile 90° about a fixed center maps every vertex to a new location while preserving side lengths and angles.

Retrieval check: Which common transformation changes size while preserving shape? Dilation.

Learning intention

Generate modular designs with transformations and explain which properties are preserved or changed.

Success evidence

  • Represent translations with vectors or coordinate rules.
  • Rotate figures about a declared center and angle.
  • Reflect figures across a specified line.
  • Distinguish rigid transformations from dilation.
  • Use repeated modules to create a pattern with a stated symmetry or design rule.
  • Verify repeated geometry rather than relying on visual approximation.

One module, many images

T⟨a,b⟩:(x,y)→(x+a,y+b)90° CCW: (x,y)→(−y,x)reflect y-axis: (x,y)→(−x,y)dilation k: (x,y)→(kx,ky)

Worked reasoning

Module: triangle A(1,1), B(4,1), C(1,3).

  1. Translate by ⟨5,0⟩: A′(6,1), B′(9,1), C′(6,3).
  2. Rotate the original 90° counterclockwise about the origin: A″(−1,1), B″(−1,4), C″(−3,1).
  3. Reflect the original across the y-axis: (−1,1), (−4,1), (−1,3).
  4. A translation, rotation, and reflection preserve distances and angle measures; they are rigid motions.
  5. A dilation with k=2 doubles every distance from the center and multiplies area by 4.

Misconception: Copying a shape by eye is repetition, but not necessarily a verified transformation. A transformation needs a rule.

Invariant check

Rigid motions preserve length, angle measure, area, parallelism, and shape.

Dilation check

Lengths scale by |k|; areas scale by k².
