📚 Math Word of the Day: Scale

Technical definition: A scale is a proportional relationship between a measurement in a representation and the corresponding measurement in the real object or space.

Student definition: Scale tells how drawing size and real size correspond.

Example: At 1:50, 1 cm on the plan represents 50 cm in the room.

Retrieval check: At 1:100, what actual distance does 3.2 cm represent? Answer after reasoning: 3.2 m.

Learning intention

Use proportional reasoning to draft rooms and openings at scale, then evaluate whether spatial constraints are satisfied.

Success evidence

  • Interpret scale notation correctly and keep units compatible.
  • Convert actual dimensions to drawing dimensions and reverse the process.
  • Represent walls, doors, and openings with defensible dimensions.
  • Check clear width, door swing, and circulation rather than judging by appearance.
  • Explain whether a proposed layout satisfies stated constraints.

Worked reasoning

Example: A room is 7.5 m by 4.2 m. Draw it at 1:50.

  1. Convert meters to centimeters: 750 cm by 420 cm.
  2. Divide each actual dimension by 50.
  3. Plan dimensions are 15 cm by 8.4 cm.
  4. A 90 cm doorway becomes 1.8 cm on the plan.
  5. After drawing the opening, test the door swing and required clear path; scale accuracy alone does not guarantee a usable layout.

Misconception: 1:50 means one drawing unit represents fifty of the same real units. Keep units compatible before applying the ratio.

OHMIC CAD lab

Draft one rectangular room at a declared scale. Include at least one doorway with swing, one fixed object, one movable object, dimensions, and a labeled clear circulation route. Predict dimensions on paper first; use CAD to verify the plan.

4

A doorway must provide 90 cm of clear width. What minimum plan width represents it at 1:50? Explain why the door swing must also be checked.

5

A plan shows a 1.6 cm passage at 1:50. The design requirement is at least 0.90 m clear width. Does the passage satisfy the stated requirement? Show the comparison.

6

A student accurately scales every wall but places a table so it blocks the only doorway. Explain why the drawing can be mathematically scaled yet still fail the design problem. Name two checks that should be added.

Scale discipline

Convert to compatible units before multiplying or dividing.

Spatial discipline

Check openings, swing, clear width, routes, and object interference.
