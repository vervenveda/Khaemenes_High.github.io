Math Word of the Day · Tolerance

tolerance — the permitted amount a measured or manufactured dimension may vary from its target value.

Student definition: How far a measurement is allowed to be above or below the intended size and still count as acceptable.

Example: A part labeled 40.00 mm ± 0.05 mm is acceptable from 39.95 mm through 40.05 mm.

Quick check: If a 25.0 mm dimension has tolerance ±0.2 mm, what is the acceptable interval?

Learning intention

Use exact coordinates, lengths, and angles in CAD, then communicate precision responsibly with dimensions and tolerances.

Success evidence

  • Construct line segments and angles from exact numerical specifications.
  • Distinguish a target dimension from its tolerance interval.
  • Compute absolute and relative error.
  • Report significant figures consistent with measurement precision.
  • Recognize that displayed decimal digits do not automatically imply real accuracy.

Precision model

absolute error = |measured − target|relative error = absolute error / |target|acceptable interval = target ± tolerance

Worked reasoning

A slot is designed to be 75.00 mm with tolerance ±0.10 mm. A prototype measures 74.94 mm.

  1. Acceptable interval: 74.90 to 75.10 mm.
  2. The measured 74.94 mm is inside the interval, so it passes.
  3. Absolute error = |74.94−75.00| = 0.06 mm.
  4. Relative error = 0.06/75.00 = 0.0008 = 0.08%.

CAD habit: Dimension labels communicate design intent; they do not excuse imprecise construction. Draw accurately first, annotate clearly second.
