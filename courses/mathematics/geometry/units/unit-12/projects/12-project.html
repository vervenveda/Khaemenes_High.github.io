OHMIC CAD Spatial Design Portfolio

How can precise digital drafting turn geometric constraints into measurable, auditable, printable, and defensible designs?

Use OHMIC CAD

Design brief

Create a room, small site, modular installation, display system, or other spatial design that uses the full Unit-12 workflow. The finished portfolio must allow another learner to inspect the assumptions, reproduce key measurements, challenge the decisions, and verify the final output.

Required evidence

  1. Setup record: units, workspace/boundary, grid, snap, and recovery/source-file strategy.
  2. Precision record: at least one target dimension with tolerance or stated precision and a note about measurement limitations.
  3. Organization: purposeful layers/groups with one documented visibility or locking decision.
  4. Scale plan: declared scale, labeled room/site dimensions, at least one opening, and one checked circulation or clearance constraint.
  5. Material schedule: at least two area-based quantities and one length-based quantity, with units, adjustments/waste assumptions, and final order quantities where appropriate.
  6. Transformation evidence: one repeated or modular feature created with a declared translation, rotation, reflection, or dilation rule.
  7. Output evidence: editable/source artifact plus a presentation/print output; include a known dimension or scale bar and verify printed/PDF scale mathematically.
  8. Design defense: 60–90 seconds or a concise written equivalent containing at least three mathematical claims, one limitation, and one revision.

Audit trail

For every major quantity or decision, identify the source measurement, mathematical relationship, unit, adjustment/assumption, and verification method. CAD output alone is not sufficient evidence.

Tool rule: OHMIC CAD and the scientific calculator may verify geometry or arithmetic, but the portfolio must preserve the setup, calculations, reasoning, and units that make the result auditable.

Rubric · 24 points

  • 4 — CAD setup, units, precision, and organization are controlled and documented.
  • 4 — Scale plan, openings, and spatial constraints are mathematically correct.
  • 4 — Area/perimeter/material quantities are accurate, unit-labeled, and assumption-aware.
  • 4 — Transformation/modular evidence uses explicit rules and geometric reasoning.
  • 4 — Source/output files and print-scale verification are complete and auditable.
  • 4 — Design defense uses evidence, identifies limitations, and documents meaningful revision.

Project mastery: 20/24 or better (at least 80%) with correction of material mathematical errors.
