Frame a Geometric Design or Investigation

Turn an idea into a mathematically testable capstone brief by defining the question, audience, constraints, measurable quantities, assumptions, and success criteria.

📚 Math Word of the Day: Constraint

Technical definition: A constraint is a requirement or limitation that a mathematical design or model must satisfy.

Student definition: A constraint is a rule your solution has to obey.

Example: “The walkway must be at least 1.2 m wide” is a measurable geometric constraint.

Retrieval check: Which is a constraint: “make it attractive” or “fit inside a 6 m × 8 m site”? The second is measurable and testable.

Learning intention

Write a capstone brief that another learner could understand, test, and reproduce.

Success evidence

  • State one focused geometric question or design problem.
  • Name the intended user or audience.
  • List measurable constraints and distinguish them from preferences.
  • Identify known measurements, unknown quantities, and assumptions.
  • Define success criteria before building the model.
  • Select at least two geometry ideas from earlier units that may support the investigation.

From idea to testable brief

questionconstraintsmeasurementsassumptionssuccess criteria

A capstone question should be narrow enough to answer with evidence. “Design a park” is too broad. “How can a 12 m × 18 m rectangular site contain a circular garden, a 1.5 m minimum-width path, and at least 120 m² of usable open area?” is testable.

Worked framing example

Idea: Design a compact study room.

  1. Question: Can a 4.8 m × 6.0 m room hold six study stations while maintaining a 1.0 m clear circulation route?
  2. Audience: School design team.
  3. Constraints: six stations; 1.0 m clear route; one 0.9 m doorway kept unobstructed.
  4. Known quantities: room dimensions and doorway width.
  5. Assumptions: wall thickness ignored; stations modeled as rectangles.
  6. Success criteria: all six stations fit, no overlap occurs, and measured circulation remains at least 1.0 m.
  7. Geometry: scale drawing, area, transformations/repetition, distance and clearance.

Tool planning

Choose tools after the question is framed. OHMIC CAD can support scale drawings and verification; the Scientific Calculator can check numerical work. The tool should serve the investigation, not determine the investigation.

Khaemenes Academy · Geometry Capstone

Capstone Design Brief

Write responses another person could audit.

U13 · L01
1

Write one focused geometric design or investigation question. It must include something measurable and a decision to be made.

2

Identify the audience or user for your investigation. Explain one way that audience affects the design criteria.

3

List at least three constraints. Mark each as geometric, practical, or communication-related.

4

Separate your starting information into verified measurements, unknown quantities, and assumptions.

5

Write at least three measurable success criteria. Explain how each could be checked later.

6

Name at least two geometry concepts from earlier units that your capstone may use. Explain why each is relevant.

Framing rule

If the question cannot be measured or tested, refine it before modeling.

Assumption rule

Assumptions are allowed—but they must be visible and revisable.
