📚 Math Word of the Day: Traceability

Technical definition: Traceability is the ability to follow a conclusion backward through its evidence, calculations, measurements, assumptions, and sources.

Student definition: Someone should be able to see where your answer came from without guessing.

Retrieval check: A portfolio states “area = 48 m²” but shows no dimensions or calculation. Is the claim traceable? No.

Learning intention

Organize capstone evidence so that the mathematical reasoning is complete, readable, auditable, and usable in print or PDF form.

Success evidence

  • Present the L01 question, audience, constraints, and success criteria.
  • Include labeled diagrams and the L02 mathematical model with claims and validation.
  • Include L03 sensitivity results, limitations, and revisions.
  • Connect each major conclusion to visible evidence.
  • Use consistent units, notation, figure labels, and captions.
  • Audit the printed/PDF output for legibility and completeness.

Recommended portfolio sequence

  1. Title + design brief: question, audience, constraints, success criteria.
  2. Geometry/model: diagrams, dimensions, constructions, formulas, claims.
  3. Validation: independent check or second representation.
  4. Stress test: low/base/high results and sensitivity judgment.
  5. Limitations + revision: bounded claims and documented changes.
  6. Conclusion: answer the original question using evidence.
  7. Evidence appendix: supporting calculations or source notes needed for audit.

From weak page to technical page

Weak: “The layout works. Area = 48 m².”

Traceable: “The usable region is modeled as an 8.0 m × 6.0 m rectangle, so A=lw=(8.0)(6.0)=48.0 m². Dimensions are shown in Figure 2. A coordinate-area check produced the same result. Sensitivity testing of width from 5.8–6.2 m produced 46.4–49.6 m²; the layout remains above the 45 m² success threshold.”

Portfolio rule: Visual polish improves communication, but it cannot substitute for missing measurements, calculations, proof, validation, or limitations.

Technical Portfolio Audit

Use the actual portfolio draft when answering.

U13 · L04
1

Write your portfolio's opening design/investigation statement in 2–4 sentences. Include the question, audience or purpose, and at least two measurable constraints.

2

Choose one major conclusion. Create its traceability chain: conclusion → calculation/proof → measurements/givens → assumption or source.

3

List the figures, diagrams, tables, or constructions your portfolio needs. For each, state what mathematical claim it supports.

4

Write the portfolio section that reports your L03 sensitivity test and limitation. Include numerical evidence and any revision made.

5

Perform a notation/unit audit. Identify at least four items you checked and record any correction you made.

6

Perform a print/PDF audit. Record at least four checks for legibility, page integrity, figure/caption visibility, and evidence completeness. Fix any failure before L05.

Capstone chain

L01 brief → L02 model → L03 stress test → L04 technical record → L05 defense.

Reader test

Can a careful reader reconstruct why each major conclusion is justified?
