Test Measurements, Sensitivity & Limitations

Stress-test the L02 model by changing uncertain inputs, checking measurement quality, and identifying where the conclusion is robust or fragile.

📚 Math Word of the Day: Sensitivity

Technical definition: Sensitivity describes how much a model output or conclusion changes when an input, measurement, parameter, or assumption changes.

Student definition: Sensitivity asks, “If this input is a little different, does my answer barely move—or change a lot?”

Retrieval check: If a 1% change in an input causes a large change in the conclusion, is the model relatively sensitive or insensitive? Sensitive.

Learning intention

Evaluate the reliability of the capstone model by testing measurement uncertainty, varying important inputs, and documenting limitations honestly.

Success evidence

  • Separate measured values from assumed or fixed values.
  • State reasonable uncertainty or a test range for at least one important input.
  • Recalculate the model at low/base/high input values.
  • Determine whether the conclusion remains stable.
  • Identify at least one limitation and explain what evidence would reduce it.
  • Revise the model or claim when testing exposes a weakness.

Stress-test structure

input → base resultinput − Δ → low resultinput + Δ → high resultcompare → robust or fragile?

A limitation is not a confession that the project failed. It defines where the evidence is strong, where it is uncertain, and what the model should not claim.

Worked sensitivity test

A rectangular design uses a measured length of 10.0 m and width of 6.0 m. The width could reasonably vary by ±0.2 m.

  1. Base area: 10.0×6.0=60.0 m².
  2. Low case: 10.0×5.8=58.0 m².
  3. High case: 10.0×6.2=62.0 m².
  4. The tested uncertainty changes area by ±2.0 m², or about ±3.33% from the base result.
  5. If a design decision only works above 61 m², the decision is fragile because a plausible measurement change can reverse it.

Important: Sensitivity analysis does not automatically tell you the correct uncertainty range. The range itself needs a defensible measurement or assumption basis.

Capstone stress-test lab

Return to your L02 model. Select at least two important inputs or assumptions. For each, record the base value, a defensible low/high test range, the resulting outputs, and whether the original conclusion survives. Add one limitation statement and one revision or verification action.

Sensitivity & Limitations Audit

Use your own capstone evidence wherever possible.

U13 · L03
1

Identify one measured input and one assumed input in your model. Explain how you know which is which.

2

Choose one important input. State its base value and a defensible low/high test range. Explain why that range is reasonable.

3

Recalculate the relevant model output for the low, base, and high cases. Include units and show the governing relationship.

4

Does the capstone conclusion remain the same across the tested range? Classify it as relatively robust or fragile and cite the numerical evidence.

5

Write one precise limitation statement. It must identify what the current model does not establish and what additional measurement, theorem, test, or evidence would reduce the limitation.

6

Describe one revision you will make because of this stress test—or explain, with evidence, why no model revision is needed.

Robust ≠ perfect

A robust conclusion survives reasonable changes in tested inputs. It can still have stated limitations.
