Unit 13 · Foundation Pathway

Capstone Reasoning with Scaffolds

Two supported tasks from each capstone stage. Use the sentence frames and prompts to make the reasoning visible.

1 · L01 Constraint

A community garden design must fit inside a 12 m × 18 m site and include a path at least 1.2 m wide. Name the two measurable constraints. Frame: “The design must ___ and ___.”

2 · L01 Constraint

Which is a testable success criterion: “looks welcoming” or “provides at least 80 m² of planting area”? Explain why.

3 · L02 Validation

A rectangle measures 8 m by 6 m. Calculate its area. Then name a different way you could check the result without simply repeating the same multiplication.

4 · L02 Validation

Label each statement measured, assumed, or proven: (a) a tape measure reads 5.2 m; (b) opposite sides of a rectangle are congruent; (c) the model treats a curved edge as straight.

5 · L03 Sensitivity

A model has base area 60 m², low case 58 m², and high case 62 m². If success requires at least 55 m², does the decision survive all three tested cases?

6 · L03 Sensitivity

Complete: “One limitation of this model is ___ . Evidence that could reduce this limitation is ___.” Use a real or plausible geometry model.

7 · L04 Traceability

Put these in a traceable order: measured dimensions; area conclusion; A=lw calculation; source/measurement record.

8 · L04 Traceability

A portfolio says “area = 48 m²” but gives no dimensions or work. Name two pieces of evidence that should be added.

9 · L05 Rebuttal

A reviewer asks, “Where did the 48 m² result come from?” Is this a clarification/request for evidence or a valid objection? What should the student do?

10 · L05 Rebuttal

A reviewer verifies that a measurement used in the model is wrong and the error changes the conclusion. Should the student defend the old answer or revise it? Explain.

11 · L06 Sufficiency

A triangle has sides 6 cm and 9 cm. Can you determine its exact area from that information alone? Choose: yes / no. If no, name one useful missing measurement.

12 · L06 Sufficiency

A quadrilateral looks rectangular, but only opposite sides are marked parallel. Can you assume its angles are 90°? Explain the rule: “looks ___ given.”

Foundation mastery habit

Before answering, underline what is given, circle what is asked, and label anything that is only an assumption. Mastery remains 80% plus corrections.
