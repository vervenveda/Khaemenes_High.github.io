Unit 13 · Core Pathway

Independent Capstone Reasoning

Two independent tasks from each lesson. Show enough evidence that another learner can audit every conclusion.

1 · L01

Frame a geometric investigation for redesigning a 9 m × 14 m room. Include audience/purpose, two measurable constraints, and one quantitative success criterion.

2 · L01

A design brief says, “Create the best possible layout.” Diagnose why this is mathematically weak and rewrite it as a testable question.

3 · L02

A rectangular model is 7.5 m by 4.8 m. Find its area and propose an independent validation method. Explain why repeating 7.5×4.8 on another calculator is weak validation.

4 · L02

Write a claim–evidence–reasoning chain proving that a translated polygon preserves one selected side length. Identify what is proven rather than merely measured.

5 · L03

A 10 m length is fixed while width is 6.0±0.3 m. Calculate low/base/high areas. If success requires 58 m², classify the decision as robust or fragile over the tested range.

6 · L03

Write a precise limitation for a model based on one measurement taken at only one location. State what additional evidence would reduce the limitation.

7 · L04

Build a traceability chain for this conclusion: “The corridor passes the width requirement.” Include the conclusion, conversion/calculation, measured plan width, scale, and requirement source.

8 · L04

A polished portfolio has beautiful diagrams but no figure labels, units, validation, or limitations. Identify four repairs required before defense and explain why visual quality alone is insufficient.

9 · L05

A critic claims your model is invalid because “the drawing looks too narrow.” Your verified dimensions and scale agree with the model. Classify the challenge and write an evidence-based response.

10 · L05

A reviewer identifies that your theorem requires parallel lines, but your portfolio never establishes parallelism. What should happen to the proof and revision log?

11 · L06

A diagram shows an angle that appears to be 90°, but no right-angle mark or theorem establishes it. Another unused side length is provided. What conclusion about the angle is justified, and what data are irrelevant?

12 · L06

A model's outputs are 72, 70, and 74 units across its tested range; the decision threshold is 68. Explain why “the output changes” does not by itself prove the decision is fragile.

Core standard

Distinguish calculation from justification. A correct number supported by invalid reasoning is not full mastery. 80% plus corrections.
