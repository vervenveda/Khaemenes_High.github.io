Unit 13 · Extended Pathway

Capstone Audit, Generalization & Counterexample

Generalize the capstone habits, expose hidden assumptions, construct counterexamples, and evaluate the limits of evidence.

1 · L01

Create a design question with at least three measurable constraints that can conflict with one another. Explain how you would rank or optimize them rather than pretending all goals can always be maximized.

2 · L01

Explain why “best,” “safe,” or “efficient” are not automatically mathematical success criteria. Convert one into an operational criterion and identify what remains outside the model.

3 · L02

Construct an example in which two methods agree because they share the same incorrect input. Explain why method independence alone does not protect against bad source data.

4 · L02

A CAD measurement and analytic calculation disagree. Design an audit sequence that can determine whether the error lies in coordinates, units, geometry construction, formula selection, or software settings.

5 · L03

Let A=Lw. Derive how a small change Δw changes A when L is fixed. Then explain the difference between absolute sensitivity ΔA and relative sensitivity ΔA/A.

6 · L03

Give an example of a model that is numerically sensitive but decision-robust, and another that is numerically modest but decision-fragile because it lies near a threshold.

7 · L04

Design a traceability convention for a technical portfolio so every major claim can be traced to figures, calculations, source measurements, assumptions, and revisions without duplicating the entire document.

8 · L04

A reader can reproduce every calculation but cannot determine where the original measurements came from. Is the portfolio mathematically traceable? Defend your answer and propose a repair.

9 · L05

Construct one challenge that should cause a capstone claim to be revised and one superficially persuasive challenge that should not. Explain the evidence standard separating them.

10 · L05

Explain why “defending your work” and “defending your original conclusion” are not equivalent. Describe what intellectual honesty requires when critique overturns a premise.

11 · L06

Create a geometry problem whose diagram strongly suggests a false conclusion but whose stated facts do not support it. Then identify the exact unsupported inference a careful reader must reject.

12 · L06

A conclusion is true, but the student's proof contains an invalid step. Explain why the proof still fails. Give an example where the same distinction matters outside geometry.

Extended standard

Strong work identifies not only what the evidence supports, but also what it cannot support. Generalization must preserve conditions; counterexamples must target the actual claim. 80% plus corrections.
