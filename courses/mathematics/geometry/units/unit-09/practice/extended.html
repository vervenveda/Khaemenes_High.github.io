Unit 09

Extended Practice

Analytic Geometry, Loci & Conic Connections

Prove, generalize, model, or audit each result. Use a second method when useful.

1

L01 · A student marks five points 3 units from A and says the locus is those five points. Explain the error and describe the complete locus.

2

L01 · Write symbolic conditions for (a) the boundary of a radius-r circle and (b) its closed interior. Explain the role of equality.

3

L02 · Prove using distance or symmetry that every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is equidistant from A and B.

4

L02 · Three noncollinear sites define pairwise perpendicular bisectors. Explain why their common intersection is equidistant from all three and connect it to circumcenter/Voronoi thinking.

5

L03 · Derive or justify why the absolute value is necessary in d=|Ax₀+By₀+C|/√(A²+B²).

6

L03 · A circle center is C(4,−1), radius 6. Find a value of k so line 3x+4y+k=0 is tangent. Show all possibilities if more than one exists.

7

L04 · Construct a line-line system with infinitely many solutions and explain the geometric condition that causes this.

8

L04 · A system gives candidate intersections (2,3) and (−1,6). Design a verification checklist that would reject an extraneous point after algebraic manipulation.

9

L05 · Explain how the discriminant of the substituted quadratic predicts secant, tangent, or external circle-line geometry.

10

L05 · For circle x²+y²=25, find all horizontal tangent lines and verify them by both distance and substitution.

11

L06 · Starting from focus (0,2) and directrix y=−2, derive x²=8y from the equal-distance definition.

12

L06 · A parabola has vertex (3,−2) and directrix x=7. Determine focus, p, opening direction, and equation; explain the sign of p.

13

L07 · Explain why an entire pairwise perpendicular bisector may be clipped in a multi-site Voronoi diagram. Include a third-site counterexample in words or coordinates.

14

L07 · Design a three-station service map. State site coordinates, identify at least two boundary equations, and name one real-world constraint that Euclidean distance fails to model.
