Parabolas as Distance Loci

Build the parabola from its defining condition: equal distance from a fixed point and a fixed line.

Focus + directrixCoordinatesMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Use the focus-directrix definition to identify, construct, and derive simple parabolas.

Success evidence

  • Define a parabola as a complete distance locus.
  • Identify focus, directrix, vertex, axis of symmetry, and parameter p.
  • Verify a point using equal distances.
  • Connect focus/directrix data to standard parabola equations.

The defining condition

PF = distance(P,directrix)(x−h)²=4p(y−k)focus (h,k+p)directrix y=k−p

The vertex lies halfway between focus and directrix along the axis of symmetry. The sign of p determines opening direction.

Derive one from the definition

Example: Focus F(0,2), directrix y=−2.

  1. Vertex V(0,0), so p=2.
  2. PF=√(x²+(y−2)²).
  3. Distance to the directrix is |y+2|. Equate and square: x²+(y−2)²=(y+2)².
  4. Simplify: x²=8y, matching x²=4py.

Meaning before formula: The equation comes from the equal-distance condition.

Vocabulary

focus — fixed point.
directrix — fixed line.
vertex — point halfway between focus and directrix.
axis of symmetry — line through vertex and focus.
p — signed vertex-to-focus distance.

Orientation

x squared → vertical axis. y squared → horizontal axis. Positive p opens up/right; negative p opens down/left.
