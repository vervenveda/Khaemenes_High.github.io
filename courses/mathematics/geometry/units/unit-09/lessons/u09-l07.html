Voronoi Thinking, Nearest-Point Regions & Design

Partition space by nearest-site distance and use perpendicular-bisector boundaries to solve design problems.

Loci synthesisDesign applicationMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Construct and interpret nearest-point regions from multiple fixed sites.

Success evidence

  • Explain why a boundary between two sites lies on their perpendicular bisector.
  • Determine which site owns a test point by comparing distances.
  • Recognize Voronoi vertices as points equidistant from three or more relevant sites.
  • Use nearest-site regions in a service, safety, ecology, or design context.

From one bisector to a partition

For sites A and B, points on their boundary satisfy d(P,A)=d(P,B). That is exactly the perpendicular-bisector locus from Lesson 02. With several sites, keep the portions of those bisectors where the tied sites are also nearest among all sites.

region(A): d(P,A) ≤ d(P,other sites)boundary AB: d(P,A)=d(P,B)vertex: multiple nearest-site ties

Worked design reasoning

Sites: A(0,0), B(8,0), C(4,6).

  1. A/B boundary: x=4.
  2. A/C midpoint is (2,3); slope AC=3/2, so its perpendicular bisector has slope −2/3 through (2,3).
  3. B/C midpoint is (6,3); slope BC=−3/2, so its perpendicular bisector has slope 2/3 through (6,3).
  4. The three bisectors meet at (4,5/3). This point is equidistant from A, B, and C and is a Voronoi vertex for the three-site configuration.

Design meaning: A boundary is not a wall placed arbitrarily; it records a change in which site is nearest.

Two clinics are at A(0,0) and B(10,0). Write the equation of their equal-distance boundary. Which clinic is nearer to P(2,3)?

2

Sites A(0,0) and B(6,4) share a nearest-site boundary. Find its midpoint and slope, then write an equation for the perpendicular bisector.

3

For sites A(0,0), B(8,0), and C(4,6), verify that V(4,5/3) is equidistant from all three sites by comparing squared distances.

4

A delivery point Q(7,2) must be assigned to the nearest of A(0,0), B(8,0), and C(4,6). Compare squared distances and assign Q.

5

Explain why not every point on the full perpendicular bisector of two sites necessarily appears as an edge in a multi-site Voronoi diagram.

6

Design scenario: three emergency stations serve a region. Describe how a Voronoi partition could guide first-response assignment and name one real-world factor that straight-line distance alone does not capture.

Vocabulary

site — fixed reference point.
Voronoi region — points nearest to one site.
Voronoi edge — active equal-distance boundary between neighboring regions.
Voronoi vertex — meeting point of edges, typically equidistant from three sites.

Efficient comparison

When only comparing distances, squared distances are enough. Avoid square roots unless the actual distance is requested.
