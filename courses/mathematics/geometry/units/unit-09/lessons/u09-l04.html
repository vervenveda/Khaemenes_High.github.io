Coordinate Intersections & Geometric Systems

Interpret intersections as points satisfying multiple geometric conditions at the same time.

Learning intention

Translate geometric conditions into equations and identify their common solutions as intersections.

Success evidence

  • Explain why an intersection satisfies every equation in a system.
  • Solve line-line systems and interpret one, zero, or infinitely many intersections.
  • Combine simple geometric loci such as lines and circles.
  • Check candidate coordinates in every governing condition.

Worked reasoning

Example: Find the intersection of y=2x−1 and y=−x+5.

  1. At an intersection, both expressions equal the same y-value.
  2. Set 2x−1=−x+5, giving 3x=6 and x=2.
  3. Substitute: y=2(2)−1=3.
  4. Check in the other line: −2+5=3. Intersection: (2,3).

Geometry check: Solving a system is not merely algebra; it locates the common point or common set of two geometric conditions.

Find the intersection of y=3x−4 and y=−x+8. Verify the point in both equations.

2

Describe the intersection behavior of y=2x+1 and y=2x−5. What does the system's solution set look like?

3

The equations 2x+y=6 and 4x+2y=12 represent what relationship between two loci? How many common points exist?

4

Find the points satisfying both x=3 and x²+y²=25.

5

A point must lie on y=x+1 and be 5 units from the origin. Write the system and find all points that satisfy both conditions.

6

Two geometric constraints produce candidate points (1,4) and (3,2). Explain a reliable procedure for deciding which candidates are actual intersections.

Vocabulary

system — equations considered simultaneously.
intersection — common point or set.
solution set — all coordinates satisfying every condition.
candidate — possible solution requiring verification.

Progression

Start with line-line intersections, then combine a line with a simple circle. Lesson 05 will specialize circle-line intersections and tangency.
