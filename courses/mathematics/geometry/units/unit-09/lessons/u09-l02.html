Perpendicular and Angle Bisector Loci

Use equidistance to identify, construct, and justify perpendicular-bisector and angle-bisector loci.

Open-AgePrintableConstruction + proofMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Recognize bisectors as complete sets of points satisfying an equidistance condition—not merely lines that look centered.

Success evidence

  • State the perpendicular-bisector locus theorem in both directions.
  • State the angle-bisector locus theorem using perpendicular distance to lines.
  • Construct or determine a bisector from geometric or coordinate information.
  • Use converse reasoning to justify that a point belongs to a locus.

Two equidistance models

PA=PB ⇔ P lies on ⟂ bisector of ABd(P,ℓ₁)=d(P,ℓ₂) ⇔ P lies on an angle bisector

For two points, equidistance creates one perpendicular-bisector line. For two intersecting lines, equidistance creates two angle-bisector lines: internal and external.

Worked reasoning

Example: A(−2,1) and B(6,1). Find the locus of points equidistant from A and B.

  1. AB is horizontal because both endpoints have y=1.
  2. Its midpoint is ((−2+6)/2,(1+1)/2)=(2,1).
  3. A perpendicular line through the midpoint is vertical.
  4. Therefore the locus is x=2.

Converse check: If P lies on x=2, then its horizontal displacement from A and B has equal magnitude; with the same vertical displacement, PA=PB.

Vocabulary

perpendicular bisector — line perpendicular to a segment at its midpoint.
angle bisector — line or ray dividing an angle into congruent angles.
equidistant — the same distance from two objects.
converse — statement formed by reversing hypothesis and conclusion.

Construction lens

Compass arcs from A and B with equal radius create intersection points equidistant from A and B. Joining those intersections constructs the perpendicular bisector.

Why this matters

Bisector loci support circumcenters, incenter reasoning, fair facility placement, nearest-site regions, and later Voronoi design.
