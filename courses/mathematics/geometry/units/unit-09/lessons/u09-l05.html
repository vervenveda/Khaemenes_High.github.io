Circle-Line Intersections & Tangency

Connect geometric distance to algebraic intersection counts: secant, tangent, or external line.

Geometry + algebraExact reasoningMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Determine how a line meets a circle by comparing center-to-line distance with radius and by solving the corresponding system.

Success evidence

  • Classify a line as secant, tangent, or external.
  • Use d(center,line) compared with r to predict 2, 1, or 0 intersections.
  • Solve simple circle-line systems by substitution.
  • Recognize tangency algebraically as one repeated intersection.

One comparison controls the geometry

d<r → 2 intersections → secantd=r → 1 intersection → tangentd>r → 0 intersections → external

Here d is the perpendicular distance from the circle's center to the line. This links Lesson 03 directly to circle-line systems.

Worked reasoning

Example: Circle x²+y²=25 and line y=4.

  1. The center is (0,0), radius 5.
  2. The center-to-line distance is 4, so d<r: predict two intersections.
  3. Substitute y=4: x²+16=25 → x²=9 → x=±3.
  4. Intersections are (3,4) and (−3,4), confirming the prediction.

Double evidence: Use geometry to predict the number of intersections and algebra to locate them.

4

For circle (x−2)²+(y+1)²=16, determine whether line x=−2 is tangent, secant, or external. Find any intersection.

5

A circle has center (3,2), radius 5. Classify line 3x+4y−42=0 by comparing center-to-line distance with the radius.

6

Explain why substituting a line equation into a circle can produce a quadratic with two, one, or no real solutions, and connect each case to the geometry.

Vocabulary

secant line — intersects a circle twice.
tangent line — intersects once.
external line — does not intersect.
repeated solution — algebraic signature of tangency.

Connection

L03 supplies perpendicular distance. L04 supplies systems/intersection meaning. L05 combines both ideas around a circle.
