Family guide · Unit 09

Analytic Geometry, Loci & Conic Connections

Learners study how distance conditions create lines, boundaries, intersections, parabolas, and nearest-site regions.

What learners will do

Across seven lessons, students move from the idea of a locus to perpendicular and angle bisectors, shortest distance to a line, coordinate intersections, circle-line tangency, parabolas, and Voronoi/nearest-site design.

They also complete differentiated printable practice, a 12-question balanced mastery check, corrections when needed, and an applied nearest-site/access investigation.

How to support without doing the mathematics for them

  • Ask, “What condition must every valid point satisfy?”
  • Ask the learner to point to the perpendicular, midpoint, focus/directrix, or boundary evidence before calculating.
  • Encourage exact fractions and radicals before calculator decimals.
  • For a proposed answer, ask, “How can you verify it in every condition?”
  • For design work, ask what real-world factor the geometric model leaves out.
  • Treat corrections as part of mastery evidence rather than as punishment for a first attempt.

Mastery evidence

Target: 80%. Each mastery attempt includes material from all seven lessons. A strong evidence record combines lesson work, practice, explanation, corrections, and the applied investigation—not a single score alone.

Technology

The Scientific Calculator may verify arithmetic after setup. The Evidence & Citation Studio may help organize sources, assumptions, limitations, and revision notes for the investigation. Both tools support the learner's reasoning; neither replaces it.
