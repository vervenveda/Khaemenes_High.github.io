Unit 02 · Lesson 03 · Transformations, Symmetry & Congruence

Rotations About the Origin and Other Centers

Rotate points and figures through specified angles and centers, recover rotation information, and verify the invariants of rotational rigid motion.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 problemsPrintable

Learning intention

I can use coordinate rules for rotations about the origin, reason about rotations around other centers, and justify the geometric properties rotations preserve.

Success evidence

  • Apply 90°, 180°, and 270° rotation rules accurately.
  • Distinguish clockwise from counterclockwise rotation.
  • Rotate around a non-origin center by reasoning with relative coordinates.
  • Use equal center-to-point distances and angle measures to verify a rotation.

Rotation rule map

90° CCW: (x,y)→(−y,x)180°: (x,y)→(−x,−y)270° CCW: (x,y)→(y,−x)

270° counterclockwise is equivalent to 90° clockwise. A rotation preserves distance, angle measure, parallelism, collinearity, and orientation.

For a center C(h,k), think: shift C to the origin, rotate the relative coordinates, then shift back.

Worked reasoning

Example: Rotate P(5,2) 90° counterclockwise about C(2,1).

  1. Relative to C, P is ⟨3,1⟩.
  2. Rotate ⟨3,1⟩ 90° CCW to ⟨−1,3⟩.
  3. Add the center back: (2−1,1+3)=(1,4).
  4. CP=√10 and CP′=√10, confirming equal radius from the center.

Misconception check: Origin rules cannot be applied directly to a rotation around another center. Re-center first.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Mark the center, direction, and angle before applying a rule.

Core: Complete all 20 and verify rotations with coordinates and center distances.

Extended: Rotate around non-origin centers, recover parameters, and justify invariants and inverses.

Mastery recovery

Below 16 of 20 secure responses: classify errors as direction, origin rule, non-origin center, or invariant/inverse reasoning; repair that family before reassessment.
