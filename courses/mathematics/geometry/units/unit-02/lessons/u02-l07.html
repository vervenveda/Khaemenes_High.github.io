Unit 02 · Lesson 07 · Transformations, Symmetry & Congruence

Tessellations, Frieze Patterns & Design Rules

Use angle structure and transformations to analyze repeating plane designs, determine whether polygons tile without gaps or overlaps, and justify pattern-generation rules.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 problemsApplied design lab

Learning intention

I can decide whether shapes tessellate, analyze repeating strip patterns, and describe the transformations that generate a geometric design.

Success evidence

  • Use angle sums around a vertex to test tessellation feasibility.
  • Distinguish gaps, overlaps, and valid edge-to-edge tilings.
  • Identify translation, reflection, rotation, and glide-reflection structure in repeating patterns.
  • Create and justify a repeat rule or tessellation using precise geometric language.

Tessellation & pattern map

angles around a point = 360°regular n-gon interior angle = ((n−2)180°)/nrepeat motif → transformation → repeat motif

A tessellation covers a plane without gaps or overlaps. At an edge-to-edge vertex, the angles meeting there must total 360°. A frieze pattern repeats indefinitely in one direction and always has translation symmetry; some friezes also have reflection, half-turn, or glide-reflection symmetry.

Worked reasoning

Example: Can regular hexagons tessellate by themselves?

  1. A regular hexagon has interior angle 120°.
  2. Three hexagons can meet at one vertex.
  3. 3×120°=360°.
  4. Therefore regular hexagons can form an edge-to-edge tessellation with no angular gap or overlap.

Misconception check: Matching one pair of edges is not enough. A valid tessellation must continue across the plane without eventually creating gaps or overlaps.
