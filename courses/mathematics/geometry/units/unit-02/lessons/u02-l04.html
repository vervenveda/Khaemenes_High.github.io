Unit 02 · Lesson 04 · Transformations, Symmetry & Congruence

Compositions of Transformations

Apply transformations in a specified order, track intermediate images, compare reversed compositions, and simplify compositions when a single equivalent motion exists.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 problemsPrintable

Learning intention

I can compose translations, reflections, and rotations accurately and explain why transformation order may change the final image.

Success evidence

  • Apply the first transformation before the second and record intermediate coordinates.
  • Use composition notation without reversing operational order.
  • Determine whether reversed compositions produce the same result.
  • Recognize equivalent motions, including combined translations and inverse pairs.

Composition map

(g∘f)(P)=g(f(P))T⟨a,b⟩ then T⟨c,d⟩ = T⟨a+c,b+d⟩P → P′ → P″

Read composition from the inside outward: in g∘f, apply f first and g second. A composition of rigid motions is still a rigid motion.

Worked reasoning

Example: P(2,−1) is translated by ⟨3,4⟩, then reflected across the y-axis.

  1. P′=(5,3).
  2. P″=(−5,3).
  3. Reverse the order: P→(−2,−1)→(1,3).
  4. The final images differ, so these operations do not commute for this point.

Misconception check: The second transformation acts on the first image, not independently on the original point.
