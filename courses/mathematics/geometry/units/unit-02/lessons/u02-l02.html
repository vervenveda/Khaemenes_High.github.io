Reflections Across Axes and Lines

Reflect points and figures across coordinate axes and other lines, recover lines of reflection, and verify the geometric properties reflections preserve or reverse.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 problemsPrintable

Learning intention

I can apply reflection rules, identify a line of reflection from corresponding points, and justify reflection properties using coordinates and perpendicular-bisector reasoning.

Success evidence

  • Use reflection rules across x-axis, y-axis, y=x, and y=−x.
  • Reflect across vertical and horizontal lines such as x=a and y=b.
  • Recognize the reflection line as the perpendicular bisector of each point-image segment.
  • Distinguish preserved distance and angle measure from reversed orientation.

Reflection rule map

x-axis: (x,y)→(x,−y)y-axis: (x,y)→(−x,y)y=x: (x,y)→(y,x)y=−x: (x,y)→(−y,−x)

Across x=a, the y-coordinate stays fixed and x′=2a−x. Across y=b, the x-coordinate stays fixed and y′=2b−y.

A reflection is a rigid motion and is its own inverse: reflecting the image across the same line returns the preimage.

Worked reasoning

Example: Reflect P(−2,5) across x=3.

  1. The line x=3 is vertical, so y stays 5.
  2. P is 5 units left of x=3.
  3. P′ must be 5 units right of x=3, so x′=8.
  4. Therefore P′=(8,5).
  5. The midpoint of PP′ is (3,5), which lies on x=3; PP′ is horizontal, so x=3 is perpendicular to PP′.

Misconception check: Reflection is not “change a sign” in every situation. The rule depends on the actual mirror line.
