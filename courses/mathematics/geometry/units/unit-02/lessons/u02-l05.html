Unit 02 · Lesson 05 · Transformations, Symmetry & Congruence

Rigid Motion Definition of Congruence

Use sequences of rigid motions to define, test, and justify congruence between figures.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 problemsPrintable

Learning intention

I can determine whether two figures are congruent by deciding whether a sequence of translations, reflections, and rotations maps one figure exactly onto the other.

Success evidence

  • Identify corresponding vertices, sides, and angles.
  • Describe or verify a rigid-motion sequence mapping a preimage to an image.
  • Use preserved lengths and angle measures as congruence evidence.
  • Distinguish rigid motions from transformations that change size.

Congruence through motion

Two plane figures are congruent when a sequence of rigid motions maps one figure exactly onto the other. Translations, rotations, and reflections preserve distance and angle measure; therefore they preserve size and shape.

rigid motion ⇒ distance preservedrigid motion ⇒ angle measure preservedF ≅ G ⇔ rigid-motion sequence maps F onto G

Orientation may be preserved or reversed. A reflection reverses orientation but still preserves congruence. A dilation with scale factor other than 1 changes lengths and therefore is not a rigid motion.

Worked reasoning

Example: Triangle A(1,1), B(4,1), C(2,3) and triangle A′(−1,1), B′(−4,1), C′(−2,3).

  1. Reflection across the y-axis maps A→A′, B→B′, and C→C′.
  2. The same rigid motion maps every corresponding vertex.
  3. Because reflection preserves all distances and angle measures, the triangles are congruent.
  4. The orientation reverses, but congruence is unchanged.

Misconception check: Figures are not proven congruent merely because they look alike. A valid rigid-motion mapping or sufficient preserved measurements must support the claim.

Learning pathways

Foundation: Match corresponding vertices and name the rigid motion.

Core: Complete all 20 and justify congruence with preserved measurements.

Extended: Design transformation sequences and critique insufficient congruence evidence.

Mastery recovery
