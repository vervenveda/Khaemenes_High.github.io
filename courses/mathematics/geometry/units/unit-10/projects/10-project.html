Efficient Enclosure & Geometric Probability Lab

Design a measurable space, optimize it under constraints, and use part of the design as a defensible geometric-probability model.

Design brief

Create a park, courtyard, garden, habitat, recreation zone, or other bounded planar design. Your submission must be mathematically reproducible by another learner.

  1. Define the site: state audience, units, available boundary/material, required features, and at least two real constraints.
  2. Draft two feasible candidates: label all dimensions and decompose composite regions so their areas can be verified.
  3. Scale test: show what happens to perimeter and area under one proposed scale factor and distinguish k from k².
  4. Measurement audit: identify the precision of at least one measurement and explain how that limits the precision of a reported result.
  5. Probability zone: define a favorable region and sample region and calculate a length- or area-based probability. State the uniform-selection assumption.
  6. Optimize: choose an objective such as maximum usable area or minimum border. Compare only feasible designs and justify the winner.
  7. Verify and revise: verify one important result with a second method, CAD model, coordinate method, or independent calculation; then document one revision.

Required portfolio evidence

  • Labeled plan or CAD export with dimensions and units.
  • At least one foundational/composite area calculation.
  • At least one regular-polygon, Heron, or coordinate-area calculation when appropriate to the design.
  • Scale-factor analysis.
  • Precision/error statement.
  • Geometric-probability model with its assumption.
  • Optimization comparison including constraints and objective.
  • Verification evidence and reflection.
