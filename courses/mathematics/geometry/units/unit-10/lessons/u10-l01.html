Area of Triangles, Parallelograms & Trapezoids

Build area formulas from base-height reasoning, decomposition, and rearrangement.

Base + perpendicular heightDecompositionMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Find and justify areas of triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoids by identifying a base and its corresponding perpendicular height.

Success evidence

  • Distinguish slanted side length from perpendicular height.
  • Explain why a parallelogram has area bh.
  • Explain why a triangle has half the area of a matching parallelogram.
  • Use decomposition or averaging of bases to justify the trapezoid formula.
  • Report every area with square units.

Three formulas, one idea

Parallelogram: A=bhTriangle: A=½bhTrapezoid: A=½(b₁+b₂)h

The height is always measured perpendicular to the chosen base. For a trapezoid, b₁ and b₂ are the parallel sides.

Worked reasoning: decompose before calculating

Example: A trapezoid has parallel bases 8 cm and 14 cm and height 5 cm.

  1. Average the parallel bases: (8+14)/2=11 cm.
  2. Multiply by the perpendicular height: 11×5=55.
  3. So A=55 cm².

Why it works: Two congruent copies of the trapezoid can be rearranged into a parallelogram with base b₁+b₂ and height h. One trapezoid therefore has half that area.

Height may lie outside the figure

In an obtuse triangle, extend the base line if necessary. The perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to that line is still the corresponding height. Do not substitute a slanted side just because it is drawn inside the figure.

Vocabulary

area — amount of two-dimensional region.
base — chosen reference side.
height — perpendicular distance to the opposite side or parallel base.
decompose — split into simpler regions.

Unit discipline

Length uses linear units. Area uses square units: cm², m², ft², and so on.
