Effects of Scale on Perimeter & Area

Connect a linear scale factor k to perimeter factor k and area factor k².

DilationsLinear vs square scaleMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Predict and justify how lengths, perimeter, and area change under dilation.

Success evidence

  • Identify the linear scale factor from corresponding lengths.
  • Apply k to all lengths and perimeter.
  • Apply k² to area.
  • Work backward from an area ratio to a positive linear scale factor.
  • Explain why area does not scale linearly.

Scaling laws

length' = k·lengthperimeter' = kParea' = k²A

A dilation multiplies every linear dimension by k. Because area is built from two independent linear dimensions, the resulting factor is k·k=k².

Worked reasoning

Example: A rectangle measures 5 cm by 8 cm and is dilated by k=3/2.

  1. New dimensions: 7.5 cm by 12 cm.
  2. Original perimeter=26 cm; new perimeter=39 cm, which is 3/2 times as large.
  3. Original area=40 cm²; new area=90 cm².
  4. 90/40=9/4=(3/2)².

Reverse reasoning: If similar figures have area ratio 25:9, their positive linear scale ratio is 5:3, not 25:9.

Why the square appears

Imagine a 1-by-1 square. Scaling each side by 3 creates a 3-by-3 square. Nine unit squares fit inside it. The linear factor is 3, but the area factor is 3²=9.

A student says, “If every side length increases by 20%, the area also increases by 20%.” Audit the claim and give the correct percentage increase in area.

Vocabulary

dilation — transformation multiplying distances from a center by k.
scale factor — ratio of corresponding linear measurements.
area scale factor — k² for similar plane figures.
similar figures — same shape with proportional corresponding lengths.

Units clue

Linear units hint at k. Square units hint at k². Unit structure can help detect a scaling mistake.
