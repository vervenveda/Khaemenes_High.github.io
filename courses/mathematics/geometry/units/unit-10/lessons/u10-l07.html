Optimization with Perimeter and Area Constraints

Turn design restrictions into mathematical constraints, compare feasible choices, and justify the best design.

ConstraintsFeasible designsMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Optimize geometric designs under fixed perimeter, fixed area, dimensional, or practical constraints.

Success evidence

  • Identify the objective separately from the constraints.
  • Represent a design with variables and an equation or inequality.
  • Generate only feasible candidates.
  • Compare candidates using area, perimeter, cost, or another stated objective.
  • Explain why a mathematically best design may still require practical revision.

Optimization workflow

1. Define variables2. Write constraints3. Define objective4. Compare + justify

For a rectangle with fixed perimeter P, 2L+2W=P. Solving W=P/2−L converts area A=LW into a one-variable comparison. In Geometry, tables, symmetry, diagrams, and algebra can all support the optimization argument.

Worked design: fixed fencing

You have 40 m of fencing for a rectangular garden.

  1. Constraint: 2L+2W=40, so L+W=20.
  2. Feasible integer pairs include (1,19), (2,18), …, (10,10).
  3. Areas include 19, 36, …, 100 m².
  4. The 10 m by 10 m square gives the largest area among these candidates.

Reasoning: As one dimension moves away from 10 while the sum stays 20, the product decreases. The balanced dimensions maximize rectangular area for this fixed perimeter.

Constraints are part of the mathematics

A design with a larger calculated area is not a valid winner if it violates the budget, boundary, access, safety, minimum-width, or material constraint. Optimization means finding the best feasible design.

Optimization with Perimeter and Area Constraints

U10 · L07
1

A rectangular pen has perimeter 36 m. Compare dimensions 4×14, 6×12, 8×10, and 9×9. Which has greatest area? Verify every option satisfies the constraint.

2

A rectangle must have area 96 m². Compare 4×24, 6×16, 8×12, and dimensions √96×√96. Which design uses the least perimeter?

3

A three-sided rectangular enclosure uses an existing wall as its fourth side. You have 30 m of fencing. If x is each perpendicular side and y is the side parallel to the wall, write the constraint and compare x=5, 7.5, 10, and 12 m to find the greatest area among these candidates.

4

A 12 m by 10 m rectangular courtyard must contain a 4 m by 3 m garden and at least 80 m² of open walking area. Determine whether the plan is feasible. Then find how much additional garden area could be added while preserving the walking-area requirement.

5

A designer has 48 m of border for a rectangle and requires width at least 8 m. Determine the feasible width interval and identify the dimensions that maximize area.

6

A CAD model suggests a design with slightly more usable area, but it creates a passage only 0.4 m wide while the project requires at least 1.2 m. Explain why the design is not optimal for the actual problem and describe the revision process.

Vocabulary

constraint — condition a valid design must satisfy.
objective — quantity being maximized or minimized.
feasible — satisfying all constraints.
optimum — best feasible result under the stated objective.
