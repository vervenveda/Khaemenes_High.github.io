Units, Precision, Error & Significant Figures

Treat measurement as information with units, resolution, uncertainty, and responsible reporting.

Measurement boundsAbsolute + relative errorMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Interpret measured quantities, calculate error, and report geometric results at a precision justified by the data.

Success evidence

  • Attach and convert units correctly.
  • Interpret a rounded measurement as an interval of possible values.
  • Calculate absolute and relative error when an accepted value is known.
  • Distinguish decimal places from significant figures.
  • Avoid claiming more precision than the measurements support.

Error and reporting

absolute error=|measured−accepted|relative error=absolute error/|accepted|percent error=relative error×100%

If a length is reported as 12.4 cm to the nearest tenth, the actual value is at least 12.35 cm and less than 12.45 cm. The displayed digits communicate measurement resolution.

Worked measurement audit

A board is measured as 8.2 cm long; an accepted calibration value is 8.16 cm.

  1. Absolute error=|8.20−8.16|=0.04 cm.
  2. Relative error=0.04/8.16≈0.00490.
  3. Percent error≈0.490%.
  4. Reporting “8.200000 cm” would falsely imply much greater measurement precision.

Geometry consequence: Measurement uncertainty propagates into calculated perimeter and area. A calculator can produce many digits; that does not make all of them meaningful.

Significant figures

Leading zeros are placeholders and are not significant. Zeros between nonzero digits are significant. Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant when written as part of a measured value. For example, 0.00450 has three significant figures.

1

A length is recorded as 7.6 cm to the nearest tenth. State the interval of possible actual lengths.

2

A measured distance is 24.7 m and the accepted value is 25.0 m. Find the absolute error and percent error.

3

How many significant figures are in each measurement: 0.00450 m, 120.05 cm, and 8.00 mm? Explain the role of the zeros.

4

A rectangle is measured as 6.4 cm by 3.2 cm, each to the nearest tenth. Compute the area from the reported measurements, then explain why reporting 20.480000 cm² is misleading.

5

Convert 2.35 m² to cm². Explain why an area conversion squares the linear conversion factor.

6

Two teams measure the same hallway. Team A reports 18.42 m; Team B reports 18 m. Without knowing their instruments, can you conclude Team A is more accurate? Distinguish precision from accuracy.

Vocabulary

accuracy — closeness to an accepted value.
precision — fineness or consistency of measurement/reporting.
absolute error — magnitude of the measurement difference.
relative error — error compared with accepted magnitude.
significant figures — digits carrying measured precision.

Calculator discipline

Use the calculator to evaluate ratios and percentages, but decide separately how many digits the evidence justifies reporting.
