Unit 10 · Lesson 06 · Area, Measurement & Geometric Probability

Geometric Probability by Length and Area

Model uniformly selected locations with favorable-to-total length or area ratios.

Uniform selectionLength + area ratiosMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Build, calculate, and critique geometric-probability models using appropriate measures.

Success evidence

  • State the sample region and favorable region.
  • Identify the uniform-selection assumption.
  • Use length ratios for one-dimensional selection and area ratios for two-dimensional selection.
  • Use complements and composite regions efficiently.
  • Recognize when a nonuniform process makes a simple geometric ratio invalid.

The model

P(E)=favorable length/total lengthP(E)=favorable area/total areaP(not E)=1−P(E)

The ratio works only when every location in the relevant sample region is equally likely under the model. Geometry supplies the measure; probability supplies the interpretation.

Worked area model

A dart is modeled as landing uniformly on a 12 cm by 10 cm rectangular board. A 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle is the scoring zone.

  1. Total area=120 cm².
  2. Favorable area=24 cm².
  3. P(score)=24/120=1/5=0.20.
  4. P(miss)=1−1/5=4/5.

Model audit: If the dart thrower tends to aim toward the center, actual landing locations may not be uniform. The area ratio is then a mathematical model, not automatically an empirical truth.

Choose the right measure

If a point is selected on a segment, compare lengths. If a point is selected inside a planar region, compare areas. Do not compare a favorable length with a total area: the measures must have the same dimension.

1

A point is chosen uniformly on a 20 cm segment. What is the probability it lies in a marked subsegment of length 7 cm?

2

A point is selected uniformly inside a 15 m by 8 m rectangle. A 5 m by 6 m region is favorable. Find the probability.

3

A circular target of radius 10 cm contains a concentric bullseye of radius 4 cm. Assuming uniform landing over the target, find the probability of hitting the bullseye.

4

A square board has side 12 cm. A circular hole of radius 3 cm is removed from its center. A point is chosen uniformly from the remaining board material. Find the probability it lies within a 4 cm by 5 cm rectangular marked region that does not overlap the hole.

5

A point is chosen uniformly inside a 10 cm by 10 cm square. Find the probability it is outside a centered 6 cm by 6 cm square. Solve using a complement.

6

A game claims the probability of landing in a painted region is exactly its area divided by board area, but a mechanical launcher consistently favors the left side. Audit the claim: what assumption fails, and what evidence would you want before using the area ratio?

Vocabulary

geometric probability — probability based on geometric measure.
uniform — equally likely by location under the model.
sample region — all possible locations.
favorable region — locations satisfying the event.
complement — outcomes outside the event.

Dimension check

Length/length and area/area produce dimensionless probability ratios. Mixed-dimensional ratios signal a broken model.

Calculator role

Use the scientific calculator to evaluate π-based ratios or decimals after setting up the geometric model.
