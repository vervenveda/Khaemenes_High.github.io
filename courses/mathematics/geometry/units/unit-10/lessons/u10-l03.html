Heron's Formula & Coordinate Area

Find triangle area when a convenient height is missing, then verify area through coordinate geometry.

Three side lengthsCoordinate verificationMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Select and use an efficient triangle-area method from side-length or coordinate information.

Success evidence

  • Compute semiperimeter correctly.
  • Use Heron's formula when three side lengths are known.
  • Use coordinate structure to obtain a base and perpendicular height when convenient.
  • Use the coordinate-area determinant/shoelace form as an independent method.
  • Compare two methods as a verification strategy.

Heron's formula

s=(a+b+c)/2A=√[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]

Heron's formula is especially useful when all three side lengths are known but no altitude is supplied. First confirm the side lengths can form a triangle.

Coordinate triangle area

A=½|x₁(y₂−y₃)+x₂(y₃−y₁)+x₃(y₁−y₂)|

Coordinates may also reveal an easier base-height route. If one side is horizontal or vertical, its length can serve as a base and the perpendicular coordinate difference gives the height.

Worked comparison

Triangle: A(0,0), B(6,0), C(2,4).

  1. Base-height: AB=6 and height from C to y=0 is 4, so A=½(6)(4)=12.
  2. Coordinate formula: A=½|0(0−4)+6(4−0)+2(0−0)|=½|24|=12.
  3. Two independent routes agree, strengthening confidence in the result.

Method choice: Do not use a more complicated formula merely because you know it. Choose the method best matched to the information.

Vocabulary

semiperimeter — half a triangle's perimeter.
Heron's formula — area from three side lengths.
coordinate area — area computed directly from vertex coordinates.
verification — independent check of a result.

Triangle check

Before Heron's formula, verify the triangle inequality. Invalid side lengths do not describe a triangle.

Calculator role

Keep exact radical work visible first; use the scientific calculator for square-root evaluation or decimal verification when needed.
