Unit 01 · Lesson 04 · Foundations, Constructions & Geometric Reasoning

Compass-and-Straightedge Constructions

Construct congruent segments and angles, perpendicular and angle bisectors, perpendiculars, and parallels while preserving the evidence of each construction.

Foundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%20 tasksPrintable

Learning intention

I can perform classical constructions and explain why the compass arcs and straightedge lines establish the intended geometric relationship.

Success evidence

  • Use an unmarked straightedge to draw lines and a compass to transfer distances.
  • Construct segment and angle copies without measuring numerically.
  • Construct perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors from equal-radius arcs.
  • Leave construction marks visible so another learner can audit the method.

Visual model — perpendicular bisector

The compass is opened to the same radius from A and B. Each arc-intersection is equidistant from A and B, so the line through those intersections is the perpendicular bisector of AB.

Worked construction — bisect an angle

  1. Center the compass at the angle's vertex and draw one arc crossing both rays.
  2. Draw equal-radius intersecting arcs from those two crossing points inside the angle.
  3. Use the straightedge to connect the original vertex to the new arc-intersection.
  4. The new ray divides the original angle into two congruent angles.

Misconception check: A construction is not the same as making a drawing that looks correct. Compass operations establish equal distances; visible arcs are evidence of those equalities.

Vocabulary

construction · compass · unmarked straightedge · congruent · perpendicular bisector · angle bisector · locus · equidistant · construction marks

Learning pathways

Foundation: Complete one construction at a time using a numbered procedure.

Core: Complete all 20 tasks and preserve construction marks.

Extended: Connect each arc-intersection to an equidistance or congruence statement that could later appear in a proof.

Mastery recovery

Below 16 of 20 secure tasks: identify whether the difficulty is tool control, construction sequence, or geometric justification; repeat only that construction family before reassessment.
