01 · Recall

What do you already know?

Before adding formal vocabulary, use what you already know about points, lines, rays, segments, and angles.

Name it

A mark shows an exact location but has no length, width, or height. What geometric object is it?

Check

Point. A point names a location.

Compare it

Which extends forever in both directions: a segment, ray, or line?

Check

Line. A segment has two endpoints; a ray has one endpoint.

03 · Worked

Follow one complete reasoning chain

Problem: Two supplementary angles measure 134° and y°. Find y.

  1. State the relationship. Supplementary angles sum to 180°.
  2. Write an equation. 134 + y = 180.
  3. Isolate the unknown. y = 180 − 134.
  4. Conclude. y = 46°.

Reasoning habit: name the definition or theorem before doing the arithmetic.

04 · Explain

Say why the conclusion is valid

A correct number is not yet a complete geometric argument. Geometry asks you to connect a conclusion to a definition, marking, postulate, or theorem.

Sentence frame: “Because ________, I know ________. Therefore ________.”

Example: “Because supplementary angles sum to 180°, I know 134 + y = 180. Therefore y = 46°.”

05 · Guided

Try one with support

Two complementary angles measure 62° and y°. Find y.

  1. What total do complementary angles make?
  2. Write the equation using 62 and y.
  3. Solve and include the degree symbol.
Check your reasoning

Complementary angles sum to 90°, so 62 + y = 90 and y = 28°.

07 · Error

Find the reasoning mistake

A learner says: “The picture looks like a right angle, so the angle must measure 90°.”

Error

The learner treated visual appearance as proof.

Repair

Use a right-angle mark, stated measure, construction, definition, or proven relationship before claiming 90°.
