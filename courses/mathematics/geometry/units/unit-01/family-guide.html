Family guide

Foundations, Constructions & Geometric Reasoning

How do precise definitions, constructions, logic, and proof establish trustworthy geometric conclusions?

What learners will study

This two-week unit contains six lessons: geometric objects and notation; segments, rays, and angles; distance, midpoint, and partition; compass-and-straightedge constructions; conditional logic and counterexamples; and three major proof formats.

What learners will produce

  • Printable lesson work with explanations and diagrams.
  • Foundation, Core, or Extended practice matched to current need.
  • Construction work that keeps compass arcs and labels visible.
  • Written conditional reasoning and counterexamples.
  • Two-column, flow, and paragraph proof evidence.
  • A Classical Construction Portfolio & Proof Gallery.

How families can support learning

  • Ask, “What do you know because it was given, and what do you know because you proved it?”
  • Ask the learner to explain why notation order matters for rays and angle names.
  • During constructions, encourage the learner not to erase the arcs; those marks show why lengths or angles are equal.
  • For a counterexample, ask whether it actually meets the original hypothesis before rejecting the claim.
  • Celebrate a corrected proof or construction as stronger evidence than an unexamined first answer.

Mastery evidence

The course mastery target is 80%. The browser mastery check automatically scores selected multiple-choice evidence from a 30-item Unit 01 bank. Open-response lesson work, constructions, explanations, proofs, and the project require human review by a teacher, parent, or program administrator.

If mastery is not yet secure, the learner should correct the specific skill family, complete targeted practice, and reassess rather than simply repeat unrelated work.

Helpful questions

  • What does the diagram actually prove, and what does it only appear to show?
  • How can you verify this distance, midpoint, or partition another way?
  • Which compass action established an equal distance?
  • Is the converse of this statement also true?
  • What reason justifies this proof step?
