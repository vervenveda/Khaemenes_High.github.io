Teacher guide

Foundations, Constructions & Geometric Reasoning

Weeks 1–2 · Six lessons · Mastery target 80%

Suggested sequence

  1. Points, Lines, Planes & Defined Terms
  2. Segments, Rays, Angles & Notation
  3. Distance, Midpoint & Segment Partition
  4. Compass-and-Straightedge Constructions
  5. Conditional Statements, Converses & Counterexamples
  6. Two-Column, Flow & Paragraph Proofs

Instructional priorities

  • Require precise notation before calculation or proof.
  • Separate a geometric object from its numerical measure.
  • Preserve construction arcs so the equality evidence remains auditable.
  • Require counterexamples to satisfy the hypothesis and violate the conclusion.
  • Treat diagrams as models, not proof of unstated relationships.
  • Teach statements and reasons as the invariant structure underneath every proof format.

Differentiated practice

Foundation: 18 supported problems emphasizing vocabulary, notation, guided construction, logic, and proof structure.

Core: 18 independent problems requiring calculation, construction justification, conditional reasoning, and proof writing.

Extended: 18 synthesis tasks requiring derivation, generalization, error analysis, construction audit trails, and multi-reason proof.

Pathways are routes through evidence, not permanent labels. Learners may move among them as needs change.

Assessment and evidence

  • Lesson worksheets provide open-response evidence and should be reviewed for mathematical reasoning, not merely final answers.
  • The Unit 01 assessment layer contains a 30-item hardened question bank, five items per lesson.
  • The browser mastery check draws 12 questions from that bank and uses the course mastery target of 80%.
  • Automatically scored multiple-choice evidence does not replace human review of constructions, explanations, and proofs.
  • Below mastery, identify the specific skill family, require corrections, provide targeted practice, and reassess.

Applied investigation

Classical Construction Portfolio & Proof Gallery: require a reproducible construction/design, at least three unit relationships, a second verification method, explicit assumptions/limitations, revision evidence, and a clear proof or justification trail.

Common misconceptions to watch

  • Reversing ray notation as though order does not matter.
  • Reporting negative distance.
  • Using unequal compass radii where equal-radius evidence is required.
  • Assuming a converse because the original conditional is true.
  • Using a non-example as a counterexample even though it fails the hypothesis.
  • Citing a conclusion as its own reason or otherwise using circular proof logic.
