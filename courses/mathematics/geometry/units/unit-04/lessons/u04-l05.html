Unit 04 · Lesson 05 · Parallel Lines, Slopes & Coordinate Proof

Coordinate Proof with Distance, Midpoint & Slope

Use slope, midpoint, distance, and line relationships as evidence in coordinate proofs.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Use coordinates to calculate slope, midpoint, and distance, then connect those measurements to geometric definitions and theorems in a concise proof.

Success evidence

  • Compute slope, midpoint, and distance accurately from ordered pairs.
  • Recognize horizontal, vertical, parallel, and perpendicular line relationships, including cases with undefined slope.
  • Select only the measurements needed to establish the geometric claim.
  • State a conclusion that explicitly connects the coordinate evidence to a definition or theorem.

Coordinate-proof toolkit

Slope can establish parallel or perpendicular relationships. Distance can establish congruent segments. Midpoint can establish that diagonals or segments bisect one another. A strong coordinate proof computes only what the claim requires.

m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)d=√((x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²)M=((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2)

Special case: horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular even though the vertical line's slope is undefined. Do not force the negative-reciprocal rule onto an undefined slope.

Worked reasoning

Claim: A quadrilateral with vertices A(0,0), B(4,2), C(6,6), and D(2,4) is a parallelogram.

  1. Find slopes: AB=(2−0)/(4−0)=1/2 and CD=(4−6)/(2−6)=1/2.
  2. Find slopes: BC=(6−2)/(6−4)=2 and AD=(4−0)/(2−0)=2.
  3. Both pairs of opposite sides have equal slopes, so both pairs are parallel.
  4. A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel is a parallelogram.

Audit check: Distance and midpoint calculations are unnecessary for this particular claim. Efficient proof means choosing sufficient evidence, not calculating everything available.
