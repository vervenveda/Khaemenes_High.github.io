David Hilbert · The Foundations of Geometry

Read selected axioms about points, lines, incidence, order, congruence, and parallels. Then compare an axiomatic geometry system with coordinate evidence based on slope, distance, and equations.

Open Free Public-Domain Source

Why this reading belongs here

Unit 04 asks students to prove parallelism, perpendicularity, alignment, and line relationships. Hilbert makes a crucial habit explicit: a proof may use only conditions that have actually been established. A diagram can suggest parallel lines; an axiom, theorem, angle relationship, slope calculation, or equation must justify the claim.

Reading caution: Hilbert is advanced historical mathematics. Students are not expected to read the entire treatise. Use the selected ideas, teacher excerpts, glossary, and mathematical reconstructions below.

Stage 1 · Read & Notice — What is assumed?

Selection focus: opening groups of axioms concerning incidence and order.

As you read, sort statements into three categories: primitive/undefined idea, axiom or assumption, and conclusion that would require proof.

Critical-thinking prompt: Why can a mathematical system begin with assumptions without making every later claim “just an assumption”?

Stage 2 · Math Connection — The parallel condition

Selection focus: Hilbert's parallel axiom and the role of conditions in a geometric system.

Connect the reading to Unit 04 lessons: transversal-angle converses can establish parallelism; equal slopes establish parallel nonvertical lines; negative reciprocal slopes establish perpendicular nonvertical lines; horizontal and vertical lines require their special case.

Reasoning trap: “These two lines look parallel” is not a premise. Neither is “the software drew them parallel” unless the construction or coordinates establish the relationship.

Stage 3 · Evidence & Synthesis — Two languages of proof

Choose one line relationship and establish it in two ways where possible: a synthetic argument using angle/theorem relationships and an analytic argument using coordinates, slopes, or equations.

Then answer: What assumptions or givens does each proof require, and what would break if one condition were missing?

6
