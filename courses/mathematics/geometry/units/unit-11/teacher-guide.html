Unit 11 · Teacher Guide

Solids, Surface Area & Volume

Teach the unit as a progression from volume structure to surface construction, spatial slicing/rotation, and dimensional scaling—not as a formula inventory.

Seven-lesson progression

  1. L01 Prisms, Cylinders & Cavalieri: base area × perpendicular height; compare right and oblique solids through cross-sectional reasoning.
  2. L02 Pyramids & Cones: establish the one-third relationship and distinguish perpendicular height from slant height.
  3. L03 Spheres: reason with radius/diameter and exact surface-area/volume expressions.
  4. L04 Nets & Surface Area: move between 3D solids and 2D face structure; distinguish bases from lateral faces.
  5. L05 Cylinders & Cones Surface Area: derive curved lateral area from circumference/sector structure and use slant height correctly.
  6. L06 Cross-Sections & Solids of Revolution: reason 3D→2D by slicing and 2D→3D by rotation.
  7. L07 Similarity, Scale & Volume: synthesize the dimensional ladder k→k²→k³ and reason backward with roots.

Instructional contract

  • Begin with a visible model, diagram, net, slice, or generating region before formal calculation.
  • Require units and distinguish linear, square, and cubic measures.
  • Ask learners to name why a formula applies and which height/radius/slant measure it requires.
  • Use Foundation, Core, and Extended as temporary pathways, not permanent labels.
  • Keep student worksheets and teacher keys separate during independent work.
  • Corrections are part of mastery evidence, not a penalty exercise.

Tools as laboratories

Scientific Calculator: use for π approximations, square/cube roots, and checking arithmetic after setup is shown. OHMIC CAD: use for nets, dimensions, cross-section visualization, rotation/model verification, and the design project. Students predict first and verify second.

Digital output alone is not mathematical evidence. Preserve sketches, formulas, substitutions, units, and written justification.

Assessment & intervention

The mastery check draws 12 questions from a dedicated 28-question bank and guarantees at least one question from every lesson. Target is 80%.

  • 80–100%: correct missed items, then advance/project.
  • 60–79%: identify missed lesson tags, assign targeted Foundation/Core work, conference briefly, then retry.
  • Below 60%: return to visual models and prerequisite measurement/formula reasoning before a new attempt.

Do not repeatedly regenerate tests without instruction between attempts.

Applied project

The Packaging Geometry Design Lab requires two candidate designs, capacity, material surface area, construction/net evidence, a cross-section, a k/k²/k³ scale study, and an evidence-based recommendation. Rubric mastery is 16/20 (80%) with correction of mathematical errors.
