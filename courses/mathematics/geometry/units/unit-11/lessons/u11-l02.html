Unit 11 · Lesson 02 · Solids, Surface Area & Volume

Pyramids & Cones

Explain why pyramids and cones with base area B and perpendicular height h occupy one-third the volume of a matching prism or cylinder.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Model and calculate pyramid and cone volume using the one-third relationship, distinguish perpendicular height from slant height, and compare pointed solids with matching prisms or cylinders.

Success evidence

  • Identify base area B and perpendicular height h.
  • Use V = ⅓Bh for pyramids and cones.
  • Explain the one-third relationship by comparison with a matching prism or cylinder.
  • Keep exact π form when appropriate and label cubic units.

Visual model · same base and height, one-third the volume

V_pyramid = ⅓BhV_cone = ⅓πr²hh = perpendicular height

A matching prism or cylinder with the same base area and perpendicular height has volume Bh. The corresponding pyramid or cone has one-third that volume.

Worked reasoning

Example: A cone has radius 6 cm and perpendicular height 10 cm. Compare it with a cylinder having the same base and height.

  1. B = π(6²) = 36π cm².
  2. Matching cylinder: V = Bh = 360π cm³.
  3. Cone: V = ⅓Bh = 120π cm³.
  4. The cone occupies exactly one-third the volume of the matching cylinder.

Misconception check: h is the perpendicular distance from apex to base plane. Slant height is not the volume height.
