Surface Area of Cylinders & Cones

Unwrap curved surfaces to see where the formulas come from, then distinguish lateral area from total surface area.

Curved surfacesCylinder netsCone sectorsMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Derive and apply cylinder and cone surface-area formulas from their nets, using circumference as the key link between a circular base and the unwrapped lateral surface.

Success evidence

  • Explain why a cylinder's curved surface unwraps to a rectangle of width 2πr and height h.
  • Distinguish cylinder lateral area 2πrh from total area 2πr²+2πrh.
  • Use cone slant height ℓ in lateral area πrℓ.
  • Add the circular base only when total cone surface area is requested.
  • Use square units and preserve exact π values when appropriate.

Visual model · unwrap the cylinder

Cylinder LA = 2πrhCylinder SA = 2πr² + 2πrhCone LA = πrℓCone SA = πr² + πrℓ

Why the cone uses slant height

Cut a cone from apex to base and flatten the curved surface. It becomes a sector of a circle whose radius is the cone's slant height ℓ. The sector's arc length matches the base circumference 2πr. This leads to lateral area πrℓ.

Height audit: Volume uses perpendicular height h. Cone surface area uses slant height ℓ. If only r and h are known for a right cone, find ℓ with ℓ²=r²+h².

Worked reasoning

Example: A right cone has radius 5 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm.

  1. Find slant height: ℓ=√(5²+12²)=13 cm.
  2. Lateral area: πrℓ=π(5)(13)=65π cm².
  3. Base area: πr²=25π cm².
  4. Total surface area: 90π cm².
