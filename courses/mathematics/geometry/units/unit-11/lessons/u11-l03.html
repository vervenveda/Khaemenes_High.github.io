Spheres: Surface Area & Volume

Connect radius to the two different measures of a sphere: its boundary area and its three-dimensional capacity.

Surface areaVolumeExact π formMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Calculate and compare sphere surface area and volume, recover radius from either measure, and use dimensional reasoning to distinguish square units from cubic units.

Success evidence

  • Identify radius or convert diameter to radius before substitution.
  • Use SA = 4πr² for boundary area.
  • Use V = ⁴⁄₃πr³ for capacity.
  • Keep exact π form unless a decimal is requested.
  • Explain why surface area and volume respond differently when radius changes.

Visual model

SA = 4πr²V = ⁴⁄₃πr³d = 2r

Surface area measures the outside boundary in square units. Volume measures the space inside in cubic units. The exponents on r reflect those dimensions.

Worked reasoning

Example: A sphere has diameter 12 cm.

  1. Radius: r=6 cm.
  2. Surface area: SA=4π(6²)=144π cm².
  3. Volume: V=⁴⁄₃π(6³)=288π cm³.
  4. Do not compare 144π and 288π as if they were the same type of quantity: one is area and one is volume.

Misconception check: Diameter is not radius. Also, surface area uses r² while volume uses r³.

Scaling insight

If radius is multiplied by k, sphere surface area is multiplied by k² and sphere volume by k³. For example, doubling radius multiplies surface area by 4 and volume by 8.

Vocabulary

sphere — points in space a fixed distance from a center.
radius — center-to-surface distance.
great circle — largest circular cross-section through the center.
