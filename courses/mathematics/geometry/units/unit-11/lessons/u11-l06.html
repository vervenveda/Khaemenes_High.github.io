Cross-Sections & Solids of Revolution

Reason in both directions: slice a three-dimensional solid to reveal a two-dimensional cross-section, or rotate a two-dimensional region to generate a solid.

Cross-sectionsPlane slicesAxis of rotationMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Predict cross-sectional shapes from the orientation of a cutting plane and identify solids generated when common planar regions rotate around an axis.

Success evidence

  • Describe a cross-section as the intersection of a plane and a solid.
  • Predict how parallel, perpendicular, and oblique slices change shape.
  • Recognize that a sphere's planar cross-sections are circles, with the great circle occurring through the center.
  • Match rectangles, right triangles, and semicircles with solids generated by rotation.
  • Identify the axis of rotation and distinguish the generating region from the resulting solid.

Visual model · slicing a cone

A plane parallel to a cone's circular base produces a circular cross-section. As the plane moves toward the apex, the circles shrink. A different plane orientation can produce different conic sections, but this lesson focuses on shape prediction from geometric structure.

Reverse the process · rotate a region

rectangle about side → cylinderright triangle about leg → conesemicircle about diameter → sphere

A solid of revolution is generated when a planar region rotates through a full turn about a fixed line called the axis of rotation. Distances from the region to the axis become radii in the resulting solid.

Worked spatial reasoning

Example: A rectangle 4 cm wide and 9 cm tall rotates about one of its 9 cm sides.

  1. The 9 cm side is the axis and does not sweep out a circular path.
  2. Every point on the opposite side remains 4 cm from the axis.
  3. The rotation generates a cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 9 cm.
  4. If the rectangle instead rotated about a 4 cm side, the cylinder would have radius 9 cm and height 4 cm—a different solid.

Orientation matters: naming the region alone is not enough. The axis of rotation determines the dimensions of the generated solid.

Cross-Sections & Solids of Revolution

Sketch when useful. Name both the shape and the geometric reason.

U11 · L06
1

A plane cuts a right circular cylinder parallel to its circular bases. What is the cross-section? What happens to its size as the plane moves along the cylinder?

2

A plane passes through the center of a sphere. Name the cross-section and explain why it is the largest possible circular cross-section.

3

A plane cuts a rectangular prism parallel to one of its rectangular faces. Describe the cross-section and its relationship to that face.

4

A right triangle with legs 5 cm and 12 cm rotates about the 12 cm leg. Identify the solid and give its radius and height.

5

A semicircle of radius 7 cm rotates about its diameter. Identify the generated solid and its radius.

6

A 3 cm by 8 cm rectangle can rotate about either a 3 cm side or an 8 cm side. Describe both resulting cylinders and explain why they are not congruent.

Two directions

3D → 2D: slice to make a cross-section.
2D → 3D: rotate to generate a solid.
