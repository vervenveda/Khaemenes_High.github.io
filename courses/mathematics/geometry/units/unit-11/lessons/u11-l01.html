Prisms, Cylinders & Cavalieri’s Principle

Connect constant cross-sectional area to volume and justify V=Bh for prisms and cylinders.

Base area BPerpendicular height hMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Calculate and compare volumes of prisms and cylinders and explain why equal-height solids with equal cross-sectional areas have equal volume.

Success evidence

  • Identify a base and its perpendicular height.
  • Use V=Bh for right or oblique prisms and cylinders.
  • Explain Cavalieri’s principle in terms of cross-sections.
  • Distinguish base area from a linear base measurement.
  • Report volume in cubic units.

Volume as stacked area

Prism: V=BhCylinder: V=πr²hCavalieri: equal cross-sectional areas + equal height ⇒ equal volume

Think of a solid as many thin slices. If two solids have the same height and every slice taken at the same level has equal area, Cavalieri’s principle says their volumes are equal—even if one solid is slanted.

Worked comparison

A right prism and an oblique prism each have base area 24 cm² and perpendicular height 10 cm.

  1. Both use V=Bh.
  2. V=24(10)=240 cm³ for each solid.
  3. The slant changes the appearance, not the perpendicular height or cross-sectional area.
  4. Cavalieri’s principle explains why their volumes agree.

Misconception check: In an oblique solid, do not use the slanted edge as h. Volume uses perpendicular height.

Vocabulary

base area B — area of a chosen congruent base.
perpendicular height — shortest distance between base planes.
cross-section — plane slice through a solid.
Cavalieri’s principle — equal-height solids with equal same-level cross-sectional areas have equal volume.

Units

Base area uses square units. Multiplying by height creates cubic units.
