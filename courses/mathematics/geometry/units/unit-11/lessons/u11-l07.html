Unit 11 · Lesson 07 · Solids, Surface Area & Volume

Similarity, Scale & Volume Relationships

Use dimensional reasoning to predict how corresponding lengths, surface areas, and volumes change in similar solids.

Similarityk → k² → k³Surface-area ratioVolume ratioMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Connect a linear scale factor k to the square and cube relationships governing surface area and volume, and reason backward from area or volume ratios to a linear scale factor.

Success evidence

  • Confirm that solids are similar before using power relationships.
  • Apply length factor k, surface-area factor k², and volume factor k³.
  • Use square roots to recover k from an area ratio and cube roots to recover k from a volume ratio.
  • Distinguish multiplying dimensions from adding a fixed amount.
  • Interpret scale relationships in models, manufacturing, storage, and design.

Dimensional ladder

length ratio = karea ratio = k²volume ratio = k³

The exponent records dimension: one-dimensional measures scale once, two-dimensional measures scale twice, and three-dimensional measures scale three times.

Worked reasoning

Example: Two similar cylinders have corresponding radii 3 cm and 6 cm.

  1. Linear scale factor from small to large: k=6/3=2.
  2. Surface-area factor: k²=4.
  3. Volume factor: k³=8.
  4. If the smaller cylinder has volume 45π cm³, the larger has 360π cm³.

Similarity check: A cylinder with doubled radius but unchanged height is not a uniform scale copy, so the simple k³ rule for similar solids does not apply.

Reason backward

If similar solids have surface-area ratio 25:49, their linear ratio is √25:√49 = 5:7. If their volume ratio is 64:125, their linear ratio is ∛64:∛125 = 4:5.

U11 · L07
1

Two similar rectangular prisms have linear scale factor 3 from smaller to larger. By what factors do surface area and volume change?

2

Two similar cones have radii 4 cm and 10 cm. Find the linear, surface-area, and volume scale factors from the smaller cone to the larger cone.

3

Two similar solids have surface areas in the ratio 36:121. Find the corresponding linear ratio and volume ratio.

4

Two similar solids have volumes in the ratio 27:216. Find the linear ratio and surface-area ratio in simplest form.

5

A scale model tank has volume 2.5 L. The full-size tank is similar with linear scale factor 4. What is the full-size volume? Explain why multiplying by 4 would be incorrect.

6

A student claims that increasing every edge of a cube by 2 cm creates a similar cube with scale factor 2. Explain why the claim is not generally true and give a condition under which the new cube would actually have scale factor 2.

Sacred ladder

k → k² → k³
length → area → volume

Inverse ladder

area ratio → square root → k
volume ratio → cube root → k

Design connection

Models, prototypes, storage capacity, packaging, architecture, and manufacturing all require dimensional scale awareness.
