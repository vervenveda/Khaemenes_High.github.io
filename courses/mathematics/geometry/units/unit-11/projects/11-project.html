Unit 11 · Applied Design Laboratory

Packaging Geometry: Capacity, Material & Scale

Design and defend a three-dimensional package or container that meets a capacity requirement while accounting for material area, construction, cross-sections, and scale.

Design brief

Create a package/container based primarily on a prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone, or a defensible composite of these solids. Your design must hold a target volume chosen by your teacher or documented by you. Compare at least two candidate designs before selecting a final design.

Required evidence

  1. Dimensioned sketch: label all measurements and distinguish radius, perpendicular height, and slant height where relevant.
  2. Capacity: calculate exact or appropriately rounded volume for both candidates.
  3. Material: calculate surface area and identify any surfaces intentionally omitted, overlapped, or opened.
  4. Construction evidence: provide a net for a polyhedral component or an unwrapped/sector model for a curved surface.
  5. Cross-section: draw and describe one meaningful planar slice through the final design.
  6. Scale study: predict how material area and capacity change under a stated uniform scale factor k.
  7. Decision: defend the final choice using mathematical evidence, not appearance alone.

Suggested workflow

  1. Set the capacity and practical constraints.
  2. Sketch Candidate A and Candidate B.
  3. Calculate volumes; revise dimensions until both satisfy the capacity constraint reasonably.
  4. Calculate material surface area.
  5. Compare efficiency using material required for the capacity delivered.
  6. Build a paper/cardstock net, physical model, or CAD model when available.
  7. Test a cross-section and a scaled version.
  8. Write the final engineering recommendation.

Tool rule: A scientific calculator or CAD model may verify arithmetic or visualization, but the submitted evidence must show the mathematical setup and reasoning.
