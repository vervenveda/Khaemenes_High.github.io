Unit 11

Unit 11 · Extended Pathway

Solids, Surface Area & Volume

Proof, generalization, model criticism, reverse reasoning, and design transfer across all seven lessons.

1

L01 · Two solids have equal heights and equal cross-sectional areas at every corresponding height, but their shapes differ. Explain why Cavalieri’s principle guarantees equal volume and identify what would fail if one cross-section did not match.

2

L01 · A cylindrical tank’s radius increases by 20% while height stays fixed. Determine the exact volume factor and explain why this is not a similar-solid k³ situation.

3

L02 · Generalize the ratio of volumes between any pyramid and prism that share the same base area and perpendicular height. Then explain why changing slant height alone does not alter this ratio.

4

L02 · A cone has volume 150π and slant height 13 with radius 5. Audit whether the dimensions are consistent and determine its perpendicular height.

5

L03 · Derive the ratio SA/V for a sphere of radius r and simplify. Interpret what happens to this ratio as r increases.

6

L03 · Two spheres have volumes in ratio 125:343. Determine their radius ratio and surface-area ratio without first finding either radius.

7

L04 · Design two different valid nets for the same rectangular prism and argue why the total surface area must remain invariant even though adjacency in the plane changes.

8

L04 · A student uses perpendicular pyramid height in ½Pℓ. Explain geometrically why this fails and describe how to recover ℓ in a regular square pyramid when base half-width and vertical height are known.

9

L05 · Derive cylinder lateral area from the unwrapped rectangle and explain why a label covering only the curved surface excludes both circular bases.

10

L05 · A cone and cylinder have equal radius r and equal total surface area. Express the cone slant height ℓ in terms of r and the cylinder height h, assuming both are closed solids.

11

L06 · A plane sweeps through a cone parallel to its base. Describe qualitatively how cross-sectional radius and area change as the plane approaches the apex, and connect the pattern to similarity.

12

L06 · A region bounded by a semicircle is rotated about a line parallel to but outside its diameter. Explain why the result is not a sphere and identify what extra geometric information is needed to characterize the solid.

13

L07 · Prove from dimensional reasoning that if all linear dimensions scale by k, any correctly computed surface area must scale by k² and any volume by k³.

14

L07 · A prototype and final design have volume ratio 8:27 but measured surface-area ratio 5:9. Audit whether the data can describe similar solids and justify your conclusion.
