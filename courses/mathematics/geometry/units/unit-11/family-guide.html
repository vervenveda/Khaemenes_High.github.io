Unit 11 · Family Guide

Solids, Surface Area & Volume

Learners move from measuring three-dimensional capacity to understanding surfaces, slices, rotations, and how scale changes length, area, and volume differently.

What your learner will study

The seven lessons cover prisms/cylinders and Cavalieri's principle; pyramids/cones; spheres; nets and surface area; curved surface area; cross-sections and solids of revolution; and similarity through the relationship k → k² → k³.

Helpful questions at home

  • “Which measurement is the radius, perpendicular height, or slant height?”
  • “Are we finding space inside the object or material covering the outside?”
  • “What would this solid look like if we unfolded or sliced it?”
  • “If every length doubles, why doesn't the volume merely double?”
  • “Can you show me where that formula comes from in the diagram?”

Boxes, cans, balls, funnels, lampshades, and packaging make useful informal models. No special supplies are required.

Practice & mastery

Foundation, Core, and Extended pathways provide different levels of support and challenge. They are not permanent tracks. The Unit-11 mastery check contains 12 questions and represents all seven lessons.

Mastery target: 80%. If a learner scores below mastery, the next step is targeted review and corrections before another attempt—not simply repeating tests until a score changes.

Design project

In the Packaging Geometry Design Lab, learners compare two container/package designs using capacity, material surface area, construction evidence, cross-sections, and scaling. A calculator or CAD tool can help verify work, but learners still show the mathematical reasoning themselves.

What progress looks like

Strong work includes labeled diagrams, correct units, sensible formulas, explanations of choices, corrected mistakes, and increasing ability to predict results before calculating. The goal is not formula memorization alone; it is spatial and quantitative reasoning.
