Unit 03 · Lesson 02 · Triangle Congruence & Proof

SSS and SAS Congruence

Decide when side information or two sides with their included angle force two triangles to be congruent.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Use correspondence, side markings, and included-angle evidence to justify triangle congruence by SSS or SAS.

Success evidence

  • Match corresponding vertices and sides before naming a theorem.
  • Recognize three matching side pairs as SSS evidence.
  • Recognize two matching side pairs plus the angle between them as SAS evidence.
  • Reject insufficient SSA evidence without assuming a diagram is drawn to scale.

Visual model

SSS: 3 corresponding side pairsSAS: 2 side pairs + included angleSSA ≠ general congruence theorem

Worked reasoning

Given: AB = DE, BC = EF, and ∠B ≅ ∠E. The marked angles lie between the two known side pairs.

  1. Match A↔D, B↔E, C↔F.
  2. Record AB≅DE and BC≅EF.
  3. Verify ∠B and ∠E are the included angles between those sides.
  4. Conclude ΔABC≅ΔDEF by SAS.

Misconception check: Two sides and a non-included angle produce SSA evidence, which does not generally force one unique triangle.

Lesson boundary

This lesson establishes SSS and SAS. ASA, AAS, HL, CPCTC, formal proof chains, and triangle centers are developed later in Unit 03.

Mastery check

Continue when you can distinguish SSS/SAS from insufficient evidence and preserve correct vertex correspondence in at least 80% of representative tasks.
