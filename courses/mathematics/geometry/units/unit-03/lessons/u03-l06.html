Triangle Inequality & Feasible Construction

Decide whether three lengths can form a nondegenerate triangle and determine the full feasible range of an unknown side.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intention

Use the triangle inequality as a construction-feasibility test rather than merely as a memorized formula.

Success evidence

  • Test whether three proposed lengths form a triangle.
  • Use strict inequalities and recognize degenerate equality cases.
  • Determine the range of a missing side with |a−b|<x<a+b.
  • Interpret feasible lengths in construction and design contexts.

The feasibility window

a+b>c|a−b|<x<a+bequality ⇒ degeneratestrict inequality ⇒ nondegenerate triangle

For positive side lengths, the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third. When two sides a and b are fixed, the unknown third side x must be greater than their difference and less than their sum.

Worked reasoning

Given: two sides are 7 cm and 12 cm. Find the possible third-side range.

  1. Difference boundary: |12−7|=5.
  2. Sum boundary: 12+7=19.
  3. The endpoints cannot be used because they collapse the triangle into a straight segment.
  4. Therefore 5<x<19.

Construction meaning: x=5 or x=19 is not a proper triangle; the inequality must be strict.
