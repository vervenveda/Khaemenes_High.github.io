Polygon Decomposition & Design Constraints

Decompose composite polygon designs, satisfy geometric constraints, compare alternatives, and justify a design decision.

Design workflow

  1. Translate every requirement into a measurable geometric constraint.
  2. Decompose the design into triangles, rectangles, trapezoids, or regular polygons.
  3. Calculate area, perimeter, angles, and material quantities.
  4. Check every constraint and revise if needed.
  5. Defend the final design with mathematical evidence.

Worked example

A pavilion floor combines a 12×8 rectangle and an attached triangle with base 8 and height 5. Total area is 96+20=116 square units. If the requirement is at least 110 square units, the design passes the area constraint.

Design check: A mathematically correct calculation does not rescue a design that violates a stated constraint.

Polygon Pavilion Design Lab

1

A floor is a 10×14 rectangle plus a triangle of base 10 and height 6. Find total area.

2

Decompose an L-shaped region into two rectangles and describe a valid area strategy.

3

A design must have area ≥180 and perimeter ≤70. Explain how both constraints should be checked.

4

Compare two layouts with equal area but different perimeters. Which uses less boundary material, and why?

5

Create a pavilion plan using at least three polygon types and label the dimensions needed to audit it.

6

Write a short design justification citing at least three numerical pieces of geometric evidence.
