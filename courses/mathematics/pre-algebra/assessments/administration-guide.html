Administration & Scoring Guide

A clear, student-centered framework for distinguishing public self-checks, supervised administration, confidential summative testing, and retained course records.

Learning Path

Public Self-Check

Use the public digital assessments for practice, self-study, correction cycles, retesting, and evidence of growth. These forms are intentionally self-scoring and are not confidential.

  • Complete every item before submission.
  • Review missed concepts after scoring.
  • Complete written corrections when appropriate.
  • Keep representative work with the student portfolio.
  • Treat browser-local scores as editable, unverified data until reviewed.

Observed Use

Supervised Public Administration

A parent, teacher, or program may supervise a public self-check when it will be retained as one piece of course evidence. Supervision improves administration consistency but does not make a public form confidential.

  • Identify permitted tools before beginning.
  • Provide a quiet, interruption-limited setting.
  • Do not provide content hints during scored work.
  • Allow ordinary accessibility supports that do not supply answers.
  • Document retesting or correction cycles clearly.

Summative Security

Confidential Midterm & Final

When confidential summative testing is required, use a separately issued supervised assessment whose questions and answer key have not been published in the learner-facing repository. Previously public questions are not eligible for confidential reuse. The public midterm and final remain useful self-check and portfolio-evidence forms, but they are not secure examinations.

Quality Standard

Definitive Answers

Scored multiple-choice items are written to have one mathematically or definitionally correct response.

  • Avoid duplicate equivalent choices.
  • State conventions when they materially affect an answer.
  • Avoid undefined or subjective “best answer” wording.
  • Use explanations to support correction and mastery.

Course Grade

Suggested Weighting

A parent, teacher, or program administrator determines the official course grade and any credit awarded from the evidence they retain and review.

  • Unit and coursework average: 40%
  • Midterm evidence: 20%
  • Final cumulative evidence: 30%
  • Mathematical modeling capstone: 10%

Mastery

Corrections & Retesting

Assessment should support learning as well as measurement. After scoring, review missed concepts and require meaningful correction where appropriate.

When a learner retests, preserve or clearly identify attempts so the academic record remains understandable.

Record Integrity

Know the Evidence Source

Browser-local result files and course records may include provenance and integrity information, but an unsigned public export is not proof of authorship or authenticity. Parent or administrator attestation remains required until a separately signed verification record is issued.

Student Record

Portfolio Evidence

Retain the materials that best demonstrate the learner’s progress and achievement.

  • Assessment result or printed score record
  • Representative lessons and practice
  • Written corrections
  • Projects and capstone evidence
  • Progress records required by the learner’s program or jurisdiction

Privacy & Stewardship

Keep Records Purposeful

Use only the student information needed for the academic record. Keep exported or printed records in the family’s, school’s, or program’s chosen secure portfolio location and follow applicable recordkeeping requirements.
