## Constructed Response 1 — Motion, Forces, Graphs, and Model Limits

- Interval velocities: `0–2 s: 2.0 m/s`; `2–4 s: 4.0 m/s`; `4–6 s: 6.0 m/s`.
- Overall average velocity: `(24−0)/6 = 4.0 m/s` in the positive direction.
- Increasing graph slope shows increasing velocity and positive acceleration.
- Net force: `16 N − 6 N = 10 N` right.
- Acceleration: `a = 10/5.0 = 2.0 m/s²` right.
- Free-body diagram: drive right, resistance left, normal force up, weight down.
- Valid measurement limits include timing resolution, position uncertainty, uneven surface and sparse sampling.
- Valid model assumptions include one-dimensional translation, constant mass, constant forces, negligible rotation and point-mass treatment.

## Constructed Response 2 — Momentum, Mechanical Energy, and Safety

- Initial momentum: `3.0 × 4.0 = 12 kg·m/s`.
- Shared speed: `12 ÷ (3.0 + 1.0) = 3.0 m/s`.
- Initial KE: `½(3.0)(4.0²) = 24 J`.
- Final KE: `½(4.0)(3.0²) = 18 J`.
- Momentum is conserved for the isolated system; 6 J of kinetic energy is transferred into deformation, sound, thermal energy or other internal pathways.
- Average stopping-force magnitude at 0.20 s: `12/0.20 = 60 N`.
- Average stopping-force magnitude at 0.50 s: `12/0.50 = 24 N`.
- Longer stopping time lowers average force for the same momentum change, but injury also depends on force distribution, direction, body region, restraint geometry, deformation and other factors.

## Constructed Response 3 — Circuits, Magnetism, and Energy Transfer

- Series equivalent resistance: `12 + 24 = 36 Ω`.
- Series current: `6.0/36 = 0.1667 A`; component voltages: `2.0 V` and `4.0 V`; total power: `6.0 × 0.1667 ≈ 1.0 W`.
- Parallel equivalent resistance: `1/(1/12 + 1/24) = 8.0 Ω`.
- Parallel total current: `6.0/8.0 = 0.75 A`; branch currents: `0.50 A` and `0.25 A`; both branches have `6.0 V`; total power: `4.5 W`.
- Adding a parallel branch lowers equivalent resistance by providing another current path.
- Electromagnet: current produces magnetic field. Motor: electrical input and magnetic interaction produce mechanical output. Generator: mechanical input and changing flux produce electrical output. Transformer: changing flux transfers electrical energy while changing voltage/current ratio.
- Valid limits include ideal wires, no source resistance, constant temperature, no magnetic saturation, no coil heating, no leakage flux and no mechanical loss.
- Safety includes approved ≤6 V battery systems, current limiting, no short circuits, no mains access and magnet precautions.

## Constructed Response 4 — Earth Observation: Waves, Sensors, and Information

- Sensor power: `P = VI = 12 × 0.25 = 3.0 W`.
- Time: `4.0 h × 3600 = 14,400 s`.
- Electrical energy: `E = Pt = 3.0 × 14,400 = 43,200 J`.
- Radio wavelength: `λ = c/f = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(150 × 10⁶) = 2.00 m`.
- A complete chain identifies Earth-system variable → sensor/transducer → electrical signal → analog-to-digital conversion or encoding → radio channel → receiving antenna/system → decoding and error checking → storage → analysis/model → interpretation.
- Direct measurement examples: temperature, pressure, acceleration, moisture or reflected intensity.
- Calculation examples: power, energy, wavelength, rate or calibrated value.
- Model examples: calibration curve, signal model, atmospheric correction or Earth-system model.
- Inference examples: storm development, soil-moisture pattern, ground motion source or changing ecosystem condition.
- Evaluation should address sampling rate, noise, calibration drift, missing data, parity/error control limits, privacy, reliability, energy demand, infrastructure access and field safety.
