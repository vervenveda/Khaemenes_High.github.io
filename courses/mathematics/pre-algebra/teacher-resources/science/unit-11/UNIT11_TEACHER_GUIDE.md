# Unit 11 Teacher and Parent-Educator Guide

## Unit Identity

**Course:** International Integrated Science 9 (`KH-SCI-IIS9`)  
**Unit:** 11 — Space Systems, Astronomy & Cosmic Evidence  
**Duration:** Three weeks, fifteen lessons, Weeks 31–33

## Purpose

Students use observation frames, geometry, scale, gravity, radiation, spectra, transit records, stellar evolution, galaxy evidence and cosmic-expansion models to explain space systems while distinguishing direct signal, processed image, calculation, model, inference, simulation, artist concept and public claim.

## Required Evidence

- Fifteen daily lessons
- Solar-System Scale & Orbit Check, 7/8
- Stellar Spectra & Light Check, 7/8
- Exoplanet Transit & Evidence Check, 7/8
- Galaxy Redshift & Expansion Check, 7/8
- Space Mission Evidence Case
- Mastery Quiz, 16/20
- Objective Assessment, 24/30
- Two constructed responses
- Corrections and final reflection

## Concept Safeguards

- Apparent sky motion depends on Earth’s rotation and the observer’s frame.
- Seasons are caused primarily by axial tilt, not large Earth–Sun distance changes.
- Ordinary lunar phases are not caused by Earth’s shadow.
- A light-year is a distance.
- Orbit diagrams rarely preserve object size and distance simultaneously.
- An orbiting body has inward acceleration even when speed is constant.
- The circular-speed equation is an average approximation for elliptical orbits.
- The Sun is a star powered by fusion; it is not chemically burning.
- A false-color or composite image can be scientifically valid when processing is labeled.
- Spectral lines require calibration and a rest-wavelength comparison.
- v≈zc is restricted to small redshift and is not a complete cosmological relation.
- Apparent brightness is not the same as luminosity.
- Stellar mass strongly affects lifetime and evolutionary path.
- A transit is indirect evidence and can have astrophysical or instrumental false positives.
- Habitable-zone location does not prove habitability or life.
- Galaxy shapes are not a simple one-way age sequence.
- The observable universe is not the same as the entire universe.
- Expansion does not require Earth to occupy a spatial center.
- The Big Bang model describes early cosmic expansion and evolution; it does not answer every metaphysical or pre-model question.
- Hubble time is a scale estimate, not automatically the exact age of the universe.
- Space science decisions involve debris, planetary protection, access, light and radio pollution, cultural astronomy, privacy and dual use.

## Accessibility

Permit calculators, enlarged diagrams, tactile orbit models, text descriptions of images, sonified or tabulated data, screen-reader-friendly tables, keyboard input, captions, color-independent spectrum labels, extended time, vocabulary scaffolds, and alternatives to outdoor observation.
