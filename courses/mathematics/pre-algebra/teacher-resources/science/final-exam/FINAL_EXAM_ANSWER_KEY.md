## Unit 00

### 1. A planned investigation has a serious risk that cannot be controlled with available equipment. What should happen first?
**Correct answer:** A. Revise or replace the method before beginning.

An investigation must not begin until serious risks are removed or reasonably controlled.

### 2. Which action is appropriate when an approved activity requires splash goggles?
**Correct answer:** B. Wear correctly fitting splash goggles for the entire exposure period.

Required protective equipment must be correctly fitted and worn throughout the relevant activity.

### 3. What is the safest rule for an unlabeled substance?
**Correct answer:** C. Do not handle or use it; inform the supervising adult.

Unknown and unlabeled substances must be treated as potentially hazardous.

### 4. A liquid splashes into a student’s eye during a supervised laboratory activity. What is the correct immediate response?
**Correct answer:** D. Use the approved eyewash procedure immediately and alert the supervisor.

Immediate flushing and adult notification are required; treatment must not be delayed.

### 5. A result does not match the other trials. What is the most responsible response?
**Correct answer:** A. Document it, check the method and equipment, and repeat if appropriate.

Unexpected data should be investigated and reported transparently.

### 6. Which study plan requires informed consent and additional ethical safeguards?
**Correct answer:** B. Collecting identifiable health information from human participants.

Identifiable human data require consent, privacy protection, and appropriate oversight.

### 7. Which field practice is appropriate?
**Correct answer:** C. Observe from a permitted stable location and minimize disturbance.

Safe scientific observation protects habitats, organisms, cultural resources, and sensitive locations.

### 8. Which notebook entry best supports reproducibility?
**Correct answer:** D. Date, materials, exact method, measurements, changes, and unexpected events.

Reproducibility requires a transparent record of what was actually done and observed.

## Unit 01

### 9. A student changes light intensity and measures the oxygen production rate of an aquatic plant. What is the independent variable?
**Correct answer:** A. Light intensity.

The independent variable is deliberately changed.

### 10. Repeated measurements are 12.2 cm, 12.2 cm, 12.3 cm, and 12.2 cm. What can be concluded from these values alone?
**Correct answer:** B. The measurements are precise.

Closely grouped values show precision; accuracy requires comparison with a trusted value.

### 11. What is the mean of 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, and 4.5 seconds?
**Correct answer:** C. 4.5 seconds.

The sum is 18.0 seconds and 18.0 ÷ 4 = 4.5 seconds.

### 12. Which graph is generally most suitable for showing the relationship between temperature and reaction rate?
**Correct answer:** D. A scatter plot or line graph with two numerical axes.

Both variables are quantitative, so paired numerical axes are appropriate.

### 13. Why are repeated trials useful?
**Correct answer:** A. They reveal variation and improve confidence in an estimate such as the mean.

Repeats show variability and strengthen estimates but do not guarantee correctness.

### 14. An observational study finds an association between screen time and sleep duration. Which conclusion is justified?
**Correct answer:** B. The variables were associated in the observed sample, but causation is not established.

Observational association alone does not establish a causal mechanism.

### 15. Which statement is scientific reasoning rather than a claim or a measurement?
**Correct answer:** C. Greater surface area increases collision opportunities, explaining the faster measured reaction.

Reasoning connects evidence to a claim through a scientific principle.

### 16. A ruler has millimetre markings. Which report best reflects its resolution for a direct length reading?
**Correct answer:** D. Report to the nearest millimetre and acknowledge reading uncertainty.

The reporting precision should match the instrument resolution and method.

## Unit 02

### 17. Which statement is part of modern cell theory?
**Correct answer:** A. All living organisms consist of one or more cells.

Cell theory identifies cells as the basic units of living systems.

### 18. Which structures occur in all known cells?
**Correct answer:** B. A plasma membrane and ribosomes.

All cells require a boundary membrane and ribosomes for protein synthesis.

### 19. Which feature distinguishes a typical eukaryotic cell from a typical prokaryotic cell?
**Correct answer:** C. Most DNA is enclosed in a membrane-bound nucleus.

Typical eukaryotes have a nucleus; prokaryotes do not.

### 20. A specimen is 0.04 mm long and its image is 20 mm long. What is the magnification?
**Correct answer:** D. 500×.

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size = 20 ÷ 0.04 = 500.

### 21. Osmosis is the net movement of:
**Correct answer:** A. Water across a selectively permeable membrane from higher water potential toward lower water potential.

Osmosis is a specific water-transport process across a selectively permeable membrane.

### 22. Why does a high surface-area-to-volume ratio support exchange?
**Correct answer:** B. More membrane area is available relative to the material requiring exchange.

Greater relative surface area can increase exchange capacity.

### 23. Which statement correctly links photosynthesis and cellular respiration?
**Correct answer:** C. Photosynthesis stores energy in organic molecules, while respiration transfers usable energy from those molecules.

The processes connect matter cycling with energy transformation.

### 24. Which sequence is ordered from smaller to larger biological organization?
**Correct answer:** D. Cell → tissue → organ → organ system.

Specialized cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work in systems.

## Unit 03

### 25. Homeostasis is best described as:
**Correct answer:** A. Dynamic regulation of internal conditions within functional ranges.

Homeostatic systems continuously adjust rather than maintaining exact fixed values.

### 26. Which sequence represents a negative-feedback system?
**Correct answer:** B. Change → receptor → coordinating centre → effector → response that reduces the change.

Negative feedback opposes a deviation from a regulated range.

### 27. What is the primary role of red blood cells?
**Correct answer:** C. Transport oxygen using haemoglobin.

Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds and transports oxygen.

### 28. Which adaptation increases gas-exchange efficiency in alveoli?
**Correct answer:** D. Large surface area and a thin exchange barrier.

A large area, short diffusion path, and maintained gradients support diffusion.

### 29. Most absorption of digested nutrients occurs in the:
**Correct answer:** A. Small intestine.

The small intestine has a large absorptive surface and rich transport network.

### 30. The kidneys help regulate:
**Correct answer:** B. Water, ions, pH, and removal of nitrogenous wastes.

Kidneys filter blood and adjust the composition of body fluids.

### 31. Compared with endocrine signalling, nervous signalling is generally:
**Correct answer:** C. Faster and more targeted.

Nervous impulses act rapidly through specific pathways; hormones often act more slowly.

### 32. A classroom heart-rate record should be used to:
**Correct answer:** D. Explore physiological patterns without diagnosing a medical condition.

Educational measurements require privacy and cannot support diagnosis.

## Unit 04

### 33. Which sequence is correct?
**Correct answer:** A. DNA contains genes and is organized into chromosomes.

Genes are DNA sequences located on chromosomes.

### 34. Mitosis usually produces:
**Correct answer:** B. Two genetically similar daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent cell.

Mitosis supports growth, repair, and many forms of asexual reproduction.

### 35. Meiosis contributes to variation through:
**Correct answer:** C. Crossing over, independent assortment, and random fertilization.

Meiosis and fertilization reshuffle inherited information.

### 36. For Aa × Aa, what is the expected probability of genotype aa?
**Correct answer:** D. 1/4.

The genotype ratio is 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa.

### 37. Phenotype is influenced by:
**Correct answer:** A. Genotype and environmental conditions.

Trait expression commonly reflects both inherited information and environment.

### 38. A mutation is:
**Correct answer:** B. A change in genetic material that may be neutral, harmful, or beneficial depending on context.

Mutation effects depend on location, cell type, environment, and biological context.

### 39. Why is cell-cycle regulation important?
**Correct answer:** C. It coordinates division and helps limit uncontrolled proliferation.

Checkpoints and regulatory systems reduce inappropriate division but are not perfect.

### 40. Which principle is essential when using identifiable genetic information?
**Correct answer:** D. Consent, privacy, limited access, and recognition of uncertainty.

Genetic data are sensitive and must not be used deterministically or discriminatorily.

## Unit 05

### 41. Which term describes all populations of different species living and interacting in an area?
**Correct answer:** A. Community.

A community contains interacting populations of multiple species.

### 42. Why is less energy available at higher trophic levels?
**Correct answer:** B. Organisms use and dissipate much energy through metabolism and heat transfer.

Energy is transferred and dissipated, so only part enters the next trophic level.

### 43. A population exceeds resource availability and then declines. Which factor most directly explains the decline?
**Correct answer:** C. Density-dependent limitation such as competition for food.

Competition, disease, and predation can intensify as density rises.

### 44. Species richness measures:
**Correct answer:** D. The number of species present.

Richness counts species, while evenness describes relative abundances.

### 45. Natural selection requires:
**Correct answer:** A. Heritable variation and differences in reproductive success.

Population traits change across generations when inherited variants affect reproductive success.

### 46. Which is direct empirical evidence used to reconstruct evolutionary relationships?
**Correct answer:** B. DNA sequence similarities and differences.

Genetic data are measured evidence; ancestry remains an inference supported by multiple evidence lines.

### 47. An ecosystem is generally more resilient when it has:
**Correct answer:** C. Functional diversity and multiple pathways that can maintain processes.

Redundancy and diversity can support recovery, though resilience remains context-dependent.

### 48. Which plan is strongest?
**Correct answer:** D. Protect habitat, involve affected communities, monitor outcomes, and revise using evidence.

Effective conservation combines ecological evidence, legal and cultural safeguards, monitoring, and adaptation.

## Unit 06

### 49. The atomic number equals the number of:

Element identity is determined by proton number.

### 50. A neutral atom has atomic number 12. How many electrons does it have?
**Correct answer:** B. 12.

A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.

### 51. Isotopes of the same element differ in their number of:
**Correct answer:** C. Neutrons.

Isotopes share proton number but have different neutron counts.

### 52. An atom with 17 protons and 18 electrons has charge:
**Correct answer:** D. −1.

One extra electron gives a net charge of −1.

### 53. Why is a periodic-table atomic mass often not a whole number?
**Correct answer:** A. It is an abundance-weighted average of naturally occurring isotopes.

The reported value reflects isotope masses and their relative abundance.

### 54. For elements 1–20, group patterns are strongly related to:
**Correct answer:** B. Outer-electron arrangements.

Recurring valence-electron patterns help explain chemical trends.

### 55. Which broad statement is accurate?
**Correct answer:** C. Group 1 metals generally become more reactive down the group.

Outer electrons are farther from the nucleus and more shielded down Group 1.

### 56. Why have atomic models changed over time?
**Correct answer:** D. New evidence required revision of earlier explanations.

Scientific models are revised when new observations are not adequately explained.

## Unit 07

### 57. Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between:
**Correct answer:** A. Oppositely charged ions.

Electron transfer can form cations and anions that attract in a lattice.

### 58. A covalent bond involves:
**Correct answer:** B. A shared pair of electrons between atoms.

Shared electron density attracts both nuclei.

### 59. What is the neutral formula formed from Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻?
**Correct answer:** C. MgCl₂.

Two chloride ions are needed to balance one magnesium ion.

### 60. Why must a chemical equation be balanced?
**Correct answer:** D. Atoms are rearranged but conserved in a closed chemical reaction.

Equal atom counts represent conservation of matter.

### 61. In an exothermic reaction:
**Correct answer:** A. The system transfers net energy to the surroundings.

Exothermic reactions have lower-energy products relative to reactants under the stated conditions.

### 62. Why does increasing temperature often increase reaction rate?
**Correct answer:** B. Particles collide more frequently and a larger fraction have sufficient energy.

Higher temperature changes collision frequency and energy distribution.

### 63. A catalyst increases reaction rate by:
**Correct answer:** C. Providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

A catalyst changes the reaction pathway and is regenerated overall.

### 64. Dynamic equilibrium in a closed system means:
**Correct answer:** D. Forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates.

Macroscopic concentrations remain constant while microscopic reactions continue.

## Unit 08

### 65. A runner moves 100 m east in 20 s. Average velocity is:
**Correct answer:** A. 5.0 m/s east.

Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time with direction.

### 66. Velocity changes from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 3 s. Average acceleration is:
**Correct answer:** B. 2 m/s².

Acceleration = (10−4)/3 = 2 m/s².

### 67. The slope of a position–time graph represents:
**Correct answer:** C. Velocity.

Position change per unit time is velocity.

### 68. A 20 N force acts right and an 8 N force acts left. Net force is:
**Correct answer:** D. 12 N right.

Opposing forces subtract, and the larger direction is right.

### 69. A 4 kg object experiences a 12 N net force. Its acceleration is:
**Correct answer:** A. 3 m/s².

a = Fnet/m = 12/4 = 3 m/s².

### 70. A 2 kg cart moves at 5 m/s. Momentum is:
**Correct answer:** B. 10 kg·m/s.

Momentum p = mv = 2×5 = 10 kg·m/s.

### 71. A 3 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Kinetic energy is:
**Correct answer:** C. 24 J.

KE = ½mv² = 0.5×3×16 = 24 J.

### 72. A device transfers 600 J in 20 s. Its average power is:
**Correct answer:** D. 30 W.

Power = energy/time = 600/20 = 30 W.

Parallel branches share the same pair of connection points.

### 76. A device operates at 9 V and 0.50 A. Electrical power is:
**Correct answer:** D. 4.5 W.

### 77. Electromagnetic induction requires:

Changing magnetic flux induces a potential difference.

### 78. A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 3 m. Wave speed is:

v = fλ = 5×3 = 15 m/s.

### 79. When a wave enters a slower medium, its frequency generally:

Frequency is continuous across the boundary in the standard model.

### 80. Why can digital systems often regenerate a noisy signal?
**Correct answer:** D. Receivers can classify values into discrete states and reconstruct them within tolerance.

Discrete levels can improve regeneration, but noise, sampling, and error-control limits remain.

### 84. Weathering differs from erosion because weathering:

### 87. Why can excess nutrient input lower dissolved oxygen in water?

### 88. Incoming solar energy is 340 W/m² and albedo is 0.30. Absorbed solar energy is:

## Constructed Response 1 — Scientific Inquiry, Safety & Evidence

A complete response should define a safe, measurable cooling investigation. Acceptable independent variables include container material, insulation thickness, exposed surface area, lid condition, or safe starting temperature within educator limits. The dependent variable may be temperature change per unit time or time to reach a specified temperature.

Required elements include: operational definition; at least four controls; instrument resolution; repeated trials; suitable time–temperature table; line graph; mean or rate analysis; anomaly review without silent deletion; uncertainty; risk controls for hot water and glassware; consent/privacy if people are involved; waste and water limits; and a claim–evidence–reasoning conclusion.

## Constructed Response 2 — Cells, Homeostasis & Inheritance

A complete response should connect membrane transport and surface-area-to-volume constraints to exchange; specialized cells and circulation to transport; kidneys to water/ion regulation; nervous and endocrine systems to coordination; and negative feedback to restoration of functional ranges. Mitosis supports repair through genetically similar somatic cells. Meiosis, crossing over, independent assortment, and fertilization create inherited variation. Phenotype reflects genotype, environment, development, and interaction rather than genetic certainty.

Acceptable quantitative work includes a magnification, percentage water change, surface-area-to-volume comparison, or feedback-data calculation. Ethical limits should include consent, privacy, non-diagnostic classroom use, uncertainty, and prohibition of discriminatory genetic interpretation.

## Constructed Response 3 — Ecosystems, Atoms & Chemical Change

A complete response should trace nutrient input through producers, consumers, decomposers, dissolved oxygen, and possible eutrophication; distinguish measured abundance, chemistry, or genetic records from historical ancestry inference; calculate a valid population density, richness/evenness index, percentage change, or ecological efficiency; connect proton number, ions, electron arrangement, or periodic position to treatment-material behavior; write and balance a chemically defensible reaction; and analyze conservation, energy, rate, catalyst, or equilibrium effects.

Evaluation should include chemical safety, habitat protection, affected communities, monitoring, uncertainty, source authority, and possible unintended ecological effects.

## Constructed Response 4 — Motion, Energy, Circuits, Waves & Information

- Acceleration: `(5.0 − 1.0)/2.0 = 2.0 m/s²`.
- Net force: `F = ma = 4.0 × 2.0 = 8.0 N` in the acceleration direction.
- Initial kinetic energy: `½(4.0)(1.0²) = 2.0 J`.
- Final kinetic energy: `½(4.0)(5.0²) = 50 J`.
- Momentum change: `4.0(5.0 − 1.0) = 16 kg·m/s`.
- Current: `I = V/R = 6.0/12 = 0.50 A`.
- Power: `P = VI = 6.0 × 0.50 = 3.0 W`.
- Charge: `Q = It = 0.50 × 30 = 15 C`.
- Electrical energy: `E = Pt = 3.0 × 30 = 90 J`.
- Wavelength: `λ = v/f = 300/150 = 2.0 m`.
- Period: `T = 1/f = 1/150 ≈ 0.00667 s`.

The explanation should accurately describe motor force or induction, encoding and sampling, noise, regeneration/error checking, system boundaries, ideal-model limitations, approved low-voltage/current-limited construction, no mains access, and no unsafe moving-cart or human-impact testing.

## Constructed Response 5 — Earth, Climate, Space & Cosmic Evidence

- Water storage change: `120 − 50 − 45 = 25 mm`.
- Runoff volume: `45 mm = 0.045 m`; `2.0 km² = 2,000,000 m²`; volume `= 90,000 m³`.
- Reflected solar: `0.30 × 340 = 102 W/m²`.
- Absorbed solar: `340 − 102 = 238 W/m²`.
- Net imbalance: `238 − 236 = +2 W/m²`.
- Signal light time: `(1.50 × 10¹¹)/(3.00 × 10⁸) = 500 s`.
- Spectral redshift: `(500.5 − 500.0)/500.0 = 0.001`.

The explanation should connect tectonic/internal-energy processes, water and biogeochemical reservoirs, greenhouse forcing and feedback, orbital motion, instrument/spectral evidence, and remote sensing. It must distinguish direct observation, calculation, model, projection, and Earth- or space-system inference.
