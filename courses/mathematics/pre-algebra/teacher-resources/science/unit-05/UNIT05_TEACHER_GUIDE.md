# Unit 05 Teacher and Parent-Educator Guide

## Unit identity

**Course:** International Integrated Science 9 (`KH-SCI-IIS9`)  
**Unit:** 05 — Ecosystems, Evolution & Biodiversity  
**Duration:** Three weeks, fifteen lessons, approximately 15–18 instructional hours

## Purpose

Unit 05 connects ecosystem structure, food webs, energy transfer, matter cycles, biodiversity, population change, disturbance, conservation, observed selection, speciation, fossil evidence, human-origins evidence, creation and design perspectives, source evaluation, and stewardship.

The unit uses an evidence-level framework throughout:

1. specimen, event, or field observation;
2. direct measurement;
3. classification or scientific model;
4. historical inference;
5. stated uncertainty or limitation; and
6. worldview, philosophical, or theological conclusion.

Students should never be told that all six categories are the same kind of claim.

## Three-week sequence

### Week 1 — Ecosystem structure, energy, matter, and biodiversity

Days 1–5 cover ecological organization, habitat and niche, food webs, trophic roles, energy efficiency, matter cycles, biodiversity survey design, richness, evenness, Simpson diversity, detection limits, and sensitive-location protection.

### Week 2 — Populations, interactions, disturbance, and conservation

Days 6–10 cover population density and sampling, mark–recapture assumptions, exponential and logistic models, changing carrying capacity, real Isle Royale wolf–moose evidence, species interactions, succession, resilience, human impacts, conservation design, equity, and community rights.

### Week 3 — Evolutionary evidence, origins interpretations, and stewardship

Days 11–15 cover observed inherited population change, antimicrobial resistance, natural selection without need-based language, microevolution, speciation, macroevolution definitions, species-concept limits, the branching equid record, human-origins evidence, young-earth and old-earth creation, intelligent design, evolutionary creation, secular naturalistic interpretation, methodological naturalism, evidence hearing, and stewardship.

## Required evidence

- Fifteen daily lessons
- Food-Web and Energy Laboratory, 7/8
- Population Evidence Laboratory using the NPS Isle Royale record, 7/8
- Biodiversity Survey using student-collected non-contact observations or a documented public dataset
- Evidence Classification Laboratory, 7/8
- Origins Evidence Hearing
- Mastery Quiz, 16/20
- Objective Unit Assessment, 24/30
- Two constructed responses
- Corrections and final reflection

## Origins and worldview safeguards

- Evolution must be identified as a scientific theory, with “theory” defined in its scientific sense rather than as an unsupported guess.
- Directly observed inherited population change, measured reproductive isolation, and deep-time ancestry reconstruction must not be presented as identical evidentiary situations.
- Macroevolution should be defined according to standard biological usage while acknowledging that some creation models use the word differently.
- No responsible evolutionary explanation requires an individual horse to make an instantaneous jump into a fundamentally unrelated organism.
- The equid record must be represented as branching rather than as a simple ladder.
- Physical specimens, measurements, classifications, proposed ancestry, uncertainty, and worldview meaning must remain visibly separated.
- Creation, old-earth creation, intelligent design, evolutionary creation, naturalistic evolution, and undecided positions must be represented accurately from primary sources.
- A student’s personal faith, lack of faith, or family worldview must not be required or graded.
- Fair treatment does not require stating that every claim is equally testable or equally supported.

## Safety and privacy

- Do not handle wildlife or unknown organisms without a qualified protocol.
- Do not collect protected species, disturb nests or dens, enter unsafe terrain, trespass, or culture unknown microbes.
- Do not publish exact locations of sensitive species.
- Do not require personal medical, ancestry, reproductive, family-belief, or identifiable genetic information.
- Human worth, disability, ethnicity, sex, gender, morality, intelligence, and social value must never be ranked genetically.

## Instructional note

The comparative worldview strand is Khaemenes Academy enrichment. It supports evidence literacy and fair source representation but is not presented as though religious doctrine were itself an NGSS, Cambridge, or Australian Curriculum performance expectation.
