# Unit 06 Definitive Answer Key

Course: International Integrated Science 9 (`KH-SCI-IIS9`)

## Atomic Composition Laboratory

### 1. Which subatomic-particle count determines an element’s identity?
**Correct answer:** A. Number of protons

Atomic number equals proton number and identifies the element.

### 2. A particle has 12 protons, 12 neutrons, and 10 electrons. What is its net charge?
**Correct answer:** B. 2+

Twelve positive charges and ten negative charges give 2+.

### 3. An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. How many neutrons does it contain?
**Correct answer:** C. 12

23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

### 4. Which particle has a negative charge and a much smaller mass than a proton?
**Correct answer:** D. Electron

Electrons have charge −1 and mass about 1/1836 of a proton.

### 5. Which statement is true for a neutral atom?
**Correct answer:** A. Protons equal electrons

Neutrality requires equal total positive and negative charge.

### 6. For ²⁷₁₃Al, which values are correct?
**Correct answer:** B. 13 protons and 14 neutrons

Atomic number 13 gives 13 protons; 27 − 13 = 14 neutrons.

### 7. Which is a limitation of a simple atom diagram with particles drawn as large colored balls?
**Correct answer:** C. Particle sizes, distances, and electron behavior are not drawn to scale

Instructional diagrams distort scale and simplify electron behavior.

### 8. What changes when a neutral chlorine atom becomes Cl⁻?
**Correct answer:** D. It gains one electron

A chloride ion forms when chlorine gains one electron.

## Isotope and Atomic-Mass Check

### 1. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with:
**Correct answer:** B. The same proton number but different neutron numbers

Isotopes share atomic number but differ in neutron number.

### 2. How many neutrons are in carbon-14?
**Correct answer:** C. 8

Carbon has 6 protons; 14 − 6 = 8 neutrons.

### 3. Why do neutral isotopes of one element usually have very similar chemical behavior?
**Correct answer:** D. They have the same electron arrangement

Chemical behavior is governed mainly by electrons.

### 4. Using 75.76% chlorine-35 at mass 34.9689 and 24.24% chlorine-37 at mass 36.9659, the weighted average is closest to:
**Correct answer:** A. 35.45

The abundance-weighted average is approximately 35.45.

### 5. Why is the periodic-table atomic weight of chlorine not a whole number?
**Correct answer:** B. It is an abundance-weighted average of naturally occurring isotopes

The listed atomic weight reflects isotope masses and representative abundances.

### 6. Before calculating a weighted atomic mass, isotope abundances expressed as fractions should total approximately:
**Correct answer:** C. 1.00

All isotope fractions should sum to approximately one.

### 7. Which statement is accurate?
**Correct answer:** D. 24.305 is a representative weighted atomic mass, not the mass number of every atom

A standard atomic weight is a representative weighted value.

### 8. What is the safest Grade 9 method for studying radioactive isotopes?
**Correct answer:** A. Use verified datasets, diagrams, simulations, and qualified demonstrations

Students should not handle or dismantle radioactive sources.

## Electron Configuration Check

### 1. What is the shell configuration of sodium (atomic number 11)?
**Correct answer:** C. 2,8,1

Eleven electrons fill simplified shells as 2,8,1.

### 2. What is the simplified shell configuration of Mg²⁺?
**Correct answer:** D. 2,8

Mg²⁺ has ten electrons: 2,8.

### 3. What is the simplified shell configuration of Cl⁻?
**Correct answer:** A. 2,8,8

Cl⁻ has eighteen electrons: 2,8,8.

### 4. In the simplified shell model for the first 20 elements, the period number indicates:
**Correct answer:** B. Number of occupied electron shells

Period number corresponds to occupied shells in this model.

### 5. Which pair has the same number of outer-shell electrons?
**Correct answer:** C. Li and Na

Lithium and sodium are Group 1 elements.

### 6. A neutral main-group atom has configuration 2,8,6. Which element is it?
**Correct answer:** D. Sulfur

The total is 16 electrons: sulfur.

### 7. Which statement about shell diagrams is most accurate?
**Correct answer:** A. They are useful simplified models but electrons are not tiny planets in fixed tracks

Shell diagrams represent energy-level patterns but not literal trajectories.

### 8. Which observation provides evidence that electron energies are quantized?
**Correct answer:** B. Elements produce characteristic line spectra

Discrete spectral lines correspond to allowed energy differences.

## Periodic Patterns Check

### 1. The modern periodic table is ordered by increasing:
**Correct answer:** D. Atomic number

Atomic number defines the sequence.

### 2. Why do main-group elements in one column often have similar chemical properties?
**Correct answer:** A. They have related outer-electron arrangements

Similar valence patterns support similar behavior.

### 3. Which first-20 element is in Period 3, Group 17?
**Correct answer:** B. Chlorine

Chlorine has three occupied shells and seven outer electrons.

### 4. Across Period 3 from sodium toward chlorine, the general character changes from:
**Correct answer:** C. Metallic toward nonmetallic

Metallic character generally decreases across a period.

### 5. Which trend is observed down Group 1 for lithium, sodium, and potassium?
**Correct answer:** D. Reactivity generally increases

Group 1 reactivity generally increases down the group.

### 6. Which trend is observed down Group 17?
**Correct answer:** A. Reactivity generally decreases

Halogen reactivity generally decreases down the group.

### 7. Why are noble gases comparatively unreactive under ordinary conditions?
**Correct answer:** B. Their outer electron level is filled in the simple model

A filled outer level is comparatively stable.

### 8. An element has atomic number 19 and configuration 2,8,8,1. Which prediction is best?
**Correct answer:** C. It is potassium, a Group 1 metal that commonly forms K⁺

Atomic number 19 identifies potassium.

### 3. Which particle has relative charge 0 and relative mass about 1?

### 9. Which is the shell configuration of calcium?

### 13. What uniquely fixes an element’s position in atomic-number order?

### 14. Which is the safest way to study alkali-metal reactivity?

### 17. Why can line spectra help identify elements?

### 18. Which statement is most responsible?

Scientific models are useful representations with limits.

### 19. An atomic-weight interval for an element indicates:

### 20. An element has two occupied shells and one outer electron. Which fits?

## Constructed Response 1 — Atomic Identity and Isotope Evidence

- `³⁷₁₇Cl⁻`: 17 protons, 20 neutrons, 18 electrons, mass number 37, atomic number 17, charge 1−.
- Changing neutron number produces another chlorine isotope; changing proton number produces a different element.
- The supplied chlorine records produce approximately **35.45294 u**.
- Mass number is a whole-number nucleon count for one isotope; relative isotope mass is measured; standard atomic weight summarizes representative isotope composition and may be an interval.
- Appropriate limitations include representative-abundance variation, rounding, reference-update date, simplified notation, and omission of nuclear stability.

## Constructed Response 2 — Periodic Prediction

- Period 3 retains three occupied shells while atomic number and outer-electron count increase.
- Broad character changes from metals toward nonmetals, ending with noble-gas argon.
- Simple monatomic-ion patterns include Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, P³⁻, S²⁻, and Cl⁻; silicon and several other elements require more nuanced bonding models.
- Atomic number 19 is potassium: Period 4, Group 1, shell configuration 2,8,8,1, with a common K⁺ tendency.
- The shell model is useful for outer-electron patterns but does not show literal electron paths, orbital shapes, or complete quantum behavior.
