# Unit 10 Definitive Answer Key

Course: International Integrated Science 9 (`KH-SCI-IIS9`)

## Earth Interior & Plate Evidence Check

### 1. Which is a flux rather than a reservoir?
**Correct answer:** A. Annual river discharge

A flux is a transfer rate; annual river discharge measures transfer.

### 2. Which observation supports a liquid outer core?
**Correct answer:** B. S waves are not detected through part of Earth’s interior

S waves do not propagate through liquids, so the S-wave shadow zone supports a liquid outer core.

### 3. A 120 g sample occupies 40 cm³. Density is:
**Correct answer:** C. 3.0 g/cm³

Density equals mass divided by volume: 120/40=3.0 g/cm³.

### 4. Which statement is accurate?
**Correct answer:** D. Compositional and mechanical layer models answer different questions

Compositional and mechanical layers are related but not identical classifications.

### 5. Thermal convection involves:
**Correct answer:** A. Matter movement driven by density differences under gravity

Convection transfers energy through slow material movement related to density differences.

### 6. A mid-ocean ridge is most closely associated with:
**Correct answer:** B. Divergence

Mid-ocean ridges form where plates diverge and new oceanic crust is generated.

### 7. A plate-boundary map can most defensibly show:
**Correct answer:** C. Regions where certain hazards are more likely

Maps support probabilistic regional patterns, not exact event timing.

### 8. One seismic station’s S–P lag can estimate:
**Correct answer:** D. Distance from the station, not direction

A single lag constrains distance but not direction or exact epicenter.

## Watershed & Surface-Change Check

### 1. For a period, precipitation is 100 mm, evapotranspiration 40 mm, and runoff 35 mm. Storage change is:
**Correct answer:** B. 25 mm

ΔS=P−ET−R=100−40−35=25 mm.

### 2. A watershed is best defined as:
**Correct answer:** C. Land draining toward a common outlet

A watershed is the land area contributing drainage to a shared outlet.

### 3. The water table is:
**Correct answer:** D. The top of the saturated zone

The water table marks the upper boundary of groundwater saturation.

### 4. Increasing impervious cover commonly causes:
**Correct answer:** A. More rapid runoff and less infiltration

Pavement often increases rapid runoff, though outcomes depend on other conditions.

### 5. Which process is chemical weathering?
**Correct answer:** B. Calcite dissolving in weak acid

Dissolution changes mineral composition chemically.

### 6. Which statement is correct?
**Correct answer:** C. Erosion removes and transports material

Erosion is removal and transport; weathering is breakdown in place.

### 7. Which activity is appropriate?
**Correct answer:** D. Observing runoff from a safe distance on stable ground

Safe observation avoids unstable or fast-water environments.

### 8. A larger runoff value alone proves:
**Correct answer:** A. No single cause without additional evidence

Runoff magnitude requires context such as rainfall, soil, slope, drainage, and antecedent moisture.

## Biogeochemical Cycle Check

### 1. A reservoir receives 65 units/year and loses 50 units/year. Its annual change is:
**Correct answer:** C. +15 units

Net change equals inputs minus outputs: 65−50=+15.

### 2. A 1200-unit reservoir has total outflow 60 units/year. Simple residence time is:
**Correct answer:** D. 20 years

Residence time=reservoir/outflow=1200/60=20 years.

### 3. Which is a relatively fast carbon-cycle process?
**Correct answer:** A. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis transfers carbon rapidly compared with geological pathways.

### 4. Nitrogen fixation converts:
**Correct answer:** B. Atmospheric N₂ into biologically available compounds

Fixation makes atmospheric nitrogen chemically available.

### 5. In a standard introductory model, phosphorus differs from nitrogen because phosphorus:
**Correct answer:** C. Has no major atmospheric gas reservoir

Phosphorus lacks a major gaseous atmospheric reservoir in the standard cycle.

### 6. Excess nutrients can lead to low dissolved oxygen because:
**Correct answer:** D. Decomposition consumes oxygen

High production followed by decomposition can increase oxygen consumption.

### 7. Biogeochemical cycling means:
**Correct answer:** A. Atoms move among reservoirs and change compounds

Matter is conserved while location, form, and rates change.

### 8. A residence-time calculation assumes most strongly that:
**Correct answer:** B. Outflow and reservoir conditions are meaningfully averaged

It is an average model and does not describe identical particle histories.

## Climate Energy-Balance Check

### 1. Incoming solar energy is 340 W/m² and albedo is 0.30. Absorbed solar energy is:
**Correct answer:** D. 238 W/m²

Absorbed=(1−0.30)×340=238 W/m².

### 2. Absorbed energy is 240 W/m² and outgoing energy is 237 W/m². Net imbalance is:
**Correct answer:** A. +3 W/m²

Net=absorbed−outgoing=+3 W/m².

### 3. The natural greenhouse effect occurs because greenhouse gases:
**Correct answer:** B. Absorb and emit infrared radiation

They interact with outgoing infrared radiation and alter energy escape.

### 4. Which is climate information?
**Correct answer:** C. A 30-year regional precipitation pattern

Climate describes long-term statistical patterns.

### 5. Melting reflective ice that exposes darker water is usually:
**Correct answer:** D. A positive ice–albedo feedback

Darker surfaces absorb more energy, amplifying warming.

### 6. A strong climate conclusion should rely on:
**Correct answer:** A. Multiple independent records and physical mechanisms

Robust conclusions synthesize several evidence lines.

### 7. A climate projection is:
**Correct answer:** B. A model-based conditional result under a scenario

Projections depend on scenarios, models, and assumptions.

### 8. Which is adaptation rather than mitigation?
**Correct answer:** C. Improving flood-resilient infrastructure

Adaptation reduces vulnerability to impacts; the others primarily reduce forcing.

## Constructed Response 1 — Interior, Plates, Water, and Surface Change

- S–P lag: `24.0 − 14.0 = 10.0 s`.
- Labeled practice distance estimate: `10.0 × 8.4 = 84 km`.
- One station gives a distance circle; direction or epicenter requires additional stations or constraints.
- Water storage change: `120 − 50 − 45 = 25 mm`.
- Runoff volume: `45 mm = 0.045 m`; area `3 km² = 3,000,000 m²`; volume `0.045 × 3,000,000 = 135,000 m³`.
- Causal chain may include internal energy → mantle flow and plate motion → uplift or volcanism → relief → weathering → erosion and transport → deposition → watershed form and sediment effects.
- Limitations may include the linear seismic factor, sparse stations, timing precision, heterogeneous crust, simplified water budget, variable rainfall, groundwater exchange, scale, and map resolution.
- Safety must prohibit floodwater, unstable slopes, cliffs, sinkholes, caves, mines, restricted land, and disaster sites.

## Constructed Response 2 — Carbon, Nutrients, Climate, and Resilience

- Carbon net change: `90 − 70 = +20 units/year`.
- Modeled amount after one year: `2400 + 20 = 2420 units`.
- Simple residence time: `2400 ÷ 70 ≈ 34.29 years`.
- Reflected solar: `340 × 0.29 = 98.6 W/m²`.
- Absorbed solar: `340 − 98.6 = 241.4 W/m²`.
- Net imbalance: `241.4 − 239 = +2.4 W/m²`.
- Fast carbon pathways include photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, fire, and air–sea exchange. Slow pathways include burial, carbonate formation, weathering, tectonics, and volcanism.
- Nitrogen has a large atmospheric N₂ reservoir and requires fixation; phosphorus has no major atmospheric gas reservoir in the introductory model and depends strongly on weathering and sediment processes.
- Eutrophication pathway: excess nutrient input → high productivity → decomposition → increased oxygen consumption → possible low dissolved oxygen.
- A forcing initiates an energy-balance change; a feedback responds to and modifies that change.
- Mitigation reduces forcing or emissions; adaptation reduces exposure or vulnerability. Evaluation should address uncertainty, benefits, burdens, access, and affected communities.
