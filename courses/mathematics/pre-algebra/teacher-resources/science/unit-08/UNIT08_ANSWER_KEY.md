# Unit 08 Definitive Answer Key

Course: International Integrated Science 9 (`KH-SCI-IIS9`)

## Motion Graph Laboratory Check

### 1. A runner travels 120 m in 20 s. Average speed is:
**Correct answer:** A. 6 m/s

120 ÷ 20 = 6 m/s.

### 2. A person walks 5 m east and then 2 m west. Displacement is:
**Correct answer:** B. 3 m east

Taking east as positive, 5 − 2 = 3 m east.

### 3. A car’s average velocity is zero when:
**Correct answer:** C. Its final position equals its initial position

Average velocity depends on displacement, which is zero when final and initial positions match.

### 4. The slope of a position–time graph gives:
**Correct answer:** D. Velocity

Change in position divided by change in time is velocity.

### 5. The signed area under a velocity–time graph gives:
**Correct answer:** A. Displacement

Velocity multiplied by time gives displacement.

### 6. Velocity changes from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Average acceleration is:
**Correct answer:** B. 2 m/s²

(10−2) ÷ 4 = 2 m/s².

### 7. An object has velocity −4 m/s and acceleration +2 m/s². It is initially:
**Correct answer:** C. Moving negative and slowing down

Velocity and acceleration have opposite signs, so speed initially decreases.

### 8. Which change most improves confidence in a walking-speed estimate?
**Correct answer:** D. Repeated trials and a documented mean

Repeated trials reveal variation and support a better estimate.

## Force and Newton Laboratory Check

### 1. Forces of 12 N right and 5 N left act on an object. Net force is:
**Correct answer:** B. 7 N right

12−5 = 7 N right.

### 2. Zero net force means an object:
**Correct answer:** C. May be at rest or move with constant velocity

Newton’s first law permits rest or constant velocity.

### 3. A 3 kg object has net force 12 N. Acceleration is:
**Correct answer:** D. 4 m/s²

a=F/m=12/3=4 m/s².

### 4. For the same net force, doubling mass makes acceleration:
**Correct answer:** A. Half as large

Acceleration is inversely proportional to mass.

### 5. A 5 kg mass near Earth with g=9.8 N/kg weighs approximately:
**Correct answer:** B. 49 N

W=mg=5×9.8=49 N.

### 6. A book pushes down on a table. The third-law partner is:
**Correct answer:** C. The table pushing up on the book

The paired interaction is book on table and table on book.

### 7. A free-body diagram should show:
**Correct answer:** D. Forces acting on the selected object

It isolates the object and displays external forces on it.

### 8. Friction commonly:
**Correct answer:** A. Opposes relative surface motion and transfers energy thermally

Friction resists relative motion and often heats surfaces.

## Momentum and Impulse Laboratory Check

### 1. A 4 kg cart moves at 3 m/s east. Momentum is:
**Correct answer:** C. 12 kg·m/s east

p=mv=4×3=12 kg·m/s east.

### 2. Momentum is a vector because it has:
**Correct answer:** D. Magnitude and direction

Momentum inherits velocity direction.

### 3. A 10 N average force acts for 0.5 s. Impulse is:
**Correct answer:** A. 5 N·s

J=FΔt=10×0.5=5 N·s.

### 4. Impulse equals:
**Correct answer:** B. Change in momentum

Impulse is the change in momentum.

### 5. For the same momentum change, increasing stopping time generally:
**Correct answer:** C. Decreases average force

Favg=Δp/Δt, so a longer time lowers average force.

### 6. Momentum conservation applies most directly when:
**Correct answer:** D. External impulse is negligible for the chosen system

A closed or effectively isolated system has conserved total momentum.

### 7. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Final shared speed is:
**Correct answer:** A. 2 m/s

Initial momentum 6 kg·m/s divided by total mass 3 kg gives 2 m/s.

### 8. During a collision, the force of A on B and B on A are:
**Correct answer:** B. Equal in magnitude and opposite in direction

Newton’s third law applies to the interaction pair.

## Energy, Work and Power Laboratory Check

### 1. A 20 N force moves an object 3 m in the force direction. Work is:
**Correct answer:** D. 60 J

W=Fd=20×3=60 J.

### 2. A 2 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Kinetic energy is:
**Correct answer:** A. 16 J

KE=½mv²=0.5×2×16=16 J.

### 3. If speed doubles at constant mass, kinetic energy becomes:
**Correct answer:** B. Four times as large

KE depends on v².

### 4. A 3 kg object rises 2 m with g=9.8 N/kg. GPE increase is approximately:
**Correct answer:** C. 58.8 J

ΔGPE=mgΔh=3×9.8×2=58.8 J.

### 5. When friction slows a moving object, mechanical energy is mainly:
**Correct answer:** D. Transferred to thermal energy of the system and surroundings

Energy is transferred, not destroyed.

### 6. 600 J is transferred in 20 s. Power is:
**Correct answer:** A. 30 W

P=E/t=600/20=30 W.

### 7. A device receives 500 J and delivers 350 J useful output. Efficiency is:
**Correct answer:** B. 70%

350/500×100=70%.

### 8. An ideal machine can:
**Correct answer:** C. Trade force for distance or direction

Machines rearrange force and distance without creating energy.

## Constructed Response 1 — Motion, Graphs and Force

- Interval velocities: `0–2 s: (3−0)/2 = 1.5 m/s`; `2–4 s: (8−3)/2 = 2.5 m/s`; `4–6 s: (15−8)/2 = 3.5 m/s`.
- Overall average velocity: `(15−0)/6 = 2.5 m/s` in the positive direction.
- Increasing graph slope shows increasing velocity and positive acceleration over the recorded intervals.
- Net force: `14 N − 4 N = 10 N` right.
- Acceleration: `a = Fnet/m = 10/5 = 2 m/s²` right.
- Free-body diagram should show pull right, resistance left, normal force up, and weight down.
- Valid limitations include sampled rather than continuous position, timing precision, unmodeled rotation, nonconstant force, surface variation, drag, and point-mass approximation.

## Constructed Response 2 — Momentum, Energy and Safety

- Initial momentum: `2 kg × 4 m/s = 8 kg·m/s`; second cart contributes zero.
- Shared speed: `8 kg·m/s ÷ 4 kg = 2 m/s`.
- Initial KE: `½(2)(4²) = 16 J`.
- Final KE: `½(4)(2²) = 8 J`.
- Momentum is conserved for the isolated system; 8 J of kinetic energy is transferred into deformation, thermal energy, sound, or other internal pathways.
- Momentum change magnitude for the joined system stopping: `8 kg·m/s`.
- Average force magnitude for 0.20 s: `8/0.20 = 40 N`; for 0.50 s: `8/0.50 = 16 N`.
- Longer stopping time lowers average force for the same momentum change, but injury risk also depends on force distribution, direction, restraint geometry, body region, deformation, and other factors.
- Safe methods include mathematical modeling, approved video analysis, or light low-speed carts without people or animals.
