Foundations & Readiness · Lesson 02

Fractions, Decimals, Percents & Ratios

Move flexibly among equivalent representations, operate accurately with rational numbers, and use ratios and unit rates to compare quantities fairly.

Today's Math Word · Equivalent

Two representations are equivalent when they have the same value even though they look different.

3/4 = 0.75 = 75% · same quantity, three representations

1 · Refresh the ideas

Fractions

For addition or subtraction, first express the fractions with a common denominator. For multiplication, multiply numerators and denominators and simplify. For division, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.

Decimals and percents

Fraction → decimal: divide numerator by denominator. Decimal → percent: multiply by 100%. Percent → decimal: divide by 100. Always ask whether the representation is sensible for the context.

Ratios and unit rates

A ratio compares two quantities. A unit rate compares one quantity to exactly one unit of another. Five notebooks costing $12.50 have a unit price of $2.50 per notebook.

Percent change

Discounts and increases are based on a reference amount. A 20% discount from $75 is $15, producing $60—not $55. Percent reasoning requires identifying the correct whole.

2 · Worked examples

Example A. Compute 2/3 + 1/6.

2/3=4/6, so 4/6+1/6=5/6.

Example B. Convert 0.375 to a percent and simplified fraction.

0.375=37.5%. As a fraction, 375/1000 simplifies to 3/8.

Example C. A car travels 180 miles on 6 gallons.

180÷6=30, so the unit rate is 30 miles per gallon.

Example D. Find 35% of 80.

35%=0.35. Then 0.35×80=28.

5 · Application Lab · Smart Shopping Audit

A jacket has a regular price of $75. It is discounted 20%, then 6% sales tax is applied to the discounted price.

  1. Estimate the final price before exact calculation.
  2. Find the discount amount and discounted price.
  3. Find the tax on the discounted price.
  4. Find the final price.
  5. Explain why taking 26% directly off $75 would not model this situation correctly.
