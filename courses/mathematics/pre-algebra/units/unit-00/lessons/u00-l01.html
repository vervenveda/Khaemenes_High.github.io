Number Sense & Operations

Rebuild fluency with magnitude, signed numbers, order of operations, estimation, and reasonableness.

Math Word: Magnitude

Magnitude is the size or distance of a number from zero. It helps you compare quantities before calculating.

Estimate first → calculate → compare the answer to the estimate.

Guided refresh

  • Order: grouping → exponents → multiplication/division → addition/subtraction.
  • Signed numbers: subtraction can be rewritten as adding the opposite.
  • Reasonableness: an exact answer that strongly disagrees with a sensible estimate deserves rechecking.

Example: 198×51 is close to 200×50=10,000, so an answer of 1,009 is not reasonable.

Independent readiness checks

1. Evaluate 18−3(4)+2.
2. Evaluate −7+12−9.
3. Which has greater magnitude: −14 or 9? Explain.
4. Estimate 397×21, then calculate exactly.
5. A learner says 48÷6×2=4. Identify the error and correct it.
6. A temperature changes from −3°C to 8°C. What is the change?

Application

A warehouse records changes of +18, −7, −11, and +9 units. Determine the net change and explain how you checked whether the sign of your answer makes sense.

Week 1 evidence: accurate computation plus a written reasonableness check. If your diagnostic showed this strand as strong, these problems may serve as your confirmation challenge.
