Foundations & Readiness · Lesson 01

Number Sense & Operations

Rebuild fluency with magnitude, signed numbers, arithmetic structure, order of operations, estimation, and reasonableness. The goal is not speed alone—it is knowing whether an answer makes mathematical sense.

Today's Math Word · Magnitude

Magnitude is the size or distance of a number from zero. The magnitude of −14 is 14 and the magnitude of 9 is 9, so −14 has the greater magnitude even though 9 is the greater number.

Estimate first → calculate carefully → compare → explain whether the result is reasonable.

1 · Refresh the ideas

Signed numbers

Positive and negative numbers can represent direction, gain/loss, elevation, temperature, or change. Rewrite subtraction as adding the opposite when that makes the structure clearer: 7 − (−4) = 7 + 4 = 11.

Order of operations

Use grouping symbols first, then exponents, multiplication/division from left to right, and addition/subtraction from left to right. Multiplication does not automatically come before division when they appear at the same level.

Estimation and reasonableness

For 198 × 51, estimate 200 × 50 = 10,000. The exact answer should be near 10,000. A calculator entry producing 1,009 should trigger an audit.

Absolute value

|−8| = 8 because −8 is eight units from zero. Absolute value describes distance, so it is never negative.

2 · Worked examples

Example A. Evaluate 18 − 3(4) + 2.

Multiply first: 3(4)=12. Then work left to right: 18−12+2=8.

Example B. Evaluate −7 + 12 − 9.

−7+12=5, then 5−9=−4. A number-line check confirms the final position is four units below zero.

Example C. Estimate 397×21.

400×20≈8,000. Exact: 397×21=8,337. The exact answer is close to the estimate, so it is plausible.

Example D. Evaluate 48÷6×2.

Division and multiplication have equal priority, so move left to right: 48÷6=8, then 8×2=16.

5 · Application Lab · Inventory Audit

A community supply room begins with 240 notebooks. During the week, deliveries and distributions are recorded as +96, −58, −71, +44, −39.

  1. Predict whether the final inventory will be above or below 240.
  2. Find the net change.
  3. Find the final inventory.
  4. Use a second method or estimate to verify your answer.
  5. One volunteer reports a final inventory of 412. Explain why that result should be questioned even before recalculating every entry.

6 · Critical-thinking challenge

A calculator displays 4,032 for 198×21. Another learner says, “The calculator is more reliable than my estimate, so it must be correct.” Determine whether 4,032 is correct and explain what role estimation should play when technology and reasoning appear to disagree.
