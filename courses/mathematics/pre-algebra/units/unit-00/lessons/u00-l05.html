Foundations & Readiness · Lesson 05

Coordinates, Patterns & Functions

Connect location, patterns, tables, rules, ordered pairs, and introductory rate of change. Learn to recognize when several representations are telling the same mathematical story.

Today's Math Word · Function

A function assigns each allowed input exactly one output. Different inputs may share an output, but one input cannot be assigned two different outputs within the same function.

input → rule → exactly one output

1 · Refresh the ideas

The coordinate plane

An ordered pair is written (x,y). Move horizontally using x first, then vertically using y. Quadrant I is (+,+), II is (−,+), III is (−,−), and IV is (+,−). Points on an axis are not in a quadrant.

Patterns and rules

A sequence can often be described with a rule. The pattern 5,8,11,14,… increases by 3. With term number n beginning at 1, a rule is 3n+2.

Tables, equations, and ordered pairs

For y=2x+1, x=0,1,2 gives y=1,3,5, producing (0,1), (1,3), (2,5). The table, equation, and ordered pairs represent the same relationship.

Rate of change

Rate of change compares the change in output with the change in input: Δy/Δx. If x increases by 2 while y increases by 10, the rate is 10/2=5 output units per input unit.

2 · Worked examples

Example A. Locate (−4,3).

x is negative and y is positive, so the point lies in Quadrant II.

Example B. Complete y=3x−2 for x=0,1,2.

The outputs are −2,1,4. Ordered pairs: (0,−2), (1,1), (2,4).

Example C. Does {(1,2),(2,4),(1,5)} define a function?

No. Input 1 has two outputs, 2 and 5.

Example D. A taxi costs $3 plus $2 per mile.

Let m be miles and C be cost: C=2m+3. The rate of change is $2 per mile; $3 is the starting value.

5 · Application Lab · Water Tank Model

A water tank contains 40 liters at the start and fills at a constant rate of 5 liters per minute.

  1. Identify the independent and dependent variables.
  2. Write an equation for volume V after m minutes.
  3. Create a table for m=0,2,4,6.
  4. Write the corresponding ordered pairs.
  5. Identify the starting value and rate of change.
  6. Predict the volume after 15 minutes and explain which representation made the prediction easiest.

6 · Critical-thinking challenge · Same pattern or coincidence?

Two relationships agree at the points (1,5) and (2,8). A learner concludes they must be the same function everywhere. Is that evidence sufficient? Explain why matching at two inputs does or does not prove two unknown rules are identical, and describe what additional information would strengthen the claim.
