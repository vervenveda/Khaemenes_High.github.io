This week prepares students to enter Unit 1 with stronger fraction, percent, and proportional reasoning. It supports later GCF/LCM work, rational number operations, rates, equations, and graphing.

Fractions are division

A fraction describes a quotient, a part-whole relationship, a point on the number line, or a ratio depending on context.

Decimals show place value

Decimal form often makes size easier to compare, especially when denominators are different.

Percent means per hundred

Percent is a standardized ratio out of 100. Converting to decimal form makes percent calculations straightforward.

Rates and proportions

A rate compares unlike units. A proportion says two ratios are equivalent through a shared multiplier.

Deeper Concept Study Guide

How to study this week

  1. 1 · Translate first
    Identify what the quantities mean before converting. A fraction can be part of a whole, a comparison, a rate, or a division problem.
  2. 2 · Convert with purpose
    Fractions, decimals, and percents are not separate topics. They are different names for the same value. Choose the form that makes the comparison easiest.
  3. 3 · Simplify by structure
    Factors reveal why 12/18 simplifies to 2/3: both numerator and denominator share a factor of 6.
  4. 4 · Unit rates answer “per one”
    A speed of 180 miles in 3 hours becomes 60 miles per 1 hour. The unit rate gives a fair comparison.
  5. 5 · Proportions preserve scale
    Equivalent ratios keep the same multiplicative relationship. If 2:5 scales by 4, it becomes 8:20.

Worked Examples

Reason out loud before calculating quickly.

Convert 3/4

3 ÷ 4 = 0.75, and 0.75 × 100 = 75%. The forms 3/4, 0.75, and 75% name the same quantity.

Find a unit rate

If 150 miles are traveled in 3 hours, divide both terms by 3: 50 miles per hour.

Solve a proportion

If 4/5 = x/20, the denominator scaled by 4, so the numerator also scales by 4: x = 16.

Misconception Clinic

Common errors to repair before Unit 1

  • Mistake: comparing fractions by numerator only. Correction: compare common denominators, decimals, or benchmarks.
  • Mistake: treating 25% as 25 instead of 0.25. Correction: percent means per hundred, so 25% = 25/100 = 0.25.
  • Mistake: adding the same number to both terms of a ratio. Correction: equivalent ratios use multiplication or division, not addition.
