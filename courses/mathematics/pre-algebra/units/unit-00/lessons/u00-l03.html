Foundations & Readiness · Lesson 03

Variables & Mathematical Language

Learn to read algebra as a language: identify its parts, translate words into symbols, evaluate expressions, simplify structure, and explain what each quantity means.

Today's Math Word · Coefficient

A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. In 7x, the coefficient is 7. In −x, the coefficient is −1 even though the 1 is not written.

expression → terms → coefficients / variables / constants → meaning

1 · Refresh the ideas

Variables and constants

A variable represents a number that may change or be unknown. A constant has a fixed value. In 4g+18, g is the variable, 4 is its coefficient, and 18 is a constant.

Terms and like terms

Terms are separated by addition or subtraction. Like terms have the same variable part. 5x+3x=8x, but 5x+3 cannot be combined into 8x.

Substitution

To evaluate an expression, replace each variable with its given value and then use the order of operations. If a=5, then 3a+4=3(5)+4=19.

Translating words

Order matters. “Four less than three times n” means 3n−4. “Four minus three times n” means 4−3n. Read the relationship before choosing symbols.

2 · Worked examples

Example A. Identify the parts of 6x−11.

Terms: 6x and −11. Variable: x. Coefficient: 6. Constant: −11.

Example B. Translate “five more than twice a number.”

Twice a number is 2x; five more gives 2x+5. It is not 2(x+5).

Example C. Simplify 5x+2+3x−7.

Combine like terms: 5x+3x=8x and 2−7=−5, giving 8x−5.

Example D. Are 2(x+3) and 2x+3 equivalent?

No. Distributing gives 2x+6, not 2x+3. Testing x=1 also gives 8 versus 5.

5 · Application Lab · Build a Pricing Model

A community art studio charges a one-time materials fee of $12 plus $3 per session.

  1. Define a variable for the number of sessions.
  2. Write an expression for total cost.
  3. Identify the coefficient and constant and explain their real-world meanings.
  4. Evaluate the expression for 1, 4, and 10 sessions.
  5. A student writes 12s+3. Explain why that model does not match the situation.

6 · Critical-thinking challenge

A learner says 3(x+4)=3x+4 because “the 3 only belongs to x.” Use a numerical test value and the distributive property to evaluate the claim. Explain why one counterexample is enough to disprove a statement that claims two expressions are always equivalent.
