Foundations & Readiness · Lesson 06

Measurement, Geometry & Integrated Readiness

Use units, formulas, diagrams, and evidence carefully enough to decide what can—and cannot—be concluded.

Math Word: Sufficiency

Sufficiency asks whether the available information is enough to justify the requested conclusion.

Given facts + valid formula/theorem → justified conclusion

Guided refresh

  • Perimeter measures distance around; area measures surface covered.
  • Always preserve units and convert before combining incompatible measures.
  • A diagram may suggest information that the problem never actually gives.
  • When information is insufficient, saying so is mathematically stronger than guessing.

Example: Knowing two side lengths of a triangle does not by itself determine its exact area.

Independent readiness checks

1. Find the perimeter and area of a 9 cm by 4 cm rectangle.
2. Convert 2.5 m to centimeters.
3. Two angles form a straight line. One is 68°. Find the other.
4. A square has side 7 cm. Find its area and explain the units.
5. A triangle has sides 6 cm and 10 cm. Can its exact area be determined from that alone? Explain.
6. A floor plan uses a scale of 1 cm : 2 m. A wall measures 4.5 cm on the plan. Find the actual length.

Integrated challenge

A rectangular room is 6 m by 4.5 m. Flooring costs $18 per square meter and the budget is $500. Determine whether the budget is sufficient. Show measurement, area, multiplication, and a final evidence-based decision.

Week 6 evidence: solve an integrated multi-step problem, preserve units, and recognize at least one situation where more information would be needed.
