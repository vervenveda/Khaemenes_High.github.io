Session 2 · Teach / Reteach

Six Foundations That Hold the Course Together

Read for meaning, not memorization. Each idea below includes the common mistake it is designed to prevent.

1 · Operation Priority

Grouping → exponents → multiplication/division left to right → addition/subtraction left to right.

Multiplication does not always come before division; they share a level. The same is true for addition and subtraction.

2 · Factor vs. Multiple

A factor divides a number evenly. A multiple is produced by multiplying that number by an integer.

6 is a factor of 24. 24 is a multiple of 6.

3 · Prime Structure

A prime number has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. A composite number has more than two.

1 is neither prime nor composite because it has only one positive factor.

4 · Signed Numbers

Signs describe position and direction. Addition can be modeled as movement; subtraction can be modeled as adding the opposite.

-7 - (-4) = -7 + 4 = -3.

5 · Absolute Value

Absolute value is distance from zero, so it is never negative.

|-9| = 9 and |6| = 6.

6 · Exponents

An exponent counts repeated multiplication of the base.

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81, not 3 × 4.

Reasonableness check: Before accepting an answer, ask whether the sign, size, units, and operation all fit the original problem. Correct procedure without a reasonableness check is fragile mathematics.

Session 3 · Guided Error Laboratory

Find the Exact Moment the Reasoning Breaks

Do not merely replace the final answer. Identify the invalid step and explain the principle that repairs it.

Error A · Order of operations

18 − 3 × 4 = 15 × 4 = 60

Error B · Prime classification

1 is prime because it can be divided by 1 and itself.

Error C · Integers

−6 + 11 = −17 because the signs are different.

Error D · Exponents

2⁵ = 10 because 2 × 5 = 10.

A: Multiplication and division are completed before subtraction: 3 × 4 = 12, then 18 − 12 = 6.

B: Prime numbers require exactly two distinct positive factors. The number 1 has only one.

C: Opposite signs compete in magnitude. Eleven positive units cancel six negative units, leaving +5.

D: 2⁵ means five factors of 2: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

Session 5 · Application

Community Supply Planning

A fictional community center is preparing supply kits. Use the information below to reason across several number foundations.

Given: 84 notebooks, 126 pencils, a storage change of −18 boxes followed by +27 boxes, and a packaging expression of 96 − 4 × 15. Each identical kit must use equal whole-number quantities with no leftovers.

1. 42 kits. The greatest common factor of 84 and 126 is 42, giving 2 notebooks and 3 pencils per kit.

2. +9 boxes. The positive change has magnitude 27 and the negative change has magnitude 18, so 27 − 18 = 9.

3. 36. Multiply first: 4 × 15 = 60; then 96 − 60 = 36.

4. A distance of 12 units from zero; absolute value measures magnitude/distance without direction.
