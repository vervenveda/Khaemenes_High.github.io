Bridge purpose

Algebra begins before variables look difficult.

Students often struggle with algebra because they see letters and forget that algebra is still number reasoning. This week rebuilds the habits that keep algebra stable: read the expression, preserve order, use inverse operations, keep equations balanced, and check solutions in the original relationship.

Read: identify numbers, variables, operations, and grouping.
Substitute: replace a variable with a value using parentheses when needed.
Solve: undo operations in the reverse order while preserving balance.
Check: put the solution back into the original statement.

Learn

Operations preserve relationships.

Expressions

Variables stand for values. Substitute carefully, use parentheses around negative replacements, and preserve the original order of operations.

Equations

An equation states equality. Applying the same valid operation to both sides preserves balance.

Inverse operations

Addition and subtraction undo one another; multiplication and division undo one another. Solving an equation means undoing what was done to the variable.

Check the solution

Substitute the result back into the original relationship. A solution is not proven until it makes the original statement true.

Concept study guide

What students must understand before formal algebra.

1 · Expression versus equation

An expression is a mathematical phrase. It can be simplified or evaluated. An equation is a mathematical sentence with an equal sign. It can be solved.

FormMeaningStudent action
3x + 5ExpressionEvaluate or simplify
3x + 5 = 20EquationSolve and check

2 · Substitution must preserve structure

Replacing a variable is not just “dropping in a number.” Students must keep grouping and operation order intact, especially when the value is negative.

For 2x² when x = −3, write 2(−3)², not 2−3².

3 · Balance reasoning

Solving is not a trick. It is a controlled sequence of balance-preserving moves. Whatever is done to one side of an equation must also be done to the other side.

  • To undo +7, subtract 7 from both sides.
  • To undo −4, add 4 to both sides.
  • To undo ×5, divide both sides by 5.
  • To undo ÷3, multiply both sides by 3.

4 · Inequalities have direction

An inequality compares two values. Students should read it aloud and identify which side is greater. This prepares them for formal inequality solving later.

At this refresher level, focus on interpreting and checking inequalities, not advanced inequality transformations.

Guided examples

Show the mathematical move, not just the answer.

Example A · Evaluate an expression

Question: Evaluate 4x − 6 when x = 5.

  1. Substitute: 4(5) − 6.
  2. Multiply first: 20 − 6.
  3. Subtract: 14.

Answer: 14.

Example B · Use inverse operations

Question: Solve x + 9 = 22.

  1. The variable has 9 added to it.
  2. Undo +9 by subtracting 9 from both sides.
  3. x = 13.
  4. Check: 13 + 9 = 22.

Example C · Solve a two-step equation

Question: Solve 3x − 4 = 11.

  1. Add 4 to both sides: 3x = 15.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 5.
  3. Check: 3(5) − 4 = 15 − 4 = 11.

Answer: x = 5.
