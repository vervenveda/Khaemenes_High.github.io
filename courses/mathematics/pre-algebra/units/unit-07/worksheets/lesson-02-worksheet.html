Directions: Show algebraic structure and steps. Define variables and units in context. Verify equivalent forms or numerical results.

1. “Nine more than x” is:

(A) 9x   (B) x+9   (C) 9−x   (D) x/9

2. “Three times the sum of n and 5” is:

(A) 3n+5   (B) 3(n+5)   (C) n+15   (D) 3n+15n

3. “The difference of a and b divided by 4” is:

(A) a−b/4   (B) (a−b)/4   (C) 4(a−b)   (D) a/(b−4)

4. Seven less than n is:

(A) n+7   (B) n−7   (C) 7−n   (D) 7n

5. The quotient of p and 6 is:

(A) 6p   (B) p−6   (C) p/6   (D) 6/p

6. A taxi costs $4 plus $2 per mile m. Cost expression:

(A) 6m   (B) 4m+2   (C) 2(m+4)   (D) 4+2m

7. Half the quantity x+10 is:

(A) x+5   (B) x+10/2   (C) 2(x+10)   (D) (x+10)/2

8. If r is dollars per ticket and t is tickets, rt has units:

(A) dollars per ticket   (B) no units   (C) dollars   (D) tickets

9. The square of a number n decreased by 6 is:

(A) 2n−6   (B) 6−n²   (C) n²−6   (D) (n−6)²

10. Why define variables before modelling?

(A) to guarantee integer answers   (B) to eliminate context   (C) to avoid parentheses   (D) to make meaning and units explicit
