Number Structure Investigation

Choose a real setting, collect at least fourteen meaningful numbers, classify them, expose their factor structure, estimate with them, and explain what the mathematics reveals.

Open Project Workspace

Project sequence

Choose a context that matters.

Select a context

Examples: weather across world cities, a sports schedule, music rhythms, environmental cleanup data, a family budget, transit cycles, architecture measurements, or a community event.

Collect and document numbers

Use at least fourteen values. Record units, source or method, date where relevant, and why each number matters.

Classify and structure

Classify each value by number set. For whole-number values, identify factors, prime factorization, GCF, or LCM where useful.

Estimate and verify

Make at least three estimates before calculating exact results. Compare estimates with exact values and discuss reasonableness.

Communicate a conclusion

Create a one-page report, poster, slide, data story, or short presentation explaining what the number structure helped you understand.

Project completion checklist

Evidence

Include at least 14 meaningful values with units or context; at least 6 number-set classifications; at least 3 factor/prime-structure examples; at least 2 GCF/LCM applications; at least 2 evaluated expressions; and at least 3 estimates used to check reasonableness.

Communication

Include one visual representation, one written conclusion, and one reflection explaining a revision you made after checking your mathematics.

Rubric
