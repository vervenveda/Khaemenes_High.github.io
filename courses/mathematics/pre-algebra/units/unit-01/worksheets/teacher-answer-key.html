Lesson 01 · Number Families & the Real Number System

1. B — Whole
In this course, natural numbers begin at 1. Zero is whole; it is also integer, rational, and real.

2. D — Integer
−17 is an integer. It is also rational and real.

3. A — √3
3 is not a perfect square, so √3 cannot be expressed as a ratio of integers.

4. C — Every integer is rational.
Any integer n can be written as n/1, so every integer is rational.

5. C — Natural
√49 = 7, and 7 is a natural number.

6. A — 0.272727…
A repeating decimal can be written as a fraction, so 0.272727… is rational.

7. D — Rational
−5/2 is a ratio of integers and is not an integer, so rational is most specific.

8. B — π
π is irrational. The other choices can all be written as ratios of integers.

9. A — Natural
12/3 simplifies to 4, which is a natural number.

10. C — Irrational
A nonterminating decimal with no repeating pattern is irrational.

Lesson 02 · Factors, Multiples & Number Structure

1. B — 6
54 ÷ 6 = 9 with no remainder.

2. D — 8, 16, 24
8×1=8, 8×2=16, and 8×3=24.

3. A — 6 and 8
6 × 8 = 48.

4. C — 5
The positive factors are 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16.

5. C — 30
30 = 5 × 6 and is divisible by both 5 and 6.

6. A — n has infinitely many positive multiples.
The multiples n, 2n, 3n, 4n, … continue without end.

7. D — 14
72 ÷ 14 is not a whole number.

8. B — 12
84 ÷ 7 = 12.

9. A — 1×20, 2×10, 4×5
The systematic pairs are 1×20, 2×10, and 4×5.

10. C — 6
30 ÷ 6 = 5 and 42 ÷ 6 = 7.

Lesson 03 · Prime Numbers, Divisibility & Prime Factorization

1. B — 31
31 has no positive factors other than 1 and 31.

2. D — 25
25 = 5 × 5, so it has more than two positive factors.

3. A — It is neither prime nor composite.
1 has only one positive factor, so it fits neither definition.

4. C — 342
3 + 4 + 2 = 9, which is divisible by 3.

5. C — 532
The last two digits of 532 are 32, and 32 is divisible by 4.

6. A — 234
234 is even and its digit sum 9 is divisible by 3.

7. D — 639
6 + 3 + 9 = 18, and 18 is divisible by 9.

8. B — 410
A number divisible by 10 ends in 0.

9. A — 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
2, 2, 2, and 3 are all prime and multiply to 24.

10. C — 3 × 3 × 5
45 = 3 × 3 × 5.

Lesson 04 · Greatest Common Factor & Least Common Multiple

1. B — 6
The common factors are 1, 2, 3, and 6; the greatest is 6.

2. D — 12
12 divides both 24 and 36 and is their greatest common factor.

3. A — 7
35 = 5×7 and 49 = 7×7, so the greatest shared factor is 7.

4. C — 12
12 is the first positive multiple shared by 4 and 6.

5. C — 40
Because 5 and 8 share no prime factor, their LCM is 5×8 = 40.

6. A — 45
45 is divisible by both 9 and 15 and is the smallest positive common multiple.

7. D — 1, 2, 4, 8
The common factors are exactly 1, 2, 4, and 8.

8. B — 36
36 = 12×3 and 18×2.

9. A — 6
The shared prime factors are 2 and 3, so GCF = 6.

10. C — 90
Use 2×3²×5 = 90.

Lesson 05 · Order of Operations & Evaluating Expressions

1. B — 17
Multiply first: 4×3=12; then 5+12=17.

2. D — 15
Divide first: 6÷2=3; then 18−3=15.

3. A — 4
Parentheses first: 12÷3=4.

4. C — 13
3²=9; 9+4=13.

5. C — 50
Exponent first: 5²=25; then 2×25=50.

6. A — 18
Work left to right: 24÷4=6, then 6×3=18.

7. D — 15
Addition and subtraction share priority: 20−8=12, then 12+3=15.

8. B — 14
Parentheses: 5; multiply: 2×5=10; add: 14.

9. A — 18
2²=4; 3×4=12; 6+12=18.

10. C — 3
Grouping first: 3×4=12; 36÷12=3.

Lesson 06 · Estimation, Compatible Numbers & Reasonableness

1. B — About 1,000
398≈400 and 607≈600, so the sum is about 1,000.

2. D — About 1,000
1,984≈2,000 and 1,021≈1,000, giving about 1,000.

3. A — About 1,000
50×20=1,000.

4. C — 600 ÷ 30 = 20
600 and 30 are both close to the original values and divide easily.

5. C — About 40
7.92≈8 and 8×5=40.

6. A — 60×40≈2,400
The expected product is around 2,400, not 24,000.

7. D — 3,000÷60=50
3,000 and 60 are close compatible numbers, producing about 50.

8. B — The sum of two numbers near 500 should be near 1,000.
A magnitude estimate near 1,000 immediately exposes the place-value error.

9. A — is approximately
Approximately signals that the value is an estimate.

10. C — 6,700
The tens digit is 4, so the hundreds digit stays 7: 6,700.
