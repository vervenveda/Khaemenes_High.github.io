Estimation, Compatible Numbers & Reasonableness

How can estimation help us predict, check, and communicate whether a numerical answer makes sense?

      Concept development

      Approximate deliberately. Check intelligently.

      This section teaches estimation as a decision process. Read the concept cards as tools for thinking, not vocabulary to memorize.

      How to study these concepts

      Use each concept as a question you ask before calculating.

      Estimation becomes powerful when a student can explain why an approximation is useful. In this lesson, every estimate should answer a purpose: predicting the size of an answer, making a quick decision, or checking whether an exact result could possibly be right.

      1. 1Name the purpose. Decide whether you are planning, checking, comparing, budgeting, or choosing a reasonable answer. A quick magnitude check may need only rough rounding, while a money decision may need a tighter estimate.
      2. 2Choose the strategy. Use rounding for sums and differences, compatible numbers for quotients, benchmark factors for products, and money estimates when dollars or quantities need a practical decision.
      3. 3State what changed. Write which numbers were replaced and why. For example, 598 ÷ 29 becomes 600 ÷ 30 because 600 and 30 are nearby values that divide easily.
      4. 4Check the scale. Compare the estimate with the exact or reported answer. If the exact answer has the wrong place value, sign, size, unit, or direction of change, it is not reasonable.
