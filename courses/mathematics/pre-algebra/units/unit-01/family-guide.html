Family Guide

What students are learning and how families can support reasoning without taking over the work.

Foundation Practice

What this unit teaches

Students study real-number families, factors and multiples, prime numbers, greatest common factors, least common multiples, order of operations, and estimation.

The deeper goal is recognizing structure, choosing efficient methods, checking whether answers make sense, and explaining reasoning.

Helpful questions

What do you notice about the number?
Can you show it with a diagram, list, or number line?
Is this a grouping problem or a repeating-cycle problem?
What operation should happen next, and why?
What estimate would check the answer?
Can you find a counterexample?

Practice naturally

  • Find factor pairs while arranging chairs, tiles, or collections.
  • Compare repeating schedules and predict when events align.
  • Estimate a grocery total before checking the receipt.
  • Classify temperatures, elevations, financial changes, and measurements.
  • Look for divisibility patterns in dates, scores, or page numbers.
  • Ask whether a calculator answer is reasonable.

Help without giving the answer

  • Ask the learner to explain the first step.
  • Return to a smaller example with the same structure.
  • Encourage an estimate or drawing.
  • Use the worked examples and vocabulary table.
  • Praise revision, explanation, and persistence rather than speed.
  • Pause when frustration rises.

Understanding pathways

Foundation adds modelling, hints, vocabulary support, and smaller steps.

Core contains the complete expected learning.

Extended adds unfamiliar problems, counterexamples, bounds, and structural reasoning.

Pathways can differ by skill and are not permanent labels.

When extra support may help

Consider targeted review when a learner consistently struggles to:

  • Recall multiplication facts needed for factoring
  • Represent fractions and decimals on a number line
  • Distinguish factors from multiples
  • Follow one operation at a time
  • Estimate the approximate size of a result

Use diagnostic and domain feedback to identify the skill rather than repeating everything.
