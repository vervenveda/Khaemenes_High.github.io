Rational Quantity Investigation

Choose a real system involving fractional or decimal quantities, represent the same values in multiple forms, perform meaningful operations, and explain the precision your conclusion requires.

Open Project Workspace

Project sequence

Investigate quantities that can be represented more than one way.

Select a context and central question

Examples: recipe scaling, garden or design measurements, environmental resource use, travel distances, art materials, package comparisons, sports data, or scientific readings.

Collect rational quantities

Use at least twelve values. Include fractions and decimals, record units and sources or measurement methods, and identify whether each value is exact or rounded.

Create equivalent representations

Convert at least six values among fraction, decimal, and percent forms. Simplify fractions and state the precision of rounded decimals.

Analyze with operations

Use addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division to answer at least four meaningful questions. Include one estimate, one multi-step calculation, and one scale or grouping calculation.

Communicate a conclusion

Create a report, poster, data story, design sheet, or short presentation. Explain representation choices, calculations, units, precision, limitations, and one surprising result.

Rubric

DimensionProficient evidence
RepresentationEquivalent fractions, decimals, and percentages are accurate and clearly connected.
AccuracyOperations, simplification, units, signs, and conversions are correct.
ReasoningEstimates, models, and explanations justify operation and representation choices.
PrecisionExact and approximate values are distinguished and rounding is justified.
ContextData is meaningful, documented, and connected to a real question.
CommunicationRepresentations are readable, labeled, and appropriate for the audience.
