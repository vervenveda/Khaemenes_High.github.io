Family Guide

How fractions, decimals, and percentages describe the same values—and how families can support reasoning without taking over the work.

Foundation Practice

What this unit teaches

Students interpret, compare, and operate with rational numbers. They connect fractions, mixed numbers, decimals, basic percentages, benchmarks, exact values, and rounded values.

The deeper goal is to choose a useful representation, preserve units, estimate before calculating, and explain why a procedure works.

Helpful questions

What does the denominator name?
Can you show an equivalent form?
Which benchmark is nearby?
Do the fractions name the same-size parts?
Will this operation enlarge or reduce the value?
Should the result be exact or rounded?

Practice naturally

  • Scale a recipe up or down.
  • Compare unit prices and package measurements.
  • Read rulers, measuring cups, maps, and data tables.
  • Convert sports statistics or survey results between forms.
  • Estimate before using a calculator.
  • Discuss whether reported decimals are exact or rounded.

Help without giving the answer

  • Ask the learner to draw or locate the value.
  • Return to a benchmark such as 0, 1/2, or 1.
  • Ask what unit each denominator represents.
  • Encourage one representation change at a time.
  • Separate sign, magnitude, and arithmetic decisions.
  • Praise explanation and correction rather than speed.

Understanding pathways

Foundation adds models, hints, conversion charts, and smaller steps.

Core contains the complete expected learning.

Extended adds proof, error analysis, repeating decimals, complex fractions, and precision analysis.

Pathways can differ by skill and are not permanent labels.

When extra support may help

Consider targeted review when a learner consistently struggles to:

  • Interpret numerator and denominator
  • Recognize equivalent fractions
  • Compare negative rational values
  • Create common denominators
  • Distinguish multiplication from division models
  • Align decimal place values
  • Estimate a reasonable magnitude
