Unit 13 · Capstone Studio — Worked Solutions

Twenty-five ungraded capstone investigation solutions. Accept alternative defensible models when variables, calculations, assumptions, evidence and limitations are clear. The original 50 scored session questions and readiness gate are unchanged.

Session 01

A · Community garden decision: Areas: 32,35,36 m² respectively. 6×6 is best by area (36 m²), assuming equal soil, access and planting conditions.

B · Safe school water plan: Both tanks satisfy 120≤capacity≤180. A defensible recommendation requires costs, site dimensions, safety and actual rain availability; capacity alone does not determine best choice.

C · Library waiting time study: Example: What is the median waiting time during lunch this week? Population all lunch checkouts, sample systematically spread across days; record minutes only, no personal identifiers.

D · Identify a hidden preference: Example: Which display plan meets the $200 budget and produces the most readable signs? Criteria cost≤$200 and legibility test; aesthetic preference should not be built into question.

E · Project scope repair: Example compare hypothetical travel times on two local routes under stated distances/speeds, reporting minutes and noting real traffic uncertainty; no global claim.

Session 02

A · Market budget model: Revenue 9n, cost 25+4n, profit 5n−25. Break-even n=5, assuming all items sell at $9.

B · Graph the alternatives: At 0: A12 B20; 5: A27 B30; 8: A36 B36; 10: A42 B40. Equal at 8km.

C · Choose a representative data set: Mean=100/5=20 minutes; median=10. The 64-minute value affects mean strongly; include sample size and variability.

D · Scale an art installation: 7cm×25=175cm=1.75m. Scale converts same units before moving to meters.

E · Variable definitions audit: Example $30 setup+$5 per ticket: C dollars and x whole nonnegative tickets. Domain x∈{0,1,...} subject to capacity.

Session 03

A · Build and validate a fence model: A=w(25−w). Areas 100,150,156.25m². For positive dimensions 0<w<25; 12.5 yields square maximum among given choices.

B · Fix the spreadsheet error: Correct 15×6+40=$130; mistaken 55×6=$330. Fixed setup cost must not be charged six times.

C · Check a prediction against data: Residuals −1,+2,−1; largest absolute error 2 at week 2.

D · Verify a proportional model: Prediction 3.6 kg; observation 4.2 kg differs by +0.6 kg. Review recipe assumptions and measurement.

E · Revision log: Actual average rate 72/10=7.2 L/min. Revised added volume V=7.2t under similar conditions; log evidence and change.

Session 04

A · Sensitivity to price: R=$300 vs $350; a $1 price change changes revenue by $50 at 50 sales, assuming quantity sold unchanged.

B · Measurement uncertainty: Minimum 9.8×5.9=57.82m²; maximum 10.2×6.1=62.22m².

C · Equity in transportation: Overall average improves by 3 minutes, but one group worsens by 8; report subgroup outcomes, sample sizes and access constraints rather than only mean.

D · Confidence without overclaim: 18/24=75%. Say 75% of these 24 volunteers favored A; volunteer selection may not represent the full community.

E · Stress-test a decision: New cost=$525, exceeding budget by $5. Add contingency, reduce scope or seek another valid option.

Session 05

A · Defend a design choice: A is feasible at $180; B exceeds budget by $40. A provides 35m², assuming same conditions; examine maintenance and safety before final commitment.

B · Transparent model poster: Example C=12+4n: question cheapest arrangement, data fees, variable n tickets, method calculate costs, findings at chosen n, limits discounts/availability. Any coherent six-part explanation valid.

C · Answer the skeptic: Mean 14 does not imply each observation equals 14. Add median, range or distribution to show variability.

D · Algebra readiness reflection: 3x+12=2x+29; x=17. Check 3(21)=63 and 34+29=63.

E · Final revision commitment: Actual−predicted=−$36; relative error magnitude 36/240=15%. Revisit sales-volume/price assumptions and document correction.
