Educator planning

Teacher Guide

Guidance for teaching, supporting, and discussing inequalities and constraints with precision and care.

Pacing

Ten-session sequence

  1. Meaning, symbols, and testing solutions
  2. Number-line graphs and endpoints
  3. One-step positive transformations
  4. Negative multipliers and reversal
  5. Multi-step inequalities
  6. Variables on both sides and classifications
  7. Compound inequalities
  8. Contextual constraints and discrete domains
  9. Project workshop and conferencing
  10. Mastery check, reflection, and targeted reteaching

Misconceptions

Listen for these errors

  • Treating the inequality sign as an arrow that always points toward the variable.
  • Forgetting to reverse order after multiplying or dividing by a negative.
  • Using a closed point for a strict boundary.
  • Ignoring whole-number, nonnegative, unit, or capacity restrictions.
  • Assuming every inequality has one boundary ray rather than an interval, union, all-real set, or empty set.

Differentiation

Three pathways

Foundation: symbol cards, floor number lines, test-value tables, and guided transformation frames.

Core: symbolic fluency, graphs, compound conditions, and contextual interpretation.

Extended: parameter signs, logical complements, optimization, proof, and model critique.

Assessment use

Use evidence responsibly

Use lesson questions formatively. Require explanations for reversal, endpoint inclusion, and domain restrictions. The static mastery check is transparent and locally stored; vary items before high-stakes use.

Safety and context

Protect learner privacy

Use public, fictional, or classroom-generated data. Do not require learners to disclose private household finances, medical measurements, immigration status, disability information, or other sensitive eligibility data.
