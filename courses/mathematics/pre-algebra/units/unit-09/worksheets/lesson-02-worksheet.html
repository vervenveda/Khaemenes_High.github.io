Directions: Show transformations, boundary inclusion, solution direction, graph when useful, and contextual constraints. Verify in the original inequality.

1. x>−1 uses which point at −1?

(A) closed   (B) open   (C) no point   (D) two points

2. Closed at 2 and shaded right represents:

(A) x>2   (B) x≥2   (C) x<2   (D) x≤2

3. Which value is not included in x<4?

(A) 3.9   (B) 0   (C) −10   (D) 4

4. x≤−2 uses which point at −2?

(A) open point   (B) no point   (C) arrow only   (D) closed point

5. For x≥1, shade:

(A) right of 1 including 1   (B) left excluding 1   (C) left including 1   (D) only 1

6. Closed point at −3 with ray left represents:

(A) x≥−3   (B) x>−3   (C) x≤−3   (D) x<−3

7. Which describes −1 through 5 inclusive?

(A) −1<x<5   (B) x≤−1 or x≥5   (C) −1<x≤5   (D) −1≤x≤5

8. Why does a ray have an arrow?

(A) the endpoint is included   (B) there is one solution   (C) the scale is unknown   (D) the solution continues indefinitely

9. A student shades right for x≤6. Correction:

(A) mark only 6   (B) shade left and include 6   (C) shade right and exclude 6   (D) shade both ways

10. 'No greater than 12' graphs like:

(A) x>12   (B) x≤12   (C) x<12   (D) x≥12
