Unit 09 · Inequality & Constraints Studio Worked Solutions

Guidance for 35 ungraded applied investigations. Accept equivalent inequality notation, accurate boundary representations, explicit integer constraints, and valid checks. Original assessment scoring remains unchanged.

Lesson 01

A · Community hall capacity: g+12≤96, so g≤84. At 84 there are 96 people (allowed); 85 produces 97 (not allowed). Guest counts must be whole numbers at least zero.

B · Science fair minimum: 17+r≥45 gives r≥28, where r is a nonnegative integer.

C · Art studio membership: p>8; 8 does not qualify and 9 does. An open circle at 8 indicates the boundary is excluded.

D · Estimate is not enough: Actual load w≤240 kg. The 250 kg estimate exceeds the limit by 10 kg and is not permitted.

E · Invent a boundary story: Example: fewer than 12 visitors versus at most 12 visitors. Exactly 12 satisfies ≤ but not <.

Lesson 02

A · Trail altitude chart: Open circle at 120 and shade to the right. 120 excluded; 121 included.

B · Below-freezing reading: t≤−4; closed circle at −4, shade left. Examples −4, −5, −10.

C · Interval detective: x≥2; interval [2,∞). x=1 is not a solution.

D · Fix the misplaced arrow: Use an open circle at −3 and shade left. The inequality excludes −3 and includes smaller numbers.

E · Create matching representations: Example x>7: greater than 7, (7,∞), open circle at 7 and shade right. x<7 is also valid with (−∞,7).

Lesson 03

A · Bus tickets: 4n≤30 gives n≤7.5; allowable whole numbers are 0 through 7.

B · Water reserve: 145−x≥38; −x≥−107, so x≤107 L, with x≥0.

C · Reverse the addition: x>9. Open circle at 9, shade right. x=10 works (−7>−8); x=9 does not.

D · Watch the meaning: n+3≥22, so n≥19. Smallest allowable whole number is 19.

E · Repair a wrong solution: Subtract 12: y≤−17. Test −17+12=−5. The claimed 7 gives 19, which fails.

Lesson 04

A · Cold-storage rule: Divide by −2, reversing the comparison: t<−9.

B · Negative credit balance: x≥−5; at −5, 20≤20 works; at −6, 24≤20 fails.

C · Wrong-way division: Correct n≤−7. n=−8 gives 24≥21; n=−6 gives 18<21.

D · Comparing inequalities: 5x<25 => x<5; −5x<25 => x>−5. Negative division reverses inequality.

E · Design a counterexample: For x=2, 2<4 is true. Multiplying by −1 gives −2>−4, not −2<−4.

Lesson 05

A · Community market budget: 35+6n≤155 => 6n≤120 => n≤20, with n≥0 integer.

B · Two gym plans: 15+4c≤9c => c≥3. For c=3 both are $27; larger c makes A cheaper.

C · Distribution sign check: 6x−19≤17 => 6x≤36 => x≤6. At x=6, 3(12−5)−4=17.

D · Variable both sides: 7x+9<4x+30 => 3x<21 => x<7. Context requires x≥0.

E · Fix an equation swap: 2x≥12 => x≥6. Equality gives a boundary, not the only solution; x=7 also satisfies.

Lesson 06

A · Museum opening hours: 12≤a<18; closed at 12, open at 18, shade between. Integers 12 through 17.

B · Either extreme temperature: t<−5 or t>35. −5 and 20 do not trigger; 40 does. Both boundaries excluded.

C · Budget and supplies intersection: 4≤n≤10 and 3n≤24 => 4≤n≤8 for integer n.

D · Empty overlap: 'And' asks for an impossible overlap, so empty set. 'Or' includes x≤5 and x>7.

E · Correct the logic: Every real number satisfies at least one condition; x<2 satisfies x≤8 and x>8 satisfies x≥2. The union is all real numbers.

Lesson 07

A · Fundraising minimum: 84+12n≥300 => n≥18. Exactly 18 yields $300.

B · School-bus safety: Each bus carries at most 44 students. 51/44≈1.16, so at least 2 buses. Assign at most 44 per bus.

C · Art supply purchase: 95−8p≥17 => 8p≤78 => p≤9.75. Maximum 9 whole packs, with 95−72=$23 remaining.

D · Invent a feasible schedule: 17h≥260 => h≥260/17≈15.294; h≤16. With whole hours, only h=16 qualifies.

E · Detect an impossible budget: Minimum cost=14×9+24=$150, exceeding budget $140. No feasible solution unless a constraint changes.
