Directions: Show transformations, boundary inclusion, solution direction, graph when useful, and contextual constraints. Verify in the original inequality.

1. Solve x+8<15.

(A) x<7   (B) x>7   (C) x≤23   (D) x≥23

2. Solve 4p≤36.

(A) p≤9   (B) p≥9   (C) p≤32   (D) p≥40

3. Which value satisfies x<2?

(A) 2   (B) 3   (C) 1.5   (D) 10

4. Solve x−4≥9.

(A) x>13   (B) x≥13   (C) x≤13   (D) x≥5

5. Solve 5x>20.

(A) x>4   (B) x<4   (C) x≥4   (D) x>100

6. Adding the same number to both sides of an inequality:

(A) makes equality   (B) removes all solutions   (C) preserves the order   (D) always reverses it

7. Does x=2 satisfy x+4<7?

(A) only if x=7   (B) cannot tell   (C) yes   (D) no

8. You need at least 30 points and have 18; earn p more:

(A) 18+p≤30   (B) 18−p≥30   (C) 30p≥18   (D) 18+p≥30

9. Solve 4x≤16. Correct result:

(A) x≥4   (B) x<4   (C) x≤64   (D) x≤4

10. Why test a boundary and nearby values?

(A) to confirm inclusion and solution direction   (B) to change the inequality   (C) to avoid solving   (D) to make all values solutions
