Directions: Show transformations, boundary inclusion, solution direction, graph when useful, and contextual constraints. Verify in the original inequality.

1. x>1 and x≤5 is:

(A) 1<x≤5   (B) x≤1 or x>5   (C) x>5   (D) x≤1

2. Solve x−3≥0 and x+1<7.

(A) x>3 and x≤6   (B) x≤3 or x>6   (C) 3≤x<6   (D) x<3 or x≥6

3. “At least −4 and less than 9” is:

(A) −4≤x<9   (B) −4<x≤9   (C) x≤−4 or x>9   (D) x<−4 and x≥9

4. x≤−3 or x≥4 describes:

(A) the interval −3 to 4 only   (B) all values between   (C) no values   (D) two outside rays

5. For x<0 or x>6, 3 is:

(A) a solution   (B) a boundary solution   (C) always included   (D) not a solution

6. Solve 3<x+1≤8.

(A) 2≤x<7   (B) x>2 or x≤7   (C) 2<x≤7   (D) 4<x≤9

7. Solve −6<−2x≤4.

(A) x<−2 or x≥3   (B) −2≤x<3   (C) −3<x≤2   (D) −2<x≤3

8. Free admission if age under 5 or at least 65.

(A) a<5 or a≥65   (B) 5≤a<65   (C) a>5 and a<65   (D) a≤5 and a≥65

9. A student graphs x<2 or x>5 as the middle interval. Correct:

(A) shade only endpoints   (B) shade everything   (C) shade outside 2 and 5   (D) shade 2 through 5

10. Why graph compound inequalities?

(A) to guarantee integers   (B) to make intersections, unions, and boundaries visible   (C) to turn them into equations   (D) to remove variables
