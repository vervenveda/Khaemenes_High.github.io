Unit 10 · Functions & Linear Modelling Studio Worked Solutions

Guidance for 40 ungraded applied investigations. Accept equivalent expressions, accurate graphs with labelled axes, justified alternative methods, and thoughtful model limitations. Original assessment scoring remains unchanged.

Lesson 01

A · Rescue-drone navigation: Coordinates (−4,3)+(6,−5)=(2,−2), in Quadrant IV.

B · Museum map reflection: (5,2). The x-coordinate changes sign; y stays fixed.

C · Robotics checkpoint: Horizontal distance 4−(−2)=6 units; it crosses the y-axis at (0,−3).

D · Confused coordinates: The order was reversed. Move right 3 then down 6 to (3,−6), Quadrant IV.

E · Create a treasure map: Examples: (2,3), (−2,3), (−2,−3), (2,−3); (0,4) lies on y-axis.

Lesson 02

A · Festival seating pattern: Arithmetic sequence adds 5. a(n)=8+5(n−1)=5n+3; a(15)=78.

B · Garden tile border: a(n)=4n+1. Stage 0 would have 1 tile under this extension.

C · Repair a prediction: Multiply by 2 each time; a(n)=2^n for n starting at 1; next terms 32 and 64.

D · Two growing savings: A(w)=12+4w; B(w)=2+6w; equality at w=5, both $32.

E · Design a counterexample: Example 2,4,6 (add 2) and 2,4,8 (double). More information is needed to establish a rule.

Lesson 03

A · Library checkout function: Each input ID has exactly one output limit; repeated outputs for different IDs are allowed.

B · Not a function: Input 1 has outputs 4 and 7. Remove (1,7) or change its first coordinate to a new input.

C · Water dispenser table: V(t)=2.5t; outputs 0,5,12.5,20 liters.

D · Inverse ambiguity: Child→birth month is a function. Month→child may not be; several children can share a month.

E · Build a domain: Domain n∈{0,1,...,12}; revenue 3n dollars, outputs {0,3,...,36}.

Lesson 04

A · Bicycle rental four ways: C=9+4h; table h=0→9,1→13,3→21. Graph includes (3,21).

B · Spot inconsistent row: Rule y=4x+6. At x=3, y=18, not 19.

C · Graph to words: Slope (28−12)/4=4; starting value 12; y=4x+12.

D · Which representation?: R=8s+25; y-intercept 25; not proportional because intercept is nonzero.

E · Create a matching model: Example account balance after buying $5 supplies, then earning $2.50 per job; x≥0 whole jobs. (2,0),(4,5).

Lesson 05

A · Trail elevation: (180−120)/(5−2)=20 m/km upward average rate.

B · Water tank drain: Slope (180−300)/6=−20 L/min. V(8)=300−160=140 L.

C · Price increase rate: (36−24)/(4−1)=$4 per year. Long-run prediction assumes a constant increase, which may not hold.

D · Uneven growth: Successive changes are 3,5,7; not constant, hence not linear for equally spaced inputs.

E · Rate versus total: Rate is $2.20/km; $6 is fixed starting fare. C=6+2.2×7=$21.40.

Lesson 06

A · Electric scooter fare: C=0.40t+3. Slope $0.40/min; y-intercept $3; C(18)=$10.20.

B · Through two points: m=(21−9)/(6−2)=3; b=9−3(2)=3; y=3x+3.

C · Negative slope: h=18−1.5t; h(7)=7.5 cm. Model valid until candle reaches zero.

D · Horizontal graph: Slope 0; y=70, with y-intercept 70.

E · Fix the sign: Slope (11−5)/(3−0)=+2; y=2x+5.

Lesson 07

A · Linear or quadratic tiles: A values 1,4,9,16, changes 3,5,7 (nonlinear). B values 4,7,10,13, changes 3 (linear).

B · Function notation: f(−2)=−15; f(0)=−7; 4x−7=13 => x=5.

C · Car rental versus distance: Constant slope $0.25 per km, intercept $40. Realistic distance d≥0 (and may be limited by contract).

D · Nonlinear growth: Differences 100,200,400 are not constant; exponential, not linear.

E · Compare rates: 5x+4=2x+13 => x=3, output 19. f grows faster because slope 5>2.

Lesson 08

A · Survey trend: Growth +7 per period; y=75+7x; period 6 prediction 117. Assumes trend continues.

B · Compare residuals: Residuals −2,+5,−1. Middle prediction has largest absolute error 5.

C · Choosing a model: T(t)=100+12t. T(5)=160 assuming constant net planting.

D · Extrapolation caution: Growth conditions and biological limits can change; a constant short-term rate need not persist for 100 weeks.

E · Design a fair prediction: Example actual (0,10),(1,13),(2,17), model y=10+3.5x predicts 10,13.5,17; residuals 0,−0.5,0. Predict x=1.5 is interpolation; x=4 extrapolation.
