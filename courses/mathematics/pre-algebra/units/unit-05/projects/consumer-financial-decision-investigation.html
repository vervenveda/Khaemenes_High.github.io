Consumer Financial Decision Investigation

Compare realistic options using percent, fees, time, total cost or return, and clearly stated assumptions—without using private family financial information.

Open Project Workspace

Project sequence

Build a defensible comparison.

Select a safe public or fictional decision

Examples: two subscriptions, two sale offers, savings plans, equipment purchases, fundraising prices, service quotes, or simple-interest options. Do not use private account information.

Record complete terms

List original prices, percent rates, discounts, tax assumptions, fees, time periods, quantities, and any limits or conditions.

Calculate each option

Show percent amounts, updated values, total cost or return, absolute differences, and at least one relative comparison.

Test assumptions

Change at least two assumptions—such as duration, usage, fee, discount, or rate—and identify whether the preferred option changes.

Communicate a recommendation

Present a table, equations, evidence, limitations, consumer-safety note, and a conclusion that distinguishes facts from preferences.
