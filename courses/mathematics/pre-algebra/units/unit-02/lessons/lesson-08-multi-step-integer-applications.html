      Deep concept study guide

      Think like a mission controller.

      In this lesson, every number has a job: locate, move, compare, repeat, split, or verify. Do not calculate until you know which job the number is doing.

      1 · Locate the starting point

      The ordered pair gives the beginning of the mission. The first value is x: left/right. The second value is y: up/down. A sign mistake in either coordinate moves the entire point to a different region of the plane.

      2 · Translate every movement

      Right and up are positive changes. Left and down are negative changes. A movement changes only one coordinate unless the problem says both horizontal and vertical position change.

      3 · Prove the final answer

      Use final minus initial to check the total signed change. A route can be complex, but its final coordinate must agree with the total x-change and total y-change.

      Concept development

      Build the idea before memorizing the procedure.

      Reading room

      Coordinates are signed stories.

      A coordinate plane is not just a picture. It is a two-direction number system that lets you model location, route, rate, and change.

      The origin is the reference point

      The origin, (0, 0), is where horizontal and vertical reference lines meet. Every coordinate is measured from that center. A point at (−4, 3) is not “negative overall.” It is 4 units left and 3 units up.

      • x > 0 means right of the origin.
      • x < 0 means left of the origin.
      • y > 0 means above the origin.
      • y < 0 means below the origin.

      Movement is signed change

      When a point moves, the coordinate changes by addition. From (−1, 4), a change of (+5, −7) means add 5 to x and add −7 to y. The result is (4, −3).

      MoveSigned changeCoordinate affected
      Rightpositivex
      Leftnegativex
      Uppositivey
      Downnegativey

      Multi-step problems need operation labels

      Before calculating, label each operation. Addition combines changes. Subtraction compares final and initial values. Multiplication represents repeated equal changes. Division finds a rate or equal share.

      Reasonableness means sign and size

      In signed-number problems, a correct magnitude with the wrong sign is still wrong. A final answer of 7 and a final answer of −7 may be the same distance from zero, but they point in opposite directions.

      Reasonableness checklist

      • Did only the coordinate that moved change?
      • Does the sign match the direction?
      • Does the magnitude match the distance moved?
      • Can final minus initial prove the total change?
