Multiplying & Dividing Integers

      Concept study guide

      Do not memorize signs blindly. Build the rule from structure.

      This lesson connects multiplication and division so students can explain the sign rules, check answers with inverse operations, and recognize when zero changes the problem.

      1 · Name the operation

      Multiplication combines equal signed groups. Division reverses multiplication by asking for an equal group size or a missing factor.

      2 · Find the magnitude

      Ignore signs briefly and compute with absolute values. This gives the size of the product or quotient before direction is attached.

      3 · Decide the sign

      Same nonzero signs give a positive result. Different nonzero signs give a negative result. Zero has its own special rules.

      4 · Check by reversing

      A division result should always check by multiplication. If a ÷ b = c, then b × c must equal a.

      Reading room

      Why the sign rules make sense.

      Multiplication as repeated change

      A product like 4(−3) can mean four equal changes of −3. The repeated change is negative each time, so the total change is −12. This connects the sign rule to real movement, temperature, money, or elevation.

      The pattern behind two negatives

      The pattern −3×3=−9, −3×2=−6, −3×1=−3, −3×0=0 continues by adding 3 each step. So −3×(−1)=3, −3×(−2)=6, and −3×(−3)=9. A negative times a negative must be positive to keep the pattern consistent.

      Division as an inverse check

      Division does not need a separate mystery rule. It follows multiplication. Since (−7)(6)=−42, the related quotient −42÷(−7)=6. The quotient must make the multiplication sentence true.

      Zero is not ordinary here

      Multiplying by zero gives zero. Dividing zero by a nonzero integer gives zero. But division by zero is undefined because there is no number that can be multiplied by zero to recover a nonzero dividend.

      Check strategy

      To check −42÷6=−7, multiply 6(−7). The product is −42, so the quotient is confirmed.

      Error warning

      Do not decide the sign by the larger magnitude. Magnitude controls size; the pattern of signs controls direction.
