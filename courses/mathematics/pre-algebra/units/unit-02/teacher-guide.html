Teacher Guide

A three-week instructional plan for building durable signed-number reasoning before formal rules become automatic.

Mastery Check

Recommended pacing

SessionsFocusEvidence

3–4Opposites, absolute value, distance, comparisonNumber-line explanations and distance problems
5–7Addition and subtraction with modelsZero-pair or movement justifications

10–11Coordinate plane and signed movementPlots, quadrants, and change vectors

High-value representations

  • Horizontal and vertical number lines
  • Positive/negative counters and zero pairs
  • Temperature and elevation scales
  • Running-balance tables
  • Coordinate grids and movement arrows
  • Sign-pattern tables derived from sequences

Common misconceptions

  • Believing −12 is greater than −3 because 12 is greater than 3
  • Treating absolute value bars as parentheses or a negative-removal command
  • Memorizing “two negatives make a positive” without identifying the operation
  • Changing both signs indiscriminately in subtraction
  • Confusing signed change with nonnegative distance
  • Reversing x and y coordinates

Foundation supports

  • Keep a number line visible throughout the unit.
  • Require subtraction to be rewritten before calculating.
  • Use color consistently for positive and negative directions.
  • Reduce quantities while preserving problem structure.
  • Preteach context vocabulary such as debt, descent, and net change.

Extended opportunities

  • Prove sign patterns using distributive reasoning.
  • Analyze parity of negative factors.
  • Compare signed difference with absolute distance.
  • Explore reflections across coordinate axes.
  • Create and critique misleading signed-data statements.

Discussion prompts

  • What does the sign communicate here?
  • How can two different numbers have the same magnitude?
  • Why is subtracting a negative equivalent to adding?
  • Which representation makes this operation most convincing?
  • How can you predict the sign before calculating?

Assessment use

Use domain feedback to identify a specific need: interpretation, comparison, addition, subtraction, multiplication/division, coordinates, or multi-step modelling.

A 75% threshold indicates readiness to continue with targeted correction. Require explanations or oral conferences when high-stakes grading depends on the result.
