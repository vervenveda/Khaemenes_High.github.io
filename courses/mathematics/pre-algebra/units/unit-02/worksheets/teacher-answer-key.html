Lesson 01 · Integers in Context

−8 is a whole-number value with a sign, so it is an integer.

2. D — 4.6
4.6 is not a whole number, so it is not an integer.

3. A — −6
Below zero indicates a negative value: −6.

4. C — +45
A deposit increases the account, so the change is +45.

5. C — −28
A withdrawal decreases the account, so the change is −28.

6. A — +320
Above sea level is positive, so the elevation is +320 feet.

7. D — −75
Below sea level is represented by −75.

8. B — No change in an account balance
Zero can represent no change relative to the reference point.

9. A — Zero is neither positive nor negative.
Zero is an integer but is neither positive nor negative.

10. C — −7

Unit 02 Lesson 01 Workshop · Teacher Guidance (ungraded)

A · Number line: From left to right, −8, −3, 0, +4, +9. The value farther left is smaller: −8 is less than −3. Magnitude is not the same as signed numerical order.

B · Elevation: Sea level is 0 m; diver = −18 m, lookout = +12 m. The signs describe position relative to sea level, not a judgment about the objects.

C · Change and balance: Withdrawal = −$35, new balance = $50 + (−$35) = $15. A signed change expresses what happened; a balance expresses the resulting state.

D · Context: Valid examples include −7°C relative to 0°C, moving −7 meters along a signed direction axis, and a −$7 transaction. Confirm that each student defines a reference point and units.

E · Zero: Zero is neither positive nor negative, although it is an integer. At sea level, 0 m marks the reference elevation; an account change of $0 means no change.

Assessment boundary: Offer feedback and corrections only. This workshop does not change the official scored ten-question worksheet, lesson score, or mastery gate.

Lesson 02 · Opposites & Absolute Value

1. B — −6
The opposite lies the same distance from zero on the other side, so the opposite of +6 is −6.

2. D — +13
The opposite of −13 is +13.

3. A — 0
Zero is its own opposite.

4. C — 9
−9 is 9 units from zero, so |−9|=9.

5. C — 17
17 is 17 units from zero.

6. A — 0
Zero is zero units from zero.

7. D — −4 and 4
−4 and +4 have equal magnitude and opposite signs.

8. B — −11 and 11
Both −11 and +11 are 11 units from zero.

9. A — Absolute value measures distance from zero.
Absolute value represents distance from zero.

10. C — −14
|−14|=14 and |9|=9, so −14 is farther from zero.

Lesson 02 Workshop · Opposites Teacher Guidance (ungraded)

A · Number line: Opposites −9 and +9, and −4 and +4, are the same distance from zero on opposite sides. Zero is its own opposite. The sign changes for nonzero opposites; the absolute value stays equal.

B · Distance: From −7 to +5 is 12 units. Count 7 units to zero plus 5 more, or calculate |5 − (−7)| = |12| = 12.

C · Signed position and magnitude: The diver is at −14 m and the balloon at +9 m. Their distances from zero are 14 m and 9 m. The diver is farther from sea level even though −14 is numerically less than +9.

D · Signs and bars: |−8| = 8; −|−8| = −8; |−(−8)| = |8| = 8; −(−8) = 8. A negative outside absolute-value bars changes the sign of the nonnegative absolute value, whereas absolute value measures distance.

E · Correct the claim: The opposite of −6 is +6, and |−6| = 6. The student confused an original signed number with its absolute value. Both −6 and +6 are six units from zero.

Assessment boundary: Use the workshop for feedback, diagrams, and corrections only. It does not award mastery, modify the ten scored questions, or change learner records.

Lesson 03 · Compare & Order Integers

1. B — −3 > −8
−3 lies to the right of −8, so −3 is greater.

2. D — <
−12 lies farther left than −5, so −12 < −5.

3. A — >
Every positive integer is greater than every negative integer.

4. C — >
Zero lies to the right of −9.

5. C — −2
Among negative integers, the value closest to zero is greatest.

6. A — −4
−4 lies farthest left on the number line.

7. D — −8, −3, 0, 5
The correct increasing order is −8, −3, 0, 5.

8. B — 6, 2, −1, −9
From right to left on the number line: 6, 2, −1, −9.

9. A — −10
−10 is farthest left and therefore least.

10. C — A > B
Numbers increase to the right.

Lesson 03 Workshop · Teacher Guidance (ungraded)

A · Ordered number line: Least to greatest: −11, −7, −2, 0, +3, +6. Values increase moving right; zero separates negative and positive integers.

B · Signed order versus magnitude: −9 < −4 because −9 is farther left. Yet |−9| = 9 and |−4| = 4, so the magnitude of −9 is greater. Absolute value measures distance, not signed order.

C · Temperatures: Coldest to warmest: −9°C, −6°C, −1°C, +3°C. Temperature span is 3 − (−9) = 12°C. Verify by counting nine degrees to zero and three more to +3.

D · Mixed values: Greatest to least: +7, +5, +2, 0, −2, −5, −8. Examples: +7 > +5; +2 > 0; −5 > −8. Check every adjacent pair.

E · Correction: −12 < −3. Comparing the unsigned magnitudes 12 and 3 is not a valid way to rank signed negatives; farther left means smaller.

Assessment boundary: This extension is formative only. Do not write scores, grades, or completion evidence from these exercises.

Lesson 04 · Adding Integers

1. B — 15
Both integers are positive, so 6+9=15.

2. D — −11
Add magnitudes 4+7=11 and keep the negative sign.

3. A — −21
Both addends are negative; 15+6=21, so the sum is −21.

4. C — −4
The signs differ; 9−5=4 and the larger magnitude belongs to −9.

5. C — 5
The signs differ; 13−8=5 and the larger magnitude is positive.

6. A — 3
20 has the greater magnitude; 20−17=3.

7. D — 0
A number plus its opposite equals zero.

8. B — −8+8
−8 and +8 are additive inverses.

9. A — 7 units left
Adding a negative integer means move left 7 units.

10. C — 4
Move 9 units right from −5 to 4.

Lesson 04 Workshop · Teacher Guidance (ungraded)

A · Number line: Start at −8. Adding +13 gives +5; adding −6 moves six units left to −1. The complete expression is −8 + 13 + (−6) = −1.

B · Zero pairs: Pair five negative counters with five positive counters to make five zero pairs. Two negative counters remain; −7 + 5 = −2.

C · Two strategies: −14 + 9 = −5 (nine steps right from −14, or 14 − 9 = 5 with the negative sign of the larger magnitude). 14 + (−9) = +5 (nine steps left from +14, or the positive larger magnitude). The magnitudes of the results are equal but the signs differ.

D · Running balance: Begin +$18, spend $25 → −$7, add $12 → +$5, spend $9 → −$4. Written as 18 + (−25) + 12 + (−9) = −4, the final account balance is a $4 debt relative to zero.

E · Repair the sign error: −6 + (−4) = −10, because both changes move left or because six negative counters plus four negative counters make ten negative counters. Subtraction of magnitudes applies to adding numbers with different signs, not two negative addends.

Assessment boundary: These workshop responses are for explanation and formative feedback only; they must not write scores, progress, or unlock evidence.

Lesson 05 · Subtracting Integers

1. B — 9 + (−4)
Subtracting 4 is equivalent to adding −4.

2. D — 5 + 7
The opposite of −7 is +7, so 5−(−7)=5+7.

3. A — −3 + (−8)
Subtracting +8 means adding −8.

4. C — −7
12−19=12+(−19)=−7.

5. C — −11
−6−5=−6+(−5)=−11.

6. A — 12
8−(−4)=8+4=12.

7. D — −7
−9−(−2)=−9+2=−7.

8. B — 5
−15−(−20)=−15+20=5.

9. A — 0
Any number minus itself equals zero.

10. C — a−b=a+(−b)
Subtraction is addition of the opposite: a−b=a+(−b).
