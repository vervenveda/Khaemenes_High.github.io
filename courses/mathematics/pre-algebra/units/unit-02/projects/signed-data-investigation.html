Integrated Learning Investigation

Use mathematics to investigate a changing system.

This project is deliberately open-ended. Choose a real or teacher-approved simulated system whose values can fall above and below a meaningful reference point. Your job is to make the signs, calculations, representations, and conclusion tell one coherent mathematical story.

ObserveTemperature, elevation, tides, floors, coordinates, scores, balances, or another signed system.
ModelUse integers, absolute value, operations, signed change, and at least one coordinate representation.
CommunicateExplain what zero means, why signs matter, what changed, and what your evidence supports.

Investigation Sequence

Frame the question

Select a context, define the reference point, state what positive and negative values mean, and write one question your data can actually answer.

Build the data record

Collect at least twelve signed values. Record units and dates/positions. If values come from an outside source, record the source; if you measured or simulated them yourself, describe the method.

Interrogate the values

Order values, compare magnitudes, identify useful opposites, and calculate at least three distances or signed changes. Explain what each result means in context.

Model with operations

Answer at least four meaningful questions using integer operations. Include one multi-step calculation and one coordinate representation. Show enough work that another learner could follow your reasoning.

Check and challenge your result

Verify arithmetic, inspect units and signs, and ask whether another interpretation is possible. Correct errors rather than hiding them; revision is part of mathematical work.

Communicate a conclusion

Create a concise report, poster, data story, map, or presentation. Explain the reference point, findings, limitations, and one insight that could transfer to another signed-number situation.

Project Rubric

DimensionProficient evidence
Reference and signsZero and sign conventions are explicit, meaningful, and consistently used.
Data integrityAt least twelve values include units and enough source/method information to understand where they came from.
AccuracyOrdering, absolute values, operations, coordinates, and units are mathematically correct.
ReasoningWork shows why operations were chosen and what signed results mean in context.
RepresentationAt least one coordinate or visual model is correctly labeled and supports the investigation.
CommunicationThe conclusion answers the central question without claiming more than the evidence supports.
ReflectionThe learner identifies a limitation, a revision/check, and a transferable insight.
