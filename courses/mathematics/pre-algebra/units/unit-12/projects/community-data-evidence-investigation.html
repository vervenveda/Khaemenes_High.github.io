Community Data Evidence Investigation

Investigate a safe fictional, public, or anonymous dataset using a clear question, sampling plan, displays, center, spread, probability or simulation, bias analysis, and a limited evidence-aware conclusion.

Open Project Workspace

Project sequence

Build and verify an evidence-aware data investigation.

Choose a safe statistical question

Use public, fictional, aggregated, or anonymous data. Do not collect names, exact home locations, medical records, account credentials, or other sensitive personal details.

Define population, sample, variables, and method

State who or what the conclusion concerns, how observations were selected, variable types, units, time frame, privacy safeguards, and possible undercoverage or nonresponse.

Create connected displays and summaries

Produce a frequency table and at least two suitable displays. Calculate and interpret center and spread, noting shape, clusters, gaps, and potential outliers.

Model uncertainty or probability

Include a probability model, relative-frequency comparison, simulation, sampling discussion, or another justified uncertainty analysis.

Evaluate bias and communicate a limited conclusion

Check denominators, graph scales, sampling bias, association versus causation, assumptions, uncertainty, and what the evidence cannot establish.

Rubric

DimensionProficient evidence
Question and ethicsQuestion, purpose, data source, privacy choice, variables, units, and consent or safety considerations are explicit.
Sampling and methodPopulation, sample, selection method, possible bias, nonresponse, and limits of generalization are evaluated.
Displays and summariesTables and graphs are suitable and labelled; center, spread, shape, and unusual observations are interpreted correctly.
Probability or uncertaintyA probability, simulation, relative-frequency, or sampling analysis is correct and connected to the question.
Evidence evaluationDenominators, graph scales, association, confounding, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations are considered.
CommunicationThe conclusion is clear, proportionate to the evidence, and avoids causal or population claims the design cannot support.
