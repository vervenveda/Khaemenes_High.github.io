Directions: Show calculations and evidence reasoning. Label displays, state units/assumptions, and distinguish what the data show from what you infer.

1. An open online poll most directly risks:

(A) perfect representation   (B) random assignment   (C) zero variability   (D) voluntary-response bias

2. An observational association means:

(A) one variable definitely causes the other   (B) the variables show a relationship in the observed data   (C) all samples will be identical   (D) bias is impossible

3. A trustworthy data conclusion should include:

(A) only the largest percentage   (B) only a colourful graph   (C) no denominator   (D) source, sample, method, center/spread or probabilities, uncertainty, bias, and limitations

4. Surveying only students nearest the cafeteria is a:

(A) census   (B) stratified sample necessarily   (C) convenience sample   (D) simple random sample

5. Nonresponse bias occurs when:

(A) selected people who do not respond differ meaningfully from responders   (B) everyone responds   (C) the mean is zero   (D) variables are categorical

6. Observational association alone usually cannot establish causation because of:

(A) confounding and alternative explanations   (B) arithmetic   (C) box plots   (D) units

7. Random sampling primarily supports:

(A) removing measurement error   (B) generalizing to a population   (C) causal inference by itself   (D) finding slope

8. Evidence-aware decisions should consider:

(A) mean only   (B) largest category only   (C) desired outcome   (D) data quality, uncertainty, effect size, costs, benefits, and limitations

9. A school polls only honors students about all-student workload. Concern:

(A) workload is categorical only   (B) selection may not represent the target population   (C) sample is automatically random   (D) honors students cannot answer surveys

10. A sample estimate should be treated as:

(A) an estimate subject to sampling variability and possible bias   (B) the exact population truth   (C) a causal proof   (D) a fixed law

Reflection / Evidence Audit

Choose one data or probability claim from this lesson. Identify the evidence source, show the relevant calculation/display reasoning, name one possible bias or uncertainty, and write a conclusion that does not overstate what the evidence proves.
