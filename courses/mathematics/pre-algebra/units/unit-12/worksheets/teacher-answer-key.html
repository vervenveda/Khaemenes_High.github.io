Unit 12 · Data & Evidence Studio — Worked Solutions

Teacher guidance for 40 ungraded investigations. Accept correct alternative displays, conventions for quartiles when declared, appropriate uncertainty, ethical data collection and defensible arguments. Keep assessment scoring independent.

Lesson 01

A · School commute survey: Population: all 1,200 students. Sample: 90 surveyed. Variable: commute time in minutes (numerical). Random selection reduces selection bias, but sampling error and nonresponse can remain.

B · Biased lunch poll: Salad buyers overrepresent salad preferences. Randomly sample all students across lunch periods and offer balanced response choices.

C · Statistical question design: The second anticipates variability and is statistical; record arrival times for a representative group, not identifiable names.

D · Protecting student privacy: Collect only needed sleep hours with appropriate consent, remove identifiers, store securely and publish aggregate results, not names/addresses.

E · Sample-size reasoning: 240 generally yields less random sampling variability; either can be biased if selection or response is flawed.

Lesson 02

A · Make a frequency table: Frequencies: 1→1,2→2,3→3,4→3,5→1. Modes 3 and 4, tied.

B · Histogram bin rules: 0–<10: 3; 10–<20: 3; 20–<30: 2. Every observation belongs to exactly one interval.

C · Box-plot landmarks: Median=7; lower half 2,4,5 gives Q1=4; upper 9,10,12 gives Q3=10; range=10. For seven values, halves omit the median.

D · Misleading dot plot: Caption implies 9 students; legend of 5/dot represents 45 students. Correct scale/legend is essential.

E · Choose a display: Genre: bar chart or category-frequency table. Reading minutes: histogram, dot plot or box plot, depending on distribution/detail.

Lesson 03

A · Mean with an outlier: Mean=100/5=20, median=9. The extreme 64 pulls mean upward; median better describes the typical value here.

B · Weighted course mark: 0.4×88+0.2×70+0.4×92=35.2+14+36.8=86.

C · Missing score: Target total=4×12=48; known total=34; missing=14.

D · Compare class centers: A mean 9, median 5. B mean 9, median 9. Equal means can conceal very different distributions.

E · When mode misleads: Mean=52/5=$10.40; median=$3; mode=$3. High-priced item inflates mean.

Lesson 04

A · Range and IQR: Range=12, median=(6+8)/2=7, Q1=(4+5)/2=4.5, Q3=(9+11)/2=10, IQR=5.5.

B · Mean absolute deviation: Mean=6; absolute deviations 4,2,0,2,4 total 12; MAD=12/5=2.4.

C · Outlier fence: Q1=2; Q3=6; IQR=4; upper fence=6+1.5×4=12. Value 30 exceeds 12, so outlier.

D · Same mean different spread: Both means 10. Ranges 4 and 18; second data set is much more variable.

E · Measurement anomaly: Check sensor calibration, context and recording; document any exclusion. An unusual value is not automatically an error.

Lesson 05

A · Truncated axis: The short axis exaggerates differences. Label clearly and use a baseline of zero for bar lengths or another justified, explicitly indicated display.

B · Compare medians and spread: Medians both 6; ranges A=4 and B=10. Group B is more spread out.

C · Which class is consistent?: Both mean 12.8 for X? X total64/5=12.8, Y total65/5=13. Medians X=13,Y=13. Ranges 2 versus 12. X more consistent despite slightly different means.

D · Graph label audit: Label months on horizontal axis, attendance counts/units on vertical, and source/sample sizes; include legend and consistent scales.

E · Fair evidence claim: Need sample sizes, variation, initial differences and other confounders; association is not causation.

Lesson 06

A · Fair spinner: P(blue)=3/8; P(not red)=6/8=3/4. Expected 80×3/8=30 blue spins, not guaranteed.

B · Sample space: 12 outcomes. Heads with 2,4,6 gives 3/12=1/4.

C · Experimental frequency: Observed 0.37 versus theoretical 0.40; difference −0.03 (3 percentage points).

D · Improbable claim: Each toss independent; P(tails on next toss)=1/2, regardless of previous five results.

E · Simulation design: One trial consists of three independent simulated die rolls; record success if any is six. Repeat many trials; estimate success frequency.

Lesson 07

A · Two coins: HH,HT,TH,TT; exactly one head HT,TH => 2/4=1/2.

B · Draw without replacement: (3/5)×(2/4)=6/20=3/10.

C · With versus without: With replacement (3/5)²=9/25=0.36; without replacement 3/10=0.30.

D · Three-child outcomes: Eight equally likely sequences; GGB,GBG,BGG => 3/8. Model assumptions are simplifications.

E · Tree diagram critique: Need conditional probabilities or defensible independence assumption; weather across days can be dependent.

Lesson 08

A · Convenience sample: Car-driver sample excludes many bus riders. Sample bus users across routes and times, including diverse riders.

B · Association and causation: Warm weather/season influences both; association alone does not show one causes the other.

C · Transparent headline: Cannot infer all residents approve at 60% from self-selected 40. Say '24 of 40 volunteer respondents reported approval'.

D · Response-rate issue: 125/500=25%. Nonrespondents might differ systematically; sample results can be biased.

E · Evidence-based decision: Need variability, selection method, time periods, outliers, comparable conditions, and uncertainty. Mean alone is insufficient.
