Unit 04 · Extended Reasoning Studio Solutions

Teacher guidance for the five ungraded missions in each lesson. Accept alternative valid methods, labelled units, and explanations; these activities do not change mastery scoring.

Lesson 01

A · Festival supply ratios: There are 8 parts; 72 ÷ 8 = 9 flags per part. Red = 27, gold = 45. 27:45 simplifies to 3:5.

B · Garden planning: 11 parts; 33 ÷ 11 = 3. Herbs = 12, vegetables = 21; 12:21 = 4:7.

C · Repair the claim: 6/9=2/3, but 10/13 differs. Multiply both by 2 to obtain 12:18.

D · Design a game: 18 ÷ 2 = 9 groups; 9 × 9 = 81 tokens. 2:9 = 18:81.

E · Write a counterexample: For example 1:2 = 0.5, while 2:3 ≠ 0.5. Equal addition generally does not preserve a ratio.

Lesson 02

A · Stage lighting schedule: 6/4 = 1.5 minutes per light; 18 × 1.5 = 27 minutes.

B · Bike route milestones: Rate = 0.3 km/min. At 10 min: 3 km; 30 min: 9 km; 80 min: 24 km.

C · Catch the table error: $3.50 per notebook; 8 notebooks must cost $28, not $30.

D · Water station model: 3 L/min; examples (3 min,9 L), (5 min,15 L), (8 min,24 L).

E · Create and verify: Answers vary; each row must preserve one constant multiplier or unit rate.

Lesson 03

A · Farmers market comparison: First = $0.75 each; second = $0.72 each. Second is cheaper; 30 cost $21.60.

B · Delivery speed: 42 ÷ 2.5 = 16.8 km/h; 4 hours gives 67.2 km.

C · Detect the misleading label: $8.40/12 = $0.70 per pen; $3.25/5 = $0.65. The 5-pack is cheaper per pen.

D · Recipe serving price: $13.50/6=$2.25 per serving; 14 × $2.25=$31.50.

E · Design a fair test: Answers vary. Divide each cost by films; note content quality, access period, and fees can also matter.

Lesson 04

A · Community mural: A=2.5h; after 7 hours A=17.5 m². At zero hours, area added is zero.

B · Proportional table test: First three have y/x = 3.5; for x=8, y should be 28, not 30.

C · Graph without drawing: (0,0),(2,3.5),(4,7). Slope 1.75 means $1.75 per ticket.

D · Competing models: A=4x proportional; B=3x+6 not proportional. 4x=3x+6 => x=6 laps.

E · Invent a representation: y=0.8x; e.g. (1,0.8),(5,4),(10,8). Interpretation depends on chosen context.

Lesson 05

A · Costume fabric: 10.5/7=1.5 m each; 18×1.5=27 m. Or 7/18=10.5/x gives x=27.

B · Solar charging: 18/45=0.4 percentage points/min; 80 min gives 32 points, assuming a constant rate and enough capacity.

C · Spot the invalid move: 8x=60, x=7.5. Check 7.5/12=0.625=5/8.

D · Direct variation mission: k=1.6/8=0.2 L/m², P=0.2A, so 4.6 liters for 23 m².

E · Choose a method: $7 per kit; 35 kits -> $245. Equation 84/12 = x/35, so x=245.

Lesson 06

A · Museum map: Scale 4 km/cm; 8.25×4=33 km.

B · Model village: 18×50=900 cm=9 m.

C · Photo enlargement: New dimensions 21 by 28 cm; original area 192 cm²; new area 588 cm², factor 1.75²=3.0625.

D · Fix the blueprint: Factors 12/4=3, 18/7≈2.57. With width 12, height must be 21.

E · Design a park map: Drawing 6 cm by 4 cm; actual perimeter 2(60+40)=200 m.

Lesson 07

A · Taxi fare puzzle: F=4+2.2d; not proportional because F(0)=4. F(8)=$21.60.

B · Club dues comparison: A=6m proportional; B=15+3m not. 6m=15+3m implies m=5.

C · Table investigation: First three follow y=4x. The fourth should be 16, not 17.

D · Origin check: Slope 2, intercept 5: y=2x+5. Not direct variation, because it misses origin.

E · Invent two businesses: Example P=3x and N=5+3x; only P is proportional. Compare cost at zero items.

Lesson 08

A · Water tank conversion: 18×3.785=68.13 L, so 70 L is a reasonable rounded estimate.

B · Marathon hydration: 18×2.5×0.6=27 L; reserve 2.7 L; total 29.7 L.

C · Scale and units: 12/2=6 scale groups; 6×7.5=45 km=45,000 m.

D · Budget and portions: 70 servings; $0.80 each => $56, assuming servings can be purchased at the same unit price.

E · Plan an event: Answers vary. Require a valid proportional model with consistent units and a separate explanation of any fixed fee.
