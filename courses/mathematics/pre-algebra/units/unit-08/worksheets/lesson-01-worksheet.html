Directions: Show equivalent operations on both sides. Preserve signs, grouping, units, and constraints. Check solutions in the original equation.

1. Which statement is an equation?

(A) 4x+7   (B) 4x+7=19   (C) 4x   (D) x,7

2. Does x=−2 solve 5x+1=−9?

(A) Yes   (B) No; left side is −11   (C) No; right side is 9   (D) Cannot tell

3. Which is equivalent to 2x=14?

(A) x=7   (B) x=12   (C) 2x=7   (D) x=28

4. Is x=4 a solution of x+5=9?

(A) no   (B) only if x=9   (C) cannot tell   (D) yes

5. If 6 is subtracted from the left side of an equation, preserving equality requires:

(A) doing nothing   (B) multiplying the right by 6   (C) subtracting 6 from the right side   (D) adding 6 to the right

6. To check x=7 in 3x−1=20, compute:

(A) 3(7)−1 and compare it with 20   (B) 3(20)−1   (C) 7−20   (D) 3+7−1

7. Which is equivalent to x+3=10?

(A) x=3   (B) x=7   (C) x=13   (D) x=30

8. Which value does NOT solve x²=9?

(A) 0   (B) 3   (C) −3   (D) both 3 and −3

9. A student changes x+8=12 to x=12+8. The error is:

(A) they should multiply by 8   (B) x must equal 8   (C) addition cannot appear in equations   (D) they used the wrong inverse direction

10. If substitution makes both sides 17, the proposed value:

(A) is not a solution   (B) changes the equation   (C) satisfies the equation   (D) must be zero
