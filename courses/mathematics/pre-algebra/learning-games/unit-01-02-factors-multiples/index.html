Factor or multiple?

The same multiplication equation can describe both relationships.

  • 6 × 8 = 48
  • 6 and 8 are factors of 48.
  • 48 is a multiple of both 6 and 8.
  • A positive factor list is finite; a positive multiple list never ends.
Ask: am I dividing a number into equal groups, or building repeated groups?

Two numbers can share structure.

A common factor divides two numbers evenly. A common multiple appears in both repeating multiple lists.

  • 18 and 24 share factors 1, 2, 3, and 6.
  • 4 and 6 share multiples 12, 24, 36, …
  • These ideas lead directly into GCF and LCM in Lesson 4.
Shared divisor → common factor. Shared repeated product → common multiple.

Most factor pairs contribute two different factors. The middle square pair contributes only one new factor.

Perfect squares have a special middle pair.

For 36, the search reaches 6 × 6. Because the two partners are the same, the middle pair contributes only one distinct factor.

  • 36 has factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36.
  • That is 9 positive factors—an odd number.
  • The same pattern explains 25, 49, 64, and other perfect squares.
A repeated middle factor explains why perfect squares have an odd number of positive factors.
