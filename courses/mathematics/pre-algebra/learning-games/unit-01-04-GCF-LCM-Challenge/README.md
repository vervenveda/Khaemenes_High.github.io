The new Animated Instruction Studio has four parts:

GCF Looks Inward — factors of 18 and 24 animate and shared factors illuminate until GCF = 6.
LCM Looks Outward — multiples of 4 and 6 expand until the first shared repeat, LCM = 12.
Prime Structure — compares 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3², showing:
GCF uses the smaller shared exponents → 12
LCM uses the larger required exponents → 72
Same Numbers, New Story — 20 and 30 are deliberately used twice:
lunch packs → GCF = 10
bus intervals → LCM = 60

That directly reinforces the lesson’s principle that the situation—not merely the numbers—determines the mathematical tool.

I also upgraded the actual game. After the student chooses GCF or LCM, an evidence panel now reveals why:

For a grouping problem such as 18 and 24:

GCF(18,24) = 6
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6
Then it explains what 6 means in the story.

For a cycle problem such as 8 and 12:

LCM(8,12) = 24
The multiple lists first meet at 24
Then it explains what 24 means in the context.

So she learns:

interpret → choose tool → inspect structure → see calculation → interpret the answer

rather than simply getting “GCF” or “LCM” marked correct.

I added Foundation / Core / Extended pathways as well. The rounds now scale from straightforward grouping/cycle situations into larger numbers and subtler contexts.
