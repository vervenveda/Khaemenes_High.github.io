# CUMULATIVE ASSESSMENT II BLUEPRINT
## Psychology 101 — Grade 9

**Checkpoint:** After Week 8

**Coverage:** Weeks 1–8, with stronger emphasis on Weeks 5–8 while still requiring transfer of the scientific reasoning established in Weeks 1–4.

### Purpose
This is not eight weekly quizzes combined. It evaluates whether students can integrate research methods, evidence, cognition, emotion, motivation, and development across units.

### Recommended Structure — 40 Items

| Category | Items |
|---|---:|
| Core concept knowledge | 10 |
| Application/scenario reasoning | 8 |
| Research design and measurement | 6 |
| Data/graph interpretation | 6 |
| Cross-unit synthesis | 6 |
| Ethics, limitations, and responsible interpretation | 4 |
| **Total** | **40** |

### Required Coverage

#### Weeks 1–4 foundation
Students must continue to use:
- operational definitions
- correlation vs causation
- experimental reasoning
- observation vs inference
- biological evidence limitations
- attention/perception boundaries
- learning-process identification

#### Week 5 — Memory & Language
- encoding vs retrieval
- working and long-term memory
- reconstructive memory
- misinformation/source monitoring
- study-strategy evidence
- language and wording as measurement variables

#### Week 6 — Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making
- heuristics and base rates
- confirmation bias
- framing
- reliability vs validity
- intelligence score interpretation
- metacognition and calibration

#### Week 7 — Emotion & Motivation
- multi-component emotion
- appraisal
- intrinsic/extrinsic motivation
- goals and incentives
- self-efficacy and persistence
- motivation vs opportunity/performance

#### Week 8 — Human Development
- lifespan domains
- cross-sectional vs longitudinal research
- cohort effects
- developmental variability
- adolescence without determinism
- adulthood and aging as continued development

### Cross-Unit Item Examples

**Example 1 — Memory × Emotion × Research Design**
Participants view the same event under two emotional framing conditions and later complete a memory test. Students identify the independent variable, dependent variable, a possible appraisal pathway, and one reason memory accuracy might differ without assuming deliberate dishonesty.

**Example 2 — Motivation × Development × Context**
Two adolescent groups differ in task persistence. Students must identify why age, peer context, self-efficacy, and opportunity should be separated before concluding that one group is “less motivated.”

**Example 3 — Intelligence × Measurement**
A cognitive test is consistent across administrations but strongly depends on unfamiliar vocabulary. Students explain why reliability does not guarantee validity for every population or interpretation.

**Example 4 — Learning × Memory**
A study method produces excellent practice performance but weak one-week retention. Students distinguish performance during practice from durable learning.

### Data Requirements
Assessment should include at least:
- one scatterplot
- one condition-comparison table
- one longitudinal or age-trajectory graph
- one confidence/calibration table
- one immediate-vs-delayed memory dataset
- one multi-variable emotion/motivation dataset

### Ethical / Limitation Requirements
At least four items should require students to reject or revise claims such as:
- diagnosing an individual from a group pattern
- treating one physiological measure as a complete emotion
- treating age as a complete explanation
- treating intelligence score as total human capability
- treating confidence as equivalent to accuracy

### Mastery
Recommended mastery threshold: **80%**.

Below mastery:
1. identify missed competency clusters;
2. complete targeted correction work;
3. review relevant lesson evidence/data;
4. complete a parallel-form retake rather than the identical item set.

### Scoring Principle
Reward reasoning proportional to evidence. A student should receive credit for saying **“cannot tell from the available evidence”** when that is the scientifically justified answer.
