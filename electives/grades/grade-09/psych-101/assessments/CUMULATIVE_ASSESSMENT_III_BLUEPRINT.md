# CUMULATIVE ASSESSMENT III — BLUEPRINT

## Position
Administer after **Week 12**.

## Coverage
Cumulative across Weeks 1–12, with the heaviest emphasis on Weeks 9–12:
- Personality & Individual Differences
- Social & Cultural Psychology
- Mental & Physical Health Psychology
- Psychological Disorders, Treatment & Ethics

Earlier scientific inquiry, research design, biological, perceptual, learning, memory, cognition, motivation, emotion, and development concepts remain eligible when they are needed to interpret later material.

## Purpose
This is not twelve weekly quizzes pasted together. It measures whether students can integrate psychological science across levels of explanation while preserving ethical and interpretive boundaries.

## Recommended Length
**40 scored items/tasks** with a balanced mix of selected response, short constructed response, data interpretation, and scenario analysis.

## Blueprint

| Domain | Items | Emphasis |
|---|---:|---|
| Core concepts Weeks 9–12 | 10 | traits, social influence, stress/health, disorders/treatments |
| Research design & measurement | 7 | operational definitions, reliability/validity, sampling, causal inference |
| Data/graph interpretation | 6 | distributions, repeated measures, group comparisons, treatment outcomes |
| Application/scenario reasoning | 7 | person × situation, attribution, coping, classification, treatment evidence |
| Cross-unit synthesis | 6 | integrate biological, cognitive, learning, social, developmental, and health explanations |
| Ethics, stigma, privacy & limitations | 4 | diagnosis boundaries, confidentiality, group inference, evidence proportionality |
| **Total** | **40** | |

## Required Cross-Unit Tasks
At least six items must require integration rather than single-unit recall. Examples:

1. A fictional student shows declining concentration during a stressful month. Students compare possible sleep, attention, learning, motivational, and contextual explanations and identify what evidence would distinguish them.
2. A personality score predicts classroom participation weakly. Students explain why trait, situation, social norms, self-efficacy, and measurement error may all matter.
3. A wellness intervention produces a modest group-average improvement. Students interpret effect, generalizability, attrition, and why individual response cannot be guaranteed.
4. A public figure is described online using a diagnostic label. Students explain why the available evidence is insufficient for diagnosis and how attribution/stigma can distort interpretation.
5. A treatment study uses self-report only. Students identify strengths, limitations, expectancy effects, and one additional measure.
6. A developmental group difference is used to justify a policy about all teenagers. Students identify the ecological/individual-inference error and propose better evidence.

## Data Requirements
The assessment must include at least:
- one scatterplot;
- one repeated-measures line graph;
- one overlapping distribution;
- one treatment/comparison table;
- one percentage or relative-risk claim that requires reconstruction;
- one graph with a potentially misleading axis or visual choice.

## Clinical-Literacy Boundary
Students are **not** asked to diagnose fictional people from symptom lists.

Instead, clinical items assess whether students can:
- identify why diagnosis would require more information;
- distinguish symptom from disorder;
- recognize heterogeneity and comorbidity;
- interpret treatment evidence;
- identify stigma and confidentiality issues;
- choose language that does not exceed the evidence.

## Scoring
Recommended raw score: 40 points.

- Selected-response items: 1 point each.
- Short constructed-response items may replace multiple selected items if the total remains equivalent.
- Rubric-scored synthesis responses should reward evidence proportionality, alternatives, and limitations rather than vocabulary density.

## Mastery
**80% or higher = mastery.**

Below mastery:
1. diagnostic feedback by concept/skill category;
2. targeted review;
3. correction/explanation task;
4. parallel-form retake with equivalent blueprint but different prompts/data.

## No Final-Exam Inflation
Cumulative Assessment III is the final conventional cumulative checkpoint. Week 13 culminates in the **Psychology Research Brief and research synthesis**, not a giant all-course exam.
