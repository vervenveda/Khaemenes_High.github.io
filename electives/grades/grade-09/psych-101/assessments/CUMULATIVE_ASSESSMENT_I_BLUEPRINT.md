## Mastery Standard
- **80% or higher:** mastery
- **Below 80%:** targeted review and correction, followed by a parallel-form retake

## Required Assessment Behaviors
Students should be able to:
- distinguish observation, inference, association, and causal evidence;
- interpret basic tables, curves, and scatterplots;
- identify variables, samples, operational definitions, and limitations;
- evaluate biological explanations without biological determinism;
- distinguish sensation, perception, and attention;
- distinguish classical, operant, and observational learning;
- identify when several explanations may operate together;
- communicate conclusions at the level supported by the evidence.

## Cross-Unit Item Model
> A student performs poorly on a learning task after sleeping only four hours. Give one plausible biological explanation, one attention-based explanation, and one learning-related explanation. What research design could help distinguish among them?

A strong response should not select one explanation merely because it is available. It should identify measurable variables, comparison conditions, alternative explanations, and the limits of the proposed design.

## Data Item Model
Provide a graph showing performance across a baseline, acquisition phase, and delayed-retention test. Ask students to identify the pattern, distinguish immediate performance from long-term learning, and state whether the design permits a causal conclusion.

## Ethics Item Model
Present a proposed classroom behavior-change or attention experiment. Ask students to identify a privacy, consent, fairness, or harm concern and propose a scientifically useful ethical redesign.

## Scoring Philosophy
This assessment rewards accurate application, research literacy, data interpretation, synthesis, and scientific restraint. Vocabulary matters because it supports reasoning—not as an isolated memorization target.
