# ASSESSMENT POLICY

## Course Weights
- Weekly Lessons & Assignments — 25%
- Knowledge Checks — 15%
- Critical Thinking Activities — 15%
- Psychology Journal — 10%
- Unit / Cumulative Assessments — 15%
- Final Psychology Research Brief — 20%

## Weekly Knowledge Check Blueprint
Each 10-question weekly assessment includes:
- 4 core knowledge questions
- 2 application/scenario questions
- 2 research/evidence questions
- 1 graph/data interpretation question
- 1 ethics, limitation, fairness, or context question

Questions should test concepts in context rather than reward vocabulary recall alone.

## Mastery
- **80% or higher = mastery**
- Below 80% = targeted review, correction, and parallel-form retake
- Retakes must preserve the same blueprint and difficulty target without simply repeating the identical question set
- Corrections should require explanation of the reasoning error, not only selection of a new answer
- Mastery is reconstructed from actual assessment evidence rather than trusted from a stale completion flag

## Cumulative Checkpoints

### Cumulative Assessment I — after Week 4
Integrates Weeks 1–4.

### Cumulative Assessment II — after Week 8
Covers Weeks 1–8 with emphasis on Weeks 5–8.

### Cumulative Assessment III — after Week 12
Cumulative Weeks 1–12 with emphasis on Weeks 9–12.

See the three blueprint files in this directory.

## Week 13
Week 13 does **not** use a giant conventional final exam.

Students complete:
- the regular 10-question Week 13 synthesis knowledge check;
- the Final Psychology Research Brief;
- a short research defense card / oral or written defense;
- a final metacognitive reflection.

## Clinical-Literacy Assessment Boundary
Assessments must not ask students to diagnose themselves or real people.

Clinical questions should instead assess whether students can:
- distinguish symptom from diagnosis;
- identify missing information;
- explain classification challenges;
- recognize stigma;
- interpret treatment evidence;
- identify provider/ethical boundaries;
- use language proportional to evidence.

## Journal Boundary
The Psychology Journal is **academic, not therapeutic**.

Students may always use:
- fictional cases;
- public/non-sensitive examples;
- course concepts;
- supplied datasets;
- hypothetical research scenarios.

No grade requires disclosure of trauma, diagnosis, medication, treatment history, family mental-health information, sexuality, substance use, or other private personal material.

## Data Literacy Requirement
Across weekly and cumulative assessments, students repeatedly interpret:
- tables;
- percentages;
- averages/distributions;
- scatterplots;
- line graphs;
- repeated-measures patterns;
- group comparisons;
- misleading axes/visual framing;
- treatment and intervention outcomes.

## Scoring Principle
A scientifically cautious answer can earn more credit than a dramatic answer when the cautious answer better matches the evidence.

**The course rewards proportional inference, not confidence theater.**
