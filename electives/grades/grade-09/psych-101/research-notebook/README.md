# PSYCHOLOGY RESEARCH NOTEBOOK

The Research Notebook runs through all 13 weeks and gradually develops the final Psychology Research Brief.

## Week 1 — Scientific Foundation
Broad question → multiple levels of explanation → variables → operational definitions → predicted data pattern → causal limitation → ethics → research design.

## Week 2 — Biological Level
Possible biological contributor → physiological measure → brain-system hypothesis if justified → sleep/arousal/attention consideration → biological method + limitation.

## Week 3 — Sensation, Perception & Attention
Direct measurement vs inference → sensory/behavioral/physiological measures → bottom-up/top-down influences → attention as mediator/alternative → context and experience.

## Week 4 — Learning & Behavior
Possible learned associations → possible consequences/contingencies → repeated-measurement baseline → possible modeling/social-learning influence → compare competing learning explanations and retain only those justified.

### Week 4 Milestone — Research Question Defense
Student presents:
- original question;
- refined question;
- variables;
- operational definitions;
- possible biological factor;
- possible perceptual/attention factor;
- possible learning factor;
- proposed evidence;
- one alternative explanation;
- one ethical issue;
- what can currently be excluded.

## Week 5 — Memory & Language
Attention/encoding as variable or mediator → specify relevant memory process → choose immediate/delayed measurement → evaluate retrospective self-report and source-monitoring problems → audit wording, translation, and linguistic context.

## Week 6 — Thinking, Measurement & Metacognition
Clarify constructs/categories → identify heuristic/base-rate risks → audit framing and wording → evaluate reliability/validity of cognitive measures → add a confidence rating and specify evidence that would increase, decrease, or overturn the working explanation.

## Week 7 — Emotion & Motivation
Determine whether emotion is relevant → operationalize emotion with more than one possible measure → consider appraisal as a mediator → identify possible goals, incentives, or expected success → distinguish motivation from opportunity and performance → identify one explanation that should be excluded if evidence is insufficient.

### Week 7 Model Check
Use the scaffold only when supported:
**Situation → Appraisal → Emotion → Motivation → Choice → Consequence**

Then challenge it:
- Which elements are observed?
- Which are measured?
- Which are inferred?
- Could the order differ?
- Could prior learning, social context, habit, or opportunity explain part of the pattern?

## Week 8 — Human Development
Determine whether age/development is relevant → select the developmental domain involved → compare cross-sectional and longitudinal evidence → identify cohort effects → audit language, schooling, peer, family, cultural, and historical context → distinguish developmental group pattern from individual prediction.

### Week 8 Milestone — Developmental Inference Check
Student states:
- whether a developmental explanation is justified;
- which developmental process is proposed;
- what design would measure change over time;
- one cohort/context alternative;
- one conclusion that cannot be made about an individual from group-level data.

## Week 9 — Personality & Individual Differences
Determine whether stable individual differences are relevant → define the trait/construct → choose self-report, informant report, behavioral observation, repeated measures, or combination → identify reliability/validity questions → identify response-bias risks → test situational moderation → distinguish description from causal explanation.

### Week 9 Milestone — Individual-Differences Audit
Student states:
- whether a personality/trait explanation is actually needed;
- exact construct;
- operationalization;
- evidence of stability across time/settings;
- likely measurement bias;
- situational moderator/alternative;
- one conclusion a trait score would **not** justify.

## Week 10 — Social & Cultural Psychology
Determine whether social influence is relevant → define social variable precisely → informational/normative processes if plausible → compare person-level and situation-level explanations → identify unit of analysis → audit sampling/representation → identify cultural, institutional, economic, environmental, or historical context.

### Week 10 Milestone — Social Context Audit
Student states:
- proposed social variable;
- unit of analysis;
- dispositional explanation;
- situational explanation;
- sample/representation limitation;
- cultural/institutional context that could alter the result;
- one group-level conclusion inappropriate for an individual;
- how explanation is separated from moral/legal excuse.

## Week 11 — Mental & Physical Health Psychology
Determine whether stress or health behavior is genuinely relevant → distinguish stressor, appraisal, response, coping, and outcome → add behavioral/contextual measures where justified → evaluate sleep/recovery/social support as mediator, moderator, or confound → separate self-report from physiological/behavioral evidence → audit health claims for evidence proportionality.

### Week 11 Milestone — Health Interpretation Boundary
Student states:
- whether the project makes any health-related claim;
- what is measured directly;
- what is only inferred;
- one environmental/access variable that could change the pattern;
- whether a wellness claim exceeds the available evidence;
- what the project **cannot** say about diagnosis, treatment, or an individual's health status.

## Week 12 — Psychological Disorders, Treatment & Ethics
Audit for accidental pathologizing → remove unsupported diagnostic labels → distinguish symptom, self-report, professional diagnosis, and functional outcome → add stigma/privacy analysis if health information is involved → distinguish treatment efficacy, effectiveness, access, adherence, burden, and individual variation → state professional expertise required beyond the student project's scope.

### Week 12 Milestone — Clinical Boundary Audit
Student answers:
- Does the project diagnose anyone directly or by implication?
- Does it use clinical labels only when supported by professionally defined samples?
- Does it protect privacy and dignity?
- Does it confuse symptoms with disorders?
- If treatment is discussed, does it evaluate evidence without prescribing?
- What provider expertise would be needed for clinical decisions?
- What conclusion must be removed because it exceeds the student's evidence or role?

## Week 13 — Applied Psychology & Research Synthesis
Translate evidence into a real-world decision → identify applied field/user of findings → audit external validity → compare effect size with practical importance → identify implementation barriers → evaluate human-factors/privacy/autonomy issues → synthesize independent sources → preserve disagreement/uncertainty → write a bounded final conclusion and next research question.

### Week 13 Milestone — Research Integrity Audit
Student states:
- original working explanation;
- strongest claim now supported;
- claim that became weaker or was abandoned;
- variables measured directly vs inferred;
- strongest converging evidence;
- conflicting evidence;
- population/settings represented;
- generalizability boundary;
- ethical/application limit;
- what evidence could change the conclusion;
- next research question.

## Final Research Brief Transfer
The notebook feeds directly into the final brief:
1. Research Question
2. Why the Question Matters
3. Psychological Background
4. Variables & Operational Definitions
5. Evidence Base
6. Data/Study Interpretation
7. Competing or Alternative Explanation
8. Real-World Application
9. Ethics / Privacy / Equity Considerations
10. Limitations & Generalizability
11. Conclusion Proportional to Evidence
12. Next Research Question
13. Sources

## Permanent Research Rule
**Justified exclusion is better science than forcing every theory or level of explanation onto every research question.**

A student may conclude that a biological, perceptual, learning, memory, cognitive, emotional, motivational, developmental, personality, social, cultural, health, clinical, or other factor is not currently relevant. The notebook rewards defensible reasoning, not the number of theories named.

## Permanent Person-Level Interpretation Rule
A group pattern, trait score, developmental average, symptom report, diagnosis, or psychological measure is evidence about a defined construct under defined conditions. It is not a complete description of a person, a moral judgment, a destiny, or permission for amateur diagnosis.

## Permanent Scientific-Humility Rule
A final conclusion should become **narrower** when the evidence is narrow. Revising, weakening, or abandoning a hypothesis in response to evidence is successful scientific reasoning, not failure.
