# FINAL PROJECT — THE PSYCHOLOGY RESEARCH BRIEF

## Purpose

The final project is the culmination of the 13-week Research Notebook. Students do not begin from a blank page in Week 13; they revise the question, measures, evidence, alternatives, ethics, and limitations developed throughout the course.

## Target Length

**2–4 polished pages plus references and visuals as appropriate.**

Quality of reasoning matters more than page inflation.

## Required Sections

1. **Research Question**  
   Precise, researchable, and appropriately limited.

2. **Why the Question Matters**  
   Academic or real-world significance without exaggeration.

3. **Psychological Background**  
   Relevant concepts/theories only. Students do not force every course unit into the brief.

4. **Variables & Operational Definitions**  
   What is measured, how, and what remains inferred.

5. **Evidence Base**  
   Multiple relevant sources/studies or a course-provided evidence packet.

6. **Data / Study Interpretation**  
   At least one graph, table, effect, or study result interpreted accurately.

7. **Competing or Alternative Explanation**  
   A plausible rival account and what evidence would distinguish it.

8. **Real-World Application**  
   Who might use the finding and what decision it could inform.

9. **Ethics / Privacy / Equity Considerations**  
   Risks, affected groups, privacy, fairness, autonomy, or access as relevant.

10. **Limitations & Generalizability**  
    What the evidence cannot establish and where the conclusion may not travel.

11. **Conclusion Proportional to Evidence**  
    No causal, diagnostic, universal, or treatment claim beyond the evidence.

12. **Next Research Question**  
    One specific follow-up that would reduce uncertainty.

13. **Sources**  
    Clear citation of all evidence used.

## Source Expectations

Students should use **at least four credible sources/evidence items**, with at least two containing empirical evidence or direct summaries of empirical findings.

The course remains usable without required internet access. A teacher/parent may provide an offline source packet, saved PDFs, printed articles, course datasets, or other approved materials.

Students should distinguish:
- empirical study;
- review/synthesis;
- authoritative educational source;
- popular-media summary;
- opinion/testimonial.

Multiple webpages repeating the same original study do not count as multiple independent lines of evidence.

## Research Integrity Requirements

The final brief must include:
- at least one claim that is directly tied to a measure;
- at least one alternative explanation;
- at least one explicit limitation;
- at least one generalizability boundary;
- at least one ethics/privacy/context consideration;
- language that distinguishes association from causation;
- no amateur diagnosis of classmates, family members, public figures, or fictional characters;
- no individualized treatment recommendation.

| Criterion | Points |

## Research Defense Card

Alongside the brief, students prepare a one-page defense card:
- research question;
- operational definitions;
- strongest evidence;
- strongest alternative explanation;
- key limitation;
- generalizability boundary;
- ethical consideration;
- final conclusion in two sentences;
- next research question.

This may be presented orally, submitted in writing, or used for a short teacher/parent conference.

## Final Principle

**The project is not successful because it proves the student's original idea. It is successful when the conclusion accurately reflects the evidence—even if the original idea must be revised or rejected.**
