# WEEK 08 — Human Development
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How do biology, experience, relationships, and culture shape development across the lifespan?

### Intellectual Anchor
> Development describes patterns of change and stability across time. Age-related averages are useful scientific summaries, but they are not rigid schedules and do not define any individual person.

### Daily Sequence
1. **Studying Development Across Time** — lifespan domains, continuity/change, longitudinal and cross-sectional methods, cohort effects, ethics.
2. **Early Development: Biology, Relationships & Experience** — prenatal influences, infancy, temperament, attachment as a relationship construct, motor and social development, developmental variability.
3. **Cognitive & Language Development** — changing reasoning, executive functions, language growth, learning through interaction, and limits of stage-based simplification.
4. **Adolescence, Identity & Social Development** — puberty, autonomy, peers, family, culture, identity exploration, decision-making, and anti-deterministic brain-development guardrails.
5. **Adulthood, Aging & Lifespan Synthesis** — stability and change in adulthood, cognitive aging, expertise, social roles, adaptation, and the Development Case Study.

### Research Methods Strand
Students compare:
- cross-sectional studies
- longitudinal studies
- cohort-sequential logic at an introductory level
- repeated measures
- developmental observation
- age-group comparisons

They learn that age differences in a cross-sectional study can reflect development, cohort differences, or both.

### Permanent Claims Laboratory Set
- “Every child should reach every milestone at exactly the same age.”
- “Early experiences permanently determine the rest of a person's life.”
- “Adolescents cannot make good decisions because their brains are unfinished.”
- “People stop developing once they become adults.”
- “Memory decline is inevitable and identical for everyone in old age.”
- “A group average tells you what a specific person should be like.”

### Data Literacy
Students interpret:
- age-by-performance tables
- developmental trajectories
- longitudinal change
- cross-sectional comparisons
- distributions and overlapping groups
- simple cohort-effect examples

### Research Notebook Progression
- determine whether age/development is relevant to the semester question
- identify whether a cross-sectional or longitudinal design would answer the question better
- consider language, schooling, family, peer, cultural, or cohort context
- identify developmental averages without turning them into individual predictions
- specify one developmental explanation and one non-developmental alternative

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Development Case Study — Pattern Without Destiny**

Students analyze a fictional person across several developmental periods. They identify physical, cognitive, social, and cultural influences; distinguish age-related patterns from individual variation; identify evidence and limitations; and avoid diagnosis or rigid stage labeling.

### Weekly Assessment
10 questions:

- 1 graph/data interpretation
- 1 ethics/limitation/context

Mastery threshold: **80%**. Below mastery: targeted review, correction, and parallel-form retake.

### Boundaries
- milestones are ranges, not deadlines
- developmental averages do not define an individual
- adolescence is not a disorder or a period of automatic irrationality
- adult development continues
- aging includes both change and preserved abilities
- no amateur developmental diagnosis
- no required disclosure of family history or private childhood experiences
