# Week 08 · Day 05
## Adulthood, Aging & Lifespan Synthesis

**Week Unit:** Human Development

**Essential Question:** How do people continue to change, adapt, and remain stable across adulthood and aging?

### Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:
- explain that psychological development continues throughout adulthood;
- distinguish broad patterns of change from inevitable individual outcomes;
- describe how expertise, experience, health, relationships, and environment can influence adult performance;
- recognize that different cognitive abilities can show different patterns across adulthood;
- synthesize physical, cognitive, social, and cultural influences in a lifespan case without diagnosis or deterministic prediction.

### Vocabulary in Context
adulthood · aging · lifespan development · expertise · cognitive change · adaptation · social role · continuity · plasticity · individual difference

### Core Reading Architecture
Development does not stop at adulthood. Adults continue to learn, change roles, build expertise, adapt to new environments, form and revise relationships, and experience biological change.

Some cognitive processes may become slower on average with age, especially under tasks demanding rapid processing. Other abilities may remain stable for long periods or benefit from accumulated knowledge and expertise. Health, education, occupation, opportunity, activity, stress, sleep, and culture can all influence performance.

Aging therefore should not be taught as simple decline. Lifespan development includes:
- gains;
- losses;
- stability;
- compensation;
- adaptation.

### Worked Example
Two adults of the same age perform differently on a memory task.

Possible contributors include:
- prior knowledge;
- sleep;
- hearing or vision;
- familiarity with the task;
- stress;
- health;
- education;
- strategy use.

Age alone is an incomplete explanation.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
**Claim:** “Memory gets worse as people age.”

Students refine it by asking:
- Which kind of memory?
- Which age range?
- Which task?
- Average change or every person?
- What health and educational factors matter?
- Is the design longitudinal or cross-sectional?

### Data Lens — Overlapping Groups
Fictional delayed-recall scores:

| Group | Mean score | Observed range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ages 20–29 | 84 | 68–96 |
| Ages 40–49 | 82 | 66–95 |
| Ages 60–69 | 77 | 61–94 |
| Ages 75–84 | 71 | 54–91 |

Students identify the average trend and the substantial overlap. They must explain why knowing a person's age does not reveal the person's exact score.

### Claims Laboratory
Students classify:
- “People stop developing when they become adults.”
- “Older adults cannot learn new things.”
- “Memory decline is identical for everyone.”
- “A developmental average predicts an individual.”

Target judgment: each is **too strong or unsupported**.

### Guided Investigation — Lifespan Systems Map
Students receive one fictional person described at four ages. For each age they identify:
- physical context;
- cognitive abilities and demands;
- relationships/social roles;
- education/work/environment;
- cultural context;
- continuity from earlier periods;
- meaningful change;
- evidence needed before making a causal inference.

### Weekly Mastery Task
## Development Case Study — Pattern Without Destiny

Students write a 750–1,000 word analysis of a fictional lifespan case.

Required sections:
1. Developmental Question
2. Physical/Biological Factors
3. Cognitive and Language Factors
4. Social/Relationship Factors
5. Cultural/Environmental Context
6. Developmental Research Method
7. Age-Related Pattern
8. Individual Variation
9. Alternative Explanation
10. Limitation
11. Ethical Boundary
12. Conclusion

The conclusion must explicitly distinguish a group-level developmental pattern from an individual prediction.

### Research/Data Lab
Students compare a cross-sectional dataset with a simplified longitudinal dataset on the same developmental question. They identify:
- what each design measures;
- possible cohort effects;
- attrition or repeated-testing limitations;
- where the conclusions agree or diverge;
- which design better supports a claim about change over time.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Update the semester model:
- Is development or age relevant?
- What domain of development is relevant?
- Would cross-sectional or longitudinal evidence be stronger?
- What cohort/context variable could matter?
- What individual-level conclusion must not be made from a group average?

### Week 8 Final Principle
> **Development is patterned without being predetermined. People change within biological, relational, historical, cultural, and individual contexts across the entire lifespan.**
