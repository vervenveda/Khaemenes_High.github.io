# WEEK 11 — Mental & Physical Health Psychology
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How do psychological science, behavior, and context relate to health and well-being without turning ordinary experiences into diagnoses?

## Intellectual Anchor

> **Health is shaped by interacting biological, psychological, behavioral, social, and environmental factors. A single habit, feeling, or stress response does not define a person's health.**

Week 11 introduces health psychology through stress, coping, sleep and behavior, social support, resilience, and the evaluation of wellness claims. It remains educational rather than therapeutic: students work with fictional cases, supplied data, and public claims instead of disclosing private health information.

The week aligns with the APA high-school Health standard: students study stress as a psychophysiological response, sources and consequences of stress, strategies for coping, resilience/flourishing, and evidence-based health-promoting behavior.

## Week 11 Intellectual Spine

| Strand | Student understanding |
|---|---|
| Stress | Stress is a psychophysiological response involving appraisal, body systems, behavior, and context |
| Stressor | A stressor is an event or condition that places demands on a person; the same event can be appraised differently |
| Acute vs. chronic | Short-term and persistent stress exposures are not psychologically identical |
| Appraisal | Perceived demands, resources, predictability, and control can alter stress responses |
| Allostatic load | Repeated or prolonged physiological adjustment can create cumulative wear; it is a population-level explanatory concept, not a casual diagnosis |
| Coping | Coping strategies differ in fit, function, timing, and evidence |
| Perceived control | Control can influence appraisal and coping, but actual constraints must be considered |
| Social support | Emotional, informational, and practical support can affect coping and health behavior |
| Health behavior | Sleep, activity, routines, adherence, substance exposure, and other behaviors interact with context and biology |
| Health inequity/context | Opportunity, resources, environment, access, discrimination, and institutions can influence health-related behavior and outcomes |
| Resilience | Resilience is adaptation under challenge, not invulnerability or a personality badge |
| Wellness claims | Health claims require evidence, appropriate measures, comparison conditions, and proportional conclusions |
| Ethics | Health education must avoid blame, diagnosis, coercion, and unsupported treatment claims |

## Claims Laboratory — Locked Set

- “Stress is always harmful.” → **Too strong. Stress responses can be adaptive; duration, intensity, appraisal, recovery, and context matter.**
- “If two people face the same stressor, they should react the same way.” → **Incorrect. Appraisal, biology, history, resources, and context can differ.**
- “A person who copes well never feels stressed.” → **Incorrect. Coping concerns response and adaptation, not absence of stress.**
- “If someone knows the healthy choice, they will make it.” → **Unsupported. Opportunity, cost, habit, reinforcement, access, fatigue, social context, and competing goals matter.**
- “Resilient people do not need support.” → **Incorrect. Social and material support can be part of resilience.**
- “Natural means safe and effective.” → **Unsupported. Source, dose, evidence, interactions, and study quality matter.**
- “One wellness study proves a strategy works for everyone.” → **Incorrect. Replication, effect size, population, comparison condition, and individual variation matter.**
- “Health outcomes reveal how disciplined a person is.” → **Unsupported moral inference. Health has multiple determinants.**

## Week 11 Research/Data Lab

### Stress, Recovery & Context Dataset

Students analyze fictional repeated-measures data across baseline, challenge, and recovery periods. They identify:
- stressor condition;
- physiological and/or behavioral measure;
- baseline;
- peak response;
- recovery slope;
- individual variation;
- one contextual moderator;
- one limitation of inferring private emotional states from a physiological measure.

A second short dataset compares a health behavior across groups with different environmental access. Students must distinguish **behavioral choice** from **available opportunity**.

## Week 11 Culminating Task

### Stress Strategy & Wellness Claim Analysis

Students receive a fictional case and one public-style wellness claim. They produce an evidence audit containing:
1. observable demands/stressors;
2. possible appraisal factors;
3. physiological, cognitive, behavioral, and social components;
4. at least two coping options with different functions;
5. one environmental or access factor;
6. one proposed outcome measure;
7. evidence needed to test the wellness claim;
8. one alternative explanation;
9. one limitation;
10. one ethical boundary;
11. a conclusion using language proportional to the evidence.

No student is required to disclose personal stress, health conditions, family health information, medications, diagnoses, or treatment history.

## Permanent Week 11 Guardrails

### Stress ≠ disorder
Stress responses are ordinary psychological and physiological phenomena and are not, by themselves, diagnoses.

### Coping ≠ moral strength
Coping depends partly on resources, context, learning, health, and available options.

### Resilience ≠ invulnerability
Resilience can coexist with distress, support-seeking, setbacks, and changing needs.

### Health behavior ≠ pure willpower
Knowledge and intention operate within habits, reinforcement histories, opportunity, cost, access, biology, and social context.

### Wellness claim ≠ evidence
Testimonials, popularity, naturalness, celebrity endorsement, and personal conviction do not establish efficacy.

### Education ≠ treatment
This course teaches psychological science. It does not prescribe individualized health or mental-health treatment.
