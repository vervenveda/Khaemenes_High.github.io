# WEEK 13 — CAPSTONE, SYNTHESIS & COURSE FADE-OUT
## STATUS: LOCKED — NO NEW LESSONS

**Central Question:** What can the student now demonstrate, defend, and apply after completing Psychology 101?

## Week 13 Purpose

Week 13 is deliberately **not another instructional unit**. Students have completed the course's new content by the end of Week 12.

The final week lowers reading load, removes new daily lessons, and gives students room to demonstrate what they know through synthesis, application, ethical reasoning, the final research work, and a separate final examination.

> **No new lessons. No new long readings. No new conceptual load. Week 13 is for retrieval, synthesis, demonstration, reflection, and closure.**

## Week 13 Components

Week 13 contains four distinct pieces:

1. **50-Question Psychology Capstone Synthesis** — mixed-format and cumulative.
2. **Final Psychology Research Brief / Capstone Project** — completed from the semester Research Notebook.
3. **Separate Final Examination** — formal cumulative exam, in addition to the 50-question capstone synthesis.
4. **Course Fade-Out / Reflection** — brief, low-pressure closure after the academic requirements are complete.

The **50-question capstone does not replace the final exam**. They are separate assessments with different purposes.

# 1. 50-Question Psychology Capstone Synthesis

The capstone measures transfer, vocabulary, evidence reasoning, application, and ethical judgment across the entire course.

## Mixed-Format Blueprint

Recommended distribution:

| Format | Count | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Multiple choice | 30 | concepts, application, research methods, data interpretation, cross-unit synthesis |
| Fill in the blank / precise term | 10 | anchor vocabulary and scientific language in context |
| Moral / ethical explanation responses | 10 | explain a responsible course of action or judgment using evidence, dignity, privacy, fairness, agency, and course ethics |
| **Total** | **50** | |

### Moral / Ethical Explanation Responses

These responses are not graded for agreement with one personal worldview. They are graded for the student's ability to:
- identify the ethical or moral tension;
- distinguish psychological explanation from excuse;
- protect human dignity and privacy;
- recognize power, consent, fairness, and potential harm;
- use evidence rather than stereotype;
- acknowledge uncertainty or competing values when appropriate;
- defend a conclusion clearly.

Examples may involve:
- whether a behavior-change system is fair;
- when persuasive design becomes manipulation;
- whether group findings justify an individual judgment;
- confidentiality and competing responsibilities;
- stigma and respectful communication;
- ethical use of psychological evidence in schools, technology, health, media, or public decisions.

No response should require disclosure of the student's personal trauma, diagnosis, treatment, family history, sexuality, substance use, or other private material.

## Capstone Coverage

The 50-question synthesis may draw from all twelve instructional weeks:
1. Psychology as a Science
2. Brain, Nervous System & Consciousness
3. Sensation, Perception & Attention
4. Learning & Behavior
5. Memory & Language
6. Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making
7. Emotion & Motivation

The capstone should emphasize **connections among units**, not obscure trivia.

# 2. Final Psychology Research Brief / Capstone Project

The Research Brief remains the student's substantial authored capstone product. It grows from the semester Research Notebook rather than appearing suddenly at the end.

Required sections:
1. Research Question
2. Why the Question Matters
3. Psychological Background
4. Variables & Operational Definitions
5. Evidence Base
6. Data / Study Interpretation
7. Competing or Alternative Explanation
8. Real-World Application
9. Ethics / Privacy / Equity Considerations
10. Limitations & Generalizability
11. Conclusion Proportional to Evidence
12. Next Research Question
13. Sources

Target: **2–4 polished pages plus references/visuals as appropriate**. Quality of reasoning matters more than page inflation.

A short oral or written defense may accompany the brief, but it should not become another long assignment.

# 3. Separate Final Examination

Psychology 101 includes a **formal cumulative final exam in addition to the 50-question capstone synthesis**.

The final exam should:
- assess cumulative course knowledge and reasoning;
- use a separate item bank from the capstone;
- avoid simply repeating weekly-assessment questions;
- preserve research/data/ethics reasoning;
- include cross-unit application;
- have its own scoring record;
- remain distinct from the Research Brief score and the 50-question capstone score.

The exact final-exam blueprint, item count, open/closed-book policy, retake policy, and weighting are maintained in the assessment architecture rather than invented inside Week 13 lesson pages.

# 4. Course Fade-Out & Closure

After the capstone work and final exam, the course intentionally becomes lighter.

Suggested closure options:
- one-page **What I Know Now** reflection;
- Research Notebook integrity audit;
- favorite Psychology Word of the Day review;
- optional Concept Mosaic / Doodle Break / calm Aurora doorway;
- local progress export;
- print/save final course record;
- brief celebration of mastered work.

No new academic content is introduced during the fade-out.

## Research Notebook — Final Integrity Audit

Students close the notebook by answering:
- What is the strongest claim my evidence supports?
- What claim did I weaken, revise, or abandon?
- Which variables were measured directly and which were inferred?
- Where did sources agree or disagree?
- What population and settings does my evidence represent?
- What ethical boundary limits application?
- What should be studied next?

## Permanent Week 13 Guardrails

### Capstone ≠ final exam
The 50-question mixed capstone and the formal final exam are two separate assessments.

### Synthesis ≠ more content
Students demonstrate accumulated knowledge rather than receiving another week of instruction.

### Moral reasoning ≠ compelled personal belief
Ethical explanation responses are evaluated for reasoning, evidence, dignity, fairness, privacy, consent, agency, and acknowledgment of limitations—not for adopting one personal ideology.

### Explanation ≠ excuse
Understanding psychological influences does not erase responsibility for harmful behavior.

### Statistical significance ≠ practical importance
Real-world decisions also depend on effect size, meaning, burden, risk, equity, and context.

### Final conclusion ≠ final truth
Scientific conclusions remain revisable when stronger evidence appears.
