# Week 13 · Day 1 — Applied Psychology Across Real-World Settings

**Essential Question:** Where does psychological science influence decisions outside the classroom?

## Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:
- define applied psychology;
- distinguish major applied settings and the kinds of problems studied in each;
- connect earlier course concepts to real-world problems;
- distinguish using psychological evidence from claiming professional qualification;
- identify outcome measures appropriate to applied questions.

## Psychology Word of the Day
**applied psychology** — the use of psychological science to understand or improve real-world problems, decisions, systems, and environments.

## Vocabulary in Context
applied psychology · educational psychology · industrial-organizational psychology · human factors · forensic psychology · health psychology · community psychology · program evaluation · outcome measure · evidence-based decision

## Core Reading
Psychological science is used in schools, workplaces, healthcare systems, technology, law, transportation, sports, public programs, product design, and community settings. Applied psychology does not mean “giving advice.” It means using psychological theory, measurement, research, and evaluation to address defined problems.

Students connect prior weeks to examples: attention and perception in interface design; learning and memory in instruction; motivation in organizations; social psychology in teamwork; health psychology in prevention; development in school policy; measurement in program evaluation.

## Worked Example
A school wants to reduce missed assignments.

Possible applied questions include:
- Is the problem memory, unclear instructions, workload, access, motivation, timing, technology, or something else?
- What behavior should be measured?
- What intervention is being proposed?
- What comparison would show improvement?

Applied psychology begins with problem definition, not a favorite intervention.

## Psychological Scientist's Desk
**Claim:** “Psychologists can tell companies how to make people do what they want.”

Students replace the claim with a more accurate account of probabilistic influence, human factors, ethics, and agency.

## Data Lens
Students compare outcomes from three fictional school programs. They identify which measure best reflects the stated goal and why a satisfaction rating is not always equivalent to improved performance.

## Claims Laboratory
Evaluate:
- “Applied psychology is common sense used professionally.”
- “If users like a program, it works.”
- “One psychological principle explains a workplace problem.”
- “Knowing psychology qualifies someone to practice clinically.”

## Guided Investigation
### Applied Field Match
Students receive eight real-world problems and match each to relevant psychological fields, then justify which evidence and measures would be needed. More than one field may be appropriate.

## Independent Assignment
Create an **Applied Psychology Problem Brief** with:
1. real-world problem;
2. target population/system;
3. two relevant psychology concepts;
4. measurable outcome;
5. one ethical concern;
6. one alternative explanation.

## Research Notebook Checkpoint
Identify the most realistic application of the semester question. State who would use the finding and what decision it could inform—without exceeding the evidence.

## Academic Journal Reflection
What is the difference between applying a psychological principle and proving an intervention works?

## Daily Review — 5 Questions
1. Define applied psychology.
2. Name three applied fields.
3. Why is problem definition important?
4. What is an outcome measure?
5. Why does psychology knowledge not equal professional qualification?
