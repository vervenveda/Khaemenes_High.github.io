# Week 13 · Day 5 — Research Symposium, Final Brief & Scientific Reflection

**Essential Question:** What can the student now claim, what remains uncertain, and what should be studied next?

## Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:
- present a research question and evidence-based conclusion concisely;
- defend operational definitions and source choices;
- identify the strongest alternative explanation and limitation;
- explain the scope of generalizability;
- revise a conclusion when challenged by evidence;
- distinguish scientific confidence from certainty.

## Psychology Word of the Day
**generalizability** — the justified extent to which findings can apply beyond the people, settings, tasks, and conditions actually studied.

## Vocabulary in Context
research defense · synthesis · generalizability · boundary condition · uncertainty · scientific humility · replication · next question · application · revision

## Core Reading
The final act of science is not certainty. It is a bounded claim that clearly states what the evidence supports, what it does not support, and what should happen next.

Students review the entire course arc: observation and measurement; biological systems; perception; learning; memory; cognition; emotion and motivation; development; personality; social/cultural context; health; disorders/treatment; and application. They do not force every unit into the final explanation. Justified exclusion remains a sign of good reasoning.

## Worked Example
A student's original question was broad and causal. By Week 13, the evidence supports only an association under certain conditions.

The student earns stronger marks by narrowing the conclusion than by preserving an unsupported causal claim.

## Psychological Scientist's Desk
**Claim:** “A strong final project should prove the hypothesis.”

Students explain why the goal is to evaluate the hypothesis, not protect it.

## Data Lens
Students revisit one dataset used earlier in the course and write two conclusions:
- an overclaim;
- a scientifically proportional claim.

They identify the exact words that make the first conclusion too strong.

## Claims Laboratory
Evaluate:
- “A failed hypothesis means the project failed.”
- “More confidence makes a conclusion stronger.”
- “A limitation makes research weak.”
- “A final project should use every theory studied in the course.”
- “Once published or presented, a scientific conclusion should not change.”

## Guided Investigation
### Research Defense Card
Students prepare a one-page card containing:
1. research question;
2. operational definitions;
3. strongest evidence;
4. strongest alternative explanation;
5. key limitation;
6. generalizability boundary;
7. ethical consideration;
8. final conclusion in two sentences;
9. next research question.

## Independent Assignment — Final Psychology Research Brief
Submit the polished brief:
1. Research Question
2. Why the Question Matters
3. Psychological Background
4. Variables & Operational Definitions
5. Evidence Base
6. Data/Study Interpretation
7. Competing or Alternative Explanation
8. Real-World Application
9. Ethics / Privacy / Equity Considerations
10. Limitations & Generalizability
11. Conclusion Proportional to Evidence
12. Next Research Question
13. Sources

Target: **2–4 polished pages plus references/visuals as appropriate**.

## Final Research Notebook Checkpoint
Complete the **Research Integrity Audit**:
- What did I originally think?
- What changed?
- Which claim became stronger?
- Which claim became weaker or was abandoned?
- What evidence mattered most?
- What remains unknown?
- What would I measure next?

## Academic Journal Reflection
How has your definition of “good evidence” changed since Week 1? This is an academic reflection; no private psychological disclosure is required.

## Week 13 Synthesis Knowledge Check
10 mixed-format questions using the permanent blueprint:
- 4 core synthesis concepts
- 2 applications
- 2 research/evidence
- 1 data interpretation
- 1 ethics/limitation

**Mastery threshold: 80%.** Parallel-form retake follows targeted correction if needed.

## Closing Principle
> **The strongest psychology student is not the one who can label the most behavior. It is the one who can ask a precise question, measure carefully, reason from evidence, protect people, and change a conclusion when the evidence requires it.**
