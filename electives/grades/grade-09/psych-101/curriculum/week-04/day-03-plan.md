# Week 04 · Day 03
## Shaping, Schedules & Habit Formation

**Essential Question:** How can complex behaviors develop through small changes in consequences and repeated practice?

### Learning Objectives
- Explain shaping through successive approximations.
- Compare fixed/variable and ratio/interval reinforcement schedules conceptually.
- Explain why intermittent reinforcement can support persistent behavior.
- Analyze a habit using cues, behavior, consequences, and repeated measurement.
- Identify possible unintended effects of a behavior-change plan.

### Vocabulary in Context
shaping · successive approximation · fixed ratio · variable ratio · fixed interval · variable interval · habit · baseline · persistence

### Core Concept
Shaping builds complex behavior gradually by reinforcing closer approximations to a target. Reinforcement schedules influence the pattern and persistence of responding, but they are not the only influences on habits. Goals, identity, cognition, motivation, biology, environment, and social context may also matter.

### Worked Example
A fictional learner who studies for two minutes is not immediately required to study for sixty. A shaping sequence may reinforce opening materials, five focused minutes, ten minutes, completion of one task, and later the full routine.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate: **“The best way to build a habit is to reward yourself every single time forever.”**

Students distinguish establishment from maintenance and consider whether intrinsic goals, environmental cues, and practicality change the best design.

### Data Lens
Interpret a 14-day task-start graph with a baseline period and an intervention period. Identify trend, variability, apparent change, and why a single uncontrolled case cannot by itself prove causation.

### Guided Investigation
Design a shaping plan for a fictional student who wants to begin homework earlier. Include target behavior, baseline, three approximations, reinforcement, measurement, and one possible unintended effect.

### Digital Application
Analyze how intermittent or variable feedback can appear in games, notifications, or online environments without assuming every digital product works like gambling.

### Independent Assignment
Create an ethical habit-design plan using cue → behavior → consequence → repetition and explain where the model may be incomplete.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Could repeated measurement establish a useful baseline for the semester research question? If yes, define the measure and observation period.

### Academic Journal
Why can gradual behavior change sometimes be more informative than demanding the full target behavior immediately?
