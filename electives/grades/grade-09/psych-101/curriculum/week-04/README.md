# WEEK 04 — Learning & Behavior
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How does experience change behavior, and how can psychologists tell what kind of learning has occurred?

### Intellectual Anchor
> Learning changes behavior or knowledge through experience, but the same behavior can arise through different learning processes. Identify the process from the evidence—not merely from how the behavior looks.

### Daily Sequence
1. **Classical Conditioning** — predictive relationships among stimuli; acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, and discrimination.
2. **Operant Conditioning** — consequences and future behavior; reinforcement, punishment, and Antecedent → Behavior → Consequence analysis.
3. **Shaping, Schedules & Habit Formation** — successive approximations, reinforcement schedules, repeated measurement, and ethical habit design.
4. **Observational Learning, Modeling & Cognition** — attention, retention, production, motivation, vicarious consequences, expectations, and social learning.
5. **Learning Systems, Applications & Ethical Behavior Change** — compare learning models, evaluate evidence, and design responsible interventions.

### Claims Laboratory — Locked Set
- “Classical conditioning means any repeated event becomes associated.”
- “Negative reinforcement means punishment.”
- “Giving a reward always reinforces behavior.”
- “People simply copy whatever they see.”
- “Punishment is always the most effective way to teach.”
- “Once learned, behavior is permanent.”

### Weekly Research/Data Lab
**Learning Experiment:** compare feedback conditions across acquisition and delayed retention. Students identify the hypothesis, independent variable, dependent variables, comparison conditions, performance trend, alternative explanations, limitations, and justified conclusion.

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Learning in Everyday Life — Evidence-Based Behavior Analysis**

Students analyze three fictional or public behaviors using observable evidence, possible classical/operant/observational processes, cognitive factors, alternative explanations, ethical considerations, and a conclusion proportional to the evidence.

### Weekly Knowledge Check
10 questions:
- 4 core knowledge
- 2 application/scenario
- 2 research/evidence
- 1 graph/data interpretation
- 1 ethics/limitation

Mastery threshold: **80%**. Below mastery: targeted review, correction, and a parallel-form retake.
