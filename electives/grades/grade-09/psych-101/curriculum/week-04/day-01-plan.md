# Week 04 · Day 01
## Classical Conditioning

**Essential Question:** How can one event acquire meaning because it reliably predicts another?

### Learning Objectives
- Distinguish unconditioned and conditioned stimuli and responses.
- Explain acquisition as learning a predictive relationship.
- Explain extinction without treating it as simple deletion.
- Identify spontaneous recovery, generalization, and discrimination in new examples.
- Evaluate whether a classical-conditioning explanation is actually supported by evidence.

### Vocabulary in Context
classical conditioning · unconditioned stimulus · unconditioned response · neutral stimulus · conditioned stimulus · conditioned response · acquisition · extinction · spontaneous recovery · generalization · discrimination

### Core Concept
Students move beyond memorizing US/UR/CS/CR labels. The central question is: **What relationship did the learner come to expect?** Timing, predictiveness, and prior experience matter. Repeated coincidence alone is not sufficient evidence of strong conditioning.

### Worked Example
Before learning: food → salivation. During learning: tone + food → salivation. After learning: tone → conditioned salivation. The tone acquires predictive meaning.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate the claim: **“If someone reacts emotionally to something, they must have consciously decided to associate it with a bad experience.”**

Required response: classical conditioning can contribute without deliberate choice, but emotional responses can also involve biological, cognitive, developmental, and situational factors.

### Data Lens
Graph a fictional acquisition curve across eight trials and a later extinction curve. Identify the fastest period of change, any plateau, and the evidence that the learned response weakened during extinction.

### Guided Investigation
For six fictional scenarios identify US, UR, CS, CR, the evidence that learning occurred, and one alternative explanation.

### Independent Assignment
Write two original classical-conditioning cases. One must show generalization; the other must show discrimination. Explain why each example fits the evidence.
