# Week 04 · Day 02
## Operant Conditioning

**Essential Question:** How do consequences influence whether behavior becomes more or less likely?

### Learning Objectives
- Define reinforcement and punishment by their effects on future behavior.
- Distinguish positive from negative procedures without confusing negative with bad.
- Use Antecedent → Behavior → Consequence analysis.
- Identify when an apparent reward did not actually function as reinforcement.
- Evaluate ethical limits on behavior-change strategies.

### Vocabulary in Context
operant conditioning · reinforcement · punishment · positive reinforcement · negative reinforcement · positive punishment · negative punishment · antecedent · consequence

### Core Concept
Reinforcement increases future behavior; punishment decreases it. Positive means something is added. Negative means something is removed. The classification depends partly on what happens to future behavior—not on whether an observer thinks the consequence looks pleasant or unpleasant.

### Worked Example
A student fastens a seat belt and the warning sound stops. If seat-belt fastening becomes more likely, removal of the unpleasant sound can function as **negative reinforcement**.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate: **“Giving someone something they like is always positive reinforcement.”**

Required reasoning: if the behavior does not increase, the consequence did not function as reinforcement for that behavior under those conditions.

### Data Lens
Compare participation frequency before and after a consequence across four sessions. Determine whether behavior increased and state whether the evidence is experimental or merely observational.

### Guided Investigation
Classify twelve fictional examples using the four consequence relationships. For each, identify antecedent, behavior, consequence, and later behavior change.

### Ethics Lens
For any proposed behavior-change strategy ask: Is the goal legitimate? Is autonomy respected? Is the consequence proportionate? Could fear, avoidance, or inequity result? Is a constructive replacement behavior being taught?

### Independent Assignment
Analyze one fictional school or household routine using Antecedent → Behavior → Consequence and propose one ethical modification.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Could consequences or environmental contingencies plausibly maintain or change the behavior in the semester research question? State what evidence would be needed.

### Academic Journal
Why is a consequence's effect more scientifically important than whether it looks like a reward or punishment?
