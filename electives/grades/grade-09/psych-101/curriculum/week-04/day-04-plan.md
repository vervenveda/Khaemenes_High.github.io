# Week 04 · Day 04
## Observational Learning, Modeling & Cognition

**Essential Question:** How can people learn without directly receiving the consequence themselves?

### Learning Objectives
- Define observational learning and modeling.
- Distinguish seeing a behavior from learning it and from performing it.
- Explain attention, retention, production, and motivation as conditions affecting modeled behavior.
- Explain vicarious consequences and expectations.
- Evaluate social-learning claims without assuming automatic imitation.

### Vocabulary in Context
observational learning · modeling · imitation · vicarious consequence · attention · retention · production · motivation · expectation

### Core Concept
People can acquire skills, strategies, norms, emotional responses, and expectations by observing others. Observation alone does not guarantee performance. The learner must attend to relevant information, remember it, be able to produce the behavior, and have some reason to enact it.

### Worked Example
A student watches a peer demonstrate a note-taking strategy. The student may learn the procedure even without immediately using it. Later use depends on memory, perceived usefulness, ability, goals, and consequences.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate the claim: **“People simply copy whatever they see.”**

Students identify the missing roles of attention, retention, ability, motivation, context, expectations, and observed consequences.

### Data Lens
Interpret a fictional four-condition study: no demonstration, model demonstration, model + positive consequence, model + negative consequence. Identify the independent variable, dependent variable, strongest condition, one pattern, and one limitation.

### Guided Investigation
Complete a Modeling Audit across fictional examples from sports, art, music, school, media, and family routines. For each identify the model, attention cue, what must be retained, ability demands, visible consequences, inferred expectations, and whether imitation actually occurred.

### Media Reasoning
Evaluate the broad claim **“violent media automatically makes viewers violent.”** Students refine the claim by specifying exposure, measured behavior, population, duration, research design, and alternative variables.

### Independent Assignment
Choose one public or fictional example of observational learning and explain why the evidence supports learning, performance, both, or neither.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Could models, peers, observed consequences, or social norms contribute to the semester research question? State what evidence would distinguish observational learning from other explanations.

### Academic Journal
