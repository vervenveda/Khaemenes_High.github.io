# Week 04 · Day 05
## Learning Systems, Applications & Ethical Behavior Change

**Essential Question:** How can we determine which learning process best explains behavior—and use that knowledge responsibly?

### Learning Objectives
- Compare classical, operant, and observational learning by the relationships each explains.
- Integrate cognitive expectations into learning analysis.
- Evaluate competing explanations using evidence rather than surface similarity.
- Design an ethical behavior-change proposal.
- Distinguish immediate performance from durable learning.

### Vocabulary in Context
classical conditioning · operant conditioning · observational learning · expectation · cognitive process · intervention · retention · transfer · ethical application

### Model Comparison
- Classical conditioning: **stimulus predicts stimulus**.
- Operant conditioning: **behavior has consequences**.
- Observational learning: **information comes from observing models and outcomes**.
- Cognitive processes can influence all three through prediction, interpretation, attention, memory, and goals.

### Worked Synthesis
A fictional student avoids public speaking. Possible contributors include an earlier unpleasant association, immediate relief following avoidance, observed peer ridicule, expectations of failure, and the current classroom climate. The correct task is not to force one theory onto the behavior; it is to identify what evidence would distinguish the explanations.

### Claims Laboratory
Evaluate: **“Once a behavior is learned, it is permanent.”**

Students consider extinction, changing contingencies, new learning, changing expectations, changing environments, and the possibility that older learning can sometimes reappear.

### Weekly Research/Data Lab
Compare two fictional feedback schedules across acquisition and delayed retention. Identify the independent variable, immediate-performance pattern, delayed-retention pattern, one alternative explanation, and one limitation. Explicitly distinguish **performance during practice** from **long-term learning**.

### Ethical Behavior-Change Lab
A school wants to improve punctuality. Students audit the goal, measurement, reinforcement plan, fairness, transportation/disability barriers, autonomy, alternatives, and evaluation method.

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Learning in Everyday Life — Evidence-Based Behavior Analysis**

Analyze three fictional or public behaviors. For each identify the observable behavior, plausible learning processes, cognitive factors, evidence needed, an alternative explanation, an ethical consideration, and the best-supported conclusion.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Compare all Week 4 learning explanations for the semester question. Retain only those supported by a plausible mechanism and measurable evidence.

### Academic Journal
What is the difference between finding a learning explanation that fits a story and finding one that is actually supported by evidence?
