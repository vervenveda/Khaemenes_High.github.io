# WEEK 09 — Personality & Individual Differences
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How can psychologists describe relatively stable individual differences without reducing a person to a label?

## Intellectual Anchor

> **A personality description summarizes patterns; it does not reveal a person's entire identity, destiny, motives, or diagnosis.**

Week 9 teaches personality as a scientific problem of description, measurement, stability, variability, and context. Students compare trait approaches, temperament, self-concept, values, person–situation interaction, culture and environment, and the limits of self-report measurement.

The unit deliberately rejects personality typing as destiny. Students should leave able to describe patterns cautiously, compare measures, interpret distributions, recognize within-person variability, and distinguish trait description from causal explanation or clinical diagnosis.

## Week 9 Intellectual Spine

| Strand | Student understanding |
|---|---|
| Personality | Relatively enduring patterns in thinking, feeling, and behavior can be studied scientifically |
| Traits | Traits describe dimensions rather than sealed personality boxes |
| Big Five | Broad trait dimensions can organize personality research without defining a whole person |
| Temperament | Early-appearing behavioral tendencies may contribute to later personality but do not determine it |
| Person–situation interaction | Behavior reflects both individual tendencies and situational conditions |
| Self-concept | People's beliefs about themselves can influence choices and interpretation |
| Values | Priorities and principles can guide decisions but should not be confused with personality traits |
| Stability and change | Personality can show continuity while also changing across development and context |
| Measurement | Self-report, informant report, behavioral observation, and repeated measures each reveal different evidence |
| Reliability and validity | A personality measure must be consistent and support the interpretation being made |
| Response bias | Social desirability, reference-group effects, memory, wording, and self-presentation can affect self-report |
| Culture and context | Social expectations and cultural settings influence trait expression and interpretation |
| Individual variation | Group averages and trait scores do not predict every action by an individual |
| Ethics | Personality information should not be used casually to rank, diagnose, shame, or stereotype people |

## Week 9 Research/Data Lab

### Trait Stability and Situational Variability

Students analyze fictional repeated-measure data for the same participants across several contexts. They calculate or interpret means, compare within-person and between-person variation, and decide which conclusions are justified.

Required reasoning:
- What is the trait measure?
- What is the observed behavior measure?
- Is the pattern consistent across situations?
- Which participants vary most across context?
- Does a group-level correlation predict an individual's exact behavior?
- What additional evidence would strengthen the conclusion?

## Week 9 Culminating Task

### Personality Without Labels — Evidence-Based Profile Analysis

Students analyze a fictional profile using multiple sources of evidence. The task must include:

1. observable behavior patterns;
2. trait-language description using dimensions rather than types;
3. at least two situations in which behavior differs;
4. one possible temperament contribution;
5. one self-concept or values factor;
6. one cultural or environmental influence;
7. one measurement limitation;
8. one alternative explanation;
9. one conclusion that is justified;
10. one conclusion that would be inappropriate because it becomes diagnosis, destiny, or stereotyping.

Students do **not** profile classmates, family members, or themselves unless an optional private self-reflection is explicitly chosen. The assessed task uses fictional material.

## Permanent Week 9 Guardrails

### Trait ≠ type
Traits are dimensions; people vary by degree rather than fitting perfectly into boxes.

### Description ≠ cause
A trait label summarizes a pattern. Repeating the label does not explain why the behavior occurs.

### Score ≠ person
A questionnaire score is one measurement under particular conditions.

### Stability ≠ permanence
Personality can show continuity without being fixed forever.

### Situation ≠ fakery
Behavior can legitimately vary with roles, goals, norms, relationships, and environments.

### Group pattern ≠ individual prediction
A population trend does not reveal exactly how one person will behave.

### Personality ≠ diagnosis
This course does not train students to diagnose personality disorders or clinical conditions.
