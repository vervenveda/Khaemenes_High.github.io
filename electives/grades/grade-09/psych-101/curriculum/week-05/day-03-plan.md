# Week 05 · Day 03
## Retrieval, Forgetting & the Science of Studying

**Essential Question:** Why do people forget, and what kinds of practice create durable learning?

### Learning Objectives
- Explain that forgetting can reflect weak encoding, retrieval failure, interference, or source problems.
- Distinguish proactive from retroactive interference.
- Interpret a serial-position curve.
- Compare retrieval practice, spacing, elaboration, interleaving, and feedback.
- Distinguish immediate performance from durable learning.

### Vocabulary in Context
retrieval · forgetting · proactive interference · retroactive interference · primacy · recency · retrieval practice · spacing · interleaving · feedback

### Core Concept
Forgetting is not one process, and poor recall does not automatically mean information has been erased. Retrieval depends on how information was encoded, what cues are available, and what competing memories are active.

### Worked Example
An old password can interfere with a newly learned password, while a new password can make the old one harder to retrieve. These examples illustrate different directions of interference.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate: **“Cramming works because I can remember everything the next morning.”**

Students distinguish short-term performance from long-term retention and ask which outcome the study strategy is supposed to optimize.

### Data Lens
Interpret a fictional serial-position curve and identify evidence for primacy and recency. Then compare massed rereading, spaced review, and retrieval practice on immediate and one-week delayed tests.

### Guided Investigation
Design a one-week study plan for another subject using retrieval practice, spacing, elaboration, interleaving where appropriate, and feedback. Explain why each feature is included.

### Independent Assignment
Audit six common study strategies. Rank them for a specific learning goal and justify the ranking using evidence rather than preference.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
If participants must learn or remember something in the semester question, decide when performance should be measured: immediately, after a delay, or both. Explain what each timing choice would reveal.

### Academic Journal
Why can a strategy that feels harder during practice sometimes produce stronger delayed learning?
