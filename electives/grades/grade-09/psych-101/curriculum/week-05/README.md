# WEEK 05 — Memory & Language
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How do people encode, store, reconstruct, retrieve, and communicate information?

### Intellectual Anchor
> Memory is not a perfect recording, and language is not a transparent copy of thought. Both are active psychological processes shaped by attention, prior knowledge, context, experience, and retrieval conditions.

### Daily Sequence
1. **Encoding, Attention & Working Memory** — attention, working-memory limits, rehearsal, elaboration, chunking, and cognitive load.
2. **Long-Term Memory Systems & Organization** — episodic, semantic, procedural, retrieval cues, recognition, recall, schemas, and organization.
3. **Retrieval, Forgetting & the Science of Studying** — interference, serial-position effects, retrieval practice, spacing, elaboration, interleaving, and feedback.
4. **Reconstructive Memory, Misinformation & Source Monitoring** — reconstruction, leading information, source confusion, confidence, and accuracy.
5. **Language, Meaning & Communication** — language structure, development, multilingualism, language and thought, ambiguity, and measurement wording.

### Claims Laboratory — Locked Set

### Weekly Research/Data Lab
**Which Study Method Creates Durable Memory?** Compare massed review, spaced review, and retrieval practice on immediate and delayed performance. Students distinguish practice performance from durable retention and propose a replication or follow-up study.

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Memory Investigation — From Recording Myth to Reconstructive Science**

Target: 700–1,000 words. Students analyze encoding, memory organization, retrieval, expectations/schema, later information, source monitoring, confidence, evidence, limitations, and real-world application.

### Permanent Boundaries
- familiarity ≠ mastery
- remembered ≠ recorded
- confident ≠ certainly correct
- forgotten ≠ necessarily erased
- language influence ≠ language imprisonment
- multilingual ≠ confused
- group finding ≠ individual conclusion
