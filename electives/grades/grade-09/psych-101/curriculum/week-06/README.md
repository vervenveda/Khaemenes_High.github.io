# WEEK 06 — Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How do people reason, solve problems, judge evidence, and evaluate their own thinking?

### Intellectual Anchor
> A thought can feel convincing without being correct. Good thinking includes strategies for checking how we reached a conclusion.

### Daily Sequence
1. **Concepts, Categories & Problem Solving** — concepts, prototypes, algorithms, heuristics, subgoals, mental set, fixation, and insight.
2. **Heuristics, Probability & Judgment** — availability, representativeness, base rates, anchoring, probability, and intuitive judgment.
3. **Confirmation Bias, Framing & Decision-Making** — disconfirming evidence, belief perseverance, gain/loss frames, risk, and choice architecture.
4. **Intelligence & Psychological Measurement** — intelligence as a construct, standardized testing, norms, reliability, validity, score interpretation, fairness, and historical misuse.
5. **Metacognition, Calibration & the Critical Thinking Lab** — confidence, accuracy, uncertainty, revision, and structured evidence auditing.

### Claims Laboratory — Locked Set
- “If a solution feels obvious, it must be correct.”
- “Gut instinct is always irrational.”
- “People only notice evidence that supports them.”
- “Facts speak for themselves.”
- “An intelligence score measures a person's total intelligence.”
- “A reliable test must be valid.”
- “Knowing about cognitive biases makes you immune to them.”
- “High confidence means high accuracy.”

### Weekly Research/Data Lab
**Decision-Making Under Different Frames:** compare gain-frame and loss-frame conditions, identify variables and assignment procedures, interpret choice percentages, state a justified conclusion, and identify one ethical concern in real-world use.

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Critical Thinking Audit**

Students analyze three fictional or public psychological claims using claim precision, assumptions, current evidence, disconfirming evidence, bias risk, alternative explanations, framing, confidence, limitations, and a conclusion proportional to evidence.

### Weekly Knowledge Check
10 questions:
- 4 core knowledge
- 2 application/scenario
- 2 research/evidence
- 1 data interpretation
- 1 limitation/fairness

Mastery threshold: **80%**, with targeted correction and a parallel-form retake when needed.

### Permanent Boundaries
- shortcut ≠ stupidity
- bias ≠ deliberate dishonesty
- confidence ≠ accuracy
- intelligence score ≠ entire person
- reliability ≠ validity
- group comparison ≠ individual destiny
- better thinking ≠ never being wrong
