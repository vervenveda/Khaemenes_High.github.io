# Week 06 · Day 04
## Intelligence & Psychological Measurement

**Essential Question:** What does an intelligence score measure—and what does it not tell us about a person?

### Learning Objectives
- Explain intelligence as a psychological construct rather than a directly visible trait.
- Describe standardized testing, norms, and percentile interpretation.
- Distinguish reliability from validity.
- Explain why cognitive performance depends partly on context and opportunity.
- Identify historical and ethical risks in overinterpreting intelligence measures.

### Vocabulary in Context
intelligence · construct · cognitive ability · standardized test · norm · percentile · reliability · validity · standardization · score interpretation

### Core Concept
Intelligence tests provide information about performance on defined cognitive tasks under standardized conditions. A score is not an entire person, does not measure every human capability, and must be interpreted within the instrument’s reliability, validity, reference group, language demands, and purpose.

### Conceptual Models
Students receive an introductory overview of general cognitive ability alongside more specific abilities such as verbal, spatial, quantitative, processing, and memory-related performance. The course does not treat one model as a complete definition of human ability.

### Worked Example
A percentile indicates how a score compares with a reference group; it is not the percentage of test questions answered correctly.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
Evaluate: **“An IQ score tells you how smart a person is.”**

Students replace the statement with precise measurement language and identify what the score cannot establish by itself.

### Data Lens
Compare repeated scores from two fictional tests. One produces consistent scores across occasions; another fluctuates widely. Identify which appears more reliable, then explain why reliability alone does not establish validity.

### Fairness & Context Case
Evaluate a fictional test being used with students whose language background differs from the population used during test development. Ask what the test measures, whether language demands affect performance, whether the use has validation evidence, and what conclusion would be too strong.

### Historical/Ethical Boundary
Students learn that psychological tests have sometimes been used with inappropriate generalization, inequitable assumptions, or discriminatory policy. The lesson is neither “testing is useless” nor “scores perfectly reveal ability.” Responsible use requires evidence, fairness, and fit between tool and purpose.

### Independent Assignment
Write a 400–600 word measurement audit of a fictional cognitive assessment using construct definition, reliability, validity, norm group, context, and limits.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
Does the semester question use a construct requiring a standardized measure? If so, what reliability and validity evidence would be needed for the intended interpretation?

### Academic Journal
Why can a consistent score still fail to measure what a researcher claims it measures?
