# WEEK 07 — Emotion & Motivation
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** Why do people feel, choose, and persist?

### Intellectual Anchor
> Emotion provides information, motivation organizes direction and effort, and neither automatically determines behavior. People interpret situations, experience changing physiological and emotional states, pursue goals, and make choices within biological, cognitive, social, and cultural contexts.

### Unified Week Model
**Situation → Appraisal → Emotion → Motivation → Choice → Consequence**

This model is a reasoning scaffold, not a claim that every psychological event follows one rigid sequence. Students should use it to identify where evidence exists, where inference begins, and what alternative processes might also contribute.

### Daily Sequence
1. **What Is Emotion?** — components of emotion, arousal, appraisal, expression, and measurement.
2. **Appraisal, Expression & Regulation** — why similar events can produce different emotional responses and how regulation strategies differ.
3. **Motivation, Needs & Goals** — intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, incentives, autonomy, competence, relatedness, and goal structure.
4. **Self-Efficacy, Persistence & Feedback** — expectations, challenge, feedback, habits, and why persistence is not simply willpower.
5. **Emotion–Motivation Systems** — integrate emotion, goals, context, choice, consequences, and ethical application.

### Permanent Claims Laboratory Set
- “Emotions are irrational and should be ignored.”
- “If two people feel differently about the same event, one of them must be wrong.”
- “You can tell exactly what someone feels from a facial expression.”
- “Rewards always increase motivation.”
- “If you want something badly enough, you can achieve anything.”
- “Lack of persistence proves laziness.”

### Data Literacy
Students interpret:
- multi-component emotion measurements
- self-report versus physiological measures
- condition comparisons involving appraisal
- motivation/persistence data across task conditions
- feedback and self-efficacy trends

### Research Notebook Progression
- identify emotional or motivational variables only if relevant
- operationalize emotion without treating one measure as the emotion itself
- consider appraisal as a mediator
- identify incentives, goals, or environmental constraints
- distinguish motivation from opportunity and performance

### Weekly Mastery Task
**Why Do People Do What They Do? — Emotion & Motivation Analysis**

Students analyze a fictional case using the unified week model and must distinguish observed behavior from inferred emotion, motivation, goals, and context. They must identify evidence, alternative explanations, limitations, and an ethical boundary.

### Weekly Assessment
10 questions:
- 4 core knowledge
- 2 application/scenario
- 2 research/evidence
- 1 graph/data interpretation
- 1 ethics/limitation/context

Mastery threshold: **80%**. Below mastery: targeted review, correction, and parallel-form retake.

### Boundaries
- emotion is not diagnosis
- physiological arousal does not reveal a single emotion by itself
- facial expression does not permit mind-reading
- motivation is not equivalent to moral worth
- performance reflects more than motivation alone
- group patterns do not define an individual
- no required private emotional disclosure
