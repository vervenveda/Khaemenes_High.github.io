# Week 07 · Day 01
## What Is Emotion?

**Week Unit:** Emotion & Motivation

**Essential Question:** What makes an emotion more than a feeling word?

### Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:
- describe emotion as a multi-component psychological process rather than a single internal feeling;
- distinguish physiological arousal, subjective experience, appraisal, expression, and action tendency;
- explain why no single measure perfectly captures an emotion;
- separate direct observation from inference about another person's emotional state;
- compare self-report, behavioral, and physiological measures.

### Vocabulary in Context
emotion · physiological arousal · subjective experience · appraisal · expression · action tendency · self-report · behavioral measure · physiological measure

### Core Reading Architecture
Emotion can involve several interacting components:
1. changes in physiological state;
2. interpretation or appraisal of a situation;
3. subjective experience;
4. expressive behavior;
5. tendencies toward action.

These components can occur together without being identical. An increased heart rate can occur during fear, excitement, exercise, embarrassment, anticipation, or other states. A facial expression can provide information but does not reveal a complete private experience.

### Worked Example
A student waits backstage before performing.

**Observed:** pacing, faster breathing, repeated checking of notes.

**Possible interpretations:** nervousness, excitement, concentration, physical exertion, or a mixture.

Scientific conclusion: the observations are compatible with several emotional interpretations. More evidence would be needed.

### Psychological Scientist's Desk
**Claim:** “Your body tells researchers exactly which emotion you are feeling.”

Audit:
- What is measured?
- Which emotional states could produce similar physiological changes?
- What additional evidence would strengthen interpretation?
- What conclusion would be too strong?

### Data Lens — Multiple Measures
Fictional data from four participants before a speech:

| Participant | Heart-rate change | Self-reported arousal (1–10) | Performance accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | +18 | 8 | 91% |
| B | +17 | 4 | 94% |
| C | +7 | 7 | 85% |
| D | +5 | 3 | 93% |

Students identify why heart-rate change and reported arousal are related imperfectly and why neither measure alone establishes a specific emotion.

### Claims Laboratory
**Claim:** “Emotions are irrational and should be ignored.”

Target judgment: **Unsupported as a universal claim.** Emotions can supply information about goals, threats, values, and social situations, while emotional reactions can also be incomplete or misleading. Good decision-making can incorporate emotion without treating it as unquestionable evidence.

### Guided Investigation — Emotion Measurement Lab
Students receive five fictional situations and propose:
- one self-report measure;
- one behavioral measure;
- one physiological measure;
- one limitation for each;
- the strongest conclusion the combined evidence could support.

### Independent Assignment
**Emotion Is a System.** Create a one-page analysis of a fictional emotional event using:
Situation → Appraisal → Physiological Change → Subjective Experience → Expression → Possible Action.

Students must label which elements are observed and which are inferred.

### Research Notebook Checkpoint
If emotion is relevant to the semester research question:
- define the emotional construct precisely;
- identify at least two possible measures;
- explain why neither measure is identical to the emotion itself.

If emotion is not relevant, state why it should currently be excluded.

### Academic Journal
What is one difference between *feeling certain* about another person's emotion and *having evidence* about that emotion?

No private emotional disclosure is required.

### Daily Review Blueprint
5 formative questions:
- 2 core concepts
- 1 observation-vs-inference scenario
- 1 multi-measure data question
- 1 limitation question

### Completion Standard
Completion requires the core reading, data analysis, guided investigation, assignment, Research Notebook checkpoint, and daily review.
