# Week 10 · Day 05
## Culture, Context & the Social Influence Report

**Week Unit:** Social & Cultural Psychology

**Essential Question:** How can social and cultural psychology explain behavior while preserving individual variation and responsibility?

## Learning Objectives
Students can:
- explain culture as a changing system of practices, meanings, institutions, histories, and norms rather than a personality label;
- identify economic, environmental, institutional, and historical context as possible psychological influences;
- compare person-level and social-context explanations;
- avoid ecological fallacies and cultural stereotyping;
- synthesize social influence evidence into a cautious written conclusion.

## Vocabulary in Context
**culture · norm · institution · socialization · context · ecological fallacy · social environment · structural factor · individual agency · generalization**

## Core Reading
Culture influences behavior through learned practices, expectations, language, institutions, social roles, opportunities, and meanings. It is not a fixed package carried identically by every person in a group.

The same psychological behavior can have different meanings in different settings. Silence can signal respect, uncertainty, disagreement, fear, concentration, or simply a conversational norm. Researchers therefore need context before interpreting behavior.

Economic and institutional conditions can also affect psychological outcomes by shaping opportunity, stress exposure, social networks, incentives, and available choices. These influences are psychological context, not excuses for reducing people to circumstances.

A strong social explanation preserves both context and agency:

> **People make choices inside environments that alter information, incentives, constraints, risks, and opportunities.**

## Worked Example
A fictional comparison finds that students in School A speak more often during class discussion than students in School B.

Possible explanations include:
- classroom participation norms;
- teacher questioning style;
- class size;
- grading incentives;
- language comfort;
- peer response;
- selection differences.

The data do not justify concluding that one group is “more confident” without additional evidence.

## Psychological Scientist's Desk
### Claim
“People from the same culture behave the same way.”

Judgment: **Unsupported stereotype.**

Students identify within-group variation, overlapping distributions, changing contexts, multiple identities, and differences in experience.

## Data Lens — Context Comparison
Fictional participation data:

| Condition | Mean voluntary comments per class |
|---|---:|
| Anonymous written response | 6.8 |
| Small-group discussion | 5.9 |
| Whole-class discussion | 3.1 |

Students ask:
- Does the behavior change by setting?
- What does this imply about interpreting “participation” as a stable personal trait?
- Which design would help distinguish confidence from response-format effects?

## Claims Laboratory
**Claim:** “Culture causes people to behave a certain way.”

Judgment: **Too broad.**

Students rewrite it into a measurable claim naming a specific norm, institution, practice, or social condition.

## Guided Investigation
### Context Matters Lab
Students analyze four fictional behaviors across two social settings. For each they identify:
1. observable behavior;
2. possible norm;
3. person-level explanation;
4. situational explanation;
5. cultural/institutional factor;
6. evidence needed;
7. conclusion that would stereotype.

## Independent Assignment — Week 10 Culminating Task
### Social Influence Report
Students analyze one fictional or public non-sensitive social situation using:
1. observable behavior;
2. relevant social norm;
3. possible informational influence;
4. possible normative influence;
5. person-level explanation;
6. situation-level explanation;
7. group process, if relevant;
8. cultural/institutional context;
9. evidence needed to distinguish explanations;
10. one ethical limitation;
11. one conclusion that explains behavior without excusing harm.

## Research/Data Lab — Norm Information and Behavior
Students analyze a fictional experiment in which participants receive different information about peer behavior.

Required analysis:
- hypothesis;
- independent variable;
- dependent variable;
- comparison condition;
- result pattern;
- alternative explanation;
- ethical issue;
- justified causal statement;
- limitation on generalization.

## Research Notebook Checkpoint
Add the Week 10 social-context audit:
- Is social influence relevant to the semester question?
- Which social variable is actually being proposed?
- Who is represented in the sample?
- Who may be missing?
- What person-level explanation competes with the social explanation?
- What cultural, institutional, or historical context could alter the result?
- Am I applying a group pattern to an individual?
