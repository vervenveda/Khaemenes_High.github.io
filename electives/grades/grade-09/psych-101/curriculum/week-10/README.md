# WEEK 10 — Social & Cultural Psychology
## STATUS: LOCKED

**Central Question:** How do groups, relationships, culture, and social environments influence behavior without erasing individual agency?

## Intellectual Anchor

> **Social context changes probabilities, pressures, information, and opportunities; it does not turn people into puppets.**

Week 10 studies how other people, groups, norms, roles, social identities, culture, and environments influence behavior. Students learn to distinguish social influence from personal disposition, understand conformity and attribution, analyze cooperation and group processes, examine stereotypes and discrimination scientifically, and interpret cultural findings without stereotyping individuals.

A permanent ethical distinction is carried throughout the week:

> **Understanding why harmful behavior occurs does not excuse the harm.**

## Week 10 Intellectual Spine

| Strand | Student understanding |
|---|---|
| Social influence | Behavior can change in response to other people, expectations, and perceived norms |
| Conformity | People may align with groups for informational or normative reasons |
| Informational influence | Others can be treated as evidence about what is true or appropriate |
| Normative influence | People may conform to gain acceptance or avoid social cost |
| Attribution | People explain behavior using personal and situational causes, often imperfectly |
| Person–situation reasoning | Social explanations require attention to both disposition and context |
| Groups | Roles, norms, coordination, status, and shared goals can influence behavior |
| Cooperation & conflict | Interdependence and incentives can shape group outcomes |
| Bystander processes | Helping depends on noticing, interpreting, responsibility, ability, and context |
| Social identity | Group memberships can become psychologically meaningful without determining the individual |
| Stereotypes | Generalized beliefs about groups can be inaccurate, overgeneralized, or misapplied |
| Prejudice | Attitudes or evaluations toward groups should be distinguished from stereotypes |
| Discrimination | Unequal behavior or treatment should be distinguished from belief and attitude |
| Culture | Shared practices, meanings, institutions, histories, and norms can shape behavior |
| Context | Economic, environmental, institutional, and historical conditions matter |
| Ethics | Explanation must not become justification, stereotyping, or dehumanization |

## Claims Laboratory — Locked Set

- “People conform because they are weak.” → **Unsupported moral interpretation.**
- “If many people believe something, it is probably true.” → **Cannot be inferred from popularity alone.**
- “Behavior shows what kind of person someone really is.” → **Too strong without situational evidence.**
- “People in groups lose all individual responsibility.” → **Incorrect.**
- “If nobody helps, nobody cared.” → **Too strong; bystander behavior has multiple possible processes.**
- “A stereotype is accurate if it describes an average difference.” → **Incorrect as an individual inference.**
- “People from the same culture behave the same way.” → **Unsupported stereotype.**
- “Explaining prejudice excuses it.” → **Incorrect. Explanation and moral/legal evaluation are different questions.**

## Week 10 Research/Data Lab

### Norm Information and Behavior

Students analyze a fictional experiment in which participants receive different information about how peers behave. They identify:
- independent variable;
- dependent variable;
- comparison condition;
- size and direction of the behavioral difference;
- one alternative explanation;
- one ethical issue;
- what the study can and cannot say about long-term behavior.

## Week 10 Culminating Task

### Social Influence Report

Students analyze a fictional or public non-sensitive social situation using:
1. observable behavior;
2. relevant social norm;
3. possible informational influence;
4. possible normative influence;
5. person-level explanation;
6. situational explanation;
7. group process, if relevant;
8. cultural/institutional context;
9. evidence needed to distinguish explanations;
10. one ethical limitation;
11. one conclusion that explains behavior without excusing harm.

Students do not investigate or label classmates, family members, or protected groups in assessed work.

## Permanent Week 10 Guardrails

### Influence ≠ control
Social pressures and information can affect behavior without eliminating choice.

### Explanation ≠ excuse
Understanding causes does not remove responsibility for harmful actions.

### Group average ≠ individual member
Population patterns never automatically describe one person.

### Culture ≠ stereotype
Culture includes changing practices, institutions, histories, values, and contexts; it is not a fixed personality assigned to everyone in a group.

### Stereotype ≠ prejudice ≠ discrimination
Belief, attitude, and behavior must be distinguished.

### Situation ≠ everything
Social context matters, but individual goals, learning, personality, cognition, and biology can also contribute.
