Week 07 · Emotion & Motivation

Emotion–Motivation Systems

How do emotion, appraisal, goals, expectations, and context work together to shape choice?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Emotion–Motivation Systems. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How do emotion, appraisal, goals, expectations, and context work together to shape choice?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

  • integrate emotion and motivation using the week model without treating it as a rigid causal chain;
  • distinguish observed behavior from inferred emotion, motive, intention, and goal;
  • compare multiple plausible explanations for the same behavior;
  • identify evidence needed to distinguish emotional, motivational, cognitive, social, and environmental explanations;
  • apply ethical limits when interpreting another person's behavior.

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Behavior rarely provides a complete explanation of itself. A person may make the same outward choice for different reasons, and different people may respond differently to the same event.

Use the week scaffold:

Situation → Appraisal → Emotion → Motivation → Choice → Consequence

Then interrogate it:

  • Which elements are directly observed?
  • Which are inferred?
  • Could the order differ?
  • Could a previous consequence change later appraisal?
  • Could social context, learning history, opportunity, or habit explain part of the pattern?

Emotion can influence attention, memory, judgment, goals, and behavior. Motivation can influence direction, effort, and persistence. Neither creates an automatic behavioral command.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

A student chooses not to join a competitive debate event.

Possible explanations include:

  • appraisal of the event as threatening;
  • low expectation of success;
  • little intrinsic interest;
  • competing commitments;
  • prior negative learning history;
  • concern about peer evaluation;
  • no transportation home afterward.

The outward choice alone does not reveal which explanation is correct.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim: “If someone avoids a challenge, they are unmotivated.”

Students identify at least five alternatives and specify evidence that could distinguish them.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Fictional dataset:

| Condition | Anticipatory arousal (1–10) | Expected success (1–10) | Choice to participate | Persistence if participating |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 8 | 3 | 31% | 7.2 min |
| B | 7 | 8 | 76% | 15.4 min |
| C | 3 | 4 | 45% | 9.1 min |
| D | 4 | 8 | 82% | 16.0 min |

Students describe relationships without claiming that arousal or expected success alone determines behavior.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Students classify and justify:

  • “Emotions are irrational and should be ignored.”
  • “Facial expression reveals exactly what someone feels.”
  • “Rewards always increase motivation.”
  • “If you want something badly enough, you can achieve anything.”
  • “Lack of persistence proves laziness.”
11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

Students receive three fictional cases. For each they complete:

  1. observable situation;
  2. observed behavior;
  3. possible appraisal;
  4. possible emotion;
  5. possible goal/motive;
  6. self-efficacy or expectancy factor;
  7. environmental opportunity/constraint;
  8. prior learning influence;
  9. evidence needed;
  10. strongest current conclusion;
  11. limitation;
  12. ethical wording.

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Weekly Mastery Task

Include one competing explanation or limitation and one ethical safeguard.

Why Do People Do What They Do? — Emotion & Motivation Analysis

Students analyze one substantial fictional case in 700–1,000 words.

Required sections:

  1. Observation
  2. Situation and Context
  3. Appraisal
  4. Emotion Evidence
  5. Motivation and Goals
  6. Self-Efficacy / Expected Success
  7. Choice and Consequence
  8. Alternative Explanation
  9. Evidence Needed
  10. Limitation
  11. Ethical Boundary
  12. Conclusion

The student must explicitly state which claims are observed, measured, and inferred.

Research/Data Lab

Students compare two fictional conditions that alter task framing or feedback and measure:

  • self-reported emotion;
  • expected success;
  • choice;
  • persistence;
  • performance.

They identify independent/dependent variables, relationships among measures, a possible mediator, alternative explanations, and the strongest justified conclusion.

Week 7 Final Principle

Emotion informs without commanding. Motivation influences without guaranteeing. Behavior must be interpreted through evidence, context, alternatives, and limits.

13 · Research Notebook

Research Notebook Checkpoint

  • Is motivation relevant?
  • Could appraisal mediate an effect?
  • Could expected success or value influence choice?
  • What opportunity or contextual variable must be separated from motivation?
  • Which proposed explanation should be excluded for lack of evidence?
