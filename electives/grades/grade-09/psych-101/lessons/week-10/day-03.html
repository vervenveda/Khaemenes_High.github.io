Essential Question

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students can:

  • explain how roles, norms, status, and shared goals influence group behavior;
  • distinguish cooperation from competition in terms of incentives and interdependence;
  • describe diffusion of responsibility and pluralistic ignorance as possible bystander processes;
  • avoid treating group membership as loss of individual responsibility;
  • analyze group outcomes using measurable variables.

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Groups can coordinate information, distribute tasks, create norms, and support cooperation. They can also create conflict when goals, resources, or incentives compete. Group behavior is therefore not automatically better or worse than individual behavior.

Helping behavior illustrates why social context matters. A person may fail to help because they did not notice the event, interpreted it as non-emergency, assumed someone else was responsible, lacked ability to help safely, or feared creating additional harm. Diffusion of responsibility describes one possible process when responsibility feels distributed across several observers. Pluralistic ignorance can occur when people use others' calm behavior as evidence that intervention is unnecessary.

These processes explain probabilities; they do not prove that people who fail to help are uncaring.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

Several people see someone drop a stack of papers. In one condition a single observer is present; in another, five people are present.

If helping decreases in the larger group, possible explanations include diffusion of responsibility, attention, uncertainty, or local norms. Additional manipulations are needed to identify the process.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

“If nobody helps, nobody cared.”

Judgment: Too strong.

Care is an internal state. Behavior alone does not identify attention, interpretation, perceived responsibility, competence, risk, or emotion.
