Evidence Synthesis & Psychology Research Brief Workshop

How do researchers combine sources without flattening disagreement or exaggerating certainty?

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How do researchers combine sources without flattening disagreement or exaggerating certainty?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

  • distinguish evidence synthesis from source listing;
  • evaluate source relevance, quality, independence, and method;
  • identify converging and conflicting evidence;
  • separate direct evidence from interpretation;
  • construct a claim-evidence-limitations paragraph;
  • prepare a polished research brief with honest visuals and citations.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

primary source

Use it in context: Explain how primary source connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Research synthesis is not “three sources agree, therefore true.” Sources can repeat one another, use the same sample, measure different outcomes, or differ greatly in quality. Students learn to ask whether evidence is independent, relevant, methodologically strong, and actually about the same claim.

A strong research paragraph follows a disciplined structure: Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Alternative/Qualification → Limitation → Proportional Conclusion.

Students also learn that disagreement is information. Good synthesis explains why findings may differ rather than hiding inconvenient results.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

Three sources discuss study habits:

  • one randomized experiment on retrieval practice;
  • one correlational survey on student preferences;
  • one popular article summarizing both.

Students explain why these are not three independent pieces of equally strong evidence.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim: “Five websites say the same thing, so the claim is well established.”

Students audit source independence, original evidence, citation chains, methods, and relevance.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Students choose between two possible graphs for the Research Brief and identify which visual communicates the data honestly. They inspect truncated axes, missing labels, inappropriate 3D effects, and percentage claims without denominators.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Evaluate:

  • “More citations make a stronger argument.”
  • “A review article counts as all of the studies it cites.”
  • “Conflicting studies mean psychology knows nothing.”
  • “A graph is objective because it uses real numbers.”
  • “You should leave out evidence that weakens your conclusion.”

11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Psychology Word of the Day

evidence synthesis — the structured integration of findings from multiple relevant sources while preserving quality differences, uncertainty, and disagreement.

Guided Investigation

Apply Evidence Synthesis & Psychology Research Brief Workshop to a fictional or de-identified case. Separate observation from interpretation, identify the psychological concept that best explains the pattern, compare at least two possible explanations, and state what additional evidence would be needed before favoring one explanation. Record one ethical boundary that limits what can responsibly be inferred.

Source Triangulation Table

For each source students record:

  1. source type;
  2. research question;
  3. sample;
  4. method;
  5. measure;
  6. key result;
  7. limitation;
  8. relationship to other sources;
  9. contribution to the final claim.
