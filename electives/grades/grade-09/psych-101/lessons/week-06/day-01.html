Week 06 · Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making

Concepts, Categories & Problem Solving

How does the mind simplify a complicated world without oversimplifying it too much?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Concepts, Categories & Problem Solving. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How does the mind simplify a complicated world without oversimplifying it too much?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

  • Explain concepts and categories as tools for organizing information.
  • Distinguish a prototype from a rigid category boundary.
  • Compare algorithms and heuristics.
  • Explain fixation, mental set, subgoals, and insight.
  • Evaluate how problem representation can change difficulty.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

cognition

Use it in context: Explain how cognition connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

cognition · concept · category · prototype · algorithm · heuristic · fixation · mental set · insight · subgoal

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Categories make thinking efficient, but a useful category is not a perfect boundary. Problem solving depends on how a task is represented, what prior strategies are available, and whether the thinker can revise an ineffective approach.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

A student repeatedly applies a familiar formula to a novel problem and fails. The difficulty may reflect mental set rather than lack of ability. Re-representing the problem visually or breaking it into subgoals can expose a better path.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Evaluate: “If you are smart enough, every problem should have an obvious solution.”

Students identify the roles of prior knowledge, strategy, fixation, representation, time, and feedback.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Compare fictional completion time and accuracy under trial-and-error, explicit-subgoal, and familiar-method-only conditions. State the pattern and one limitation.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Calibrate this claim: “One observation about Concepts, Categories & Problem Solving tells us everything important about a person.”

11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

Problem Representation Lab: solve or inspect the same underlying problem in numerical, verbal, and visual formats. Compare which representation makes the structure easiest to see and why.

12 · Independent Assignment

Independent Assignment

Write a short problem-solving protocol: define the goal, identify constraints, generate subgoals, test a strategy, monitor results, and revise if needed.

13 · Research Notebook

Research Notebook Checkpoint

Does the semester question contain a category, construct, or judgment that needs a clearer definition? Refine it and identify possible boundary cases.

14 · Academic Journal

Academic Journal Reflection

When can a familiar strategy help problem solving, and when can it become a trap?

Privacy boundary: The journal is academic, not therapeutic. Fictional, public, non-sensitive, or course-supplied examples are always acceptable.
