Week 06 · Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making

Metacognition, Calibration & the Critical Thinking Lab

How can we become better at noticing when our own thinking may be wrong?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Metacognition, Calibration & the Critical Thinking Lab. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How can we become better at noticing when our own thinking may be wrong?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

  • Define metacognition as monitoring and regulating one's own thinking.
  • Explain calibration as the relationship between confidence and accuracy.
  • Distinguish uncertainty from ignorance and revision from failure.
  • Apply a structured critical-thinking audit to psychological claims.
  • State conclusions at a confidence level justified by evidence.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

metacognition

Use it in context: Explain how metacognition connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

metacognition · monitoring · confidence · calibration · uncertainty · revision · cognitive bias · assumption · alternative explanation

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Better thinking does not mean never being wrong. It means monitoring understanding, testing assumptions, comparing confidence with performance, seeking disconfirming evidence, and revising conclusions when stronger evidence appears.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

Use a fictional example to distinguish description from explanation in Metacognition, Calibration & the Critical Thinking Lab.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Evaluate:

  • “Knowing about cognitive biases makes you immune to them.”
  • “High confidence means high accuracy.”

Both claims are unsupported as general rules.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Students answer ten items and record confidence for each answer. They compare confidence levels with actual accuracy, then identify overconfidence, underconfidence, and better-calibrated judgments.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Calibrate this claim: “One observation about Metacognition, Calibration & the Critical Thinking Lab tells us everything important about a person.”

11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

For three fictional or public psychological claims complete:

  1. What exactly is the claim?
  2. What assumptions are embedded in it?
  3. What evidence currently supports it?
  4. What evidence would challenge it?
  5. What alternative explanation exists?
  6. What study or data would help?
  7. What limitation remains?
  8. How confident should we be right now?
11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Weekly Research/Data Lab

Decision-Making Under Different Frames: compare gain-frame and loss-frame conditions, identify independent/dependent variables, evaluate assignment procedures, interpret choice percentages, state a causal conclusion only if justified, and identify an ethical concern if the framing technique were used outside research.

12 · Independent Assignment

Independent Assignment

Critical Thinking Audit — analyze three claims using concepts, assumptions, evidence, bias risk, alternatives, framing, confidence, limitations, and conclusions proportional to the evidence.

13 · Research Notebook

Research Notebook Checkpoint

Add a confidence rating to the current working explanation for the semester question. State what evidence would increase confidence, decrease it, or change the explanation entirely.

14 · Academic Journal

Academic Journal Reflection

What is the difference between being certain and being well calibrated?

Privacy boundary: The journal is academic, not therapeutic. Fictional, public, non-sensitive, or course-supplied examples are always acceptable.
