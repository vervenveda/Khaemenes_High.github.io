Week 06 · Thinking, Intelligence & Decision-Making

Confirmation Bias, Framing & Decision-Making

How can beliefs and presentation influence what evidence people notice and which choices they make?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Confirmation Bias, Framing & Decision-Making. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How can beliefs and presentation influence what evidence people notice and which choices they make?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

  • Define confirmation bias as a tendency affecting search, attention, interpretation, or memory.
  • Explain belief perseverance without treating every stable belief as irrational.
  • Distinguish gain and loss frames.
  • Explain how choice architecture can influence behavior.
  • Practice seeking disconfirming evidence before reaching a conclusion.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

confirmation bias

Use it in context: Explain how confirmation bias connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

confirmation bias · belief perseverance · disconfirming evidence · framing · gain frame · loss frame · risk · choice architecture · decision-making

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Good reasoning does not require having no prior beliefs. It requires making room for evidence that could challenge them. Framing can change which consequences become psychologically salient even when the underlying numerical information is equivalent.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

“90% survival” and “10% mortality” communicate equivalent numerical outcomes but emphasize different aspects of the same result. A good decision-maker translates between frames before judging.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Evaluate: “Facts speak for themselves.”

Facts are not arbitrary, but evidence must still be measured, selected, represented, communicated, and interpreted. Scientific procedures help prevent a preferred explanation from controlling every stage.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Compare fictional choice percentages in survival-frame and mortality-frame conditions. Identify the independent variable, dependent variable, difference between conditions, and the strongest justified conclusion.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Calibrate this claim: “One observation about Confirmation Bias, Framing & Decision-Making tells us everything important about a person.”

11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

Frame Flip Lab: rewrite five claims in equivalent gain and loss frames, then identify what changed psychologically and what remained mathematically constant.

11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Critical Thinking Tool

For four claims write:

  1. supporting evidence,
  2. disconfirming evidence,
  3. an alternative explanation,
  4. what evidence would change your mind.

Ethics Lens

Choice architecture can be used to simplify beneficial decisions, but hidden manipulation, coercion, unfair defaults, and reduced autonomy require ethical scrutiny.
