Week 08 · Human Development

Cognitive & Language Development

How do reasoning, language, and self-regulation change as people develop?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic

Retrieval Warm-Up

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How do reasoning, language, and self-regulation change as people develop?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

  • describe cognitive development as change in attention, memory, reasoning, language, and executive control;
  • explain why stage models can be useful summaries without treating them as rigid universal schedules;
  • identify the roles of social interaction, instruction, practice, and culture in cognitive growth;
  • connect language development with communication, categorization, memory, and social experience;
  • interpret developmental findings without assuming that difference means deficiency.

Vocabulary in Context

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Cognitive development includes changes in how people attend, remember, plan, reason, solve problems, and use language. Some developmental theories organize change into broad stages. Stage models can help describe common patterns, but real development often shows overlap, variability, task effects, and influence from prior knowledge and culture.

Executive functions include processes such as:

  • inhibiting an automatic response;
  • keeping information active in working memory;
  • shifting strategies when circumstances change.

Language development depends on biological capacity, exposure, interaction, memory, learning, and social context. Children do not simply memorize isolated words; they learn patterns, meanings, social uses, and relationships among symbols.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

A young child fails one conservation-style task but succeeds on a similar task with familiar materials and simpler language.

Possible interpretation:

  • reasoning ability may matter;
  • task wording and familiarity may also matter;
  • one failed task should not be treated as a complete measure of cognitive development.
8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim: “Once psychologists know a child's age, they know how that child thinks.”

Students challenge the claim using:

  • individual variation;
  • task demands;
  • language comprehension;
  • schooling;
  • prior experience;
  • culture.
9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Fictional data:

| Age group | Mean correct on inhibition task | Mean reaction time |
|---|---:|---:|
| 7–8 | 71% | 890 ms |
| 10–11 | 82% | 730 ms |
| 13–14 | 89% | 640 ms |
| 16–17 | 93% | 590 ms |

Students describe the group trend, note overlapping performance is still possible, and explain why the table does not determine any individual student's ability.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Claim: “Development happens in exact stages that every person enters at the same age.”

Target judgment: Too rigid. Stage models can summarize broad patterns, but individual development is variable and influenced by context, experience, task demands, and measurement.

11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

Students receive four fictional learning tasks and identify:

  • current skill level;
  • support or scaffold;
  • what the learner must do independently;
  • how support could be reduced over time;
  • what measure would show genuine learning rather than temporary assistance.
12 · Independent Assignment

Independent Assignment

From Support to Independence. Analyze one fictional learning case in 400–550 words. Explain working memory, attention, language demands, scaffolding, practice, and how progress should be measured.

13 · Research Notebook

Research Notebook Checkpoint

Ask:

  • Could developmental differences in language, executive function, or prior knowledge affect my research question?
  • Would the same task mean the same thing at different ages?
  • Does my measure accidentally depend on reading level or language ability?
14 · Academic Journal

Academic Journal Reflection

Why can a task measure several abilities at once even when researchers intend to measure only one?

Privacy boundary: The journal is academic, not therapeutic. Fictional, public, non-sensitive, or course-supplied examples are always acceptable.
