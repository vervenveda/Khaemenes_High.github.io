2 · Essential Question

How are consequences classified?

Reinforcement increases future behavior; punishment decreases it. Positive means add. Negative means remove. None of these terms means good, bad, kind, or cruel by itself.

4 · Psychology Word of the Day

negative reinforcement

Formal definition

A process in which removal or reduction of a stimulus following a behavior increases the future probability of that behavior.

Student-friendly definition

A behavior becomes more likely because something is removed afterward. “Negative” means removal—not punishment.

Antecedent = event or condition before behavior. Behavior = observable action. Consequence = event following behavior that may affect future behavior.

6 · Core Reading

Consequences are defined by function, not appearance

Operant conditioning studies how consequences relate to future behavior. Reinforcement and punishment are functional terms. A consequence is called reinforcement only when the target behavior increases. A consequence is called punishment only when the target behavior decreases.

Positive and negative describe whether something is added or removed. Positive reinforcement adds a consequence and behavior increases. Negative reinforcement removes or reduces a consequence and behavior increases. Positive punishment adds a consequence and behavior decreases. Negative punishment removes a consequence and behavior decreases.

The classic seat-belt example demonstrates negative reinforcement: fastening the belt stops an unpleasant warning sound. If fastening becomes more likely, removal of the sound reinforced the behavior.

Rewards and reinforcers are not identical. A teacher might give stickers because students appear to like them, but if the target behavior does not increase, the stickers did not function as reinforcement for that behavior under those conditions. Likewise, a consequence intended as punishment may accidentally reinforce behavior if it supplies attention or escape that increases future responding.

ABC analysis—Antecedent → Behavior → Consequence—helps organize observations. But it does not prove causation from one event. Baseline data, repeated measurements, comparison conditions, and alternative explanations strengthen conclusions.

Ethics matters. A behavior-change goal should be legitimate and transparent. Strategies should respect autonomy and dignity, avoid unnecessary fear or humiliation, consider disability and environmental barriers, teach constructive alternatives, and evaluate whether the intervention actually helps rather than merely suppressing visible behavior.

Explanation ≠ control: understanding consequences does not justify manipulating people without consent, fairness, or respect.
7 · Worked Example

Seat-belt warning

Antecedent: car starts and warning tone sounds.

Behavior: driver fastens seat belt.

Consequence: warning tone stops.

If fastening becomes more likely, removal of the tone is negative reinforcement.

8 · Psychological Scientist's Desk

Reward claim audit

Claim: “Giving someone something they like is always positive reinforcement.”

Incorrect. Whether the consequence functions as reinforcement depends on whether the target behavior increases. Preference may make reinforcement more plausible, but the behavioral effect defines the term.

10 · Claims Laboratory

“Negative reinforcement means punishment.”

Verdict

Incorrect. Negative reinforcement removes something and behavior increases.

Memory rule

Ask two separate questions: Was something added or removed? Did future behavior increase or decrease?

11 · Guided Investigation

ABC & consequence classification

Analyze at least six fictional examples. For each identify antecedent, behavior, consequence, whether something was added/removed, and whether future behavior increased/decreased.

Ethical behavior analysis
