Classical Conditioning

How can one event acquire meaning because it reliably predicts another?

Classical conditioning is about predictive relationships between stimuli, not mere repetition.

4 · Psychology Word of the Day

conditioned stimulus

Formal definition

A previously neutral stimulus that, after a predictive relationship is learned, elicits a conditioned response.

Student-friendly definition

A cue that gains learned meaning because it predicts another event.

5 · Vocabulary in Context

Classical-conditioning language

US / UR

An unconditioned stimulus naturally elicits an unconditioned response without the relevant learning history.

Neutral stimulus / CS

A cue initially lacking the conditioned meaning can become a conditioned stimulus after learning.

CR

The learned response elicited by the conditioned stimulus.

Acquisition

The period in which the predictive relationship is learned.

Extinction / spontaneous recovery

The conditioned response weakens when the CS no longer predicts the US; an extinguished response may later reappear.

Generalization / discrimination

Generalization spreads responding to similar cues; discrimination distinguishes among cues.

6 · Core Reading

Conditioning is learning about prediction

Classical conditioning occurs when organisms learn that one event predicts another. Before conditioning, an unconditioned stimulus can produce an unconditioned response without the relevant learned association. A neutral stimulus does not initially have that predictive meaning. During acquisition, the neutral cue is paired with or otherwise reliably signals the important event. After learning, the cue becomes a conditioned stimulus capable of eliciting a conditioned response.

Timing and predictiveness matter. Two events simply occurring together does not guarantee strong conditioning. A cue that reliably signals when an event will occur is more informative than a cue that appears randomly whether or not the event happens.

Extinction occurs when the conditioned stimulus repeatedly appears without the event it once predicted and the conditioned response weakens. Extinction should not be described as deleting the original learning. Spontaneous recovery—the reappearance of some conditioned responding after time has passed—shows why that language is too simple.

Generalization allows similar cues to elicit related responses. Discrimination occurs when learning becomes specific enough that one cue predicts the outcome while another does not. Both can be adaptive. Generalization can prepare an organism for similar situations; discrimination prevents responding to every vaguely similar cue.

Classical conditioning can contribute to emotional and physiological responses without deliberate conscious choice. But an emotional response is not proof of conditioning. Biology, appraisal, memory, social context, and other learning processes can also contribute.

Explanation boundary: identifying a possible conditioned response does not diagnose a person or establish the origin of an emotion.
7 · Worked Example

Tone and food

Before learning: food (US) → salivation (UR). Tone = neutral.

During acquisition: tone + food → salivation.

After learning: tone (CS) → conditioned salivation (CR).

The tone now predicts food. The learning is about that relationship.

8 · Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim audit

Claim: “If someone reacts emotionally to something, they consciously decided to associate it with a bad experience.”

Classical conditioning can contribute without deliberate choice. But the observed reaction still does not reveal its full cause. Evidence must distinguish conditioning from biological, cognitive, developmental, and situational alternatives.

10 · Claims Laboratory

“Any repeated pair of events becomes conditioned.”

Verdict

Too broad. Timing, predictiveness, salience, prior learning, and biological constraints matter.

Better statement

Conditioning is stronger when a cue reliably predicts an outcome under conditions that support learning.
