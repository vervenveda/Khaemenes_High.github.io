Sensation & Transduction

How does information from the physical world become information the nervous system can use?

2 · Essential Question

How does the nervous system detect the world?

The environment provides information; sensory systems detect some of it; attention selects some of it; perception organizes and interprets it.
3 · Learning Objectives

By the end of today

4 · Psychology Word of the Day

transduction

Formal definition

The conversion of physical stimulus energy into neural signals that the nervous system can process.

Student-friendly definition

The step where light, sound, pressure, chemicals, or motion become a form of neural information.

Related: sensory receptor · sensation · threshold · neural signal

5 · Vocabulary in Context

Detecting information

Sensation

Detection and initial processing of physical stimulation by sensory systems.

Sensory receptor

A specialized cell or structure responsive to particular forms of physical or chemical energy.

Absolute threshold

The stimulus intensity detected on a defined proportion of trials, often 50%, under specified conditions.

Difference threshold

The smallest difference between stimuli that can be reliably detected under defined conditions.

Sensory adaptation

Reduced responsiveness to a constant or unchanging stimulus over time.

Perception

Organization and interpretation of sensory information into meaningful experience.

6 · Core Reading

Sensory systems sample the world; they do not copy it

Every sensory system begins with physical information. Vision depends on light. Hearing depends on pressure changes traveling as sound waves. Touch depends on mechanical and thermal events at the body. Taste and smell depend on chemical molecules. Vestibular and proprioceptive systems provide information about movement, balance, and body position.

Transduction converts these forms of energy into neural signals. That conversion is selective. Human eyes detect only a narrow band of electromagnetic radiation; ears detect only a limited range of sound frequencies; chemical receptors respond to some molecules and not others. Therefore, not sensing something does not mean it does not exist. It may fall outside the range of the relevant sensory system or below the detection conditions of the moment.

Thresholds are also not hard switches. A faint tone may be detected on some trials and missed on others because attention, background noise, expectation, fatigue, and random variation affect performance. Psychologists therefore define thresholds statistically—for example, the intensity detected on 50% of trials.

A difference threshold concerns noticing change. Whether a change is detectable often depends on the starting intensity. A 1-kilogram change is much more noticeable when lifting 2 kilograms than when lifting 50 kilograms. This principle illustrates that sensory systems respond to proportional as well as absolute differences.

Sensory adaptation occurs when constant stimulation produces reduced responsiveness. A smell may seem strong when entering a room and then fade from awareness. Adaptation is not the same as forgetting or choosing to ignore something. It reflects changes within sensory processing.

Sensation provides input, but perception goes further. The same retinal image can be interpreted differently depending on context; the same sound can be understood differently depending on language knowledge; the same bodily signal can be interpreted differently depending on expectations. Week 3 will build that distinction carefully.

7 · Worked Example

Thresholds are probabilities

A participant hears a very faint tone on 2 of 10 trials at one intensity, 5 of 10 at the next, and 9 of 10 at a stronger level.

The middle intensity may be near the participant's operationally defined absolute threshold under those testing conditions. It is not a permanent boundary of hearing.
8 · Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim audit

Claim: “If humans cannot sense something, it is not really there.”
  1. What kind of physical signal is being discussed?
  2. What sensory system could detect it?
  3. What is the human detection range?
  4. Could instruments detect information beyond that range?
  5. What conclusion is justified?

10 · Claims Laboratory

“Sensory adaptation means the stimulus stopped affecting receptors completely.”

Verdict

Too strong. Responsiveness may decrease, but adaptation varies by sensory system and condition.

Better statement

Continuous stimulation can reduce sensory responsiveness and awareness over time.
