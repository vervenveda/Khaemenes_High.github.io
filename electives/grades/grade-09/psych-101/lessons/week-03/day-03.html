Perception: Building Meaning

How does the brain organize sensory information into meaningful objects and events?

2 · Essential Question

How does perception create stable meaning?

Perception is constrained by sensory information, but it is shaped by organization, context, prior knowledge, and expectation.

4 · Psychology Word of the Day

perceptual constancy

Formal definition

The tendency to perceive important properties of objects as relatively stable despite changes in sensory input.

Student-friendly definition

Your perception keeps objects seeming stable even when their retinal size, angle, lighting, or distance changes.

Related: size constancy · shape constancy · brightness/lightness constancy · context

5 · Vocabulary in Context

Organizing perception

Bottom-up processing

Processing driven strongly by incoming sensory features.

Top-down processing

Processing influenced by expectations, knowledge, goals, and context.

Figure-ground

Organization of a scene into a focal object or figure and its background.

Proximity / similarity

Gestalt grouping tendencies that organize nearby or similar elements together.

Continuity / closure

Tendencies to perceive smooth continuation and complete forms even when information is incomplete.

Retinal disparity / linear perspective

Depth cues based respectively on differences between the two eyes' views and convergence of parallel lines in the visual image.

6 · Core Reading

Perception organizes rather than merely receives

Sensation provides neural information, but the brain must organize that information into objects, locations, events, and meanings. Bottom-up processing begins with features in the stimulus: edges, colors, tones, movements, and other input. Top-down processing uses knowledge, expectations, goals, and context to influence interpretation. Everyday perception usually involves both.

Gestalt psychologists emphasized that people perceive organized wholes, not just isolated sensory pieces. Figure-ground organization helps distinguish focal objects from background. Proximity and similarity support grouping. Continuity favors smooth patterns, and closure can lead us to perceive a complete form even when parts are missing.

Depth perception also combines cues. Retinal disparity uses the slightly different views from two eyes. Linear perspective, texture, overlap, relative size, motion, and other cues help estimate distance. Many depth cues remain useful with one eye, while binocular cues require both.

Perceptual constancy helps maintain a stable world. A person walking away projects a smaller retinal image, yet is not usually perceived as physically shrinking. A door produces very different retinal shapes as it opens, yet we perceive the same rectangular door. These constancies are useful achievements of perception.

Illusions reveal the assumptions perceptual systems usually make. When a visual display creates misleading context, normal perceptual rules can produce an inaccurate judgment. An illusion therefore does not prove the brain is “bad at reality.” It shows that perception uses efficient rules that usually work well but can be fooled under specially arranged conditions.

This leads to an important Week 3 distinction: perception is not identical to raw physical reality, but neither is it arbitrary. It is an active interpretation constrained by the information available and by the biological organization of sensory systems.

7 · Worked Example

Size constancy

Two equally tall people stand at different distances. The farther person's retinal image is smaller, yet the observer usually perceives both as roughly the same physical height.

Why? Distance cues contribute to interpreting retinal size. Perception combines image size with estimated distance.

10 · Claims Laboratory

“Illusions prove our senses are unreliable.”

Verdict

Too strong. Illusions reveal how normal perceptual rules can produce predictable errors under special conditions.

Better statement

Perception is generally useful but can be systematically influenced by context and assumptions.
