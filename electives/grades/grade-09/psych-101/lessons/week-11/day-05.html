Week 11 · Mental & Physical Health Psychology

Resilience, Flourishing & Evaluating Wellness Claims

How can psychologists evaluate claims about well-being without oversimplifying health?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Resilience, Flourishing & Evaluating Wellness Claims. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How can psychologists evaluate claims about well-being without oversimplifying health?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

  • define resilience as adaptation under challenge rather than invulnerability;
  • distinguish flourishing/well-being constructs from absence of disorder;
  • identify protective factors at individual, relational, and environmental levels;
  • evaluate wellness claims for evidence quality, effect size, comparison condition, and generalizability;
  • separate education from individualized treatment advice.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

resilience

Use it in context: Explain how resilience connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

resilience · flourishing · protective factor · risk factor · social connection · recovery · well-being · effect size · generalizability · evidence-based

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Resilience is not a fixed trait possessed by “strong” people. It can depend on relationships, resources, skills, safety, opportunity, community, biology, and the nature of the challenge. People can be resilient while still experiencing distress or needing help.

Students then learn to audit wellness claims. A claim may be plausible without being established. Good evidence requires appropriate measures, meaningful comparison groups, transparent methods, replication, and conclusions that match the size and scope of the observed effect.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

Claim: “A five-minute activity improves well-being.”

Students ask:

  • What is “well-being” operationally?
  • Compared with what?
  • Immediately or later?
  • How large was the effect?
  • Who participated?
  • Was the measure self-report only?
  • Was the result replicated?
8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim: “Natural wellness products are safer because they are natural.”

Students identify the naturalistic fallacy and explain why safety and efficacy require evidence rather than labels.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Students compare two fictional wellness studies:

  • Study A: small uncontrolled convenience sample with a large-looking percentage claim;
  • Study B: larger comparison study with modest but more credible effect.

They identify which study supports the stronger inference and why.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Evaluate:

  • “Resilient people do not need support.”
  • “Well-being means never feeling negative emotion.”
  • “A statistically noticeable difference must be important in real life.”
  • “Testimonials prove a wellness strategy works.”
  • “Natural means safe.”

11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Psychology Word of the Day

resilience — processes of adaptation and recovery in the presence of challenge or adversity.

Guided Investigation

Apply Resilience, Flourishing & Evaluating Wellness Claims to a fictional or de-identified case. Separate observation from interpretation, identify the psychological concept that best explains the pattern, compare at least two possible explanations, and state what additional evidence would be needed before favoring one explanation. Record one ethical boundary that limits what can responsibly be inferred.

Wellness Claim Forensic Audit

Students inspect a fictional advertisement or social-media claim using: Claim → Measure → Evidence → Comparison → Alternative Explanation → Limitation → Conclusion

They label the claim: Supported / Partly Supported / Unsupported / Cannot Tell.

Independent Assignment — Week 11 Culminating Task

Create an evidence brief on Resilience, Flourishing & Evaluating Wellness Claims. Use one course concept, one concrete fictional example or dataset, and one alternative explanation. Explain how the evidence supports the concept, identify a limitation, and end with a conclusion written in probability-aware language rather than a label or diagnosis.

Stress Strategy & Wellness Claim Analysis

Using supplied fictional material, students write 700–1,000 words combining:

  1. stressor and appraisal analysis;
  2. coping strategy comparison;
  3. health-behavior/context analysis;
  4. wellness-claim evidence audit;
  5. one data interpretation;
  6. one ethical boundary;
  7. a proportional conclusion.

No private health disclosure is required.
