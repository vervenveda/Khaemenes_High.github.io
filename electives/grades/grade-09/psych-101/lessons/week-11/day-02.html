Week 11 · Mental & Physical Health Psychology

Stress, Sleep, Health & Cumulative Load

How can repeated demands interact with physiological systems and everyday functioning?

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How can repeated demands interact with physiological systems and everyday functioning?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

  • distinguish acute from chronic stress exposure;
  • explain allostasis and allostatic load at an introductory level;
  • describe reciprocal relationships among stress, sleep, attention, and health behavior;
  • interpret longitudinal-style data without confusing association with diagnosis;
  • identify why group-level health patterns do not determine an individual outcome.
4 · Psychology Word of the Day

Use it in context: Explain how acute connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

The body's adaptive systems are designed to respond and recover. Short-lived activation can be useful. Problems can arise when demands are intense, repeated, prolonged, or combined with limited opportunities for recovery. Students learn allostatic load as a scientific model of cumulative physiological adjustment, not as a label that can be assigned casually to a person.

Sleep is treated as both a biological process and a health behavior shaped by schedules, environment, opportunity, stress, technology use, work, caregiving, and other conditions. The lesson explicitly rejects “sleep is just discipline” explanations.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

A fictional dataset shows that students reporting shorter sleep across several weeks also report more daytime fatigue.

Supported: shorter sleep and fatigue are associated in this sample.

Not established: short sleep is the only cause of fatigue, or every individual with short sleep will report fatigue.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim: “Chronic stress permanently damages everyone in the same way.”

Students identify problems with universality, measurement, mechanism, individual variation, recovery, and causal inference.

9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Students examine a fictional 6-week table containing sleep duration, perceived demand ratings, and attention-task performance. They:

  • graph one variable over time;
  • identify a trend;
  • compare within-person and between-person patterns;
  • identify one possible confound;
  • state one conclusion that the data do not justify.
10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Evaluate:

  • “People who sleep less simply need more discipline.”
  • “A risk factor means an outcome will happen.”

11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Psychology Word of the Day

allostatic load — cumulative physiological burden associated with repeated or prolonged adaptation to demands.

Guided Investigation

Apply Stress, Sleep, Health & Cumulative Load to a fictional or de-identified case. Separate observation from interpretation, identify the psychological concept that best explains the pattern, compare at least two possible explanations, and state what additional evidence would be needed before favoring one explanation. Record one ethical boundary that limits what can responsibly be inferred.

Recovery Window Analysis

Students compare four fictional schedules that differ in demands, sleep opportunity, breaks, social support, and unpredictability. They identify which features might increase or reduce recovery opportunity without diagnosing the fictional people.

Completion Challenge

Optional after completion. Best fit: Focus Catch or Doodle Break.
