Week 05 · Memory & Language

Language, Meaning & Communication

How does language allow people to represent, communicate, and organize information?

80% Daily ReviewLocal-firstAcademic, not therapeutic
1 · Retrieval Warm-Up

Retrieval Warm-Up

Without notes, write one idea you already know that might connect to Language, Meaning & Communication. Then write one question the lesson should help you answer.

2 · Essential Question

Essential Question

How does language allow people to represent, communicate, and organize information?

3 · Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

  • Describe language as a structured symbolic system.
  • Distinguish phoneme, morpheme, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics at an introductory level.
  • Explain language development as an interaction of biological capacity, experience, and social interaction.
  • Evaluate claims about language and thought without linguistic determinism.
  • Treat multilingualism as normal human linguistic experience rather than confusion.

Use it in context: Explain how language connects to today’s evidence, measure, or explanation. Do not treat knowing the word as the same thing as understanding the construct.
5 · Vocabulary in Context

Vocabulary in Context

language · phoneme · morpheme · syntax · semantics · pragmatics · language development · multilingualism · linguistic context · ambiguity

6 · Core Reading

Substantial Core Reading

Language is more than a collection of words. Sound patterns, meaningful units, grammatical organization, meaning, and context interact to support communication. Language can influence attention, categorization, memory, and habitual ways of describing experience without rigidly determining everything a person can think.

7 · Worked Example

Worked Example / Synthesis

“I saw the person with the telescope” has at least two plausible interpretations. The physical words are identical, but syntax and context affect meaning.

8 · Scientist's Desk

Psychological Scientist's Desk

Evaluate:

  • “Language completely determines thought.” — too strong.
  • “Bilingual children become confused because they have two languages in the brain.” — unsupported oversimplification.
  • “Speaking two languages automatically makes everyone more intelligent.” — also too strong.
9 · Data Lens

Data Lens

Compare fictional recall under same-language and changed-language cue conditions. Students identify the pattern while noting that proficiency, context, familiarity, and individual variation limit broad conclusions.

10 · Claims Laboratory

Claims Laboratory

Calibrate this claim: “One observation about Language, Meaning & Communication tells us everything important about a person.”

11 · Guided Investigation

Guided Investigation

Communication Ambiguity Lab: analyze six ambiguous sentences or short messages. Identify the syntactic or contextual source of ambiguity and rewrite each to make the intended meaning clearer.

11A · Extended Inquiry

Extended Inquiry from the Locked Plan

Research Method Connection

Audit a fictional survey for ambiguous wording, leading language, reading level, translation issues, and culturally specific phrasing. Revise the items.

12 · Independent Assignment

Independent Assignment

Memory Investigation — From Recording Myth to Reconstructive Science

700–1,000 words addressing encoding, organization, retrieval, schema, later information, source monitoring, confidence, evidence, limitation, and real-world application.

Research Notebook Checkpoint
