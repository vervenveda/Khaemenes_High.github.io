4 · Psychology Word of the Day

homeostasis

Formal definition

The dynamic regulation of internal conditions around functional ranges despite changing demands.

Student-friendly definition

The body's ongoing work to keep important internal conditions within workable ranges—not perfectly constant, but regulated.

Related: regulation · autonomic nervous system · endocrine system · feedback · arousal

5 · Vocabulary in Context

Major coordinating systems

Central nervous system

The brain and spinal cord.

Peripheral nervous system

Nerves outside the brain and spinal cord that connect the CNS with the rest of the body.

Somatic system

Supports voluntary skeletal-muscle control and carries sensory information.

Autonomic system

Helps regulate organs and glands, including heart rate, digestion, and other internal functions.

Sympathetic / parasympathetic

Two major autonomic branches that support different patterns of regulation; their effects depend on the organ and situation.

Endocrine system / hormone

Glands release chemical messengers into the bloodstream that can influence tissues throughout the body.

6 · Core Reading

Coordination happens across connected systems

The nervous system is often divided into the central nervous system (CNS) and peripheral nervous system (PNS). The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord. The PNS includes nerves that connect the CNS with muscles, sensory receptors, organs, and glands. This division helps organize anatomy, but real behavior depends on communication across the entire system.

The PNS includes somatic and autonomic functions. Somatic pathways support voluntary movement and carry sensory information. Autonomic pathways help regulate internal organs. The sympathetic and parasympathetic branches are often taught as “fight or flight” versus “rest and digest,” but those phrases are only rough summaries. The branches can have different effects on different organs, and both participate in flexible regulation.

The endocrine system communicates through hormones released into the bloodstream. Hormonal signaling is usually slower than the millisecond-scale electrical signaling of neurons, but its effects can last longer and influence metabolism, growth, reproduction, arousal, and many other processes. The nervous and endocrine systems interact extensively.

Homeostasis does not mean the body stays perfectly fixed. It means regulatory systems respond to changing conditions so that important internal variables remain within workable ranges. Body temperature, blood glucose, hydration, and other variables are regulated dynamically.

Psychologists may measure heart rate, skin conductance, pupil size, hormone concentrations, or other physiological variables. These are useful measures, but they are not direct readings of private mental states. A fast heart rate can occur during fear, excitement, exercise, illness, caffeine use, heat, or anticipation. Interpretation requires context and usually more than one measure.

Scientific boundary: physiology is evidence about bodily state. It does not automatically reveal motive, emotion, honesty, or diagnosis.
7 · Worked Example

Performance scenario

Before a public performance, a student's heart rate rises from 72 to 104 beats per minute.

Observation: heart rate increased by 32 bpm.

Possible explanations: anticipatory arousal, physical movement backstage, caffeine, heat, excitement, or a combination.

Not justified: “The student was definitely afraid.”

8 · Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim audit

Claim: “A fast heart rate proves anxiety.”
  1. Define anxiety and heart-rate measurement.
  2. Ask what other conditions increase heart rate.
  3. Consider combining physiological, behavioral, and self-report measures.
  4. State what the measure can and cannot reveal.

10 · Claims Laboratory

“The sympathetic system is bad stress and the parasympathetic system is good calm.”

Verdict

Oversimplified. Both branches are normal regulatory systems with context-dependent roles.

Better statement

Autonomic branches coordinate bodily functions in changing situations; neither is a moral category.
