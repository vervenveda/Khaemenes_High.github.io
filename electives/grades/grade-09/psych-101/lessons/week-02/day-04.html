4 · Psychology Word of the Day

circadian rhythm

Formal definition

An approximately 24-hour biological rhythm that helps organize sleep-wake timing and other physiological processes.

Student-friendly definition

An internal daily timing pattern that helps your body anticipate when to be more awake or ready for sleep.

Related: biological clock · sleep-wake cycle · light cues · alertness · timing

5 · Vocabulary in Context

States of consciousness

Consciousness

Awareness of internal and external experiences, studied through behavior, report, and physiological measures.

Biological clock

Internal timing mechanisms that help coordinate recurring biological rhythms.

non-REM sleep

Several sleep stages that differ in depth and brain/body activity.

REM sleep

A recurring sleep state associated with rapid eye movements, distinctive brain activity, and vivid dreaming in many episodes.

Attention

Selective allocation of processing resources to some information rather than all available input.

Memory consolidation

Processes through which newly acquired information becomes more stable and integrated over time.

6 · Core Reading

Sleep is an active biological state, not simply “turning off”

Human consciousness changes across waking, drowsiness, and sleep. Psychologists and neuroscientists study these states using behavior, self-report, brain electrical activity, eye movement, muscle activity, and other measures. No single measure captures the entire experience.

Circadian rhythms help coordinate daily changes in sleepiness, alertness, hormone release, temperature, and other processes. Light is an important environmental cue for circadian timing, but schedules, activity, meals, and individual differences also matter. Circadian timing is related to—but not identical with—how long someone has been awake.

Sleep cycles through non-REM and REM states. These stages show distinct patterns of brain and body activity. Across a typical night, the proportion and timing of stages changes. Sleep is associated with restoration, learning, memory, immune and metabolic regulation, and many other processes, but those relationships are complex and should not be reduced to a single slogan.

Sleep loss can impair attention, reaction time, decision-making, and memory performance. Yet group findings do not mean every person shows the same effect at the same amount of sleep. Age, prior sleep, circadian timing, health, task difficulty, caffeine, motivation, and measurement method can influence results.

Dreaming is also commonly misunderstood. Dreams can occur in REM and non-REM sleep. There is no scientifically supported rule that every dream contains a hidden universal message. Psychology can study dream reports and sleep states without turning dream interpretation into certainty.

Because sleep is connected to health, this lesson remains educational rather than diagnostic. Students are not asked to disclose private sleep problems or use the course to diagnose a sleep disorder.

Health boundary: course examples describe general psychological science. Persistent or concerning sleep problems belong with a qualified health professional, not a classroom self-diagnosis.
7 · Worked Example

Immediate performance versus biological state

A participant completes the same reaction-time task after a normal night's sleep and after staying awake much later than usual.

Observation: mean reaction time is 310 ms in the rested condition and 390 ms in the late-wake condition.

Possible interpretation: extended wakefulness or altered circadian timing contributed to slower responding.

Not justified: “The participant became less intelligent.”

8 · Psychological Scientist's Desk

Claim audit

Claim: “I only need four hours of sleep because I feel fine.”
  1. Define “need” and “feel fine.”
  2. Measure sleep duration and timing accurately.
  3. Measure performance, not just subjective alertness.
  4. Consider individual variation and chronic versus one-night patterns.
  5. Avoid turning a personal anecdote into a universal rule.

10 · Claims Laboratory

“Dreams happen only during REM sleep.”

Verdict

Unsupported. Dream reports can occur after awakenings from REM and non-REM sleep, though qualities may differ.

Better statement

REM is strongly associated with vivid dreaming, but dreaming is not exclusive to REM.
