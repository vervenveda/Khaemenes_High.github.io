# Grade 09 Retention & Curriculum Depth Standard · v1.0.0

## Purpose

This standard governs Grade 09 curriculum-depth and retention design without changing existing 80% mastery thresholds, evaluator authority, course-credit rules, or previously earned learner records.

It distinguishes:

- **instructional quantity**: substantive lessons, investigations, assignments and practice;
- **learning quality**: explanation, modeling, application, evidence, error analysis, revision and transfer;
- **retention architecture**: spaced retrieval, cumulative review, alternate reassessment and long-lag transfer;
- **observed retention**: longitudinal learner performance on previously mastered material.

Repository structure can verify the first three. Observed retention must be calculated only from learner attempt evidence over time.

## Academy Grade 09 retention contract

### Weekly instruction

A full-year five-day subject should normally expose 36 instructional weeks and approximately 180 substantive daily learning encounters unless the course is intentionally designated as a shorter credit/module.

### Mastery

Existing Academy mastery remains 80% unless the course's canonical authority explicitly requires a higher standard.

### Delayed retrieval

For objective weekly mastery systems with ten questions, the preferred blueprint is:

- 8 questions from current learning;
- 2 questions retrieved from earlier learning;
- delayed questions must preserve the same difficulty and reasoning expectations rather than becoming vocabulary-only recall.

For 20-question weekly systems, the preferred blueprint is:

- 15 current;
- 5 delayed.

Human-scored disciplines may use non-objective delayed retrieval instead: prior-skill reconstruction, comparison, revision, unseen transfer, oral defense, or evidence-based writing.

### Cumulative checkpoints

A full-year course should include meaningful medium-lag and long-lag cumulative evidence. A cumulative assessment must sample earlier material, not simply rename the newest unit review.

### Reassessment

Below-mastery work should require correction or targeted relearning. When practical, reassessment should use a parallel or newly generated form testing the same standard rather than the identical answer sequence.

### Transfer

Every full-year course must require learners to apply prior knowledge to unfamiliar evidence, contexts, data, texts, problems, or decisions.

### Retention measurement

Retention reports must distinguish:

- first-attempt current-learning score;
- first-attempt delayed-retrieval score;
- retake score;
- cumulative checkpoint score;
- lag from original mastery;
- skill/domain;
- whether the evidence was objective, constructed, performance, or evaluator reviewed.

No repository audit may claim an actual learner retention percentage without longitudinal learner evidence.

## Current Grade 09 reference models

- Pre-Algebra: 8-current + 2-delayed weekly mastery across five reasoning dimensions.
- Algebra I: 8-current + 2-delayed weekly mastery plus mixed-evidence midterm/final.
- Global Studies Honors: retrieval-before-review, unfamiliar-source transfer, corrections, constructed response and cumulative synthesis.
- Psychology 101: targeted correction and parallel-form reassessment.

## 2026-11-20 implementation priorities

1. Integrated Science 9: guarantee 8 current + 2 delayed objective questions in weekly mastery after Week 1.
2. English 9: add lightweight non-grading delayed retrieval prompts that preserve the evaluator-reviewed weekly mastery model.
3. PE 9: guarantee 15 current + 5 delayed questions in the 20-question weekly local mastery form after Week 1, preserving teacher-review authority.
4. French 101: increase meaningful language encounters before increasing assessment volume; retain cumulative mastery while reducing assessment-to-instruction imbalance in the next curriculum revision.
5. Build retention reporting only after delayed-item provenance is explicitly recorded.
