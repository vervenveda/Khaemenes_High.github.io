Science 9 · Unit 01 · Lesson demonstration

From Phenomena to Questions

Ask precise questions, evaluate evidence, and distinguish conclusions from assumptions.

45–60 minutes · Paper-based inquiry · Simulated data only

Existing roadmap preserved for no-script fallback.

Prepare

Essential question

How can we turn an observable phenomenon into a scientific question that identifies measurable evidence without assuming the answer in advance?

Learning objectives

Separate observations from explanations and propose one investigable scientific question.

Materials

Notebook, pencil or keyboard. No equipment or hands-on experiments are necessary.

Safety is always visible.

Do not handle chemicals, flames, biological samples, electrical equipment, or hazardous materials. This exercise uses fictional data and safe paper reasoning.

Explore

1. Begin with a phenomenon, not an answer

Two identical ice cubes placed on different surfaces seem to melt at different rates. What can you observe? What explanation are you tempted to assume?

Write one observation and one separate hypothesis.

Vocabulary & Language of Inquiry

Phenomenon · investigable question · variable · operational definition · evidence · limitation.

2. Build an investigable question

A question should identify a comparison, measurable result, and consistent conditions.

How does the surface beneath an equal-size ice cube affect melting time in the same room conditions?

3. Operational definitions turn vague words into measurable rules

Define exactly how a measurement would be recorded. Replace “melts fast” with a measurable time and an agreed stopping rule.

Investigate

4. Not every scientific question requires a controlled experiment

Observational studies, historical records, safe simulations and existing datasets are often appropriate. Choose the method that fits the question.

5. Evidence sufficiency: what would actually answer the question?

One observation may suggest a question, but repeated measurements, controls and alternative explanations strengthen an inference.

6. Interactive evidence audit

Identify observation, interpretation, proposed evidence, and limitations. Use the fictional sample dataset below.

Explain and Reflect

7. Mentor conference prompt

Ask a mentor to challenge the clarity of your scientific question rather than supply an answer.

Portfolio evidence

Create one question, identify evidence, and explain what your observations cannot prove.

Exit check

Can you describe what is measured, how the evidence will be obtained, and one limitation?
